Where not to use fancy text

Fancy text is fun in a bio or a nickname. It is the wrong choice for anything people must find, read aloud or fill in. Use this list before you paste.

Do not use it for

  • Your real name on forms, bank, exam or government sites. The system compares your entry with a record. A styled letter is a different character, so it will not match, and may be rejected.
  • Anything people search for. A shop name, a business page or a channel name in fancy letters is often not found when someone types the plain name.
  • Long text. A paragraph in script letters is hard to read on a small screen, for everyone.
  • Email addresses and passwords. They accept only a limited set of characters. A password made of fancy letters can lock you out if you cannot type it again on another device.
  • Links and file names. They may break or be changed.

Why accessibility matters

People who are blind or have low vision use a screen reader that speaks the page. Many screen readers handle a styled letter in one of two ways: they read out its full name ("mathematical bold capital A, mathematical bold small l ...") or they skip it. Either way a name in fancy letters can become a long stream of noise or silence. If your audience may include such readers, keep the plain name nearby, for example in the bio text or the caption.

Where it is usually fine

  • A short display name or bio line where the plain name is also given.
  • A short title for a post or a story.
  • A game name where the game accepts it. Check the rules of the game first: some reject special characters.

A simple rule

If a person or a machine has to recognise the text to do something, use plain letters. If the text is for decoration, a few fancy words are fine.

Platforms differ

Whether a platform accepts these characters in a name changes over time and from place to place, and we do not claim that any one platform accepts them. Paste the result into the field and look: if it is rejected or changes, use another style.

Questions

Will fancy text hurt my search ranking?

It can: a search engine reads the characters, not how they look, so a name in fancy letters may not match the plain name. Keep the plain name in the text too.

Do screen readers read fancy text?

Often badly: they may speak each character by its long Unicode name or skip it. Keep the plain name in the same place.
