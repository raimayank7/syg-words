Group Names

Group description and rules: a template that works

The name tells people what a group is; the description tells them how it works. Most groups never write one, and then the same problems come back every week: forwarded messages, off-topic photos, late-night posts, questions asked ten times. A three-line description prevents most of them.

What a good description says

  1. The purpose. One sentence: "This group is for notices from Class 5A to parents."
  2. Who can post. "Admins post notices; parents reply by personal message." Or "Everyone can post."
  3. One or two rules. "No forwards. Please keep messages between 8 am and 9 pm."
  4. Who to ask. "For problems, message the admin."

A template you can copy

Purpose: [what the group is for]. Please post only about this. Timing: please do not post between [10 pm] and [7 am] unless it is urgent. Respect: no forwards of unverified news, no jokes about anyone's religion, caste or looks. Questions: ask the admin, [name].

Rules that members accept

Short rules are read, and long ones are not. Give a reason: "No forwards, because we cannot check them" is easier to accept than "No forwards." Write the rules in the language of the group. Ask the group to agree when it starts, and remind them kindly the first time someone breaks one.

Admin habits that help

  • Make two admins, so the group does not stop if one leaves.
  • Use "Only admins can send messages" for notice groups, and open a second group for chat.
  • Pin the important message, such as dates and addresses.
  • Do not add people without asking; ask them first.

When a group is large

Large groups tend to fill with forwards. Consider a notice-only group plus a small chat group, or use a community with separate groups for separate topics. Remind members of the rules once a month in a friendly way.

Questions

Where do I write the description?

Open the group, tap its name, and choose Add description or Edit. Members can read it at any time.

Should the rules be in the name?

No. A short word such as "Notices" in the name is enough; the rest belongs in the description.
