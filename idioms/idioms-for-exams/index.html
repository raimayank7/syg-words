Idioms and phrases in exams: question types and how to answer

Idioms and phrases appear in school English papers and in the English section of many competitive examinations in India. The questions come in a small number of patterns. Once you know the patterns, your preparation becomes much lighter. The practice questions on this page are written by us; they are not taken from any question paper.

Question patterns and marks differ from one examination to another and can change. Always read the current official syllabus and sample paper of your own board or examination.

Type 1: Choose the meaning

The idiom is given, alone or underlined in a sentence, with four meanings. This is the most common type.

Practice: "The manager decided to turn a blind eye to the late arrivals." (a) punish (b) pretend not to notice (c) look carefully (d) praise. Answer: (b).

How to answer: read the whole sentence first. The sentence usually tells you whether the meaning is good or bad, which removes two options at once.

Type 2: Use it in a sentence

Common in school papers: "Use the following idioms in sentences of your own." The sentence must show the meaning.

Weak: "He burnt the midnight oil." Strong: "He burnt the midnight oil for a month and cleared the entrance test."

Type 3: Fill in the blank

A sentence has a gap, and you choose the idiom or the missing word of an idiom.

Practice: "The news of her selection came out of the ______." (a) red (b) blue (c) green (d) white. Answer: (b), out of the blue.

How to answer: this type tests the fixed words. Learn idioms exactly, including small words such as "a", "the" and "in".

Type 4: Replace the phrase

A plain phrase is underlined, and you choose the idiom that could replace it, or the reverse.

Practice: "The two brothers agree completely on money matters." (a) see eye to eye (b) pull each other's leg (c) face the music (d) cry wolf. Answer: (a).

Type 5: Spot the wrong use

Four sentences use the same idiom; one uses it wrongly. This type needs a real understanding of when an idiom fits.

Practice: Which sentence uses "a piece of cake" wrongly? (a) The quiz was a piece of cake. (b) For her, driving in traffic is a piece of cake. (c) He gave me a piece of cake to solve the sum. (d) Fixing the fan was a piece of cake for the electrician. Answer: (c); there it is literal cake, and the sentence makes no sense.

How to prepare in three weeks

  1. Week 1: learn by theme, about ten a day, using the method on our how to learn idioms page.
  2. Week 2: finish the themes and start the practice quiz daily. Note every idiom you get wrong in a separate list.
  3. Week 3: revise only the wrong list and the pairs that confuse you, such as "pull someone's leg" (tease) and "pull your socks up" (improve).

Hindi papers

Hindi papers ask मुहावरे और लोकोक्तियाँ in the same patterns: अर्थ लिखिए, वाक्य में प्रयोग कीजिए, रिक्त स्थान भरिए. Our page वाक्य प्रयोग कैसे करें covers the sentence question, and this page covers the difference question.

On the day

  • Do the idiom questions early; they take seconds when you know them and cannot be worked out when you do not.
  • If negative marking applies and you cannot remove even one option, think before guessing.
  • In a written answer, underline the idiom in your sentence so that the examiner sees it.
