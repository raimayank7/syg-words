    What you will find here

    An idiom is a phrase that means something other than what its words say. "A piece of cake" is an easy job; "नौ दो ग्यारह होना" is to run away. Idioms make speech lively, and they are asked in almost every English and Hindi paper, from class 5 to competitive examinations. This site collects the common ones and explains each in the simplest words we could find.

    Each English idiom comes with its meaning, the way the idea is said in Hindi, and a sentence from everyday Indian life. Each Hindi muhavara comes in Devanagari with a roman reading, its अर्थ, the sense in English and a वाक्य. All the explanations and sentences are our own writing.

    How to use this site

    1. To look up one idiom, type any word of it in the search box above.
    2. To learn, open one theme and take five idioms a day. The learning guide gives a 15 minute daily plan.
    3. To prepare for a test, read idioms in exams for the question types, then use the practice quiz.
    4. To write better, read how to use idioms in writing before you put one in an essay or a speech.

    Common questions

    What is the difference between an idiom and a muhavara?

    None in kind. "Muhavara" is the Hindi word for an idiom: a phrase whose meaning differs from the meaning of its words. This site has English idioms and Hindi muhavare, each explained in both languages.

    Are the meanings and sentences copied from a book?

    No. The idioms themselves are traditional, but every meaning, Hindi explanation and example sentence here is written by us.

    Can I use these sentences in my homework?

    You may. It is better to read the sentence and then write one of your own about your own life: you will remember the idiom longer and the teacher will see that it is yours.

    Which idioms should I learn first?

    Start with body, animal and food idioms. They are the most common in speech and the easiest to picture. Then follow the four-week plan on the "how to learn idioms" page.

    Is the Hindi form the same in every textbook?

    Not always. Some muhavare have small variants. For an examination, write the form given in your own textbook.
