How the quiz works

Each question shows one idiom or muhavara chosen at random from the 406 on this site, with four meanings. One is right; the other three are the real meanings of other idioms, so guessing by elimination is harder than it looks. After you answer, the right meaning turns green and a link opens the idiom with its example sentence. The score is kept only while this page is open. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.

How to practise so that it helps

  1. Learn first, test later. Read one theme, such as food idioms or आँख पर मुहावरे, before you play.
  2. Say the meaning before you look at the options. Recalling an answer yourself builds memory far better than recognising it in a list.
  3. Write down every miss. Keep a small "wrong list" and read it the next day. Those are the idioms that need you.
  4. Ten questions a day is enough. Short daily practice beats one long sitting.

For teachers

Put the quiz on the classroom screen and let two teams answer in turn. Ask the team to make a sentence with the idiom for a bonus point. For written practice, the exam-style questions on idioms in exams can be copied to the board.

What the quiz does not test

Choosing a meaning is the first half of knowing an idiom. The second half is using it. After every ten questions, pick two idioms you got right and say a sentence with each, aloud. If the sentence comes easily, the idiom is yours. If it does not, open its page from the link under the question and read the example once more.

Common question

Why did I get the same idiom twice? Questions are picked at random, so a repeat can happen. Treat it as free revision.
