English proverbs with meaning and Hindi sayings

30 proverbs every student meets, each with what it means, the Hindi saying closest to it and a sentence that shows how to quote it.

A proverb is a short, complete sentence that carries advice or a truth people have tested over generations. Unlike an idiom, it is not bent to fit your sentence. You quote it whole, usually after the example that proves it: "He blamed the pen for his poor handwriting. A bad workman blames his tools."

Proverbs are useful in three places. In an essay, one proverb makes a strong first or last line. In a speech, it gives listeners something to remember. In daily talk, it settles a point kindly, without a lecture. Use them sparingly: one proverb in a piece of writing is wise, three sound like a sermon.

Many English proverbs have a Hindi saying with the very same thought, which shows how alike people are everywhere. Where such a twin exists it is given under "Hindi saying"; where it does not, we give the meaning in plain Hindi. A few of the Hindi lines are well-known lines from old dohe that have passed into daily speech as sayings.

  • A stitch in time saves nine

    MeaningFixing a small problem at once prevents a bigger one later.

    Hindi sayingसमय पर किया छोटा काम बड़ी मुसीबत से बचाता है

    ExampleI mended the small leak in the tap the same day. A stitch in time saves nine.

How to quote a proverb

  1. Give the situation first, then the proverb. It should arrive as the conclusion.
  2. Do not change its words or tense.
  3. Do not explain it afterwards. If it needs explaining, it was the wrong proverb.
  4. Say "as the saying goes" or "as they say" if you want to introduce it gently.
