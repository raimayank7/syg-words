More than a one-line meaning

Most people look a word up, read one line and close the page. That is enough to get through a sentence and not enough to own the word. A word is really known when you know its different senses, the words it can replace, its opposite, the phrases it forms and the family it belongs to. The word pages on this site are laid out to show exactly those things, sense by sense, with an example sentence for each.

How to use the word finder

  1. Type a word in the box. Its main meanings appear at once, grouped by part of speech, with synonyms.
  2. If the word has a full page, a link takes you to it for the opposites, phrases and word family.
  3. Tap a synonym to look that word up in turn. Following words from one to the next is one of the best ways to grow a vocabulary.

For students and writers

Besides the dictionary, there are six word lists made for school and examination needs: synonyms and antonyms, one-word substitutions, commonly confused words, prefixes and suffixes, collective nouns and words to use instead of "very". The guide how to build your vocabulary gives a fifteen minute daily method.

Common questions

Where do the definitions come from?

From WordNet 3.0, a large database of English words made at Princeton University and free for anyone to use. The Hindi meanings, word lists and guides are written by us. Details are on the About page.

Why does a word have so many meanings?

Common English words are used in many ways. Each numbered meaning is called a sense. Decide the part of speech first, then read down the senses until one fits your sentence.

Does every word have its own page?

No. About a thousand of the most used words have a full page. Thousands of other words can be looked up in the word finder on this page, which shows their main meanings and synonyms.

Does the site give meanings in Hindi?

Many of the full word pages give the commonest meanings of the word in Hindi. These are written by us and are a quick help, not a full translation of every sense.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no login and no fee. What you type in the search box is looked up in your own browser and is not sent to us.
