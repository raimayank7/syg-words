Choosing a baby name: a practical checklist

A name is chosen in a few weeks and used for a lifetime. This checklist covers the things that parents often find out too late. It does not tell you which name to choose, because that is for your family.

1. Say it

Say the full name aloud, with the surname, three times fast. Say it as a teacher would call it from a register. Say it as a grandparent would shout across a courtyard.

2. Check the spelling

Write it in English letters and in your own script. If there are two common spellings, pick one and use it everywhere, since school, passport and Aadhaar records are hard to change. Check how the name appears when the letters are put in other scripts, using a tool such as our name in other scripts page, if your child may live abroad.

3. Think of nicknames and initials

Every name gets a short form. Write down the nicknames your family, friends and classmates are likely to use. Write the initials: do they spell a word you would rather avoid?

4. Check the meaning

Look up the meaning in a dictionary and ask an elder. Some names carry a meaning in one language and a rude sound in another.

5. Think of the future

A name that suits a baby should also suit a doctor, a driver and a grandmother. Imagine it on a business card.

6. Family wishes

Many families have a tradition: a grandparent's name, a first letter, a ceremony. Decide early how much weight to give it, and talk to the elders before the birth, not after.

7. Two lists and a vote

Each parent writes five names. Keep those both like. Sleep on it, and choose with the baby in your arms.

Questions

When should we choose a name?

Many families choose before the birth and confirm at the naming ceremony.

Can a name be changed later?

It can, but it takes legal steps in most places, so choose carefully the first time.
