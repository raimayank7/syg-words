Naming customs in India: ceremonies, letters and surnames

India has many ways to name a child, and they vary by religion, region and family. This page describes some common customs in general terms. Families differ, so treat it as a map, not a rule.

Hindu families

The naming ceremony, namkaran, is held some days after birth (families choose the day, often the eleventh or twelfth). Some families consult a priest or an astrologer for the first letter. Names are often Sanskrit, often taken from gods, epics or virtues. In some regions the father's name or the village name is part of the full name.

Sikh families

After a birth, the family goes to the gurdwara and the granthi opens the Guru Granth Sahib at random. The first letter of the first hymn on the left page gives the first letter of the name. Boys take Singh and girls take Kaur as a second name.

Muslim families

Many families choose a name of Arabic origin, often a name from the Islamic tradition or one that means a virtue, and some hold an aqiqah on the seventh day. Spelling in English varies widely.

Christian families

Many families choose a saint's or a biblical name and a second, local name. The name is formally given at the baptism.

Regional habits

In South India a person may carry the father's name or the village as an initial before the given name. In Maharashtra and Gujarat the father's name is often the middle name. In Bengal a child has a daak naam (pet name) and a bhalo naam (good name).

On documents

Whatever the family custom, the name on the birth certificate is the name used for school, passport and Aadhaar, so choose the spelling with care.

Questions

Do all families follow these customs?

No. They are common, not universal. Families adapt them.

Who decides the name?

Parents, usually with elders. The customs above help; they do not decide.
