Nickname Ideas

How to choose a nickname that sticks

The best nicknames are not invented in a meeting. They appear in a moment, someone says it, people smile, and it stays. You can help one along, and this guide shows how.

Where good nicknames come from

  • The real name, shortened. Rohan becomes Ro, Priyanka becomes Pri, Siddharth becomes Sid. This is the safest source.
  • A sound. A baby who says "bubu" for everything may be Bubu for life.
  • A habit. A person who is always first to eat can be Biryani Boss, if the person laughs.
  • A shared moment. A trip, a match or a joke that only two people remember.
  • A food or a sweet word. Jalebi, Mithu, Laddoo: friendly and easy to say.

Shape the name

Short names last. One or two syllables are easy to call across a room. Repeated syllables (Gudu, Tuktuk) and endings in -u, -i or -oo are soft. Say it aloud in your own voice: if you feel shy or silly saying it, it may not be right yet.

Test it gently

  1. Use it once in private and watch the person's face.
  2. If they smile, use it again. If they go quiet, stop.
  3. Ask: "Do you like it when I call you that?" Many people will tell you.
  4. Check that it does not mean something rude in a language your family speaks.

Public or private?

Some nicknames belong to the home. A pet name that is lovely between a couple can embarrass a person in front of a colleague. Ask where the name may be used, and respect the answer.

When to let it go

People grow out of names. A nickname that suited a child may not suit a manager. If the person asks you to stop, stop, and find a new one if you both want it.

Questions

How long should a nickname be?

One or two syllables is easiest, such as Ro, Pri, Chiku or Mithu.

What if the person does not like it?

Stop using it at once. A nickname is a gift, and a gift can be declined.
