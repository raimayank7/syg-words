Nickname Ideas

Keeping nicknames kind: what to leave out

A nickname can be a hug or a slap. The same word can be both, depending on who says it, where, and how the other person feels. This page gives a few simple rules so that your nicknames stay on the hug side.

Topics to leave out

  • Body and looks. Weight, height, skin colour, hair, glasses and spots are poor sources, even in jest. Children remember such names for decades.
  • Health and ability. Anything about illness, a limp, a stammer or marks at school.
  • Family, religion, caste and region. These are not jokes to most people who carry them.
  • A past mistake. A name that keeps a failure alive is a punishment.

The two-question test

  1. Would the person say it about themselves? If yes, it is probably fine.
  2. Would you say it aloud in front of their grandmother, or their boss? If not, think again.

If a nickname stings

Say so, once, kindly: "I do not like that name, please call me Aman." Most friends will stop. If someone keeps using a name after you have asked, that is not a nickname any more: it is teasing, and you may ask an adult or a teacher for help if you are young.

Old nicknames

A school nickname may follow a person into adult life. When you meet an old friend, use the name they use now, unless they invite you to use the old one.

For parents

A pet name for a child is a gift of affection. Choose one that will not embarrass the child in front of friends as a teenager, and drop it when the child asks.

Questions

Is a teasing nickname ever fine?

Only when the person has clearly shown that they enjoy it, and only among people they trust.

What should a child do about a hurtful nickname?

Say that they do not like it, and tell a teacher or a parent if it continues.
