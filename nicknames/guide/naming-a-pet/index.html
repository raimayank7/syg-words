Naming a pet: dogs, cats, rabbits and birds

A pet name is used hundreds of times a week for years, so it is worth a day of thought. This guide covers what makes a good name and how to teach it.

What makes a good name

  • One or two syllables. Bruno, Coco, Biscuit, Mimi. Long names get shortened anyway.
  • A clear ending. Names ending in a vowel or a firm consonant carry well across a park.
  • Different from your commands. A dog named Bo may confuse "no". A cat named Nia may not.
  • Suits the pet. Colour (Ginger, Smokey), character (Lazy, Zoomie) or food (Jalebi, Mango).
  • Comfortable to call in public. You will shout it in a lane at night.

How to teach the name

  1. Say the name in a happy voice and give a small treat when the pet looks at you.
  2. Repeat ten times a day for a few days. Do not use the name to scold.
  3. Call from a short distance, then from farther away, rewarding each time.
  4. If you change the name of an adult pet, do the same: a new name with treats usually sticks within a week or two.

Small pets

Rabbits, hamsters, fish and birds are named mostly for the family. Let the children vote. Birds learn short names with clear sounds best. Do not expect a goldfish to come when called; the name is for you.

Ending a family argument

Each person writes three names on paper. Remove any that two people dislike. Say each of the rest aloud and watch the pet. Pick the one that gets a smile, a wag or a stretch. Keep the other names for nicknames.

A note on care

A name is the smallest part of owning a pet. Check what the animal needs in food, space, exercise and vet care before you bring it home. This site gives name ideas only, and not medical or care advice: ask a vet.

Questions

Can I rename a rescue dog or cat?

Yes. Use the new name in a happy voice with treats, and most pets adjust in a week or two.

Should I avoid names like commands?

Yes. A name that sounds like "no", "sit" or "down" can confuse the pet.
