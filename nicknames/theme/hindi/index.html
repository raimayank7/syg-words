About hindi and punjabi pet names

In many Indian homes a child, a husband, a wife or a grandparent has a pet name that the school never hears. These names are usually short, repeat a sound and end in a vowel, and they follow the person for life.

How to choose

  • Repeat a syllable: Guddu, Pappu, Munni, Tinku.
  • End in -u, -i or -oo for a soft sound.
  • Use the home language, since the name is private.
  • Let each family decide: the same word can sound loving in one house and rude in another.

What to avoid

Avoid pet names that tease a child about looks or ability, and check that a word does not mean something rude in a nearby language.

Build your own

Words that fit this theme: Chhota, Bada, Pyaara, Meetha, Lalla, Nanha, Sohna, Rani. Words to join them with: Babu, Beta, Raja, Gudiya, Munna, Sonu, Pappu, Laddu. Put one from the first list in front of one from the second, then shorten it until it is easy and warm to say.

The name generator mixes these words into new ideas, and the guides on choosing a nickname, keeping nicknames kind and naming a pet explain what to check first.

Questions

Why do Indian homes have pet names?

A pet name is a sign of affection, used in the family while the official name is used at school and work.

Should I give a pet name to a baby?

Many families do. Pick one that is soft, easy to say and not teasing.
