Prefixes and suffixes

Many long English words are short words wearing extra pieces. A prefix is a piece added at the front: un + happy = unhappy. A suffix is a piece added at the end: happy + ness = happiness. The middle, which carries the main meaning, is the root.

Knowing the common pieces is the fastest honest way to enlarge your vocabulary. A reader who knows that pre- means "before", re- means "again" and -less means "without" can work out "prepaid", "rebuild" and "homeless" without a dictionary, and can make a sensible guess at words never seen before.

Hindi works the same way, with उपसर्ग and प्रत्यय: अ + ज्ञान = अज्ञान, सुंदर + ता = सुंदरता. If you learnt those in school, this is the same idea in English. The first table gives twenty-four prefixes; the suffixes follow below it.

A family from one root

From the single root act, English builds: act, action, active, actor, activity, activate, react, reaction, inactive, interact, transaction, actual, actually. A learner who knows the root and the pieces has thirteen words for the price of one. Our word pages show each word's "word family" for this reason; open act or form to see.

Three cautions

  • Spelling can change at the join. happy + ness = happiness (y becomes i); make + ing = making (e drops); run + er = runner (n doubles).
  • Not every "in-" means "not". "Inside", "income" and "invaluable" (which means very valuable) are traps. "Inflammable" means it can catch fire.
  • Prefixes do not attach freely. We say "unhappy" but "dishonest" and "impossible". The right prefix for each word has to be noticed and remembered.
