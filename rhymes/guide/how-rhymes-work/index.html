How rhymes work: perfect rhymes, near rhymes and sound

Two words rhyme when they sound the same from a certain point to the end. Spelling does not matter: light and bite rhyme, though bough and cough do not. This guide shows the rule that the rhyme finder uses.

The rhyming part

Find the last vowel sound that carries stress, the "beat" of the word. The rhyming part is that vowel and every sound after it. In light it is the sound "eye" plus "t". In delight it is the same, because the stress falls on the last syllable. In lighting it is "eye", "t", "ing", because the stress is on the first syllable.

A perfect rhyme

A perfect (or true) rhyme has the same rhyming part and a different sound just before it. Light and night rhyme: both end in "eye + t", and the sounds before the vowel (l and n) differ. Light and delight do not count as a rhyme in poetry: they are the same sounds repeated, which is called an identical rhyme. The finder leaves such pairs out.

Matching syllables

If the stress falls earlier, the rhyming part is longer. Nation and station rhyme on "ay-shun". These are sometimes called double rhymes. Words with the stress on the third syllable from the end give triple rhymes, which are rare and often used for comic effect.

Near rhymes

A near rhyme (also called slant rhyme, half rhyme or imperfect rhyme) matches part of the rhyming part. The finder uses a simple rule: the same vowel, and an ending that is close in sound, for example m, n and ng, or p and b, or t and d. Time and mine are near rhymes. Writers use near rhymes when a perfect rhyme would force the meaning, and many modern songs rely on them.

Spelling can mislead

  • The same sound is spelled in many ways: blue, through, flew, shoe.
  • The same letters can be said in different ways: rough, though, through, cough.
  • This is why a good rhyme finder works with sounds from a pronunciation dictionary and not with letters.

Accents

Whether two words rhyme can depend on how you speak. The dictionary behind this site records North American English. Some pairs rhyme in one accent and not in another, so read your lines aloud in your own voice.

Questions

Do words need the same spelling to rhyme?

No. They need the same sound from the last stressed vowel to the end.

What is a slant rhyme?

A near rhyme: the words share the vowel or the ending sounds only in part, like time and mine.
