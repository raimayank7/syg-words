Rhyming for songs and poems: tips that work

A rhyme should feel like a small surprise that also feels right. Here are simple habits that help, for a school poem, a birthday song or a rap verse.

1. Write the meaning first

Do not choose your words because they rhyme. Write what you want to say in plain lines. Then find the rhyme for the last word of a line, or change the last word to one that has a good rhyme.

2. Pick the last word with care

The rhyme falls on the last stressed word of the line, so put your most important word there. A line that ends on "the" or "and" cannot carry a rhyme.

3. Mix perfect and near rhymes

Too many perfect rhymes sound like a nursery rhyme. Use a near rhyme now and then (time and mine, home and stone) to keep the line natural. The finder lists both kinds.

4. Avoid the obvious pairs

Pairs like moon and June, heart and apart, fire and desire have been used so often that listeners expect them. Look at the second and third words in the list: a less common word often says something fresh.

5. Vary the rhyme scheme

  • AABB: each pair of lines rhymes. Easy and bouncy, good for children.
  • ABAB: alternate lines rhyme. Common in songs and ballads.
  • ABCB: only the second and fourth lines rhyme. It gives more freedom and is common in folk songs.

6. Rhyme inside the line

A rhyme can sit in the middle of a line as well as at the end ("the light of the night"). Rap writers use these a lot.

7. Read it aloud

Read each line aloud in your own accent and clap the beat. Cut or add a word where the rhythm trips. Never trust a rhyme you have only seen on a page.

A note on other languages

This page and the finder are about English. Hindi, Urdu and Punjabi poetry has its own rhyme tradition (qafiya in ghazal, for example), which follows different rules and is not covered here.

Questions

How many lines should rhyme?

There is no rule. Rhyme every line in a short children's poem; rhyme only some lines in a song so that it does not sound forced.

Is a repeated word a rhyme?

Not a perfect rhyme. Repeating the same word is a refrain or an identical rhyme, which can be used on purpose.
