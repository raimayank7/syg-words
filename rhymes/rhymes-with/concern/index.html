How concern sounds

"concern" has 2 syllables and its rhyming part is the sounds ER (as in "hurt"), N (as in "knee") (the capital letters are sound codes used by the dictionary). A perfect rhyme must match all of them and must start the rhyming part with a different sound, which is why turn is a rhyme and not a repeat.

10 of the 11 perfect rhymes have one syllable, which are the easiest to use at the end of a line.

How the rhymes are spelled

The same sound is written in different ways. Among the perfect rhymes for concern, the commonest word endings are "-urn" (5), "-ern" (4), "-arn" (2). Do not choose a rhyme by its spelling alone: always say both words aloud.

Sound, stress and spelling

Concern is a very common word. Its 7 letters stand for 6 sounds (K AH N S ER N). It has 2 syllables and the main stress falls on syllable 2 of 2. The stress is on the last syllable, which gives a "masculine" rhyme: a single sound that closes the line firmly. There are 1 more letters than sounds, which is normal in English: some letters are silent or work in pairs.

Which rhyme to choose

The most familiar perfect rhymes are turn, return, learn, burn; a reader will understand them at once. The rarest on the list is churn, which may need a dictionary. If a line needs a certain meaning, start with the common words and only then try the less common ones.

Look-alike endings that do not rhyme

Words that end in "-ern" like concern but have a different ending sound include modern, western, northern, southern, eastern, pattern. They look like rhymes on paper and are called eye rhymes; read the pair aloud before you trust the spelling.

Rhymes that are spelled differently

Some perfect rhymes for concern do not end in "-ern" at all: turn, return, learn, burn, earn, urn. This is why a rhyme list is built from sounds, not from letters.

Using a near rhyme

Near rhymes keep the vowel and end in a similar sound (for example m, n and ng, or p and b). Songwriters use them when a perfect rhyme would sound forced. The near rhymes for concern are listed above in the second list.
