Surname Stats

How the Census counts surnames, and what the numbers mean

The figures on this site come from one file, "Frequently Occurring Surnames from the 2010 Census", published by the US Census Bureau. It is a work of the US government and in the public domain, and this page explains in plain words what is in it.

What is counted

The file lists every surname that at least 100 people had in the 2010 Census, with the number of people. It does not count families or households, and it does not count people who gave no surname. Surnames shared by fewer than 100 people are grouped by the Bureau under one line, which this site leaves out, as it has no single name to show.

Rank, count and per 100,000

  • Rank orders names by number of people. Rank 1 is the surname held by the most people.
  • People is the count of people with that surname in 2010.
  • Per 100,000 is the count divided by the population, multiplied by 100,000, so it shows how common a name is regardless of the population size.

Spelling

Surnames are counted as spelled. Variants such as Smith and Smyth are separate entries. Names with spaces or punctuation appear as the Bureau recorded them.

Limits

The file is a count from 2010 and does not show where a name comes from or how common it is in other countries. Names change over time with immigration and marriage.

Questions

Is a surname missing from the list rare?

It had fewer than 100 people with it in 2010, or it was spelled in another way. It may still be common in another country.

Is this the number of families?

No. It counts people, so a family of five with one surname adds five.
