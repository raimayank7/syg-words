How common is your surname?

Type at least two letters of a surname.

What this shows

The US Census Bureau counted how many people had each surname in 2010 and published every name held by at least 100 people. Type a name above to see its rank, the number of people, how many in 100,000 people it is, and how those people described themselves in the six Census groups. The top names are in ten pages of 100, and six pages list the names most often held by each group.

How to read it

A high rank means many people share the name, not that it is old or special. The percentages describe a group, not a person. How the Census counts surnames and reading the percentages explain both, and research tips help if you want to trace your own name.

Questions

Where do the numbers come from?

From the US Census Bureau's 2010 surname file, a public domain government work.

Why is my surname missing?

The file lists only surnames held by at least 100 people in 2010, so rare names and different spellings may be missing.
