Username safety: what not to put in your name

A username looks harmless, but it is public and permanent in many places. Strangers can read it, search for it and combine it with other things you share. This page lists the things a username should not give away and a short checklist.

What a username can give away

  • Your real identity. A full name, a surname with a birth year, or the name of your school tells people who you are.
  • Your place. A town, a street or a school name can help a stranger find you offline.
  • Your age. A birth year or a class in the name tells people how young you are, which is a risk for children.
  • Your passwords. People who use a pet's name or a birth date in a password often use the same in their username. Keep the two apart.

For children and teenagers

Children should use a name that does not show their name, age, school or town. Parents can help by choosing together, and by agreeing that the child will not share the username with strangers. Many games and apps have a rule about the minimum age; follow it.

Impersonation and scams

Scammers copy names that look like a bank, a government office or a shop, changing a letter (for example a capital I for a lowercase l). Do not trust a message only because the sender's name looks right. Do not choose a name that imitates an official body: it can lead to your account being closed and, in some cases, to a legal problem.

A short checklist

  1. No phone number, birth date, address or school.
  2. No full real name for games and open social apps.
  3. Not the same as your email or your password.
  4. Not copying a person, a brand or an office.
  5. Easy to change if you are uncomfortable later.

If you already chose badly

Most apps let you change a username, sometimes only once in a while. If yours gives away too much, change it and review who can see your profile. If someone is bothering you through a username, use the app's block and report options.

Questions

Is it safe to put my first name in a username?

A first name alone is common and generally low risk. Do not add your surname, age or town.

Can a username be changed?

Often yes, but each app has its own rules. Check in the profile settings.
