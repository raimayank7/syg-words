Lists with explanations

A bare list of words teaches very little. A learner needs to know when a word is right and when it is not. So each list here opens with a short explanation of what the list is for, and ends with the grammar points, the common mistakes and a way to practise. The lists are our own work, written for Indian students and everyday writers, with Hindi meanings where they help.

Which list is for whom

  • Writing essays, letters and stories: words instead of "very" and commonly confused words.
  • School grammar: collective nouns, prefixes and suffixes, and synonyms and antonyms.
  • Competitive examinations: one-word substitutions and the synonyms and antonyms list. Always check the current syllabus of your own examination.

How to learn from a list

  1. Do not read the whole list. Choose six words that you will actually use this week.
  2. Write one true sentence of your own with each.
  3. Look the word up in the word finder to see its other meanings and its examples.
  4. Come back in three days and test yourself by covering one column.

The full method is in how to build your vocabulary.
