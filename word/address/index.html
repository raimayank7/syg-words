Address: meaning, synonyms and examples

Address is used as a noun (8 senses) and verb (10 senses). Its most common meaning is: (computer science) the code that identifies where a piece of information is stored.

In Hindi
पता; संबोधित करना
Synonyms
computer address, reference, speech, destination, name and address, savoir-faire, turn to, speak, direct, call
Word family
addressee, addressable

Address as a noun

  1. (computer science) the code that identifies where a piece of information is stored.

    Synonyms: computer address, reference · A kind of: code · Kinds: argument

  2. The place where a person or organization can be found or communicated with.

    A kind of: geographic point · Kinds: mailing address, street address, residence, business address

  3. The act of delivering a formal spoken communication to an audience.

    “He listened to an address on minor Roman poets.”

    Synonyms: speech · A kind of: speech act · Kinds: allocution, colloquium, dithyramb, impromptu, inaugural address, lecture, litany, oratory

  4. The manner of speaking to another individual.

    “He failed in his manner of address to the captain.”

    A kind of: manner of speaking

  5. A sign in front of a house or business carrying the conventional form by which its location is described.

    A kind of: street sign

  6. Written directions for finding some location; written on letters or packages that are to be delivered to that location.

    Synonyms: destination, name and address · A kind of: direction · Kinds: return address

  7. The stance assumed by a golfer in preparation for hitting a golf ball.

    A kind of: stance

  8. Social skill.

    Synonyms: savoir-faire · A kind of: tact

Address as a verb

  1. Speak to.

    “He addressed the crowd outside the window.”

    Synonyms: turn to · A kind of: communicate · Kinds: ask, address

  2. Give a speech to.

    “The chairman addressed the board of trustees.”

    Synonyms: speak · Kinds: blaze away, memorialize, keynote, harangue

  3. Put an address on (an envelope).

    Synonyms: direct · A kind of: label · Kinds: misdirect, instrument, re-address

  4. Direct a question at someone.

    A kind of: target

  5. Address or apply oneself to something, direct one's efforts towards something, such as a question.

    A kind of: use

  6. Greet, as with a prescribed form, title, or name.

    “He always addresses me with `Sir'.” “Call me Mister.” “She calls him by first name.”

    Synonyms: call · A kind of: address

  7. Access or locate by address.

    A kind of: access

  8. Act on verbally or in some form of artistic expression.

    “This book deals with incest.” “The course covered all of Western Civilization.” “The new book treats the history of China.”

    Synonyms: cover, treat, handle, plow, deal · A kind of: broach · Kinds: theologize, discourse

  9. Speak to someone.

    Synonyms: accost, come up to · A kind of: come · Kinds: greet, approach

  10. Adjust and aim (a golf ball) at in preparation of hitting.

    A kind of: align

Phrases and compounds with "address"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • re-address (verb) Put a new address on (an envelope), as for forwarding.
  • return address (noun) The address of the sender of a letter or parcel indicating where it should be returned if it cannot be delivered.
  • street address (noun) The address where a person or organization can be found.
  • form of address (noun) An identifying appellation signifying status or function: e.g. `Mr.' or `General'.
  • keynote address (noun) A speech setting forth the keynote.
  • mailing address (noun) The address where a person or organization can be communicated with.
  • business address (noun) The address at which a business is located.
  • computer address (noun) (computer science) the code that identifies where a piece of information is stored.
  • name and address (noun) Written directions for finding some location; written on letters or packages that are to be delivered to that location.
  • inaugural address (noun) An address delivered at an inaugural ceremony (especially by a United States president).
  • gettysburg address (noun) A three-minute address by Abraham Lincoln during the American Civil War at the dedication of a national cemetery on the site of the Battle of Gettysburg (November 19, 1863).
  • nominating address (noun) An address (usually at a political convention) proposing the name of a candidate to run for election.
  • salutatory address (noun) An opening or welcoming statement (especially one delivered at graduation exercises).
  • valedictory address (noun) A farewell oration (especially one delivered during graduation exercises by an outstanding member of a graduating class).
  • public address system (noun) An electronic amplification system used as a communication system in public areas.
