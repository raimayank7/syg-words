Base: meaning, synonyms and examples

Base is used as a verb (2 senses), noun (14 senses) and adjective (7 senses). Its most common meaning is: Use as a basis for; found on.

In Hindi
आधार
Synonyms
establish, ground, found, base of operations, foundation, fundament, foot, groundwork, substructure, understructure
Word family
basal, basilar, basilary, basify, baseness

Base as a verb

  1. Use as a basis for; found on.

    “Base a claim on some observation.”

    Synonyms: establish, ground, found · Kinds: build

  2. Situate as a center of operations.

    “We will base this project in the new lab.”

    A kind of: situate

Base as a noun

  1. Installation from which a military force initiates operations.

    “The attack wiped out our forward bases.”

    Synonyms: base of operations · A kind of: military installation · Kinds: air base, army base, firebase, navy base, rocket base

  2. Lowest support of a structure.

    “It was built on a base of solid rock.” “He stood at the foot of the tower.”

    Synonyms: foundation, fundament, foot, groundwork, substructure, understructure · A kind of: support · Kinds: bed, raft foundation

  3. A place that the runner must touch before scoring.

    “He scrambled to get back to the bag.”

    Synonyms: bag · A kind of: baseball equipment · Kinds: first base, home plate, second base, third base

  4. The bottom or lowest part.

    “The base of the mountain.”

    A kind of: part

  5. (anatomy) the part of an organ nearest its point of attachment.

    “The base of the skull.”

    A kind of: bottom

  6. A lower limit.

    “The government established a wage floor.”

    Synonyms: floor · A kind of: control · Kinds: price floor, wage floor

  7. The fundamental assumptions from which something is begun or developed or calculated or explained.

    “The whole argument rested on a basis of conjecture.”

    Synonyms: basis, foundation, fundament, groundwork, cornerstone · A kind of: assumption · Kinds: meat and potatoes

  8. A support or foundation.

    “The base of the lamp.”

    Synonyms: pedestal, stand · A kind of: support · Kinds: brass monkey, staddle, trivet, trivet

  9. A phosphoric ester of a nucleoside; the basic structural unit of nucleic acids (DNA or RNA).

    Synonyms: nucleotide · Kinds: base pair, adenosine monophosphate, adenosine diphosphate, adenosine triphosphate, deoxyadenosine monophosphate, deoxycytidine monophosphate, deoxyguanosine monophosphate, deoxythymidine monophosphate

  10. Any of various water-soluble compounds capable of turning litmus blue and reacting with an acid to form a salt and water.

    “Bases include oxides and hydroxides of metals and ammonia.”

    Synonyms: alkali · A kind of: compound · Kinds: pyridine, purine, purine, imidazole, melamine, pyrimidine

  11. The bottom side of a geometric figure from which the altitude can be constructed.

    “The base of the triangle.”

    A kind of: flank

  12. The most important or necessary part of something.

    “The basis of this drink is orange juice.”

    Synonyms: basis · A kind of: part

  13. (numeration system) the positive integer that is equivalent to one in the next higher counting place.

    “10 is the radix of the decimal system.”

    Synonyms: radix · A kind of: number

  14. The place where you are stationed and from which missions start and end.

    Synonyms: home · A kind of: location

Base as an adjective

  1. Serving as or forming a base.

    “The painter applied a base coat followed by two finishing coats.”

    Synonyms: basal · Similar: basic

  2. Of low birth or station (`base' is archaic in this sense).

    “Baseborn wretches with dirty faces.” “Of humble (or lowly) birth.”

    Synonyms: baseborn, humble, lowly · Similar: lowborn

  3. (used of metals) consisting of or alloyed with inferior metal.

    “Base coins of aluminum.” “A base metal.”

    Similar: inferior

  4. Not adhering to ethical or moral principles.

    “Base and unpatriotic motives.” “A base, degrading way of life.” “Cheating is dishonorable.”

    Synonyms: immoral · Similar: wrong

  5. Having or showing an ignoble lack of honor or morality.

    “That liberal obedience without which your army would be a base rabble.” “Taking a mean advantage.” “Chok'd with ambition of the meaner sort.”

    Synonyms: mean, meanspirited · Similar: ignoble

  6. Illegitimate.

    Synonyms: baseborn · Similar: illegitimate

  7. Debased; not genuine.

    “An attempt to eliminate the base coinage.”

    Similar: counterfeit

Phrases and compounds with "base"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • on base (adverb) On first or second or third base.
  • air base (noun) A base for military aircraft.
  • base hit (noun) (baseball) the successful act of striking a baseball in such a way that the batter reaches base safely.
  • off-base (adjective) Located outside a military base.
  • tax base (noun) Collective value of taxable assets.
  • army base (noun) A large base of operations for an army.
  • base pair (noun) One of the pairs of chemical bases joined by hydrogen bonds that connect the complementary strands of a DNA molecule or of an RNA molecule that has two strands; the base pairs are adenine with thymine and guanine with cytosine in DNA and adenine with uracil and guanine with cytosine in RNA.
  • base rate (noun) The interest rate set by the Bank of England for lending to other banks.
  • home base (noun) (baseball) base consisting of a rubber slab where the batter stands; it must be touched by a base runner in order to score.
  • navy base (noun) Base of operations for a naval fleet.
  • base metal (noun) A metal that is common and not considered precious.
  • first base (noun) The base that must be touched first by a base runner in baseball.
  • third base (noun) The base that must be touched third by a base runner in baseball.
  • touch base (verb) Establish communication with someone.
  • base runner (noun) A baseball player on the team at bat who is on base (or attempting to reach a base).
  • rocket base (noun) A military base for rocket missiles.
