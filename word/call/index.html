Call: meaning, synonyms and examples

Call is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (13 senses). Its most common meaning is: Assign a specified (usually proper) proper name to.

In Hindi
बुलाना, फ़ोन करना
Synonyms
name, telephone, call up, phone, ring, shout, shout out, cry, yell, scream
Word family
caller, callable, calling

Call as a verb

  1. Assign a specified (usually proper) proper name to.

    “They named their son David.” “The new school was named after the famous Civil Rights leader.”

    Synonyms: name · A kind of: label · Kinds: baptize, refer, style, dub, rename, entitle, term, tag

  2. Ascribe a quality to or give a name of a common noun that reflects a quality.

    “She called her children lazy and ungrateful.”

    A kind of: label · Kinds: misname, post

  3. Get or try to get into communication (with someone) by telephone.

    “I tried to call you all night.” “Take two aspirin and call me in the morning.”

    Synonyms: telephone, call up, phone, ring · A kind of: telecommunicate · Kinds: cell phone, call in

  4. Utter a sudden loud cry.

    “She cried with pain when the doctor inserted the needle.” “I yelled to her from the window but she couldn't hear me.”

    Synonyms: shout, shout out, cry, yell, scream, holler, hollo, squall · A kind of: utter · Kinds: hollo, hurrah, halloo, whoop, shriek, howl, squawk

  5. Order, request, or command to come.

    “She was called into the director's office.” “Call the police!”

    Synonyms: send for · A kind of: order · Kinds: summon, beep, recall, call in, lift, muster, hail, summon

  6. Pay a brief visit.

    “The mayor likes to call on some of the prominent citizens.”

    Synonyms: visit, call in · A kind of: meet · Kinds: see, see, drop by

  7. Call a meeting; invite or command to meet.

    “The Wannsee Conference was called to discuss the `Final Solution'.” “The new dean calls meetings every week.”

    A kind of: request

  8. Read aloud to check for omissions or absentees.

    “Call roll.”

    A kind of: read

  9. Send a message or attempt to reach someone by radio, phone, etc.; make a signal to in order to transmit a message.

    “Hawaii is calling!” “A transmitter in Samoa was heard calling.”

    A kind of: telecommunicate

  10. Utter a characteristic note or cry.

    “Bluejays called to one another.”

    A kind of: utter

  11. Stop or postpone because of adverse conditions, such as bad weather.

    “Call a football game.”

    A kind of: postpone

  12. Greet, as with a prescribed form, title, or name.

    “He always addresses me with `Sir'.” “Call me Mister.” “She calls him by first name.”

    Synonyms: address · A kind of: address

  13. Make a stop in a harbour.

    “The ship will call in Honolulu tomorrow.”

    A kind of: stop

  14. Demand payment of (a loan).

    “Call a loan.”

    Synonyms: call in · A kind of: demand

Call as a noun

  1. A telephone connection.

    “She reported several anonymous calls.” “He placed a phone call to London.” “He heard the phone ringing but didn't want to take the call.”

    Synonyms: phone call, telephone call · A kind of: telephone · Kinds: call-back, collect call, call-in, crank call, local call, long distance, conference call, wake-up call

  2. A special disposition (as if from a divine source) to pursue a particular course.

    “He was disappointed that he had not heard the Call.”

    A kind of: inclination

  3. A loud utterance; often in protest or opposition.

    “The speaker was interrupted by loud cries from the rear of the audience.”

    Synonyms: cry, outcry, yell, shout, vociferation · A kind of: utterance · Kinds: bellow, catcall, clamor, halloo, hoot, hosanna, noise, scream

  4. A demand especially in the phrase "the call of duty".

    “The call of duty.”

    Synonyms: claim · A kind of: demand

  5. The characteristic sound produced by a bird.

    “A bird will not learn its song unless it hears it at an early age.”

    Synonyms: birdcall, birdsong, song · A kind of: animal communication · Kinds: bell-like call, two-note call

  6. A brief social visit.

    “Senior professors' wives no longer make afternoon calls on newcomers.” “The characters in Henry James' novels are forever paying calls on each other, usually in the parlor of some residence.”

    A kind of: visit

  7. A demand by a broker that a customer deposit enough to bring his margin up to the minimum requirement.

    Synonyms: margin call · A kind of: demand

  8. A demand for a show of hands in a card game.

    “After two raises there was a call.”

    A kind of: demand

  9. A request.

    “Many calls for Christmas stories.” “Not many calls for buggywhips.”

    A kind of: request · Kinds: recall

  10. An instruction that interrupts the program being executed.

    “Pascal performs calls by simply giving the name of the routine to be executed.”

    A kind of: instruction · Kinds: function call, system call

  11. A visit in an official or professional capacity.

    “The pastor's calls on his parishioners.” “The salesman's call on a customer.”

    A kind of: visit · Kinds: round

  12. (sports) the decision made by an umpire or referee.

    “He was ejected for protesting the call.”

    A kind of: decision

  13. The option to buy a given stock (or stock index or commodity future) at a given price before a given date.

    Synonyms: call option · A kind of: option

Phrases and compounds with "call"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • call at (verb) Enter a harbor.
  • call in (verb) Summon to enter.
  • call on (verb) Have recourse to or make an appeal or request for help or information to.
  • call up (verb) Bring forward for consideration.
  • call-in (noun) A telephone call to a radio station or a television station in which the caller participates in the on-going program.
  • call box (noun) Booth for using a telephone.
  • call for (verb) Express the need or desire for; ask for.
  • call off (verb) Postpone indefinitely or annul something that was scheduled.
  • call out (verb) Utter aloud; often with surprise, horror, or joy.
  • call-out (noun) A challenge to a fight or duel.
  • call back (verb) Cause to be returned.
  • call down (verb) Summon into action or bring into existence, often as if by magic.
  • call fire (noun) Fire delivered on a specific target in response to a request from the supported unit.
  • call loan (noun) A loan that is repayable on demand.
  • call mark (noun) A mark consisting of characters written on a book; used to indicate shelf location.
  • call-back (noun) A return call.
