Cover: meaning, synonyms and examples

Cover is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (10 senses). Its most common meaning is: Provide with a covering or cause to be covered.

In Hindi
ढकना; ढक्कन, आवरण
Synonyms
spread over, continue, extend, treat, handle, plow, deal, address, embrace, encompass
Opposites
uncover
Word family
covering, coverage, coverlet

Cover as a verb

  1. Provide with a covering or cause to be covered.

    “Cover her face with a handkerchief.” “Cover the child with a blanket.” “Cover the grave with flowers.”

    Opposite: uncover · Kinds: frost, frost, daub, roof, mulch, turf, bank, carpet

  2. Form a cover over.

    “The grass covered the grave.”

    Synonyms: spread over · A kind of: touch · Kinds: flood, bloody, mist, glaciate, strew, grass, coat, submerge

  3. Span an interval of distance, space or time.

    “The war extended over five years.” “The period covered the turn of the century.” “My land extends over the hills on the horizon.”

    Synonyms: continue, extend · A kind of: be · Kinds: cross, sweep, overlap, spread, ridge, dot

  4. Provide for.

    “The grant doesn't cover my salary.”

    A kind of: satisfy

  5. Act on verbally or in some form of artistic expression.

    “This book deals with incest.” “The course covered all of Western Civilization.” “The new book treats the history of China.”

    Synonyms: treat, handle, plow, deal, address · A kind of: broach · Kinds: theologize, discourse

  6. Include in scope; include as part of something broader; have as one's sphere or territory.

    “This group encompasses a wide range of people from different backgrounds.” “This should cover everyone in the group.”

    Synonyms: embrace, encompass, comprehend · A kind of: include

  7. Travel across or pass over.

    “The caravan covered almost 100 miles each day.”

    Synonyms: traverse, track, cross, pass over, get over, get across, cut through, cut across · A kind of: pass · Kinds: tramp, stride, walk, crisscross, ford, bridge, jaywalk, drive

  8. Be responsible for reporting the details of, as in journalism.

    “Snow reported on China in the 1950's.” “The cub reporter covered New York City.”

    Synonyms: report · A kind of: inform

  9. Hold within range of an aimed firearm.

    A kind of: protect

  10. To take an action to protect against future problems.

    “Count the cash in the drawer twice just to cover yourself.”

    A kind of: protect

  11. Hide from view or knowledge.

    “The President covered the fact that he bugged the offices in the White House.”

    Synonyms: cover up · A kind of: hide · Kinds: whitewash

  12. Protect or defend (a position in a game).

    “He covered left field.”

    A kind of: protect · Kinds: double-team

  13. Maintain a check on; especially by patrolling.

    “The second officer covered the top floor.”

    A kind of: see

  14. Protect by insurance.

    “The insurance won't cover this.”

    Synonyms: insure, underwrite · A kind of: guarantee · Kinds: reinsure, indemnify

Cover as a noun

  1. A covering that serves to conceal or shelter something.

    “A screen of trees afforded privacy.” “Under cover of darkness.” “The brush provided a covert for game.”

    Synonyms: screen, covert, concealment · A kind of: covering · Kinds: blind, camouflage, shoji, stalking-horse

  2. Bedding that keeps a person warm in bed.

    “He pulled the covers over his head and went to sleep.”

    Synonyms: blanket · A kind of: bedclothes · Kinds: afghan, electric blanket, mackinaw, manta, security blanket

  3. The act of concealing the existence of something by obstructing the view of it.

    “The cover concealed their guns from enemy aircraft.”

    Synonyms: covering, screening, masking · A kind of: concealment

  4. The protective covering on the front, back, and spine of a book.

    “The book had a leather binding.”

    Synonyms: binding, book binding, back · A kind of: protective covering · Kinds: half binding, three-quarter binding

  5. A natural object that covers or envelops.

    “Under a covering of dust.” “The fox was flushed from its cover.”

    Synonyms: covering, natural covering · A kind of: natural object · Kinds: scale, shell, test, body covering, sheath, integument, blanket, crust

  6. Covering for a hole (especially a hole in the top of a container).

    “He removed the top of the carton.” “He couldn't get the top off of the bottle.” “Put the cover back on the kettle.”

    Synonyms: top · A kind of: covering · Kinds: cap, lid, manhole cover, screwtop

  7. Fire that makes it difficult for the enemy to fire on your own individuals or formations.

    “Artillery provided covering fire for the withdrawal.”

    Synonyms: covering fire · A kind of: fire

  8. A fixed charge by a restaurant or nightclub over and above the charge for food and drink.

    Synonyms: cover charge · A kind of: fixed charge

  9. A recording of a song that was first recorded or made popular by somebody else.

    “They made a cover of a Beatles' song.”

    Synonyms: cover version, cover song · A kind of: recording

  10. A false identity and background (especially one created for an undercover agent).

    “Her new name and passport are cover for her next assignment.”

    A kind of: concealment

Phrases and compounds with "cover"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • cover up (verb) Hide from view or knowledge.
  • cover-up (noun) Concealment that attempts to prevent something scandalous from becoming public.
  • air cover (noun) The use of military aircraft to provide protection against attack by enemy aircraft during ground or naval operations.
  • bed cover (noun) Decorative cover for a bed.
  • cover for (verb) Provide an excuse or alibi for someone so as to cover up guilt.
  • cover crop (noun) Crop planted to prevent soil erosion and provide green manure.
  • cover girl (noun) A very pretty girl who works as a photographer's model.
  • cover slip (noun) A small and very thin piece of glass used to cover the specimen on a microscope slide.
  • cover song (noun) A recording of a song that was first recorded or made popular by somebody else.
  • dust cover (noun) A paper jacket for a book; a jacket on which promotional information is usually printed.
  • lens cover (noun) Cap used to keep lens free of dust when not in use.
  • soft-cover (noun) A book with paper covers.
  • cloud cover (noun) The state of the sky when it is covered by clouds.
  • cover glass (noun) A small and very thin piece of glass used to cover the specimen on a microscope slide.
  • cover plate (noun) Covering consisting of a plate used to cover over or close in a chamber or receptacle.
  • floor cover (noun) A covering for a floor.
