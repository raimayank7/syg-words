Cut: meaning, synonyms and examples

Cut is used as a verb (14 senses), adjective (9 senses) and noun (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: Separate with or as if with an instrument.

In Hindi
काटना
Synonyms
reduce, cut down, cut back, trim, trim down, trim back, bring down, swerve, sheer, curve
Opposites
uncut
Word family
cutter, cutting

Cut as a verb

  1. Separate with or as if with an instrument.

    “Cut the rope.”

    A kind of: separate · Kinds: manicure, scissor, plane, slice, pink, jag, carve, carve

  2. Cut down on; make a reduction in.

    “Reduce your daily fat intake.” “The employer wants to cut back health benefits.”

    Synonyms: reduce, cut down, cut back, trim, trim down, trim back, bring down · A kind of: decrease · Kinds: shorten, spill, quench, retrench, slash, thin out, thin, take away

  3. Turn sharply; change direction abruptly.

    “The car cut to the left at the intersection.” “The motorbike veered to the right.”

    Synonyms: swerve, sheer, curve, trend, veer, slue, slew · A kind of: turn · Kinds: peel off, yaw

  4. Make an incision or separation.

    “Cut along the dotted line.”

  5. Discharge from a group.

    “The coach cut two players from the team.”

    A kind of: free

  6. Form by probing, penetrating, or digging.

    “Cut a hole.” “Cut trenches.” “The sweat cut little rivulets into her face.”

    A kind of: make · Kinds: blast

  7. Style and tailor in a certain fashion.

    “Cut a dress.”

    Synonyms: tailor · A kind of: design · Kinds: gore

  8. Hit (a ball) with a spin so that it turns in the opposite direction.

    “Cut a Ping-Pong ball.”

    A kind of: hit

  9. Make out and issue.

    “Write out a check.” “Cut a ticket.” “Please make the check out to me.”

    Synonyms: write out, issue, make out · A kind of: write · Kinds: check

  10. Cut and assemble the components of.

    “Edit film.” “Cut recording tape.”

    Synonyms: edit, edit out · A kind of: change

  11. Intentionally fail to attend.

    “Cut class.”

    Synonyms: skip · A kind of: miss · Kinds: bunk off

  12. Be able to manage or manage successfully.

    “I can't hack it anymore.” “She could not cut the long days in the office.”

    Synonyms: hack · A kind of: cope

  13. Give the appearance or impression of.

    “Cut a nice figure.”

    A kind of: look

  14. Move (one's fist).

    “His opponent cut upward toward his chin.”

    A kind of: move

Cut as an adjective

  1. Separated into parts or laid open or penetrated with a sharp edge or instrument.

    “The cut surface was mottled.” “Cut tobacco.” “Blood from his cut forehead.”

    Opposite: uncut · Similar: chopped, cut up, incised, perforated, pierced, severed

  2. Fashioned or shaped by cutting.

    “A well-cut suit.” “Cut diamonds.” “Cut velvet.”

    Opposite: uncut · Similar: cut out, hewn, sheared, slashed

  3. With parts removed.

    “The drastically cut film.”

    Synonyms: shortened · Similar: abridged

  4. Made neat and tidy by trimming.

    “His neatly trimmed hair.”

    Synonyms: trimmed · Similar: clipped

  5. (used of grass or vegetation) cut down with a hand implement or machine.

    “The smell of newly mown hay.”

    Synonyms: mown · Similar: new-mown

  6. (of pages of a book) having the folds of the leaves trimmed or slit.

    “The cut pages of the book.”

    Opposite: uncut

  7. (of a male animal) having the testicles removed.

    “A cut horse.”

    Synonyms: emasculated, gelded

  8. (used of rates or prices) reduced usually sharply.

    “The slashed prices attracted buyers.”

    Synonyms: slashed · Similar: decreased

  9. Mixed with water.

    “A cup of thinned soup.”

    Synonyms: thinned, weakened · Similar: diluted

Cut as a noun

  1. A share of the profits.

    “Everyone got a cut of the earnings.”

    A kind of: share · Kinds: rake-off

  2. (film) an immediate transition from one shot to the next.

    “The cut from the accident scene to the hospital seemed too abrupt.”

    A kind of: transition · Kinds: jump cut

  3. A trench resembling a furrow that was made by erosion or excavation.

    Synonyms: gash · A kind of: furrow

  4. A step on some scale.

    “He is a cut above the rest.”

    A kind of: gradation

  5. A wound made by cutting.

    “He put a bandage over the cut.”

    Synonyms: gash, slash, slice · A kind of: wound

  6. A piece of meat that has been cut from an animal carcass.

    Synonyms: cut of meat · A kind of: meat · Kinds: roast, confit, chop, chine, leg, side, forequarter, hindquarter

  7. A remark capable of wounding mentally.

    “The unkindest cut of all.”

    Synonyms: stinger · A kind of: abuse

  8. A distinct selection of music from a recording or a compact disc.

    “He played the first cut on the cd.” “The title track of the album.”

    Synonyms: track · A kind of: excerpt

  9. The omission that is made when an editorial change shortens a written passage.

    “An editor's deletions frequently upset young authors.” “Both parties agreed on the excision of the proposed clause.”

    Synonyms: deletion, excision · A kind of: editing

  10. The style in which a garment is cut.

    “A dress of traditional cut.”

    A kind of: fashion

  11. A canal made by erosion or excavation.

    A kind of: canal

  12. A refusal to recognize someone you know.

    “The snub was clearly intentional.”

    Synonyms: snub, cold shoulder · A kind of: rebuff

  13. In baseball; a batter's attempt to hit a pitched ball.

    “He took a vicious cut at the ball.”

    Synonyms: baseball swing, swing · A kind of: stroke

  14. (sports) a stroke that puts reverse spin on the ball.

    “Cuts do not bother a good tennis player.”

    Synonyms: undercut · A kind of: stroke

Phrases and compounds with "cut"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • cut in (verb) Allow someone to have a share or profit.
  • cut to (verb) Move to another scene when filming.
  • cut up (verb) Cut to pieces.
  • cut-in (noun) (broadcasting) a local announcement inserted into a network program.
  • cut-up (noun) Someone who plays practical jokes on others.
  • cut off (verb) Make a break in.
  • cut out (verb) Delete or remove.
  • low-cut (adjective) (of a garment) having a low-cut neckline.
  • pay cut (noun) The act of reducing a salary.
  • tax cut (noun) The act of reducing taxation.
  • crew cut (noun) A closely cropped haircut; usually for men.
  • cut away (verb) Move quickly to another scene or focus when filming.
  • cut back (verb) Return in time.
  • cut down (verb) Cut down on; make a reduction in.
  • cut into (verb) Turn up, loosen, or remove earth.
  • cut rate (noun) A price below the standard price.
