Day: meaning, synonyms and examples

Day is used as a noun (10 senses). Its most common meaning is: Time for Earth to make a complete rotation on its axis.

In Hindi
दिन
Synonyms
twenty-four hours, twenty-four hour period, solar day, mean solar day, daytime, daylight, sidereal day
Opposites
night
Word family
daily

Day as a noun

  1. Time for Earth to make a complete rotation on its axis.

    “Two days later they left.” “They put on two performances every day.” “There are 30,000 passengers per day.”

    Synonyms: twenty-four hours, twenty-four hour period, solar day, mean solar day · A kind of: time unit · Kinds: tomorrow, today, yesterday, morrow, eve, date, date

  2. Some point or period in time.

    “It should arrive any day now.” “After that day she never trusted him again.” “Those were the days.”

    A kind of: time · Kinds: off-day

  3. A day assigned to a particular purpose or observance.

    “Mother's Day.”

    A kind of: calendar day · Kinds: commencement day, leap day, rag day, red-letter day, payday, polling day, field day, field day

  4. The time after sunrise and before sunset while it is light outside.

    “The dawn turned night into day.” “It is easier to make the repairs in the daytime.”

    Synonyms: daytime, daylight · Opposite: night · A kind of: time period · Kinds: afternoon, midafternoon, evening

  5. The recurring hours when you are not sleeping (especially those when you are working).

    “My day began early this morning.” “It was a busy day on the stock exchange.” “She called it a day and went to bed.”

    A kind of: work time · Kinds: workday

  6. An era of existence or influence.

    “In the day of the dinosaurs.” “In the days of the Roman Empire.” “In the days of sailing ships.”

    A kind of: era

  7. The period of time taken by a particular planet (e.g. Mars) to make a complete rotation on its axis.

    “How long is a day on Jupiter?”

    A kind of: time period · Kinds: lunar day

  8. The time for one complete rotation of the earth relative to a particular star, about 4 minutes shorter than a mean solar day.

    Synonyms: sidereal day · A kind of: time unit, sidereal time

  9. A period of opportunity.

    “He deserves his day in court.” “Every dog has his day.”

    A kind of: opportunity

  10. United States writer best known for his autobiographical works (1874-1935).

Phrases and compounds with "day"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • d-day (noun) Date of the Allied landing in France, World War II.
  • v-day (noun) The day of a victory.
  • day bed (noun) Convertible consisting of an upholstered couch that can be converted into a double bed.
  • day off (noun) A day when you are not required to work.
  • day-old (adjective) Not fresh today.
  • may day (noun) Observed in many countries to celebrate the coming of spring; observed in Russia and related countries in honor of labor.
  • off-day (noun) A day when things go poorly.
  • rag day (noun) A day on which university students hold a rag.
  • v-e day (noun) The date of Allied victory in Europe, World War II.
  • v-j day (noun) The date of Allied victory over Japan, World War II.
  • day book (noun) The daily written record of events (as arrests) in a police station.
  • day camp (noun) A camp providing care and activities for children during the daytime.
  • day care (noun) Childcare during the day while parents work.
  • day game (noun) A game played in daylight.
  • day lily (noun) Any of numerous perennials having mounds of sumptuous broad ribbed leaves and clusters of white, blue, or lilac flowers; used as ground cover.
  • fast day (noun) A day designated for fasting.
