Drive: meaning, synonyms and examples

Drive is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (12 senses). Its most common meaning is: Operate or control a vehicle.

In Hindi
गाड़ी चलाना; अभियान
Synonyms
motor, force, ram, repel, repulse, force back, push back, beat back, tug, labor
Word family
driver, driving, drift

Drive as a verb

  1. Operate or control a vehicle.

    “Drive a car or bus.” “Can you drive this four-wheel truck?”

    A kind of: operate · Kinds: coach, test drive, drive, tool

  2. Travel or be transported in a vehicle.

    “We drove to the university every morning.” “They motored to London for the theater.”

    Synonyms: motor · A kind of: travel

  3. Cause someone or something to move by driving.

    “She drove me to school every day.” “We drove the car to the garage.”

    A kind of: move · Kinds: pull, drive around

  4. Force into or from an action or state, either physically or metaphorically.

    “She rammed her mind into focus.” “He drives me mad.”

    Synonyms: force, ram · A kind of: thrust · Kinds: toe

  5. To compel or force or urge relentlessly or exert coercive pressure on, or motivate strongly.

    “She is driven by her passion.”

    A kind of: coerce

  6. Cause to move back by force or influence.

    “Repel the enemy.” “Push back the urge to smoke.” “Beat back the invaders.”

    Synonyms: repel, repulse, force back, push back, beat back · A kind of: push

  7. Compel somebody to do something, often against his own will or judgment.

    “She finally drove him to change jobs.”

    A kind of: make

  8. Push, propel, or press with force.

    “Drive a nail into the wall.”

    A kind of: propel

  9. Cause to move rapidly by striking or throwing with force.

    “Drive the ball far out into the field.”

    A kind of: throw

  10. Strive and make an effort to reach a goal.

    “She tugged for years to make a decent living.” “We have to push a little to make the deadline!” “She is driving away at her doctoral thesis.”

    Synonyms: tug, labor, labour, push · A kind of: fight · Kinds: strive

  11. Move into a desired direction of discourse.

    “What are you driving at?”

    Synonyms: get, aim · A kind of: mean

  12. Have certain properties when driven.

    “This car rides smoothly.” “My new truck drives well.”

    Synonyms: ride

  13. Work as a driver.

    “He drives a bread truck.” “She drives for the taxi company in Newark.”

    A kind of: work

  14. Move by being propelled by a force.

    “The car drove around the corner.”

    A kind of: drive, travel · Kinds: pull

Drive as a noun

  1. The act of applying force to propel something.

    “After reaching the desired velocity the drive is cut off.”

    Synonyms: thrust, driving force · A kind of: propulsion · Kinds: firewall, impulse

  2. A mechanism by which force or power is transmitted in a machine.

    “A variable speed drive permitted operation through a range of speeds.”

    A kind of: mechanism · Kinds: fusee drive

  3. A series of actions advancing a principle or tending toward a particular end.

    “He supported populist campaigns.” “They worked in the cause of world peace.” “The team was ready for a drive toward the pennant.”

    Synonyms: campaign, cause, crusade, movement, effort · A kind of: venture · Kinds: advertising campaign, anti-war movement, charm campaign, consumerism, campaigning, fund-raising campaign, feminist movement, lost cause

  4. A road leading up to a private house.

    “They parked in the driveway.”

    Synonyms: driveway, private road · A kind of: road

  5. The trait of being highly motivated.

    “His drive and energy exhausted his co-workers.”

    A kind of: trait · Kinds: action, enterprise, ambition, energy, aggressiveness

  6. Hitting a golf ball off of a tee with a driver.

    “He sliced his drive out of bounds.”

    Synonyms: driving · A kind of: golf stroke

  7. The act of driving a herd of animals overland.

    A kind of: transportation · Kinds: cattle drive

  8. A journey in a vehicle (usually an automobile).

    “He took the family for a drive in his new car.”

    Synonyms: ride · A kind of: journey · Kinds: lift, joyride, spin

  9. A physiological state corresponding to a strong need or desire.

    A kind of: physical condition · Kinds: hunger, thirst, hypoxia

  10. (computer science) a device that writes data onto or reads data from a storage medium.

    A kind of: device · Kinds: disk drive, external drive, internal drive

  11. A wide scenic road planted with trees.

    “The riverside drive offers many exciting scenic views.”

    Synonyms: parkway · A kind of: road

  12. (sports) a hard straight return (as in tennis or squash).

    A kind of: return · Kinds: forehand drive, backhand drive

Phrases and compounds with "drive"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • u-drive (noun) A rented car.
  • cd drive (noun) A drive that reads a compact disc and that is connected to an audio system.
  • drive in (verb) Cause a run or runner to be scored.
  • drive up (verb) Approach while driving.
  • drive-in (noun) Any installation designed to accommodate patrons in their automobiles.
  • drive off (verb) Force to go away; used both with concrete and metaphoric meanings.
  • drive out (verb) Force to go away; used both with concrete and metaphoric meanings.
  • let drive (verb) Fire as from a gun.
  • you-drive (noun) A rented car.
  • disc drive (noun) Computer hardware that holds and spins a magnetic or optical disk and reads and writes information on it.
  • disk drive (noun) Computer hardware that holds and spins a magnetic or optical disk and reads and writes information on it.
  • drive away (verb) Force to go away; used both with concrete and metaphoric meanings.
  • drive back (verb) Force or drive back.
  • drive home (verb) Carry out or perform.
  • drive line (noun) Mechanism that transmits power from the engine to the driving wheels of a motor vehicle.
  • hard drive (noun) Computer hardware that holds and spins a magnetic or optical disk and reads and writes information on it.
