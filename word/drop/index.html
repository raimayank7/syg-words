Drop: meaning, synonyms and examples

Drop is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (8 senses). Its most common meaning is: Let fall to the ground.

In Hindi
गिराना; बूँद
Synonyms
sink, drop down, knock off, drop off, set down, put down, unload, discharge, fell, strike down
Word family
dropper, droplet

Drop as a verb

  1. Let fall to the ground.

    “Don't drop the dishes.”

    A kind of: move · Kinds: plop, dump, hang, plonk down

  2. To fall vertically.

    A kind of: descend · Kinds: plunge, plummet, flump, decline

  3. Go down in value.

    “Stock prices dropped.”

    A kind of: decline · Kinds: slump, tumble

  4. Fall or descend to a lower place or level.

    “He sank to his knees.”

    Synonyms: sink, drop down · A kind of: change posture · Kinds: drop open, sag, sag

  5. Terminate an association with.

    “Drop him from the Republican ticket.”

    A kind of: remove

  6. Utter with seeming casualness.

    “Drop a hint.”

    A kind of: express

  7. Stop pursuing or acting.

    “Drop a lawsuit.” “Knock it off!”

    Synonyms: knock off · A kind of: discontinue · Kinds: nolle pros

  8. Leave or unload.

    “Unload the cargo.” “Drop off the passengers at the hotel.”

    Synonyms: drop off, set down, put down, unload, discharge · A kind of: deliver · Kinds: wharf, air-drop

  9. Cause to fall by or as if by delivering a blow.

    “Strike down a tree.” “Lightning struck down the hikers.”

    Synonyms: fell, strike down, cut down · A kind of: cut · Kinds: chop down, poleax, log, cut

  10. Lose (a game).

    “The Giants dropped 11 of their first 13.”

    A kind of: lose

  11. Pay out.

    “Spend money.”

    Synonyms: spend, expend · A kind of: pay · Kinds: consume, consume, afford, blow, wanton, underspend, misspend, penny-pinch

  12. Lower the pitch of (musical notes).

    Synonyms: flatten · A kind of: change

  13. Hang freely.

    “The ornaments dangled from the tree.” “The light dropped from the ceiling.”

    Synonyms: dangle, swing · A kind of: hang · Kinds: droop

  14. Stop associating with.

    “They dropped her after she had a child out of wedlock.”

    Synonyms: dismiss, send packing, send away · A kind of: displace

Drop as a noun

  1. A shape that is spherical and small.

    “He studied the shapes of low-viscosity drops.” “Beads of sweat on his forehead.”

    Synonyms: bead, pearl · A kind of: sphere · Kinds: dewdrop, teardrop

  2. A small indefinite quantity (especially of a liquid).

    “He had a drop too much to drink.” “A drop of each sample was analyzed.” “There is not a drop of pity in that man.”

    Synonyms: drib, driblet · A kind of: small indefinite quantity · Kinds: tear, raindrop, droplet, eyedrop

  3. A sudden sharp decrease in some quantity.

    “A drop of 57 points on the Dow Jones index.” “There was a drop in pressure in the pulmonary artery.” “A dip in prices.”

    Synonyms: dip, fall, free fall · A kind of: decrease · Kinds: correction, voltage drop

  4. A steep high face of rock.

    “He stood on a high cliff overlooking the town.” “A steep drop.”

    Synonyms: cliff, drop-off · A kind of: geological formation · Kinds: crag, precipice

  5. A free and rapid descent by the force of gravity.

    “It was a miracle that he survived the drop from that height.”

    Synonyms: fall · A kind of: descent, gravitation · Kinds: free fall, plunge, precipitation

  6. A curtain that can be lowered and raised onto a stage from the flies; often used as background scenery.

    Synonyms: drop curtain, drop cloth · A kind of: curtain

  7. A central depository where things can be left or picked up.

    A kind of: depository · Kinds: maildrop

  8. The act of dropping something.

    “They expected the drop would be successful.”

    A kind of: descent

Phrases and compounds with "drop"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • drop by (verb) Visit informally and spontaneously.
  • drop in (verb) Visit informally and spontaneously.
  • air-drop (verb) Drop (an object) from the air; unload from a plane or helicopter.
  • drop off (verb) Fall or diminish.
  • drop out (verb) Give up in the face of defeat of lacking hope; admit defeat.
  • drop-off (noun) A noticeable deterioration in performance or quality.
  • eye-drop (noun) A drop from an eye dropper.
  • dead drop (noun) A drop used for the clandestine exchange of intelligence information.
  • drop arch (noun) A blunt pointed arch drawn from two centers within the span.
  • drop away (verb) Get worse.
  • drop back (verb) Take position in the rear, as in a military formation or in the line of scrimmage in football.
  • drop dead (verb) Pass from physical life and lose all bodily attributes and functions necessary to sustain life.
  • drop down (verb) Fall or descend to a lower place or level.
  • drop keel (noun) A retractable fin keel used on sailboats to prevent drifting to leeward.
  • drop line (noun) A headline with the top line flush left and succeeding lines indented to the right.
  • drop open (verb) Open involuntarily.
