Energy: meaning, synonyms and examples

Energy is used as a noun (7 senses). Its most common meaning is: (physics) a thermodynamic quantity equivalent to the capacity of a physical system to do work; the units of energy are joules or ergs.

Energy as a noun

  1. (physics) a thermodynamic quantity equivalent to the capacity of a physical system to do work; the units of energy are joules or ergs.

    “Energy can take a wide variety of forms.”

    Synonyms: free energy · A kind of: physical phenomenon · Kinds: activation energy, alternative energy, atomic energy, binding energy, chemical energy, electricity, energy level, rest energy

  2. Forceful exertion.

    “He plays tennis with great energy.” “He's full of zip.”

    Synonyms: vigor, vigour, zip · A kind of: force · Kinds: athleticism

  3. Enterprising or ambitious drive.

    “Europeans often laugh at American energy.”

    Synonyms: push, get-up-and-go · A kind of: drive · Kinds: second wind

  4. An imaginative lively style (especially style of writing).

    “His writing conveys great energy.” “A remarkable muscularity of style.”

    Synonyms: muscularity, vigor, vigour, vim · A kind of: liveliness · Kinds: vitality

  5. A healthy capacity for vigorous activity.

    “Jogging works off my excess energy.” “He seemed full of vim and vigor.”

    Synonyms: vim, vitality · A kind of: good health · Kinds: juice, qi

  6. Any source of usable power.

    “The DOE is responsible for maintaining the energy policy.”

    A kind of: physical phenomenon

  7. The federal department responsible for maintaining a national energy policy of the United States; created in 1977.

    A kind of: executive department

Phrases and compounds with "energy"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • energy unit (noun) A unit of measurement for work.
  • free energy (noun) (physics) a thermodynamic quantity equivalent to the capacity of a physical system to do work; the units of energy are joules or ergs.
  • heat energy (noun) A form of energy that is transferred by a difference in temperature.
  • high-energy (adjective) Of or relating to elementary particles having energies of hundreds of thousands of electron volts.
  • mass energy (noun) (physics) the mass of a body regarded relativistically as energy.
  • rest energy (noun) The energy equivalent to the mass of a particle at rest in an inertial frame of reference; equal to the rest mass times the square of the speed of light.
  • energy level (noun) A definite stable energy that a physical system can have; used especially of the state of electrons in atoms or molecules.
  • energy state (noun) A definite stable energy that a physical system can have; used especially of the state of electrons in atoms or molecules.
  • solar energy (noun) Energy from the sun that is converted into thermal or electrical energy.
  • atomic energy (noun) The energy released by a nuclear reaction.
  • mental energy (noun) An actuating force or factor.
  • binding energy (noun) The energy required to separate particles from a molecule or atom or nucleus; equals the mass defect.
  • elastic energy (noun) Potential energy that is stored when a body is deformed (as in a coiled spring).
  • energy-storing (adjective) Of or relating to anabolism.
  • kinetic energy (noun) The mechanical energy that a body has by virtue of its motion.
  • nuclear energy (noun) The energy released by a nuclear reaction.
