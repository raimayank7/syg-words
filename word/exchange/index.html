Exchange: meaning, synonyms and examples

Exchange is used as a noun (11 senses) and verb (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: Chemical process in which one atom or ion or group changes places with another.

In Hindi
अदला-बदली; विनिमय
Synonyms
interchange, central, telephone exchange, rally, substitution, commutation, change, commute, convert, switch over
Word family
exchanger

Exchange as a noun

  1. Chemical process in which one atom or ion or group changes places with another.

    A kind of: chemical phenomenon · Kinds: photochemical exchange

  2. A mutual expression of views (especially an unpleasant one).

    “They had a bitter exchange.”

    A kind of: conversation

  3. The act of changing one thing for another thing.

    “Adam was promised immortality in exchange for his disobedience.” “There was an interchange of prisoners.”

    Synonyms: interchange · A kind of: group action · Kinds: tradeoff

  4. The act of giving something in return for something received.

    “Deductible losses on sales or exchanges of property are allowable.”

    A kind of: transaction · Kinds: logrolling

  5. A workplace that serves as a telecommunications facility where lines from telephones can be connected together to permit communication.

    Synonyms: central, telephone exchange · A kind of: workplace · Kinds: centrex, switchboard

  6. A workplace for buying and selling; open only to members.

    A kind of: workplace · Kinds: commodity exchange, corn exchange, stock exchange

  7. (sports) an unbroken sequence of several successive strokes.

    “After a short rally Connors won the point.”

    Synonyms: rally · A kind of: group action

  8. Reciprocal transfer of equivalent sums of money (especially the currencies of different countries).

    “He earns his living from the interchange of currency.”

    Synonyms: interchange · A kind of: commerce · Kinds: conversion, barter, foreign exchange

  9. The act of putting one thing or person in the place of another: "he sent Smith in for Jones but the substitution came too late to help".

    “He sent Smith in for Jones but the substitution came too late to help.”

    Synonyms: substitution, commutation · A kind of: change · Kinds: replacement, subrogation, weaning

  10. (chess) gaining (or losing) a rook in return for a knight or bishop.

    “Black lost the exchange.”

    A kind of: capture

  11. (chess) the capture by both players (usually on consecutive moves) of pieces of equal value.

    “The endgame began after the exchange of queens.”

    A kind of: capture

Exchange as a verb

  1. Give to, and receive from, one another.

    “Would you change places with me?” “We have been exchanging letters for a year.”

    Synonyms: change, interchange · A kind of: transfer · Kinds: sell, cash, ransom, redeem, substitute, trade, barter, trade

  2. Exchange or replace with another, usually of the same kind or category.

    “Could you convert my dollars into pounds?” “He changed his name.” “Convert centimeters into inches.”

    Synonyms: change, commute, convert · A kind of: replace · Kinds: rectify, utilize, capitalize, launder, break

  3. Change over, change around, as to a new order or sequence.

    Synonyms: switch over, switch · A kind of: change by reversal

  4. Hand over one and receive another, approximately equivalent.

    “Exchange prisoners.” “Exchange employees between branches of the company.”

    A kind of: transfer · Kinds: alternate

  5. Put in the place of another; switch seemingly equivalent items.

    “The con artist replaced the original with a fake Rembrandt.” “Substitute regular milk with fat-free milk.” “Synonyms can be interchanged without a changing the context's meaning.”

    Synonyms: substitute, replace, interchange · A kind of: change · Kinds: shift, reduce, truncate, retool, subrogate

  6. Exchange a penalty for a less severe one.

    Synonyms: commute, convert · A kind of: change

Phrases and compounds with "exchange"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • ion exchange (noun) A process in which ions are exchanged between a solution and an insoluble (usually resinous) solid; widely used in industrial processing.
  • corn exchange (noun) An exchange where grains are bought and sold.
  • exchange rate (noun) The charge for exchanging currency of one country for currency of another.
  • post exchange (noun) A commissary on a United States Army post.
  • stock exchange (noun) An exchange where security trading is conducted by professional stockbrokers.
  • bill of exchange (noun) A document ordering the payment of money; drawn by one person or bank on another.
  • exchange premium (noun) A fee charged for exchanging currencies.
  • foreign exchange (noun) The system by which one currency is exchanged for another; enables international transactions to take place.
  • futures exchange (noun) A commodity exchange where futures contracts are traded.
  • rate of exchange (noun) The charge for exchanging currency of one country for currency of another.
  • commodity exchange (noun) An exchange for buying and selling commodities for future delivery.
  • medium of exchange (noun) Anything that is generally accepted as a standard of value and a measure of wealth in a particular country or region.
  • telephone exchange (noun) A workplace that serves as a telecommunications facility where lines from telephones can be connected together to permit communication.
  • commodities exchange (noun) An exchange for buying and selling commodities for future delivery.
  • exchange traded fund (noun) A mutual fund that is traded on a stock exchange.
  • exchange transfusion (noun) Slow removal of a person's blood and its replacement with equal amounts of a donor's blood.
