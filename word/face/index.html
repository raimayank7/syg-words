Face: meaning, synonyms and examples

Face is used as a noun (13 senses) and verb (9 senses). Its most common meaning is: The front of the human head from the forehead to the chin and ear to ear.

In Hindi
चेहरा; सामना करना
Synonyms
human face, expression, look, aspect, facial expression, side, grimace, font, fount, typeface
Word family
facial, facing

Face as a noun

  1. The front of the human head from the forehead to the chin and ear to ear.

    “He washed his face.” “I wish I had seen the look on his face when he got the news.”

    Synonyms: human face · A kind of: external body part · Kinds: countenance

  2. The feelings expressed on a person's face.

    “A sad expression.” “A look of triumph.” “An angry face.”

    Synonyms: expression, look, aspect, facial expression · A kind of: countenance · Kinds: leer, sparkle

  3. The general outward appearance of something.

    “The face of the city is changing.”

    A kind of: appearance

  4. The striking or working surface of an implement.

    A kind of: surface

  5. A part of a person that is used to refer to a person.

    “He looked out at a roomful of faces.” “When he returned to work he met many new faces.”

    A kind of: person

  6. A surface forming part of the outside of an object.

    “He examined all sides of the crystal.” “Dew dripped from the face of the leaf.”

    Synonyms: side · A kind of: surface · Kinds: beam-ends, bottom, front, lee, windward, rear, top

  7. The part of an animal corresponding to the human face.

    A kind of: external body part · Kinds: muzzle

  8. The side upon which the use of a thing depends (usually the most prominent surface of an object).

    “He dealt the cards face down.”

    A kind of: front · Kinds: clock face, dial

  9. A contorted facial expression.

    “She made a grimace at the prospect.”

    Synonyms: grimace · A kind of: facial expression · Kinds: pout

  10. A specific size and style of type within a type family.

    Synonyms: font, fount, typeface, case · A kind of: type · Kinds: unicameral script, bicameral script, typewriter font, proportional font, font cartridge, boldface, italic, screen font

  11. Status in the eyes of others.

    “He lost face.”

    A kind of: status

  12. Impudent aggressiveness.

    “I couldn't believe her boldness.” “He had the effrontery to question my honesty.”

    Synonyms: boldness, nerve, brass, cheek · A kind of: aggressiveness · Kinds: audacity

  13. A vertical surface of a building or cliff.

    A kind of: vertical surface · Kinds: perpendicular, coalface

Face as a verb

  1. Deal with (something unpleasant) head on.

    “You must confront your problems.” “He faced the terrible consequences of his mistakes.”

    Synonyms: confront, face up · A kind of: set about · Kinds: undertake

  2. Oppose, as in hostility or a competition.

    “You must confront your opponent.” “Jackson faced Smith in the boxing ring.” “The two enemies finally confronted each other.”

    Synonyms: confront · A kind of: meet · Kinds: front, take the bull by the horns, pit

  3. Be oriented in a certain direction, often with respect to another reference point; be opposite to.

    “The house looks north.” “My backyard look onto the pond.” “The building faces the park.”

    Synonyms: front, look · A kind of: lie · Kinds: confront

  4. Be opposite.

    “The facing page.” “The two sofas face each other.”

    A kind of: be

  5. Turn so as to face; turn the face in a certain direction.

    “Turn and face your partner now.”

    A kind of: turn

  6. Present somebody with something, usually to accuse or criticize.

    “We confronted him with the evidence.” “He was faced with all the evidence and could no longer deny his actions.” “An enormous dilemma faces us.”

    Synonyms: confront, present

  7. Turn so as to expose the face.

    “Face a playing card.”

    A kind of: disclose

  8. Line the edge (of a garment) with a different material.

    “Face the lapels of the jacket.”

    A kind of: line · Kinds: reface

  9. Cover the front or surface of.

    “The building was faced with beautiful stones.”

    A kind of: cover · Kinds: revet, reface

Phrases and compounds with "face"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • face up (verb) Deal with (something unpleasant) head on.
  • face off (verb) Start a game by a face-off.
  • face-off (noun) A hostile disagreement face-to-face.
  • wry face (noun) A disdainful grimace.
  • bold face (noun) A typeface with thick heavy lines.
  • face card (noun) One of the twelve cards in a deck bearing a picture of a face.
  • face lift (noun) Plastic surgery to remove wrinkles and other signs of aging from your face; an incision is made near the hair line and skin is pulled back and excess tissue is excised.
  • face mask (noun) Mask that provides a protective covering for the face in such sports as baseball or football or hockey.
  • face pack (noun) A cream that cleanses and tones the skin.
  • face soap (noun) Soap used as a toiletry.
  • face time (noun) Work time spent at the location of or in the presence of other people.
  • face veil (noun) A piece of more-or-less transparent material that covers the face.
  • face-lift (verb) Perform cosmetic surgery on someone's face.
  • full-face (adjective) Looking forward.
  • about-face (verb) Turn, usually 180 degrees.
  • clock face (noun) The face of a clock showing hours and minutes of the day.
