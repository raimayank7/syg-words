Factor: meaning, synonyms and examples

Factor is used as a noun (7 senses) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: Anything that contributes causally to a result.

In Hindi
कारक; गुणनखंड
Synonyms
component, constituent, element, ingredient, divisor, agent, broker, gene, cistron, factor in
Word family
factorize, factorization, factorisation, factoring

Factor as a noun

  1. Anything that contributes causally to a result.

    “A number of factors determined the outcome.”

    A kind of: cause · Kinds: fundamental, parameter, unknown quantity, wild card, releasing factor, intrinsic factor

  2. An abstract part of something.

    “Jealousy was a component of his character.” “Two constituents of a musical composition are melody and harmony.” “The grammatical elements of a sentence.”

    Synonyms: component, constituent, element, ingredient · A kind of: part · Kinds: be-all and end-all, plot element, point

  3. One of two or more integers that can be exactly divided into another integer.

    “What are the 4 factors of 6?”

    Synonyms: divisor · A kind of: integer · Kinds: prime factor, equivalent-binary-digit factor, common divisor

  4. A businessman who buys or sells for another in exchange for a commission.

    Synonyms: agent, broker · A kind of: businessperson · Kinds: auctioneer, insurance broker, investment banker, real estate broker, ship broker, stockbroker, syndic, travel agent

  5. Any of the numbers (or symbols) that form a product when multiplied together.

    A kind of: number · Kinds: deflator, conversion factor, factor of proportionality

  6. An independent variable in statistics.

    A kind of: independent variable

  7. (genetics) a segment of DNA that is involved in producing a polypeptide chain; it can include regions preceding and following the coding DNA as well as introns between the exons; it is considered a unit of heredity.

    “Genes were formerly called factors.”

    Synonyms: gene, cistron · A kind of: sequence · Kinds: dominant gene, allele, genetic marker, homeotic gene, lethal gene, linkage group, modifier, mutant gene

Factor as a verb

  1. Resolve into factors.

    “A quantum computer can factor the number 15.”

    Synonyms: factor in, factor out · A kind of: calculate

  2. Be a contributing factor.

    “Make things factor into a company's profitability.”

    A kind of: lend

  3. Consider as relevant when making a decision.

    “You must factor in the recent developments.”

    Synonyms: factor in, factor out · A kind of: study

Phrases and compounds with "factor"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • factor v (noun) A coagulation factor.
  • factor x (noun) Coagulation factor that is converted to an enzyme that converts prothrombin to thrombin in a reaction that depends on calcium ions and other coagulation factors.
  • factor ii (noun) A protein in blood plasma that is the inactive precursor of thrombin.
  • factor in (verb) Consider as relevant when making a decision.
  • factor iv (noun) Ion of calcium; a factor in the clotting of blood.
  • factor ix (noun) Coagulation factor whose absence is associated with hemophilia B.
  • factor xi (noun) Coagulation factor whose deficiency results in a hemorrhagic tendency.
  • rh factor (noun) A blood group antigen possessed by Rh-positive people; if an Rh-negative person receives a blood transfusion from an Rh-positive person it can result in hemolysis and anemia.
  • factor iii (noun) An enzyme liberated from blood platelets that converts prothrombin into thrombin as blood starts to clot.
  • factor out (verb) Consider as relevant when making a decision.
  • factor vii (noun) A coagulation factor formed in the kidney under the influence of vitamin K.
  • factor xii (noun) Coagulation factor whose deficiency results in prolongation of clotting time of venous blood.
  • factor viii (noun) A coagulation factor (trade name Hemofil) whose absence is associated with hemophilia A.
  • factor xiii (noun) In the clotting of blood thrombin catalyzes factor XIII into its active form (fibrinase) which causes fibrin to form a stable clot.
  • load factor (noun) (aeronautics) the ratio of an external load to the weight of the aircraft (measured in g).
