Fund: meaning, synonyms and examples

Fund is used as a noun (3 senses) and verb (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: A reserve of money set aside for some purpose.

In Hindi
कोष, निधि
Synonyms
monetary fund, store, stock, investment company, investment trust, investment firm
Word family
funding

Fund as a noun

  1. A reserve of money set aside for some purpose.

    Synonyms: monetary fund · A kind of: money · Kinds: mutual fund, revolving fund, sinking fund, savings, pension fund, war chest, slush fund, trust fund

  2. A supply of something available for future use.

    “He brought back a large store of Cuban cigars.”

    Synonyms: store, stock · A kind of: accumulation · Kinds: infrastructure, hoard, provision, issue, seed stock

  3. A financial institution that sells shares to individuals and invests in securities issued by other companies.

    Synonyms: investment company, investment trust, investment firm · A kind of: nondepository financial institution · Kinds: hedge fund, mutual fund, closed-end fund, face-amount certificate company, unit investment trust, nondiscretionary trust

Fund as a verb

  1. Convert (short-term floating debt) into long-term debt that bears fixed interest and is represented by bonds.

    A kind of: finance

  2. Place or store up in a fund for accumulation.

    A kind of: roll up

  3. Provide a fund for the redemption of principal or payment of interest.

    A kind of: supply

  4. Invest money in government securities.

    A kind of: invest

  5. Accumulate a fund for the discharge of a recurrent liability.

    “Fund a medical care plan.”

    A kind of: roll up

  6. Furnish money for.

    “The government funds basic research in many areas.”

    A kind of: support · Kinds: grubstake, bankroll, absorb

Phrases and compounds with "fund"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • fund raise (verb) Raise money for a cause or project.
  • fund-raise (verb) Raise money for a cause or project.
  • hedge fund (noun) A flexible investment company for a small number of large investors (usually the minimum investment is $1 million); can use high-risk techniques (not allowed for mutual funds) such as short-selling and heavy leveraging.
  • index fund (noun) A mutual fund whose assets are stocks on a given list.
  • slush fund (noun) A fund for buying votes or bribing public officials.
  • trust fund (noun) A fund held in trust.
  • mutual fund (noun) The pooled money that is invested in assets.
  • pension fund (noun) A fund reserved to pay workers' pensions when they retire from service.
  • reserve fund (noun) Funds taken out of earnings to provide for anticipated future payments.
  • sinking fund (noun) A fund accumulated regularly in a separate account and used to redeem debt securities.
  • monetary fund (noun) A reserve of money set aside for some purpose.
  • open-end fund (noun) A regulated investment company with a pool of assets that regularly sells and redeems its shares.
  • endowment fund (noun) The capital that provides income for an institution.
  • revolving fund (noun) A fund which, if borrowed or used, is intended to be replenished so it may be loaned or spent repeatedly.
  • closed-end fund (noun) A regulated investment company that issues a fixed number of shares which are listed on a stock market.
  • retirement fund (noun) A monthly payment made to someone who is retired from work.
