Ground: meaning, synonyms and examples

Ground is used as a noun (11 senses) and verb (12 senses). Its most common meaning is: The solid part of the earth's surface.

In Hindi
ज़मीन, मैदान
Synonyms
land, dry land, earth, solid ground, terra firma, reason, footing, basis, background, soil
Opposites
figure
Word family
grounder, grounding

Ground as a noun

  1. The solid part of the earth's surface.

    “The plane turned away from the sea and moved back over land.” “The earth shook for several minutes.” “He dropped the logs on the ground.”

    Synonyms: land, dry land, earth, solid ground, terra firma · A kind of: object · Kinds: archipelago, beachfront, cape, coastal plain, floor, foreland, forest, island

  2. A rational motive for a belief or action.

    “The reason that war was declared.” “The grounds for their declaration.”

    Synonyms: reason · A kind of: rational motive · Kinds: occasion, score, why

  3. The loose soft material that makes up a large part of the land surface.

    “They dug into the earth outside the church.”

    Synonyms: earth · A kind of: material · Kinds: moraine, diatomaceous earth, saprolite, soil

  4. A relation that provides the foundation for something.

    “They were on a friendly footing.” “He worked on an interim basis.”

    Synonyms: footing, basis · A kind of: foundation · Kinds: common ground

  5. A position to be won or defended in battle (or as if in battle).

    “They gained ground step by step.” “They fought to regain the lost ground.”

    A kind of: military position

  6. The part of a scene (or picture) that lies behind objects in the foreground.

    “He posed her against a background of rolling hills.”

    Synonyms: background · A kind of: view

  7. Material in the top layer of the surface of the earth in which plants can grow (especially with reference to its quality or use).

    “The land had never been plowed.” “Good agricultural soil.”

    Synonyms: land, soil · A kind of: object · Kinds: badlands, bottomland, coastland, cultivated land, overburden, permafrost, polder, rangeland

  8. A relatively homogeneous percept extending back of the figure on which attention is focused.

    Opposite: figure · A kind of: percept

  9. A connection between an electrical device and a large conducting body, such as the earth (which is taken to be at zero voltage).

    Synonyms: earth · A kind of: connection

  10. (art) the surface (as a wall or canvas) prepared to take the paint for a painting.

    A kind of: surface

  11. The first or preliminary coat of paint or size applied to a surface.

    Synonyms: flat coat, primer, priming, primer coat, priming coat, undercoat · A kind of: coat of paint · Kinds: couch

Ground as a verb

  1. Fix firmly and stably.

    “Anchor the lamppost in concrete.”

    Synonyms: anchor · A kind of: fasten

  2. Confine or restrict to the ground.

    “After the accident, they grounded the plane and the pilot.”

    A kind of: restrain

  3. Place or put on the ground.

    A kind of: put

  4. Instruct someone in the fundamentals of a subject.

    A kind of: teach

  5. Bring to the ground.

    “The storm grounded the ship.”

    Synonyms: strand, run aground · A kind of: land

  6. Hit or reach the ground.

    Synonyms: run aground · A kind of: reach

  7. Throw to the ground in order to stop play and avoid being tackled behind the line of scrimmage.

    A kind of: throw

  8. Hit a groundball.

    “He grounded to the second baseman.”

    A kind of: hit

  9. Hit onto the ground.

    A kind of: hit

  10. Cover with a primer; apply a primer to.

    Synonyms: prime, undercoat · A kind of: paint

  11. Connect to a ground.

    “Ground the electrical connections for safety reasons.”

    A kind of: connect · Kinds: earth

  12. Use as a basis for; found on.

    “Base a claim on some observation.”

    Synonyms: establish, base, found · Kinds: build

Phrases and compounds with "ground"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • bed ground (noun) An area on which a drove of cattle or sheep can sleep for a night.
  • bed-ground (noun) An area on which a drove of cattle or sheep can sleep for a night.
  • ground fir (noun) A variety of club moss.
  • ground ivy (noun) Trailing European aromatic plant of the mint family having rounded leaves and small purplish flowers often grown in hanging baskets; naturalized in North America; sometimes placed in genus Nepeta.
  • ground out (verb) Make an out by hitting the ball on the ground.
  • gain ground (verb) Obtain advantages, such as points, etc.
  • ground bait (noun) Bait scattered on the water to attract fish.
  • ground ball (noun) (baseball) a hit that travels along the ground.
  • ground bass (noun) A short melody in the bass that is constantly repeated.
  • ground beef (noun) Beef that has been ground.
  • ground crew (noun) The crew of technicians and mechanics who service aircraft on the ground.
  • ground fire (noun) A forest fire that burns the humus; may not appear on the surface.
  • ground loop (noun) A sharp uncontrollable turn made by an airplane while moving along the ground.
  • ground pine (noun) Any of several club mosses having long creeping stems and erect branches.
  • ground pink (noun) Low wiry-stemmed branching herb or southern California having fringed pink flowers.
  • ground plan (noun) A floor plan for the ground level of a building.
