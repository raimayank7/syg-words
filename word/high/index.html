High: meaning, synonyms and examples

High is used as an adjective (6 senses), adverb (4 senses) and noun (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: Greater than normal in degree or intensity or amount.

In Hindi

Synonyms

Opposites

High as an adjective

  1. Greater than normal in degree or intensity or amount.

    “A high temperature.” “A high price.” “The high point of his career.”

    Opposite: low · Similar: advanced, broad, graduate, higher, higher, last

  2. (literal meaning) being at or having a relatively great or specific elevation or upward extension (sometimes used in combinations like `knee-high').

    “A high mountain.” “High ceilings.” “High buildings.”

    Opposite: low · Similar: altitudinous, commanding, eminent, high-level, high-stepped, high-top

  3. Standing above others in quality or position.

    “People in high places.” “The high priest.” “Eminent members of the community.”

    Synonyms: eminent · Similar: superior

  4. Used of sounds and voices; high in pitch or frequency.

    Synonyms: high-pitched · Opposite: low · Similar: adenoidal, altissimo, alto, countertenor, falsetto, peaky

  5. Happy and excited and energetic.

    Synonyms: in high spirits · Similar: elated

  6. (used of the smell of meat) smelling spoiled or tainted.

    Synonyms: gamey, gamy · Similar: malodorous

High as an adverb

  1. At a great altitude.

    “He climbed high on the ladder.”

    Synonyms: high up

  2. In or to a high position, amount, or degree.

    “Prices have gone up far too high.”

  3. In a rich manner.

    “He lives high.”

    Synonyms: richly, luxuriously

  4. Far up toward the source.

    “He lives high up the river.”

High as a noun

  1. A lofty level or position or degree.

    “Summer temperatures reached an all-time high.”

    Opposite: low · A kind of: degree

  2. An air mass of higher than normal pressure.

    “The east coast benefits from a Bermuda high.”

    A kind of: air mass

  3. A state of sustained elation.

    “I'm on a permanent high these days.”

    Opposite: low spirits · A kind of: elation

  4. A high place.

    “They stood on high and observed the countryside.” “He doesn't like heights.”

    Synonyms: heights · A kind of: topographic point

  5. A public secondary school usually including grades 9 through 12.

    “He goes to the neighborhood highschool.”

    Synonyms: senior high school, senior high, highschool, high school · A kind of: secondary school

  6. A forward gear with a gear ratio that gives the greatest vehicle velocity for a given engine speed.

    Synonyms: high gear · A kind of: gear · Kinds: overdrive

Phrases and compounds with "high"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • high up (adverb) At a great altitude.
  • high-up (noun) An important or influential (and often overbearing) person.
  • fly high (verb) Be elated.
  • high bar (noun) Gymnastic apparatus consisting of a bar supported in a horizontal position by uprights at both ends.
  • high dam (noun) One of the world's largest dams on the Nile River in southern Egypt.
  • high hat (noun) Cymbals that are operated by a foot pedal.
  • high sea (noun) The open seas of the world outside the territorial waters of any nation.
  • high tea (noun) Substantial early evening meal including tea.
  • high-low (noun) Poker in which the high and low hands split the pot.
  • high-top (adjective) (of shoes or boots) having relatively high uppers.
  • sky-high (adverb) (with verb `to blow') destroyed completely; blown apart or to pieces.
  • high beam (noun) The beam of a car's headlights that provides distant illumination.
  • high gear (noun) A forward gear with a gear ratio that gives the greatest vehicle velocity for a given engine speed.
  • high jinx (noun) Noisy and mischievous merrymaking.
  • high jump (noun) A competition that involves jumping as high as possible over a horizontal bar.
  • high life (noun) Excessive spending.
