Interest: meaning, synonyms and examples

Interest is used as a noun (7 senses) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: A sense of concern with and curiosity about someone or something.

In Hindi
रुचि; ब्याज
Synonyms
involvement, sake, interestingness, stake, interest group, pastime, pursuit, concern, occupy, worry
Opposites
bore

Interest as a noun

  1. A sense of concern with and curiosity about someone or something.

    “An interest in music.”

    Synonyms: involvement · A kind of: curiosity · Kinds: enthusiasm, concern

  2. A reason for wanting something done.

    “For your sake.” “Died for the sake of his country.” “In the interest of safety.”

    Synonyms: sake · A kind of: benefit · Kinds: behalf

  3. The power of attracting or holding one's attention (because it is unusual or exciting etc.).

    “They said nothing of great interest.” “Primary colors can add interest to a room.”

    Synonyms: interestingness · A kind of: power · Kinds: charisma, newsworthiness, topicality, color, shrillness

  4. A fixed charge for borrowing money; usually a percentage of the amount borrowed.

    “How much interest do you pay on your mortgage?”

    A kind of: fixed charge · Kinds: compound interest, simple interest

  5. (law) a right or legal share of something; a financial involvement with something.

    “They have interests all over the world.” “A stake in the company's future.”

    Synonyms: stake · A kind of: share · Kinds: grubstake, controlling interest, insurable interest, vested interest, security interest, terminable interest, undivided interest, fee

  6. (usually plural) a social group whose members control some field of activity and who have common aims.

    “The iron interests stepped up production.”

    Synonyms: interest group · A kind of: social group · Kinds: special interest, vested interest

  7. A diversion that occupies one's time and thoughts (usually pleasantly).

    “Sailing is her favorite pastime.” “He counts reading among his interests.” “They criticized the boy for his limited pursuits.”

    Synonyms: pastime, pursuit · A kind of: diversion · Kinds: avocation

Interest as a verb

  1. Excite the curiosity of; engage the interest of.

    Opposite: bore · A kind of: arouse · Kinds: absorb

  2. Be on the mind of.

    “I worry about the second Germanic consonant shift.”

    Synonyms: concern, occupy, worry

  3. Be of importance or consequence.

    “This matters to me!”

    Synonyms: matter to · A kind of: refer · Kinds: intrigue

Phrases and compounds with "interest"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • low-interest (adjective) (used of loans) charging a relatively small percentage of the amount borrowed.
  • high-interest (adjective) (used of loans) charging a relatively large percentage of the amount borrowed.
  • interest rate (noun) The percentage of a sum of money charged for its use.
  • self-interest (noun) Taking advantage of opportunities without regard for the consequences for others.
  • interest group (noun) (usually plural) a social group whose members control some field of activity and who have common aims.
  • simple interest (noun) Interest paid on the principal alone.
  • vested interest (noun) (law) an interest in which there is a fixed right to present or future enjoyment and that can be conveyed to another.
  • interest expense (noun) Interest paid on loans.
  • interest-bearing (adjective) Of financial obligations on which interest is paid.
  • rate of interest (noun) The percentage of a sum of money charged for its use.
  • special interest (noun) An individual or group who are concerned with some particular part of the economy and who try to influence legislators or bureaucrats to act in their favor.
  • compound interest (noun) Interest calculated on both the principal and the accrued interest.
  • security interest (noun) Any interest in a property that secures the payment of an obligation.
  • insurable interest (noun) An interest in a person or thing that will support the issuance of an insurance policy; an interest in the survival of the insured or in the preservation of the thing that is insured.
  • undivided interest (noun) The interest in property owned by tenants whereby each tenant has an equal right to enjoy the entire property.
  • pooling of interest (noun) An accounting method used in the merging of companies; the balance sheets are added together item by item; this method is tax-free.
