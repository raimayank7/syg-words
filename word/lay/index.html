Lay: meaning, synonyms and examples

Lay is used as a verb (5 senses), adjective (2 senses) and noun (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Put into a certain place or abstract location.

In Hindi
रखना, बिछाना
Synonyms
put, set, place, pose, position, put down, repose, laic, secular, ballad
Word family
layer

Lay as a verb

  1. Put into a certain place or abstract location.

    “Put your things here.” “Set the tray down.” “Set the dogs on the scent of the missing children.”

    Synonyms: put, set, place, pose, position · A kind of: move · Kinds: insert, docket, cock, postpose, prepose, step, replace, stratify

  2. Put in a horizontal position.

    “Lay the books on the table.” “Lay the patient carefully onto the bed.”

    Synonyms: put down, repose · A kind of: put · Kinds: rail, lay, blow, bury, rebury

  3. Prepare or position for action or operation.

    “Lay a fire.” “Lay the foundation for a new health care plan.”

    A kind of: organize

  4. Lay eggs.

    “This hen doesn't lay.”

    A kind of: lay · Kinds: spawn

  5. Impose as a duty, burden, or punishment.

    “Lay a responsibility on someone.”

    A kind of: levy

Lay as an adjective

  1. Characteristic of those who are not members of the clergy.

    “Set his collar in laic rather than clerical position.” “The lay ministry.”

    Synonyms: laic, secular · Similar: profane

  2. Not of or from a profession.

    “A lay opinion as to the cause of the disease.”

    Similar: nonprofessional

Lay as a noun

  1. A narrative song with a recurrent refrain.

    Synonyms: ballad · A kind of: song · Kinds: minstrelsy

  2. A narrative poem of popular origin.

    Synonyms: ballad · A kind of: poem

Phrases and compounds with "lay"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • lay in (verb) Keep or lay aside for future use.
  • lay up (verb) Disable or confine, as with an illness.
  • lay-by (noun) Designated paved area beside a main road where cars can stop temporarily.
  • lay-up (noun) A basketball shot made with one hand from a position under or beside the basket (and usually banked off the backboard).
  • lay off (verb) Put an end to a state or an activity.
  • lay out (verb) Lay out orderly or logically in a line or as if in a line.
  • easy lay (noun) Street names for gamma hydroxybutyrate.
  • lay away (verb) Save up as for future use.
  • lay down (verb) Institute, enact, or establish.
  • lay into (verb) Hit violently, as in an attack.
  • lay over (verb) Interrupt a journey temporarily, e.g., overnight.
  • lay aside (verb) Accumulate money for future use.
  • lay claim (verb) Demand as being one's due or property; assert one's right or title to.
  • lay figure (noun) Dummy in the form of an artist's jointed model of the human body.
  • lay reader (noun) A layman who is authorized by the bishop to read parts of the service in an Anglican or Episcopal church.
  • lay eyes on (verb) See with attention.
