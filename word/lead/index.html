Lead: meaning, synonyms and examples

Lead is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: Take somebody somewhere.

In Hindi
नेतृत्व करना; सीसा
Synonyms
take, direct, conduct, guide, leave, result, head, run, go, pass
Opposites
deficit
Word family
leader

Lead as a verb

  1. Take somebody somewhere.

    “We lead him to our chief.” “Can you take me to the main entrance?” “He conducted us to the palace.”

    Synonyms: take, direct, conduct, guide · Kinds: beacon, hand, mislead, usher

  2. Have as a result or residue.

    “The water left a mark on the silk dress.” “Her blood left a stain on the napkin.”

    Synonyms: leave, result · A kind of: produce · Kinds: lead

  3. Tend to or result in.

    “This remark lead to further arguments among the guests.”

    A kind of: leave · Kinds: entail, necessitate

  4. Travel in front of; go in advance of others.

    “The procession was headed by John.”

    Synonyms: head · A kind of: precede · Kinds: draw away

  5. Cause to undertake a certain action.

    “Her greed led her to forge the checks.”

    A kind of: induce · Kinds: give

  6. Stretch out over a distance, space, time, or scope; run or extend between two points or beyond a certain point.

    “Service runs all the way to Cranbury.” “His knowledge doesn't go very far.” “My memory extends back to my fourth year of life.”

    Synonyms: run, go, pass, extend · A kind of: be · Kinds: come, radiate, go far

  7. Be in charge of.

    “Who is heading this project?”

    Synonyms: head · A kind of: direct · Kinds: chair, captain, spearhead, take hold

  8. Be ahead of others; be the first.

    “She topped her class every year.”

    Synonyms: top

  9. Be conducive to.

    “The use of computers in the classroom lead to better writing.”

    Synonyms: contribute, conduce · A kind of: promote

  10. Lead, as in the performance of a composition.

    “Conduct an orchestra; Barenboim conducted the Chicago symphony for years.”

    Synonyms: conduct, direct · A kind of: perform

  11. Lead, extend, or afford access.

    “This door goes to the basement.” “The road runs South.”

    Synonyms: go · A kind of: be

  12. Move ahead (of others) in time or space.

    Synonyms: precede · A kind of: travel · Kinds: lead

  13. Cause something to pass or lead somewhere.

    “Run the wire behind the cabinet.”

    Synonyms: run · A kind of: pass

  14. Preside over.

    “John moderated the discussion.”

    Synonyms: moderate, chair · A kind of: hash out

Lead as a noun

  1. An advantage held by a competitor in a race.

    “He took the lead at the last turn.”

    A kind of: advantage

  2. A soft heavy toxic malleable metallic element; bluish white when freshly cut but tarnishes readily to dull grey.

    “The children were playing with lead soldiers.”

    A kind of: metallic element · Kinds: hard lead, hard lead, pig lead

  3. Evidence pointing to a possible solution.

    “The police are following a promising lead.” “The trail led straight to the perpetrator.”

    Synonyms: track, trail · A kind of: evidence

  4. A position of leadership (especially in the phrase `take the lead').

    “He takes the lead in any group.” “We were just waiting for someone to take the lead.” “They didn't follow our lead.”

    A kind of: leadership

  5. The angle between the direction a gun is aimed and the position of a moving target (correcting for the flight time of the missile).

    A kind of: angle

  6. The introductory section of a story.

    “It was an amusing lead-in to a very serious matter.”

    Synonyms: lead-in, lede · A kind of: section

  7. (sports) the score by which a team or individual is winning.

    Opposite: deficit · A kind of: score

  8. An actor who plays a principal role.

    Synonyms: star, principal · A kind of: actor · Kinds: co-star, film star, idol, television star

  9. (baseball) the position taken by a base runner preparing to advance to the next base.

    “He took a long lead off first.”

    A kind of: position

  10. An indication of potential opportunity.

    “He got a tip on the stock market.” “A good lead for a job.”

    Synonyms: tip, steer, confidential information, wind, hint · A kind of: guidance

  11. A news story of major importance.

    Synonyms: lead story · A kind of: news article

  12. The timing of ignition relative to the position of the piston in an internal-combustion engine.

    Synonyms: spark advance · A kind of: timing

  13. Restraint consisting of a rope (or light chain) used to restrain an animal.

    Synonyms: leash, tether · A kind of: restraint

  14. Thin strip of metal used to separate lines of type in printing.

    Synonyms: leading · A kind of: strip

Phrases and compounds with "lead"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • lead on (verb) Entice or induce especially when unwise or mistaken.
  • lead up (verb) Set in motion, start an event or prepare the way for.
  • lead-in (noun) The introductory section of a story.
  • lead off (verb) Teach immoral behavior to.
  • lead ore (noun) Ore containing lead.
  • pig lead (noun) Lead that is cast in pigs.
  • red lead (noun) A reddish oxide of lead (Pb3O4) used as a pigment in paints and in glass and ceramics.
  • clip lead (noun) A short piece of wire with alligator clips on both ends.
  • hard lead (noun) A lead alloy that contains about 5% antimony.
  • lead bank (noun) A bank named by a lending syndicate of several banks to protect their interests.
  • lead line (noun) (nautical) plumb line for determining depth.
  • lead time (noun) The time interval between the initiation and the completion of a production process.
  • lead tree (noun) Low scrubby tree of tropical and subtropical North America having white flowers tinged with yellow resembling mimosa and long flattened pods.
  • lead-free (adjective) (of gasoline) not containing tetraethyl lead.
  • black lead (noun) Used as a lubricant and as a moderator in nuclear reactors.
  • lead colic (noun) Symptom of chronic lead poisoning and associated with obstinate constipation.
