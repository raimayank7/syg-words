Let: meaning, synonyms and examples

Let is used as a verb (6 senses) and noun (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: Make it possible through a specific action or lack of action for something to happen.

In Hindi
देना (अनुमति), होने देना
Synonyms
allow, permit, countenance, get, have, lease, rent, net ball
Opposites
prevent
Word family
letter, lessor

Let as a verb

  1. Make it possible through a specific action or lack of action for something to happen.

    “This permits the water to rush in.” “This sealed door won't allow the water come into the basement.” “This will permit the rain to run off.”

    Synonyms: allow, permit · Opposite: prevent · Kinds: pass

  2. Actively cause something to happen.

    “I let it be known that I was not interested.”

    A kind of: induce

  3. Consent to, give permission.

    “She permitted her son to visit her estranged husband.” “I won't let the police search her basement.” “I cannot allow you to see your exam.”

    Synonyms: permit, allow, countenance · A kind of: accept · Kinds: digest, furlough, give, allow, authorize, admit, privilege, legalize

  4. Cause to move; cause to be in a certain position or condition.

    “He got his squad on the ball.” “This let me in for a big surprise.” “He got a girl into trouble.”

    Synonyms: get, have · A kind of: make

  5. Leave unchanged.

    “Let it be.”

    A kind of: leave

  6. Grant use or occupation of under a term of contract.

    “I am leasing my country estate to some foreigners.”

    Synonyms: lease, rent · A kind of: give · Kinds: sublet

Let as a noun

  1. A serve that strikes the net before falling into the receiver's court; the ball must be served again.

    Synonyms: net ball · A kind of: serve

Phrases and compounds with "let"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • let go (verb) Release, as from one's grip.
  • let in (verb) Allow participation in or the right to be part of; permit to exercise the rights, functions, and responsibilities of.
  • let on (verb) Make known to the public information that was previously known only to a few people or that was meant to be kept a secret.
  • let up (verb) Become less in amount or intensity.
  • let fly (verb) Fire as from a gun.
  • let off (verb) Grant exemption or release to.
  • let out (verb) Express audibly; utter sounds (not necessarily words).
  • let down (verb) Move something or somebody to a lower position.
  • let alone (adverb) Much less.
  • let drive (verb) Fire as from a gun.
  • let go of (verb) Release, as from one's grip.
  • let it go (verb) Not act.
  • let loose (verb) Express audibly; utter sounds (not necessarily words).
  • let the cat out of the bag (verb) Divulge confidential information or secrets.
