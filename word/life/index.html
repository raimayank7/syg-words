Life: meaning, synonyms and examples

Life is used as a noun (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: A characteristic state or mode of living.

In Hindi
जीवन, ज़िंदगी
Synonyms
living, animation, aliveness, lifetime, life-time, lifespan, liveliness, spirit, sprightliness, biography
Word family
lifer

Life as a noun

  1. A characteristic state or mode of living.

    “Social life.” “City life.” “Real life.”

    A kind of: being · Kinds: ghetto

  2. The experience of being alive; the course of human events and activities.

    “He could no longer cope with the complexities of life.”

    Synonyms: living · A kind of: experience

  3. The course of existence of an individual; the actions and events that occur in living.

    “He hoped for a new life in Australia.” “He wanted to live his own life without interference from others.”

    A kind of: being

  4. The condition of living or the state of being alive.

    “While there's life there's hope.” “Life depends on many chemical and physical processes.”

    Synonyms: animation, living, aliveness · A kind of: being · Kinds: eternal life, skin, survival

  5. The period during which something is functional (as between birth and death).

    “The battery had a short life.” “He lived a long and happy life.”

    Synonyms: lifetime, life-time, lifespan · A kind of: time period · Kinds: afterlife

  6. The period between birth and the present time.

    “I have known him all his life.”

    A kind of: time period

  7. The period from the present until death.

    “He appointed himself emperor for life.”

    A kind of: time period · Kinds: days

  8. A living person.

    “His heroism saved a life.”

    A kind of: person

  9. Animation and energy in action or expression.

    “It was a heavy play and the actors tried in vain to give life to it.”

    Synonyms: liveliness, spirit, sprightliness · A kind of: animation · Kinds: pertness, airiness, alacrity, energy, elan, esprit, breeziness, irrepressibility

  10. Living things collectively.

    “The oceans are teeming with life.”

    A kind of: living thing · Kinds: wildlife

  11. The organic phenomenon that distinguishes living organisms from nonliving ones.

    “There is no life on the moon.”

    A kind of: organic phenomenon · Kinds: biology, aerobiosis

  12. An account of the series of events making up a person's life.

    Synonyms: biography, life story, life history · A kind of: history · Kinds: autobiography, hagiography, profile

  13. A motive for living.

    “Pottery was his life.”

    A kind of: motivation

  14. A prison term lasting as long as the prisoner lives.

    Synonyms: life sentence · A kind of: prison term

Phrases and compounds with "life"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • half life (noun) The time required for something to fall to half its initial value (in particular, the time for half the atoms in a radioactive substance to disintegrate).
  • half-life (noun) The time required for something to fall to half its initial value (in particular, the time for half the atoms in a radioactive substance to disintegrate).
  • high life (noun) Excessive spending.
  • life belt (noun) Belt attaching you to some object as a restraint in order to prevent you from getting hurt.
  • life buoy (noun) A life preserver in the form of a ring of buoyant material.
  • life form (noun) The characteristic bodily form of a mature organism.
  • life line (noun) A crease on the palm; its length is said by palmists to indicate how long you will live.
  • life mask (noun) A cast taken from the face of a living person.
  • life peer (noun) A British peer whose title lapses at death.
  • life raft (noun) A raft to use if a ship must be abandoned in an emergency.
  • life ring (noun) A life preserver in the form of a ring of buoyant material.
  • life vest (noun) Life preserver consisting of a sleeveless jacket of buoyant or inflatable design.
  • life-size (adjective) Being of the same size as an original.
  • life-time (noun) The period during which something is functional (as between birth and death).
  • long-life (adjective) (of perishable goods) treated to stay fresh longer than usual.
  • real life (noun) The practical world as opposed to the academic world.
