Lift: meaning, synonyms and examples

Lift is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (12 senses). Its most common meaning is: Raise from a lower to a higher position.

In Hindi
उठाना; लिफ़्ट
Synonyms
raise, elevate, get up, bring up, rise, arise, move up, go up, come up, uprise
Word family
lifter

Lift as a verb

  1. Raise from a lower to a higher position.

    “Raise your hands.” “Lift a load.”

    Synonyms: raise, elevate, get up, bring up · A kind of: move · Kinds: get up, jack, shoulder, kick up, hoist, trice, hoist, hoist

  2. Take hold of something and move it to a different location.

    “Lift the box onto the table.”

    A kind of: move · Kinds: heave, pitchfork

  3. Move upwards.

    “Lift one's eyes.”

    Synonyms: raise · A kind of: move

  4. Move upward.

    “The fog lifted.” “The smoke arose from the forest fire.” “The mist uprose from the meadows.”

    Synonyms: rise, arise, move up, go up, come up, uprise · A kind of: travel · Kinds: scend, climb, soar, go up, rocket, bubble, uplift, chandelle

  5. Make audible.

    “He lifted a war whoop.”

    A kind of: utter

  6. Cancel officially.

    “He revoked the ban on smoking.” “Lift an embargo.” “Vacate a death sentence.”

    Synonyms: revoke, annul, countermand, reverse, repeal, overturn, rescind, vacate · A kind of: cancel · Kinds: renege

  7. Make off with belongings of others.

    Synonyms: pilfer, cabbage, purloin, pinch, abstract, snarf, swipe, hook · A kind of: steal

  8. Raise or haul up with or as if with mechanical help.

    “Hoist the bicycle onto the roof of the car.”

    Synonyms: hoist, wind · A kind of: raise · Kinds: trice

  9. Invigorate or heighten.

    “Lift my spirits.” “Lift his ego.”

    Synonyms: raise · A kind of: better

  10. Raise in rank or condition.

    “The new law lifted many people from poverty.”

    Synonyms: raise, elevate · A kind of: change · Kinds: dignify, exalt

  11. Take off or away by decreasing.

    “Lift the pressure.”

    A kind of: remove

  12. Rise up.

    “The building rose before them.”

    Synonyms: rise, rear · A kind of: look · Kinds: loom

  13. Pay off (a mortgage).

    A kind of: liquidate

  14. Take without referencing from someone else's writing or speech; of intellectual property.

    Synonyms: plagiarize, plagiarise · A kind of: steal · Kinds: crib

Lift as a noun

  1. The act of giving temporary assistance.

    A kind of: aid

  2. The component of the aerodynamic forces acting on an airfoil that opposes gravity.

    Synonyms: aerodynamic lift · A kind of: aerodynamic force · Kinds: ground effect

  3. The event of something being raised upward.

    “An elevation of the temperature in the afternoon.” “A raising of the land resulting from volcanic activity.”

    Synonyms: elevation, raising · A kind of: rise

  4. A wave that lifts the surface of the water or ground.

    Synonyms: rise · A kind of: wave

  5. A powered conveyance that carries skiers up a hill.

    Synonyms: ski tow, ski lift · A kind of: conveyance · Kinds: chairlift, rope tow, surface lift

  6. A device worn in a shoe or boot to make the wearer look taller or to correct a shortened leg.

    A kind of: device

  7. One of the layers forming the heel of a shoe or boot.

    A kind of: layer · Kinds: top lift

  8. Lifting device consisting of a platform or cage that is raised and lowered mechanically in a vertical shaft in order to move people from one floor to another in a building.

    Synonyms: elevator · A kind of: lifting device · Kinds: dumbwaiter, freight elevator, paternoster

  9. Plastic surgery to remove wrinkles and other signs of aging from your face; an incision is made near the hair line and skin is pulled back and excess tissue is excised.

    “Some actresses have more than one face lift.”

    Synonyms: face lift, facelift, face lifting, cosmetic surgery, rhytidectomy, rhytidoplasty, nip and tuck · A kind of: plastic surgery · Kinds: nose job

  10. Transportation of people or goods by air (especially when other means of access are unavailable).

    Synonyms: airlift · A kind of: transportation

  11. A ride in a car.

    “He gave me a lift home.”

    A kind of: drive

  12. The act of raising something.

    “He responded with a lift of his eyebrow.” “Fireman learn several different raises for getting ladders up.”

    Synonyms: raise, heave · A kind of: propulsion

Phrases and compounds with "lift"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • lift up (verb) Take and lift upward.
  • lift off (verb) Depart from the ground.
  • lift out (verb) Take out or up with or as if with a scoop.
  • ski lift (noun) A powered conveyance that carries skiers up a hill.
  • top lift (noun) The bottom layer of a heel.
  • face lift (noun) Plastic surgery to remove wrinkles and other signs of aging from your face; an incision is made near the hair line and skin is pulled back and excess tissue is excised.
  • face-lift (verb) Perform cosmetic surgery on someone's face.
  • lift pump (noun) Pump used to lift rather than force a liquid up.
  • chair lift (noun) A ski lift on which riders (skiers or sightseers) are seated and carried up or down a mountainside; seats are hung from an endless overhead cable.
  • t-bar lift (noun) A surface lift where riders hold a bar and are pulled up the hill on their skis.
  • alpine lift (noun) A surface lift where riders hold a bar and are pulled up the hill on their skis.
  • lift bridge (noun) A bridge that can be raised to block passage or to allow boats or ships to pass beneath it.
  • weight-lift (verb) Lift weights.
  • surface lift (noun) A ski tow that pulls skiers up a slope without lifting them off the ground.
  • aerodynamic lift (noun) The component of the aerodynamic forces acting on an airfoil that opposes gravity.
