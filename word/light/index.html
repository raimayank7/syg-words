Light: meaning, synonyms and examples

Light is used as a noun (14 senses), adjective (14 senses), verb (6 senses) and adverb (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: (physics) electromagnetic radiation that can produce a visual sensation.

In Hindi
रोशनी; हल्का
Synonyms
visible light, visible radiation, light source, luminosity, brightness, brightness level, luminance, luminousness, illumination, lightness
Opposites
dark, heavy
Word family
lightness, lighter

Light as a noun

  1. (physics) electromagnetic radiation that can produce a visual sensation.

    “The light was filtered through a soft glass window.”

    Synonyms: visible light, visible radiation · A kind of: actinic radiation · Kinds: beam, candlelight, corona, counterglow, daylight, firelight, fluorescence, friar's lantern

  2. Any device serving as a source of illumination.

    “He stopped the car and turned off the lights.”

    Synonyms: light source · A kind of: source of illumination · Kinds: anchor light, blinker, fairy light, flood, headlight, houselights, jacklight, navigation light

  3. A particular perspective or aspect of a situation.

    “Although he saw it in a different light, he still did not understand.”

    A kind of: position

  4. The quality of being luminous; emitting or reflecting light.

    “Its luminosity is measured relative to that of our sun.”

    Synonyms: luminosity, brightness, brightness level, luminance, luminousness · A kind of: physical property · Kinds: illuminance, incandescence, luminescence

  5. An illuminated area.

    “He stepped into the light.”

    A kind of: scene

  6. A condition of spiritual awareness; divine illumination.

    “Follow God's light.”

    Synonyms: illumination · A kind of: condition

  7. The visual effect of illumination on objects or scenes as created in pictures.

    “He could paint the lightest light and the darkest dark.”

    Synonyms: lightness · A kind of: visual property · Kinds: aura, sunniness, highlight, brightness

  8. A person regarded very fondly.

    “The light of my life.”

    A kind of: friend

  9. Having abundant light or illumination.

    “They played as long as it was light.” “As long as the lighting was good.”

    Synonyms: lighting · Opposite: dark · A kind of: illumination

  10. Mental understanding as an enlightening experience.

    “He finally saw the light.” “Can you shed light on this problem?”

    A kind of: insight

  11. Merriment expressed by a brightness or gleam or animation of countenance.

    “He had a sparkle in his eye.” “There's a perpetual twinkle in his eyes.”

    Synonyms: sparkle, twinkle, spark · A kind of: vitality, expression

  12. Public awareness.

    “It brought the scandal to light.”

    A kind of: public knowledge

  13. A divine presence believed by Quakers to enlighten and guide the soul.

    A kind of: ethical motive

  14. A visual warning signal.

    “They saw the light of the beacon.” “There was a light at every corner.”

    A kind of: visual signal · Kinds: traffic light

Light as an adjective

  1. Of comparatively little physical weight or density.

    “A light load.” “Magnesium is a light metal--having a specific gravity of 1.74 at 20 degrees C.”

    Opposite: heavy · Similar: lightweight, airy, buoyant, lighter-than-air, low-density

  2. (used of color) having a relatively small amount of coloring agent.

    “Light blue.” “Light colors such as pastels.” “A light-colored powder.”

    Synonyms: light-colored · Opposite: dark · Similar: pale, palish, pastel, powdery

  3. Of the military or industry; using (or being) relatively small or light arms or equipment.

    “Light infantry.” “Light cavalry.” “Light industry.”

    Opposite: heavy · Similar: light-armed

  4. Not great in degree or quantity or number.

    “A light sentence.” “A light accent.” “Casualties were light.”

    Opposite: heavy

  5. Psychologically light; especially free from sadness or troubles.

    “A light heart.”

    Opposite: heavy · Similar: fooling

  6. Characterized by or emitting light.

    “A room that is light when the shutters are open.” “The inside of the house was airy and light.”

    Opposite: dark · Similar: ablaze, autofluorescent, bioluminescent, bright, candescent, floodlit

  7. (used of vowels or syllables) pronounced with little or no stress.

    “A syllable that ends in a short vowel is a light syllable.” “A weak stress on the second syllable.”

    Synonyms: unaccented, weak · Similar: unstressed

  8. Easily assimilated in the alimentary canal; not rich or heavily seasoned.

    “A light diet.”

    Similar: digestible

  9. (used of soil) loose and large-grained in consistency.

    “Light soil.”

    Similar: loose

  10. (of sound or color) free from anything that dulls or dims.

    “Efforts to obtain a clean bass in orchestral recordings.” “Clear laughter like a waterfall.” “Clear reds and blues.”

    Synonyms: clean, clear, unclouded · Similar: pure

  11. Moving easily and quickly; nimble.

    “The dancer was light and graceful.” “A lightsome buoyant step.” “Walked with a light tripping step.”

    Synonyms: lightsome, tripping · Similar: light-footed

  12. Demanding little effort; not burdensome.

    “Light housework.” “Light exercise.”

    Similar: undemanding

  13. Of little intensity or power or force.

    “The light touch of her fingers.” “A light breeze.”

    Opposite: heavy · Similar: easy

  14. (physics, chemistry) not having atomic weight greater than average.

    “Light water is ordinary water.”

    Opposite: heavy

Light as a verb

  1. Make lighter or brighter.

    “This lamp lightens the room a bit.”

    Synonyms: illume, illumine, light up, illuminate · A kind of: lighten · Kinds: floodlight, spotlight

  2. Begin to smoke.

    “After the meal, some of the diners lit up.”

    Synonyms: light up, fire up · A kind of: ignite

  3. To come to rest, settle.

    “Misfortune lighted upon him.”

    Synonyms: alight, perch · A kind of: land

  4. Cause to start burning; subject to fire or great heat.

    “Great heat can ignite almost any dry matter.” “Light a cigarette.”

    Synonyms: ignite · A kind of: burn · Kinds: light up, reignite, kindle, light up, flare up

  5. Fall to somebody by assignment or lot.

    “The task fell to me.” “It fell to me to notify the parents of the victims.”

    Synonyms: fall · A kind of: fall

  6. Alight from (a horse).

    Synonyms: unhorse, dismount, get off, get down · A kind of: descend

Light as an adverb

  1. With few burdens.

    “Experienced travellers travel light.”

    Synonyms: lightly

Phrases and compounds with "light"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • light up (verb) Start to burn with a bright flame.
  • arc light (noun) A lamp that produces light when electric current flows across the gap between two electrodes.
  • light air (noun) Wind moving 1-3 knots; 1 on the Beaufort scale.
  • light arm (noun) A rifle or pistol.
  • light pen (noun) (computer science) a pointer that when pointed at a computer display senses whether or not the spot is illuminated.
  • red light (noun) A cautionary sign of danger.
  • wax light (noun) Stick of wax with a wick in the middle.
  • half-light (noun) A greyish light (as at dawn or dusk or in dim interiors).
  • light beam (noun) A column of light (as from a beacon).
  • light bulb (noun) Electric lamp consisting of a transparent or translucent glass housing containing a wire filament (usually tungsten) that emits light when heated by electricity.
  • light diet (noun) Diet prescribed for bedridden or convalescent people; does not include fried or highly seasoned foods.
  • light hour (noun) The distance light travels in a vacuum in one hour; approximately one billion kilometers.
  • light show (noun) A display of colored lights moving in shifting patterns.
  • light time (noun) Distance measured in terms of the speed of light (or radio waves).
  • light unit (noun) A measure of the visible electromagnetic radiation.
  • light upon (verb) Find unexpectedly.
