Live: meaning, synonyms and examples

Live is used as a verb (7 senses), adjective (11 senses) and adverb (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: Inhabit or live in; be an inhabitant of.

In Hindi
रहना, जीना; सीधा प्रसारण
Synonyms
populate, dwell, inhabit, survive, last, live on, go, endure, hold up, hold out
Opposites
recorded, dead
Word family
liveable, livable, liver, living, liveness

Live as a verb

  1. Inhabit or live in; be an inhabitant of.

    “People lived in Africa millions of years ago.” “The people inhabited the islands that are now deserted.” “This kind of fish dwells near the bottom of the ocean.”

    Synonyms: populate, dwell, inhabit · A kind of: be · Kinds: tenant, neighbor, occupy, reside, people, overpopulate, cohabit, lodge

  2. Lead a certain kind of life; live in a certain style.

    “We had to live frugally after the war.”

    Kinds: move, swing, unlive, wanton, vegetate, pig, bushwhack, buccaneer

  3. Continue to live through hardship or adversity.

    “We went without water and food for 3 days.” “These superstitions survive in the backwaters of America.” “The race car driver lived through several very serious accidents.”

    Synonyms: survive, last, live on, go, endure, hold up, hold out · Kinds: stand up, perennate, live out

  4. Support oneself.

    “He could barely exist on such a low wage.” “Can you live on $2000 a month in New York City?” “Many people in the world have to subsist on $1 a day.”

    Synonyms: exist, survive, subsist · Kinds: breathe, freewheel

  5. Have life, be alive.

    “Our great leader is no more.” “My grandfather lived until the end of war.”

    Synonyms: be

  6. Have firsthand knowledge of states, situations, emotions, or sensations.

    “I know the feeling!” “Have you ever known hunger?” “The holocaust survivors have lived a nightmare.”

    Synonyms: know, experience · A kind of: experience · Kinds: taste, relive

  7. Pursue a positive and satisfying existence.

    “You must accept yourself and others if you really want to live.”

Live as an adjective

  1. Actually being performed at the time of hearing or viewing.

    “A live television program.” “Brought to you live from Lincoln Center.” “Live entertainment involves performers actually in the physical presence of a live audience.”

    Synonyms: unrecorded · Opposite: recorded · Similar: unfilmed

  2. Exerting force or containing energy.

    “Live coals.” “Tossed a live cigarette out the window.” “Got a shock from a live wire.”

    Opposite: dead · Similar: in play, living

  3. Possessing life.

    “The happiest person alive.” “The nerve is alive.” “Doctors are working hard to keep him alive.”

    Synonyms: alive · Similar: liveborn, viable, vital

  4. Highly reverberant.

    “A live concert hall.”

    Similar: reverberant

  5. Charged with an explosive.

    “Live ammunition.”

    Similar: loaded

  6. Elastic; rebounds readily.

    “Clean bouncy hair.” “A lively tennis ball.” “As resilient as seasoned hickory.”

    Synonyms: bouncy, lively, resilient, springy · Similar: elastic

  7. Abounding with life and energy.

    “The club members are a really live bunch.”

    Similar: lively

  8. In current use or ready for use.

    “Live copy is ready to be set in type or already set but not yet proofread.”

    Similar: current

  9. Of current relevance.

    “A live issue.” “Still a live option.”

    Similar: current

  10. Charged or energized with electricity.

    “A hot wire.” “A live wire.”

    Synonyms: hot · Similar: charged

  11. Capable of erupting.

    “A live volcano.” “The volcano is very much alive.”

    Synonyms: alive · Similar: active

Live as an adverb

  1. Not recorded.

    “The opera was broadcast live.”

Phrases and compounds with "live"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • live in (verb) Live in the house where one works.
  • live on (verb) Continue to live through hardship or adversity.
  • live oak (noun) Any of several American evergreen oaks.
  • live out (verb) Live out one's life; live to the end.
  • live axle (noun) The axle of a self-propelled vehicle that provides the driving power.
  • live body (noun) The body of a living animal or person.
  • live down (verb) Live so as to annul some previous behavior.
  • live load (noun) A variable load on a structure (e.g. a bridge) such as moving traffic.
  • live over (verb) Experience again, often in the imagination.
  • live wire (noun) An alert and energetic person.
  • live with (verb) Tolerate or accommodate oneself to.
  • live birth (noun) The birth of a living fetus (regardless of the length of gestation).
  • live it up (verb) Enjoy oneself.
  • live steam (noun) Steam coming from a boiler at full pressure.
  • live up to (verb) Meet the requirements or expectations of.
  • live-bearer (noun) Small usually brightly-colored viviparous surface-feeding fishes of fresh or brackish warm waters; often used in mosquito control.
