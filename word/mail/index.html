Mail: meaning, synonyms and examples

Mail is used as a verb (2 senses) and noun (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: Send via the postal service.

In Hindi
डाक
Synonyms
get off, post, send, mail service, postal service, chain mail, ring mail, chain armor, chain armour, ring armor
Word family
mailer, mailing

Mail as a verb

  1. Send via the postal service.

    “I'll mail you the check tomorrow.”

    Synonyms: get off · A kind of: send · Kinds: pouch

  2. Cause to be directed or transmitted to another place.

    “Send me your latest results.” “I'll mail you the paper when it's written.”

    Synonyms: post, send · A kind of: transfer · Kinds: express, airmail, register, express-mail

Mail as a noun

  1. The bags of letters and packages that are transported by the postal service.

    A kind of: message · Kinds: first class, express, parcel post, bulk mail, third-class mail, airmail, air mail, surface mail

  2. The system whereby messages are transmitted via the post office.

    “The mail handles billions of items every day.” “He works for the United States mail service.” “In England they call mail `the post'.”

    Synonyms: mail service, postal service, post · A kind of: communication · Kinds: airmail, snail mail, rural free delivery

  3. A conveyance that transports the letters and packages that are conveyed by the postal system.

    A kind of: conveyance

  4. Any particular collection of letters or packages that is delivered.

    “Your mail is on the table.” “Is there any post for me?” “She was opening her post.”

    Synonyms: post · A kind of: collection · Kinds: fan mail, mailing

  5. (Middle Ages) flexible armor made of interlinked metal rings.

    Synonyms: chain mail, ring mail, chain armor, chain armour, ring armor, ring armour · A kind of: body armor · Kinds: brigandine, habergeon, hauberk, voider

Phrases and compounds with "mail"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • e-mail (noun) (computer science) a system of world-wide electronic communication in which a computer user can compose a message at one terminal that can be regenerated at the recipient's terminal when the recipient logs in.
  • air mail (noun) Mail that is sent by air transport.
  • fan mail (noun) Mail sent to public figures from their admirers.
  • mail car (noun) A railway car in which mail is transported and sorted.
  • mail out (verb) Transmit by mail.
  • bulk mail (noun) Mail consisting of large numbers of identical items (circulars or advertisements) sent to individual addresses at less than 1st-class rates and paid for in one lot.
  • dead mail (noun) Mail that can neither be delivered nor returned.
  • junk mail (noun) Third-class mail consisting of advertising and often addressed to `resident' or `occupant'.
  • mail boat (noun) A boat for carrying mail.
  • mail call (noun) A call of names of those receiving mail.
  • mail slot (noun) A slot (usually in a door) through which mail can be delivered.
  • mail-clad (adjective) Wearing protective mail.
  • ring mail (noun) (Middle Ages) flexible armor made of interlinked metal rings.
  • chain mail (noun) (Middle Ages) flexible armor made of interlinked metal rings.
  • mail clerk (noun) A clerk in a post office.
  • mail fraud (noun) Use of the mails to defraud someone.
