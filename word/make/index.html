Make: meaning, synonyms and examples

Make is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Engage in.

In Hindi
बनाना
Synonyms
do, get, create, induce, stimulate, cause, have, produce, draw, gain
Opposites
unmake, break
Word family
making, maker

Make as a verb

  1. Engage in.

    “Make love, not war.” “Make an effort.” “Do research.”

    Synonyms: do · Kinds: overdo

  2. Give certain properties to something.

    “Get someone mad.” “She made us look silly.” “He made a fool of himself at the meeting.”

    Synonyms: get · A kind of: change · Kinds: render, get, leave

  3. Make or cause to be or to become.

    “Make a mess in one's office.” “Create a furor.”

    Synonyms: create · Kinds: beget, reproduce, regenerate, clear, distill, derive, froth, lay down

  4. Cause to do; cause to act in a specified manner.

    “The ads induced me to buy a VCR.” “My children finally got me to buy a computer.” “My wife made me buy a new sofa.”

    Synonyms: induce, stimulate, cause, have, get · Kinds: decide, persuade, bring, solicit, encourage, let, lead, prompt

  5. Give rise to; cause to happen or occur, not always intentionally.

    “Cause a commotion.” “Make a stir.” “Cause an accident.”

    Synonyms: cause, do · A kind of: make · Kinds: determine, initiate, effect, make, occasion, provoke, engender, motivate

  6. Create or manufacture a man-made product.

    “We produce more cars than we can sell.” “The company has been making toys for two centuries.”

    Synonyms: produce, create · Kinds: breed, remake, prefabricate, underproduce, output, pulse, clap up, custom-make

  7. Make, formulate, or derive in the mind.

    “I draw a line here.” “Draw a conclusion.” “Draw parallels.”

    Synonyms: draw · A kind of: create by mental act

  8. Compel or make somebody or something to act in a certain way.

    “People cannot be made to integrate just by passing a law!” “Heat makes you sweat.”

    A kind of: cause · Kinds: drive

  9. Create by artistic means.

    “Create a poem.” “Schoenberg created twelve-tone music.” “Picasso created Cubism.”

    Synonyms: create · A kind of: make · Kinds: do, design, design

  10. Earn on some commercial or business transaction; earn as salary or wages.

    “How much do you make a month in your new job?” “She earns a lot in her new job.” “This merger brought in lots of money.”

    Synonyms: gain, take in, clear, earn, realize, realise, pull in, bring in · A kind of: get · Kinds: eke out, profit, rake off, take home, rake in, gross, yield

  11. Create or design, often in a certain way.

    “Do my room in blue.” “I did this piece in wood to express my love for the forest.”

    Synonyms: do · Opposite: unmake · A kind of: create

  12. To compose or represent:"This wall forms the background of the stage setting".

    “This wall forms the background of the stage setting.” “The branches made a roof.” “This makes a fine introduction.”

    Synonyms: form, constitute · A kind of: constitute · Kinds: chelate, add

  13. Reach a goal, e.g., "make the first team".

    “Make the first team.” “We made it!” “She may not make the grade.”

    Synonyms: reach, get to, progress to · A kind of: achieve

  14. Be or be capable of being changed or made into.

    “He makes a great host.” “He will make a fine father.”

    A kind of: become

Make as a noun

  1. A recognizable kind.

    “There's a new brand of hero in the movies now.” “What make of car is that?”

    Synonyms: brand · A kind of: kind

  2. The act of mixing cards haphazardly.

    Synonyms: shuffle, shuffling · A kind of: reordering · Kinds: reshuffle, riffle

Phrases and compounds with "make"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • make do (verb) Come to terms with.
  • make it (verb) Continue in existence after (an adversity, etc.).
  • make up (verb) Form or compose.
  • make-do (noun) Something contrived to meet an urgent need or emergency.
  • make-up (noun) An event that is substituted for a previously cancelled event.
  • make for (verb) Cause to happen or to occur as a consequence.
  • make fun (verb) Subject to laughter or ridicule.
  • make hay (verb) Turn to one's advantage.
  • make off (verb) Run away; usually includes taking something or somebody along.
  • make out (verb) Detect with the senses.
  • make way (verb) Get out of the way.
  • film-make (verb) Make films; be a film maker.
  • make bold (verb) Take upon oneself; act presumptuously, without permission.
  • make full (verb) Make full, also in a metaphorical sense.
  • make good (verb) Act as promised.
  • make grow (verb) Cause to grow and differentiate in ways conforming to its natural development.
