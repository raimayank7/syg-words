Master: meaning, synonyms and examples

Master is used as a noun (10 senses), verb (4 senses) and adjective (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: An artist of consummate skill.

In Hindi
मालिक; उस्ताद; महारत पाना
Synonyms
maestro, overlord, lord, victor, superior, headmaster, schoolmaster, master copy, original, captain
Word family
masterly, mastership, mastery, mastering

Master as a noun

  1. An artist of consummate skill.

    “A master of the violin.” “One of the old masters.”

    Synonyms: maestro · A kind of: artist · Kinds: old master

  2. A person who has general authority over others.

    Synonyms: overlord, lord · A kind of: ruler · Kinds: feudal lord

  3. A combatant who is able to defeat rivals.

    Synonyms: victor, superior · A kind of: combatant · Kinds: conqueror

  4. Directs the work of others.

    A kind of: employer · Kinds: postmaster, spymaster, stationmaster

  5. Presiding officer of a school.

    Synonyms: headmaster, schoolmaster · A kind of: principal · Kinds: housemaster

  6. An original creation (i.e., an audio recording) from which copies can be made.

    Synonyms: master copy, original · A kind of: creation

  7. An officer who is licensed to command a merchant ship.

    Synonyms: captain, sea captain, skipper · A kind of: officer

  8. Someone who holds a master's degree from academic institution.

    A kind of: scholar

  9. An authority qualified to teach apprentices.

    Synonyms: professional · A kind of: authority · Kinds: past master

  10. Key that secures entrance everywhere.

    Synonyms: passkey, passe-partout, master key · A kind of: key · Kinds: skeleton key

Master as a verb

  1. Be or become completely proficient or skilled in.

    “She mastered Japanese in less than two years.”

    Synonyms: get the hang · A kind of: know

  2. Get on top of; deal with successfully.

    “He overcame his shyness.”

    Synonyms: overcome, get over, subdue, surmount · A kind of: beat · Kinds: bulldog

  3. Have dominance or the power to defeat over.

    “Her pain completely mastered her.” “The methods can master the problems.”

    Synonyms: dominate · A kind of: control · Kinds: command, subjugate

  4. Have a firm understanding or knowledge of; be on top of.

    “Do you control these data?”

    Synonyms: control · A kind of: know · Kinds: cinch

Master as an adjective

  1. Most important element.

    “The chief aim of living.” “The main doors were of solid glass.” “The principal rivers of America.”

    Synonyms: chief, main, primary, principal · Similar: important

Phrases and compounds with "master"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • master key (noun) Key that secures entrance everywhere.
  • old master (noun) A great European painter prior to 19th century.
  • master copy (noun) An original creation (i.e., an audio recording) from which copies can be made.
  • master file (noun) (computer science) a computer file that is used as the authority in a given job and that is relatively permanent.
  • master plan (noun) A long-term outline of a project or government function.
  • master race (noun) A race that considers itself superior to all others and fitted to rule the others.
  • past master (noun) Someone who was formerly a master.
  • chess master (noun) A chess player of great skill.
  • dance master (noun) A professional teacher of dancing.
  • drill master (noun) A noncommissioned officer who instructs recruits in military marching and discipline.
  • games-master (noun) The teacher in charge of games at a school.
  • master class (noun) A class (especially in music) given to talented students by an expert.
  • ballet master (noun) A man who directs and teaches and rehearses dancers for a ballet company.
  • riding master (noun) Someone who teaches horsemanship.
  • dancing-master (noun) A professional teacher of dancing.
  • master bedroom (noun) The principal bedroom in a house; usually occupied by the head of the household.
