Place: meaning, synonyms and examples

Place is used as a noun (14 senses) and verb (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: A point located with respect to surface features of some region.

In Hindi
जगह, स्थान; रखना
Synonyms
topographic point, spot, property, stead, position, lieu, shoes, home, post, berth
Word family
placement

Place as a noun

  1. A point located with respect to surface features of some region.

    “This is a nice place for a picnic.” “A bright spot on a planet.”

    Synonyms: topographic point, spot · A kind of: point · Kinds: junction, birthplace, polling place, end, hiding place, high, hole-in-the-wall, holy place

  2. Any area set aside for a particular purpose.

    “Who owns this place?” “The president was concerned about the property across from the White House.”

    Synonyms: property · A kind of: geographical area · Kinds: boatyard, sanctuary, center, colony, hatchery

  3. An abstract mental location.

    “He has a special place in my thoughts.” “A place in my heart.” “A political system with no place for the less prominent groups.”

    A kind of: cognition · Kinds: level

  4. A general vicinity.

    “He comes from a place near Chicago.”

    A kind of: vicinity

  5. The post or function properly or customarily occupied or served by another.

    “Can you go in my stead?” “Took his place.” “In lieu of.”

    Synonyms: stead, position, lieu · A kind of: function · Kinds: behalf

  6. A particular situation.

    “If you were in my place what would you do?”

    Synonyms: shoes · A kind of: situation

  7. Where you live at a particular time.

    “Deliver the package to my home.” “He doesn't have a home to go to.” “Your place or mine?”

    Synonyms: home · A kind of: residence · Kinds: home away from home

  8. A job in an organization.

    “He occupied a post in the treasury.”

    Synonyms: position, post, berth, office, spot, billet, situation · A kind of: occupation · Kinds: academicianship, accountantship, admiralty, ambassadorship, apostleship, apprenticeship, associateship, attorneyship

  9. The particular portion of space occupied by something.

    “He put the lamp back in its place.”

    Synonyms: position · A kind of: point · Kinds: wing, half-mast, lead, pole position, polls, pride of place, anomaly, site

  10. Proper or designated social situation.

    “He overstepped his place.” “The responsibilities of a man in his station.” “Married above her station.”

    Synonyms: station · A kind of: social station · Kinds: niche

  11. A space reserved for sitting (as in a theater or on a train or airplane).

    “He booked their seats in advance.” “He sat in someone else's place.”

    Synonyms: seat · A kind of: space · Kinds: perch

  12. The passage that is being read.

    “He lost his place on the page.”

    A kind of: passage

  13. Proper or appropriate position or location.

    “A woman's place is no longer in the kitchen.”

    A kind of: condition

  14. A public square with room for pedestrians.

    “They met at Elm Plaza.” “Grosvenor Place.”

    Synonyms: plaza, piazza · A kind of: public square

Place as a verb

  1. Put into a certain place or abstract location.

    “Put your things here.” “Set the tray down.” “Set the dogs on the scent of the missing children.”

    Synonyms: put, set, pose, position, lay · A kind of: move · Kinds: insert, docket, cock, postpose, prepose, step, replace, stratify

  2. Place somebody in a particular situation or location.

    “He was placed on probation.”

    A kind of: delegate

  3. Assign a rank or rating to.

    “How would you rank these students?” “The restaurant is rated highly in the food guide.”

    Synonyms: rate, rank, range, order, grade · A kind of: evaluate · Kinds: superordinate, shortlist, seed, reorder, subordinate, prioritize, sequence, downgrade

  4. Assign a location to.

    “The company located some of their agents in Los Angeles.”

    Synonyms: locate, site · A kind of: station

  5. To arrange for.

    “Place a phone call.”

    A kind of: order

  6. Take a place in a competition; often followed by an ordinal.

    “Jerry came in third in the Marathon.”

    Synonyms: come in, come out · A kind of: rank

  7. Intend (something) to move towards a certain goal.

    “He aimed his fists towards his opponent's face.” “Criticism directed at her superior.” “Direct your anger towards others, not towards yourself.”

    Synonyms: target, aim, direct, point · A kind of: aim · Kinds: address, range in

  8. Recognize as being; establish the identity of someone or something.

    “She identified the man on the 'wanted' poster.”

    Synonyms: identify · A kind of: determine · Kinds: distinguish

  9. Assign to (a job or a home).

    A kind of: delegate

  10. Locate.

    “The film is set in Africa.”

    Synonyms: set, localize, localise · A kind of: stage

  11. Estimate.

    “We put the time of arrival at 8 P.M.”

    Synonyms: put, set · A kind of: estimate

  12. Identify the location or place of.

    “We localized the source of the infection.”

    Synonyms: localize, localise · A kind of: situate

  13. Make an investment.

    “Put money into bonds.”

    Synonyms: invest, put, commit · A kind of: spend · Kinds: fund, roll over, shelter, tie up, speculate, buy into

  14. Assign to a station.

    Synonyms: station, post, send · A kind of: move · Kinds: garrison, fort, locate

Phrases and compounds with "place"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • in place (adverb) In the original or natural place or site.
  • place mat (noun) A mat serving as table linen for an individual place setting.
  • farm-place (noun) A farm together with its buildings.
  • holy place (noun) A sacred place of pilgrimage.
  • last-place (adjective) Lowest in rank or importance.
  • place down (verb) Cause to sit or seat or be in a settled position or place.
  • place kick (noun) (sports) a kick in which the ball is placed on the ground before kicking.
  • place name (noun) The name by which a geographical place is known.
  • place-kick (verb) Kick (a ball) from a stationary position, in football.
  • take place (verb) Come to pass.
  • eating place (noun) A building where people go to eat.
  • hiding place (noun) A place suitable for hiding something (such as yourself).
  • market place (noun) An area in a town where a public mercantile establishment is set up.
  • out of place (adjective) Of an inappropriate or misapplied nature.
  • place-kicker (noun) (football) a kicker who makes a place kick for a goal.
  • at that place (adverb) In or at that place.
