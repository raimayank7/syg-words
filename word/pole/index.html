Pole: meaning, synonyms and examples

Pole is used as a noun (10 senses) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: A long (usually round) rod of wood or metal or plastic.

In Hindi
खंभा; ध्रुव
Synonyms
perch, rod, celestial pole, terminal, magnetic pole, punt
Word family
polar

Pole as a noun

  1. A long (usually round) rod of wood or metal or plastic.

    A kind of: rod · Kinds: barge pole, boom, caber, clothes tree, mast, ski pole, spar, stilt

  2. A native or inhabitant of Poland.

    Kinds: polack

  3. One of two divergent or mutually exclusive opinions.

    “They are at opposite poles.” “They are poles apart.”

    A kind of: opinion

  4. A linear measure of 16.5 feet.

    Synonyms: perch, rod · A kind of: linear unit

  5. A square rod of land.

    Synonyms: perch, rod · A kind of: area unit

  6. One of two points of intersection of the Earth's axis and the celestial sphere.

    Synonyms: celestial pole · A kind of: celestial point

  7. One of two antipodal points where the Earth's axis of rotation intersects the Earth's surface.

    A kind of: geographic point

  8. A contact on an electrical device (such as a battery) at which electric current enters or leaves.

    Synonyms: terminal · A kind of: contact · Kinds: anode, negative pole, positive pole

  9. A long fiberglass sports implement used for pole vaulting.

    A kind of: sports implement

  10. One of the two ends of a magnet where the magnetism seems to be concentrated.

    Synonyms: magnetic pole · A kind of: end · Kinds: negative magnetic pole, positive pole

Pole as a verb

  1. Propel with a pole.

    “Pole barges on the river.” “We went punting in Cambridge.”

    Synonyms: punt · A kind of: propel

  2. Support on poles.

    “Pole climbing plants like beans.”

    A kind of: hold

  3. Deoxidize molten metals by stirring them with a wooden pole.

    A kind of: deoxidize
