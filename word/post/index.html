Post: meaning, synonyms and examples

Post is used as a noun (11 senses) and verb (12 senses). Its most common meaning is: The position where someone (as a guard or sentry) stands or is assigned to stand.

In Hindi
डाक; खंभा; पद
Synonyms
station, military post, position, berth, office, spot, billet, place, situation, mail
Word family
postal, poster, posting, postage

Post as a noun

  1. The position where someone (as a guard or sentry) stands or is assigned to stand.

    “A soldier manned the entrance post.” “A sentry station.”

    Synonyms: station · A kind of: position · Kinds: bridgehead, lookout, outstation

  2. Military installation at which a body of troops is stationed.

    “This military post provides an important source of income for the town nearby.” “There is an officer's club on the post.”

    Synonyms: military post · A kind of: military installation · Kinds: garrison, outpost

  3. A job in an organization.

    “He occupied a post in the treasury.”

    Synonyms: position, berth, office, spot, billet, place, situation · A kind of: occupation · Kinds: academicianship, accountantship, admiralty, ambassadorship, apostleship, apprenticeship, associateship, attorneyship

  4. An upright consisting of a piece of timber or metal fixed firmly in an upright position.

    “He set a row of posts in the ground and strung barbwire between them.”

    A kind of: upright · Kinds: bollard, carrick bitt, corner post, gatepost, goalpost, hitching post, hop pole, king post

  5. United States aviator who in 1933 made the first solo flight around the world (1899-1935).

  6. United States female author who wrote a book and a syndicated newspaper column on etiquette (1872-1960).

  7. United States manufacturer of breakfast cereals and Postum (1854-1914).

  8. Any particular collection of letters or packages that is delivered.

    “Your mail is on the table.” “Is there any post for me?” “She was opening her post.”

    Synonyms: mail · A kind of: collection · Kinds: fan mail, mailing

  9. A pole or stake set up to mark something (as the start or end of a race track).

    “A pair of posts marked the goal.” “The corner of the lot was indicated by a stake.”

    Synonyms: stake · A kind of: visual signal · Kinds: starting post, winning post

  10. The system whereby messages are transmitted via the post office.

    “The mail handles billions of items every day.” “He works for the United States mail service.” “In England they call mail `the post'.”

    Synonyms: mail, mail service, postal service · A kind of: communication · Kinds: airmail, snail mail, rural free delivery

  11. The delivery and collection of letters and packages.

    “It came by the first post.” “If you hurry you'll catch the post.”

    A kind of: delivery

Post as a verb

  1. Affix in a public place or for public notice.

    “Post a warning.”

    A kind of: affix · Kinds: placard

  2. Publicize with, or as if with, a poster.

    “I'll post the news on the bulletin board.”

    A kind of: announce · Kinds: placard

  3. Assign to a post; put into a post.

    “The newspaper posted him in Timbuktu.”

    A kind of: delegate

  4. Assign to a station.

    Synonyms: station, send, place · A kind of: move · Kinds: garrison, fort, locate

  5. Display, as of records in sports games.

    A kind of: record

  6. Enter on a public list.

    A kind of: list

  7. Transfer (entries) from one account book to another.

    Synonyms: carry · A kind of: transfer

  8. Ride Western style and bob up and down in the saddle in rhythm with a horse's trotting gait.

    A kind of: ride horseback

  9. Mark with a stake.

    “Stake out the path.”

    Synonyms: stake · A kind of: mark

  10. Place so as to be noticed.

    “Post a sign.” “Post a warning at the dump.”

    Synonyms: put up · A kind of: install

  11. Cause to be directed or transmitted to another place.

    “Send me your latest results.” “I'll mail you the paper when it's written.”

    Synonyms: mail, send · A kind of: transfer · Kinds: express, airmail, register, express-mail

  12. Mark or expose as infamous.

    “She was branded a loose woman.”

    Synonyms: brand · A kind of: call

Phrases and compounds with "post"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • post-it (noun) Brand name for a slip of notepaper that has an adhesive that allows it to stick to a surface and be removed without damaging the surface.
  • post doc (noun) A grant that funds postdoctoral study or research.
  • post hoc (noun) The logical fallacy of believing that temporal succession implies a causal relation.
  • post oak (noun) Small deciduous tree of eastern and central United States having dark green lyrate pinnatifid leaves and tough moisture-resistant wood used especially for fence posts.
  • king post (noun) Post connecting the crossbeam to the apex of a triangular truss.
  • post card (noun) A card for sending messages by post without an envelope.
  • post hole (noun) A hole dug in the ground to hold a fence post.
  • post horn (noun) Wind instrument used by postilions of the 18th and 19th centuries.
  • post road (noun) A road over which mail is carried.
  • post-free (adjective) Postpaid.
  • post-paid (adverb) Having the postage paid by the sender.
  • emily post (noun) United States female author who wrote a book and a syndicated newspaper column on etiquette (1872-1960).
  • newel post (noun) The post at the top or bottom of a flight of stairs; it supports the handrail.
  • post horse (noun) A horse kept at an inn or post house for use by mail carriers or for rent to travelers.
  • post house (noun) An inn for exchanging post horses and accommodating riders.
  • post-haste (adverb) As fast as possible; with all possible haste.
