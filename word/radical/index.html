Radical: meaning, synonyms and examples

Radical is used as an adjective (5 senses) and noun (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: (used of opinions and actions) far beyond the norm.

In Hindi
आमूल; उग्र; मूल
Synonyms
extremist, ultra, revolutionary, basal, group, chemical group, free radical, root, root word, base
Opposites
cauline

Radical as an adjective

  1. (used of opinions and actions) far beyond the norm.

    “Extremist political views.” “Radical opinions on education.” “An ultra conservative.”

    Synonyms: extremist, ultra · Similar: immoderate

  2. Markedly new or introducing radical change.

    “A revolutionary discovery.” “Radical political views.”

    Synonyms: revolutionary · Similar: new

  3. Arising from or going to the root or source.

    “A radical flaw in the plan.”

    Similar: basic

  4. Of or relating to or constituting a linguistic root.

    “A radical verb form.”

  5. Especially of leaves; located at the base of a plant or stem; especially arising directly from the root or rootstock or a root-like stem.

    “Basal placentation.” “Radical leaves.”

    Synonyms: basal · Opposite: cauline

Radical as a noun

  1. (chemistry) two or more atoms bound together as a single unit and forming part of a molecule.

    Synonyms: group, chemical group · A kind of: unit · Kinds: acyl, aldehyde group, alkyl, allyl, amino, amyl, azido group, azo group

  2. An atom or group of atoms with at least one unpaired electron; in the body it is usually an oxygen molecule that has lost an electron and will stabilize itself by stealing an electron from a nearby molecule.

    “In the body free radicals are high-energy particles that ricochet wildly and damage cells.”

    Synonyms: free radical · A kind of: atom

  3. A person who has radical ideas or opinions.

    A kind of: person · Kinds: anarchist, extremist, leveler, revolutionist

  4. (mathematics) a quantity expressed as the root of another quantity.

    A kind of: measure

  5. A character conveying the lexical meaning of a logogram.

    A kind of: character

  6. (linguistics) the form of a word after all affixes are removed.

    “Thematic vowels are part of the stem.”

    Synonyms: root, root word, base, stem, theme · A kind of: form

Phrases and compounds with "radical"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • azo radical (noun) The bivalent group -N=N- united to two hydrocarbon groups.
  • free radical (noun) An atom or group of atoms with at least one unpaired electron; in the body it is usually an oxygen molecule that has lost an electron and will stabilize itself by stealing an electron from a nearby molecule.
  • radical chic (noun) An affectation of radical left-wing views and the fashionable dress and lifestyle that goes with them.
  • radical sign (noun) A sign indicating the extraction of a root.
  • alkyl radical (noun) Any of a series of univalent groups of the general formula CnH2n+1 derived from aliphatic hydrocarbons.
  • allyl radical (noun) The univalent unsaturated organic radical C3H5; derived from propylene.
  • azido radical (noun) The univalent group N3- derived from hydrazoic acid.
  • cyano radical (noun) The monovalent group -CN in a chemical compound.
  • ethyl radical (noun) The univalent hydrocarbon radical C2H5 derived from ethane by the removal of one hydrogen atom.
  • vinyl radical (noun) A univalent chemical radical derived from ethylene.
  • acetyl radical (noun) The organic group of acetic acid (CH3CO-).
  • benzyl radical (noun) The univalent radical derived from toluene.
  • methyl radical (noun) The univalent radical CH3- derived from methane.
  • propyl radical (noun) The monovalent organic group C3H7- obtained from propane.
  • uranyl radical (noun) The bivalent radical UO2 which forms salts with acids.
  • benzoyl radical (noun) The univalent radical derived from benzoic acid.
