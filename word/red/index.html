Red: meaning, synonyms and examples

Red is used as an adjective (3 senses) and noun (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: Of a color at the end of the color spectrum (next to orange); resembling the color of blood or cherries or tomatoes or rubies.

Red as an adjective

  1. Of a color at the end of the color spectrum (next to orange); resembling the color of blood or cherries or tomatoes or rubies.

    Synonyms: reddish, ruddy, blood-red, carmine, cerise, cherry, cherry-red, crimson · Similar: chromatic

  2. Characterized by violence or bloodshed.

    “Writes of crimson deeds and barbaric days.” “Fann'd by Conquest's crimson wing.” “Convulsed with red rage.”

    Synonyms: crimson, violent · Similar: bloody

  3. (especially of the face) reddened or suffused with or as if with blood from emotion or exertion.

    “Crimson with fury.” “Turned red from exertion.” “With puffy reddened eyes.”

    Synonyms: crimson, reddened, red-faced, flushed · Similar: colored

Red as a noun

  1. Red color or pigment; the chromatic color resembling the hue of blood.

    Synonyms: redness · A kind of: chromatic color · Kinds: sanguine, chrome red, cardinal, crimson, dark red, purplish red, cerise, scarlet

  2. A tributary of the Mississippi River that flows eastward from Texas along the southern boundary of Oklahoma and through Louisiana.

  3. Emotionally charged terms used to refer to extreme radicals or revolutionaries.

    Synonyms: bolshie, bolshy · A kind of: radical

  4. The amount by which the cost of a business exceeds its revenue.

    “The company operated at a loss last year.” “The company operated in the red last year.”

    Synonyms: loss, red ink · A kind of: sum · Kinds: paper loss, squeeze
