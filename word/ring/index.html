Ring: meaning, synonyms and examples

Ring is used as a verb (6 senses) and noun (9 senses). Its most common meaning is: Sound loudly and sonorously.

In Hindi
अँगूठी; घंटी बजना
Synonyms
peal, resound, echo, reverberate, knell, call, telephone, call up, phone, surround
Word family
ringing, ringer

Ring as a verb

  1. Sound loudly and sonorously.

    “The bells rang.”

    Synonyms: peal · A kind of: sound · Kinds: ding, tintinnabulate, peal, knell

  2. Ring or echo with sound.

    “The hall resounded with laughter.”

    Synonyms: resound, echo, reverberate · A kind of: sound · Kinds: consonate, reecho, reecho, bong

  3. Make (bells) ring, often for the purposes of musical edification.

    “Ring the bells.” “My uncle rings every Sunday at the local church.”

    Synonyms: knell · A kind of: sound · Kinds: toll

  4. Get or try to get into communication (with someone) by telephone.

    “I tried to call you all night.” “Take two aspirin and call me in the morning.”

    Synonyms: call, telephone, call up, phone · A kind of: telecommunicate · Kinds: cell phone, call in

  5. Extend on all sides of simultaneously; encircle.

    “The forest surrounds my property.”

    Synonyms: surround, environ, skirt, border · A kind of: touch · Kinds: fringe, girdle, cloister, enclose, hem in, cloister

  6. Attach a ring to the foot of, in order to identify.

    “Ring birds.” “Band the geese to observe their migratory patterns.”

    Synonyms: band · A kind of: attach

Ring as a noun

  1. A characteristic sound.

    “It has the ring of sincerity.”

    A kind of: sound

  2. A toroidal shape.

    “A ring of ships in the harbor.” “A halo of smoke.”

    Synonyms: halo, annulus, doughnut, anchor ring · A kind of: toroid · Kinds: fairy ring

  3. A rigid circular band of metal or wood or other material used for holding or fastening or hanging or pulling.

    “There was still a rusty iron hoop for tying a horse.”

    Synonyms: hoop · A kind of: band · Kinds: carabiner, collar, curtain ring, key ring, napkin ring, nose ring, rim, rim

  4. (chemistry) a chain of atoms in a molecule that forms a closed loop.

    Synonyms: closed chain · A kind of: chain · Kinds: heterocyclic ring

  5. An association of criminals.

    “Police tried to break up the gang.” “A pack of thieves.”

    Synonyms: gang, pack, mob · A kind of: association · Kinds: youth gang

  6. The sound of a bell ringing.

    “The distinctive ring of the church bell.” “The ringing of the telephone.” “The tintinnabulation that so voluminously swells from the ringing and the dinging of the bells.”

    Synonyms: ringing, tintinnabulation · A kind of: sound · Kinds: bell ringing

  7. A platform usually marked off by ropes in which contestants box or wrestle.

    A kind of: platform · Kinds: boxing ring, sumo ring, wrestling ring

  8. Jewelry consisting of a circlet of precious metal (often set with jewels) worn on the finger.

    “She had rings on every finger.” “He noted that she wore a wedding band.”

    Synonyms: band · A kind of: jewelry · Kinds: annulet, engagement ring, mourning ring, ringlet, signet ring, wedding ring

  9. A strip of material attached to the leg of a bird to identify it (as in studies of bird migration).

    Synonyms: band · A kind of: strip

Phrases and compounds with "ring"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • gas ring (noun) Gas burner consisting of a circular metal pipe with several small holes through which gas can escape to be burned.
  • key ring (noun) A circular ring of metal for holding keys.
  • ring out (verb) Sound loudly.
  • ring rot (noun) Disease of tomatoes and potatoes and tobacco etc caused by the bacterium Pseudomonas solanacearum.
  • life ring (noun) A life preserver in the form of a ring of buoyant material.
  • lock ring (noun) Washer that prevents a nut from loosening.
  • nose ring (noun) A ring worn on the nose as an ornament or on the nose of an animal to control it.
  • ring girl (noun) A young woman who holds up cards indicating the number of the next round at prize fights.
  • ring mail (noun) (Middle Ages) flexible armor made of interlinked metal rings.
  • ring road (noun) A highway that encircles an urban area so that traffic does not have to pass through the center.
  • seal ring (noun) A ring bearing a signet.
  • slip ring (noun) Connection consisting of a metal ring on a rotating part of a machine; provides a continuous electrical connection through brushes on stationary contacts.
  • snap ring (noun) An oblong metal ring with a spring clip; used in mountaineering to attach a rope to a piton or to connect two ropes.
  • sumo ring (noun) The circular ring in which Sumo wrestlers compete.
