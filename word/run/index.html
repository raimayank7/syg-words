Run: meaning, synonyms and examples

Run is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: Move fast by using one's feet, with one foot off the ground at any given time.

In Hindi
दौड़ना; चलाना
Synonyms
scat, scarper, turn tail, lam, run away, hightail it, bunk, head for the hills, take to the woods, escape
Opposites
idle
Word family
runner, running, runny

Run as a verb

  1. Move fast by using one's feet, with one foot off the ground at any given time.

    “Don't run--you'll be out of breath.” “The children ran to the store.”

    A kind of: travel rapidly · Kinds: trot, scurry, romp, run bases, run, outrun, jog, sprint

  2. Flee; take to one's heels; cut and run.

    “If you see this man, run!” “The burglars escaped before the police showed up.”

    Synonyms: scat, scarper, turn tail, lam, run away, hightail it, bunk, head for the hills · A kind of: leave · Kinds: flee, skedaddle

  3. Stretch out over a distance, space, time, or scope; run or extend between two points or beyond a certain point.

    “Service runs all the way to Cranbury.” “His knowledge doesn't go very far.” “My memory extends back to my fourth year of life.”

    Synonyms: go, pass, lead, extend · A kind of: be · Kinds: come, radiate, go far

  4. Direct or control; projects, businesses, etc.

    “She is running a relief operation in the Sudan.”

    Synonyms: operate · A kind of: direct · Kinds: financier, work, block, warm up

  5. Have a particular form.

    “The story or argument runs as follows.” “As the saying goes...”

    Synonyms: go · A kind of: be

  6. Move along, of liquids.

    “Water flowed into the cave.” “The Missouri feeds into the Mississippi.”

    Synonyms: flow, feed, course · A kind of: move · Kinds: flush, jet, tide, circulate, eddy, waste, run down, pour

  7. Perform as expected when applied.

    “The washing machine won't go unless it's plugged in.” “Does this old car still run well?” “This old radio doesn't work anymore.”

    Synonyms: function, work, operate, go · Kinds: double, roll, run, cut, service

  8. Change or be different within limits.

    “Estimates for the losses in the earthquake range as high as $2 billion.” “Interest rates run from 5 to 10 percent.” “The instruments ranged from tuba to cymbals.”

    Synonyms: range · A kind of: be

  9. Run, stand, or compete for an office or a position.

    “Who's running for treasurer this year?”

    Synonyms: campaign · A kind of: race · Kinds: stump, rerun, whistlestop, cross-file

  10. Cause to emit recorded audio or video.

    “They ran the tapes over and over again.” “I'll play you my favorite record.” “He never tires of playing that video.”

    Synonyms: play

  11. Move about freely and without restraint, or act as if running around in an uncontrolled way.

    “Who are these people running around in the building?” “She runs around telling everyone of her troubles.” “Let the dogs run free.”

    A kind of: travel

  12. Have a tendency or disposition to do or be something; be inclined.

    “She tends to be nervous before her lectures.” “These dresses run small.” “He inclined to corpulence.”

    Synonyms: tend, be given, lean, incline · A kind of: be · Kinds: take kindly to, suffer, gravitate

  13. Be operating, running or functioning.

    “The car is still running--turn it off!”

    Opposite: idle · A kind of: function

  14. Change from one state to another.

    “Run amok.” “Run rogue.” “Run riot.”

    A kind of: become

Run as a noun

  1. A score in baseball made by a runner touching all four bases safely.

    “The Yankees scored 3 runs in the bottom of the 9th.” “Their first tally came in the 3rd inning.”

    Synonyms: tally · A kind of: score · Kinds: earned run, unearned run, run batted in

  2. The act of testing something.

    “In the experimental trials the amount of carbon was measured separately.” “He called each flip of the coin a new trial.”

    Synonyms: test, trial · A kind of: attempt · Kinds: double blind, audition, field trial, fitting, pilot project

  3. A race run on foot.

    “She broke the record for the half-mile run.”

    Synonyms: footrace, foot race · A kind of: race · Kinds: funrun, marathon, obstacle race, steeplechase, track event

  4. An unbroken series of events.

    “Had a streak of bad luck.” “Nicklaus had a run of birdies.”

    Synonyms: streak · A kind of: succession · Kinds: losing streak, winning streak

  5. (American football) a play in which a player attempts to carry the ball through or past the opposing team.

    “The defensive line braced to stop the run.” “The coach put great emphasis on running.”

    Synonyms: running, running play, running game · Kinds: draw, end run, return, reverse, rush

  6. A regular trip.

    “The ship made its run in record time.”

    A kind of: trip

  7. The act of running; traveling on foot at a fast pace.

    “He broke into a run.” “His daily run keeps him fit.”

    Synonyms: running · A kind of: locomotion · Kinds: dash

  8. The continuous period of time during which something (a machine or a factory) operates or continues in operation.

    “The assembly line was on a 12-hour run.”

    A kind of: time period · Kinds: print run, run-time

  9. Unrestricted freedom to use.

    “He has the run of the house.”

    A kind of: liberty

  10. The production achieved during a continuous period of operation (of a machine or factory etc.).

    “A daily run of 100,000 gallons of paint.”

    A kind of: indefinite quantity

  11. A small stream.

    Synonyms: rivulet, rill, runnel, streamlet · A kind of: stream

  12. A race between candidates for elective office.

    “I managed his campaign for governor.” “He is raising money for a Senate run.”

    Synonyms: political campaign, campaign · A kind of: race · Kinds: governor's race, senate campaign

  13. A row of unravelled stitches.

    “She got a run in her stocking.”

    Synonyms: ladder, ravel · A kind of: damage

  14. The pouring forth of a fluid.

    Synonyms: discharge, outpouring · A kind of: flow · Kinds: jet, escape

Phrases and compounds with "run"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • run by (verb) Pass by while running.
  • run on (verb) Talk or narrate at length.
  • run up (verb) Pile up (debts or scores).
  • run-in (noun) An angry dispute.
  • run-on (adjective) (verse) without a rhetorical pause between lines.
  • run-up (noun) A substantial increase over a relatively short period of time.
  • dry run (noun) A practice session in preparation for a public performance (as of a play or speech or concert).
  • end run (noun) (American football) an attempt to advance the ball by running around the end of the line.
  • fun run (noun) A footrace run for fun (often including runners who are sponsored for a charity).
  • pit run (noun) Gravel as found in natural deposits.
  • run dry (verb) Become empty of water.
  • run for (verb) Extend or continue for a certain period of time.
  • run low (verb) To be spent or finished.
  • run off (verb) Run away; usually includes taking something or somebody along.
  • run out (verb) Become used up; be exhausted.
  • ski run (noun) Trail or slope prepared for skiing.
