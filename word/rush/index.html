Rush: meaning, synonyms and examples

Rush is used as a verb (7 senses), noun (7 senses) and adjective (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Move fast.

In Hindi
जल्दबाज़ी; दौड़ पड़ना
Synonyms
hotfoot, hasten, hie, speed, race, pelt along, rush along, cannonball along, bucket along, belt along
Opposites
linger, delay
Word family
rushing, rusher, rushy

Rush as a verb

  1. Move fast.

    “He rushed down the hall to receive his guests.” “The cars raced down the street.”

    Synonyms: hotfoot, hasten, hie, speed, race, pelt along, rush along, cannonball along · Opposite: linger · A kind of: travel · Kinds: barge, tear, dart

  2. Attack suddenly.

    A kind of: assail · Kinds: charge

  3. Urge to an unnatural speed.

    “Don't rush me, please!”

    Synonyms: hurry · Opposite: delay · A kind of: urge

  4. Act or move at high speed.

    “We have to rush!” “Hurry--it's late!”

    Synonyms: hasten, hurry, look sharp, festinate · A kind of: act

  5. Run with the ball, in football.

    A kind of: run

  6. Cause to move fast or to rush or race.

    “The psychologist raced the rats through a long maze.”

    Synonyms: race · A kind of: move

  7. Cause to occur rapidly.

    “The infection precipitated a high fever and allergic reactions.”

    Synonyms: induce, stimulate, hasten · A kind of: effect

Rush as a noun

  1. The act of moving hurriedly and in a careless manner.

    “In his haste to leave he forgot his book.”

    Synonyms: haste, hurry, rushing · A kind of: motion · Kinds: dash, scamper

  2. A sudden forceful flow.

    Synonyms: spate, surge, upsurge · A kind of: flow · Kinds: debris surge, onrush

  3. Grasslike plants growing in wet places and having cylindrical often hollow stems.

    A kind of: marsh plant · Kinds: bulrush, jointed rush, toad rush, hard rush, salt rush, slender rush

  4. Physician and American Revolutionary leader; signer of the Declaration of Independence (1745-1813).

  5. The swift release of a store of affective force.

    “They got a great bang out of it.” “What a boot!” “He does it for kicks.”

    Synonyms: bang, boot, charge, flush, thrill, kick · A kind of: exhilaration

  6. A sudden burst of activity.

    “Come back after the rush.”

    A kind of: outburst

  7. (American football) an attempt to advance the ball by running into the line.

    “The linebackers were ready to stop a rush.”

    Synonyms: rushing · A kind of: run

Rush as an adjective

  1. Not accepting reservations.

    Synonyms: first-come-first-serve · Similar: unreserved

  2. Done under pressure.

    “A rush job.”

    Synonyms: rushed · Similar: hurried

Phrases and compounds with "rush"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • rush nut (noun) European sedge having small edible nutlike tubers.
  • rush off (verb) Depart in a hurry.
  • rush out (verb) Jump out from a hiding place and surprise (someone).
  • gold rush (noun) A sudden happening that brings good fortune (as a sudden opportunity to make money).
  • hard rush (noun) Tall rush of temperate regions.
  • rush away (verb) Depart in a hurry.
  • rush hour (noun) The times at the beginning and end of the working day when many people are traveling to or from work.
  • rush rose (noun) Woody yellow-flowered perennial of southeastern United States.
  • salt rush (noun) Rush of the Pacific coast of North America.
  • soft rush (noun) Tall rush with soft erect or arching stems found in Eurasia, Australia, New Zealand, and common in North America.
  • toad rush (noun) Low-growing annual rush of damp low-lying ground; nearly cosmopolitan.
  • paper rush (noun) Tall sedge of the Nile valley yielding fiber that served many purposes in historic times.
  • rush along (verb) Move fast.
  • rush aster (noun) A variety of aster.
  • rush grass (noun) Grass having wiry stems and sheathed panicles.
  • rush-grass (noun) Grass having wiry stems and sheathed panicles.
