Sense: meaning, synonyms and examples

Sense is used as a noun (5 senses) and verb (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: A general conscious awareness.

In Hindi
इंद्रिय; समझ; अर्थ
Synonyms
signified, sensation, sentience, sentiency, sensory faculty, common sense, good sense, gumption, horse sense, mother wit
Word family
sensify, sensorial, sensuous, sensible, sensitive, sensation, sensor, sensing

Sense as a noun

  1. A general conscious awareness.

    “A sense of security.” “A sense of happiness.” “A sense of danger.”

    A kind of: awareness · Kinds: sense of direction, sense of responsibility

  2. The meaning of a word or expression; the way in which a word or expression or situation can be interpreted.

    “The dictionary gave several senses for the word.” “In the best sense charity is really a duty.” “The signifier is linked to the signified.”

    Synonyms: signified · A kind of: meaning · Kinds: word meaning

  3. The faculty through which the external world is apprehended.

    “In the dark he had to depend on touch and on his senses of smell and hearing.”

    Synonyms: sensation, sentience, sentiency, sensory faculty · A kind of: faculty · Kinds: modality, sensitivity

  4. Sound practical judgment.

    “Common sense is not so common.” “He hasn't got the sense God gave little green apples.” “Fortunately she had the good sense to run away.”

    Synonyms: common sense, good sense, gumption, horse sense, mother wit · A kind of: sagacity · Kinds: logic, nous, road sense

  5. A natural appreciation or ability.

    “A keen musical sense.” “A good sense of timing.”

    A kind of: appreciation

Sense as a verb

  1. Perceive by a physical sensation, e.g., coming from the skin or muscles.

    “He felt the wind.” “She felt an object brushing her arm.” “He felt his flesh crawl.”

    Synonyms: feel · A kind of: perceive

  2. Detect some circumstance or entity automatically.

    “This robot can sense the presence of people in the room.” “Particle detectors sense ionization.”

    A kind of: detect

  3. Become aware of not through the senses but instinctively.

    “I sense his hostility.” “I smell trouble.” “Smell out corruption.”

    Synonyms: smell, smell out · A kind of: perceive

  4. Comprehend.

    “I sensed the real meaning of his letter.”

    A kind of: understand

Phrases and compounds with "sense"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • good sense (noun) Sound practical judgment.
  • make sense (verb) Be reasonable or logical or comprehensible.
  • road sense (noun) Good judgment in avoiding trouble or accidents on the road.
  • word sense (noun) The accepted meaning of a word.
  • horse sense (noun) Sound practical judgment.
  • moral sense (noun) Motivation deriving logically from ethical or moral principles that govern a person's thoughts and actions.
  • sense datum (noun) An unelaborated elementary awareness of stimulation.
  • sense organ (noun) An organ having nerve endings (in the skin or viscera or eye or ear or nose or mouth) that respond to stimulation.
  • sixth sense (noun) Grasping the inner nature of things intuitively.
  • common sense (noun) Sound practical judgment.
  • muscle sense (noun) The ability to feel movements of the limbs and body.
  • sense tagger (noun) A tagging program whose labels indicate the meanings of words or expressions.
  • visual sense (noun) The ability to see; the visual faculty.
  • sense of duty (noun) A motivating awareness of ethical responsibility.
  • somatic sense (noun) The faculty of bodily perception; sensory systems associated with the body; includes skin senses and proprioception and the internal organs.
  • auditory sense (noun) The ability to hear; the auditory faculty.
