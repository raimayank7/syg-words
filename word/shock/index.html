Shock: meaning, synonyms and examples

Shock is used as a noun (10 senses) and verb (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: The feeling of distress and disbelief that you have when something bad happens accidentally.

In Hindi
झटका, सदमा
Synonyms
daze, stupor, impact, electric shock, electrical shock, seismic disturbance, blow, jolt, jar, jounce
Word family
shocker

Shock as a noun

  1. The feeling of distress and disbelief that you have when something bad happens accidentally.

    “His mother's death left him in a daze.” “He was numb with shock.”

    Synonyms: daze, stupor · A kind of: stupefaction

  2. The violent interaction of individuals or groups entering into combat.

    “The armies met in the shock of battle.”

    Synonyms: impact · A kind of: fight

  3. A reflex response to the passage of electric current through the body.

    “Subjects received a small electric shock when they made the wrong response.” “Electricians get accustomed to occasional shocks.”

    Synonyms: electric shock, electrical shock · A kind of: reflex

  4. (pathology) bodily collapse or near collapse caused by inadequate oxygen delivery to the cells; characterized by reduced cardiac output and rapid heartbeat and circulatory insufficiency and pallor.

    “Loss of blood is an important cause of shock.”

    A kind of: collapse · Kinds: cardiogenic shock, hypovolemic shock, obstructive shock, distributive shock, insulin shock

  5. An instance of agitation of the earth's crust.

    “The first shock of the earthquake came shortly after noon while workers were at lunch.”

    Synonyms: seismic disturbance · A kind of: earthquake

  6. An unpleasant or disappointing surprise.

    “It came as a shock to learn that he was injured.”

    Synonyms: blow · A kind of: surprise · Kinds: blip

  7. A pile of sheaves of grain set on end in a field to dry; stalks of Indian corn set up in a field.

    “Corn is bound in small sheaves and several sheaves are set up together in shocks.” “Whole fields of wheat in shock.”

    A kind of: pile

  8. A bushy thick mass (especially hair).

    “He had an unruly shock of black hair.”

    A kind of: mass

  9. A sudden jarring impact.

    “The door closed with a jolt.” “All the jars and jolts were smoothed out by the shock absorbers.”

    Synonyms: jolt, jar, jounce · A kind of: blow

  10. A mechanical damper; absorbs energy of sudden impulses.

    “The old car needed a new set of shocks.”

    Synonyms: shock absorber, cushion · A kind of: damper · Kinds: air cushion

Shock as a verb

  1. Surprise greatly; knock someone's socks off.

    “I was floored when I heard that I was promoted.”

    Synonyms: floor, ball over, blow out of the water, take aback · A kind of: surprise · Kinds: startle

  2. Strike with disgust or revulsion.

    “The scandalous behavior of this married woman shocked her friends.”

    Synonyms: offend, scandalize, scandalise, appal, appall, outrage · A kind of: disgust

  3. Collide violently.

    A kind of: collide

  4. Collect or gather into shocks.

    “Shock grain.”

    A kind of: gather

  5. Subject to electrical shocks.

    A kind of: treat · Kinds: galvanize

  6. Inflict a trauma upon.

    Synonyms: traumatize, traumatise · A kind of: injure

Phrases and compounds with "shock"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • shock wave (noun) A region of high pressure travelling through a gas at a high velocity.
  • shell shock (noun) A mental disorder caused by stress of active warfare.
  • toxic shock (noun) Syndrome resulting from a serious acute (sometimes fatal) infection associated with the presence of staphylococcus; characterized by fever and diarrhea and nausea and diffuse erythema and shock; occurs especially in menstruating women using highly absorbent tampons.
  • shock troops (noun) Soldiers who are specially trained and armed to lead an assault.
  • shock-headed (adjective) Having a shock (or untidy mass) of hair.
  • culture shock (noun) A condition of disorientation affecting someone who is suddenly exposed to an unfamiliar culture or way of life or set of attitudes.
  • insulin shock (noun) Hypoglycemia produced by excessive insulin in the system causing coma.
  • shock therapy (noun) Treatment of certain psychotic states by the administration of shocks that are followed by convulsions.
  • electric shock (noun) The use of electricity to administer punishment or torture.
  • metrazol shock (noun) The administration of sufficient Metrazol to induce convulsions and coma.
  • shock absorber (noun) A mechanical damper; absorbs energy of sudden impulses.
  • shock treatment (noun) Treatment of certain psychotic states by the administration of shocks that are followed by convulsions.
  • shock-absorbent (adjective) Having the capacity to absorb the energy of an impact.
  • electrical shock (noun) A reflex response to the passage of electric current through the body.
  • cardiogenic shock (noun) Shock caused by cardiac arrest.
  • hypovolemic shock (noun) Shock caused by severe blood or fluid loss.
