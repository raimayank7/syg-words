Sit: meaning, synonyms and examples

Sit is used as a verb (10 senses). Its most common meaning is: Be seated.

In Hindi
बैठना
Synonyms
sit down, sit around, model, pose, posture, ride, baby-sit, seat
Opposites
stand, lie
Word family
sitting, sitter

Sit as a verb

  1. Be seated.

    Synonyms: sit down · Opposite: stand, lie · Kinds: lounge, sprawl, perch, squat

  2. Be around, often idly or without specific purpose.

    “The object sat in the corner.” “We sat around chatting for another hour.”

    Synonyms: sit around · A kind of: be

  3. Take a seat.

    Synonyms: sit down · A kind of: change posture

  4. Be in session.

    “When does the court of law sit?”

    A kind of: convene

  5. Assume a posture as for artistic purposes.

    “We don't know the woman who posed for Leonardo so often.”

    Synonyms: model, pose, posture · A kind of: expose · Kinds: ramp

  6. Sit and travel on the back of animal, usually while controlling its motions.

    “She never sat a horse!” “Did you ever ride a camel?” “The girl liked to drive the young mare.”

    Synonyms: ride · A kind of: travel · Kinds: override, ride herd, outride, ride horseback, prance, canter, gallop

  7. Be located or situated somewhere.

    “The White House sits on Pennsylvania Avenue.”

    A kind of: be

  8. Work or act as a baby-sitter.

    “I cannot baby-sit tonight; I have too much homework to do.”

    Synonyms: baby-sit · A kind of: guard

  9. Show to a seat; assign a seat for.

    “The host seated me next to Mrs. Smith.”

    Synonyms: seat, sit down · A kind of: put · Kinds: reseat

  10. Serve in a specific professional capacity.

    “The priest sat for confession.” “She sat on the jury.”

    A kind of: serve

Phrases and compounds with "sit"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • sit by (verb) Be inactive or indifferent while something is happening.
  • sit in (verb) Attend as a visitor.
  • sit up (verb) Not go to bed.
  • sit-in (noun) A form of civil disobedience in which demonstrators occupy seats and refuse to move.
  • sit-up (noun) A stomach exercise in which a person sits up from a supine position without using the arms for leverage.
  • sit out (verb) Not participate in (an activity, such as a dance or a sports event).
  • baby-sit (verb) Take watchful responsibility for.
  • sit back (verb) Settle into a comfortable sitting position.
  • sit down (verb) Take a seat.
  • sit-down (noun) A strike in which workers refuse to leave the workplace until a settlement is reached.
  • sit tight (verb) Maintain the same position; wait it out.
  • sit around (verb) Be around, often idly or without specific purpose.
  • sit-down strike (noun) A strike in which workers refuse to leave the workplace until a settlement is reached.
