Snap: meaning, synonyms and examples

Snap is used as a verb (13 senses) and noun (13 senses). Its most common meaning is: Utter in an angry, sharp, or abrupt tone.

In Hindi
चटकना; चुटकी
Synonyms
snarl, tear, rupture, bust, crack, click, snatch, snatch up, flick, break down
Word family
snapper, snappy

Snap as a verb

  1. Utter in an angry, sharp, or abrupt tone.

    “The sales clerk snapped a reply at the angry customer.” “The guard snarled at us.”

    Synonyms: snarl · A kind of: talk

  2. Separate or cause to separate abruptly.

    “The rope snapped.” “Tear the paper.”

    Synonyms: tear, rupture, bust · A kind of: separate · Kinds: lacerate, rend, shred

  3. Break suddenly and abruptly, as under tension.

    “The pipe snapped.”

    Synonyms: crack · A kind of: break

  4. Move or strike with a noise.

    “He clicked on the light.” “His arm was snapped forward.”

    Synonyms: click · A kind of: move

  5. Close with a snapping motion.

    “The lock snapped shut.”

    A kind of: close

  6. Make a sharp sound.

    “His fingers snapped.”

    Synonyms: crack · A kind of: sound

  7. Move with a snapping sound.

    “Bullets snapped past us.”

    A kind of: move

  8. To grasp hastily or eagerly.

    “Before I could stop him the dog snatched the ham bone.”

    Synonyms: snatch, snatch up · A kind of: seize · Kinds: swoop

  9. Put in play with a snap.

    “Snap a football.”

    A kind of: hit

  10. Cause to make a snapping sound.

    “Snap your fingers.”

    Synonyms: click, flick · A kind of: move

  11. Lose control of one's emotions.

    “When she heard that she had not passed the exam, she lost it completely.” “When her baby died, she snapped.”

    Synonyms: break down, lose it · A kind of: act · Kinds: dissolve, die, fall apart

  12. Bring the jaws together.

    “He snapped indignantly.”

    A kind of: bite

  13. Record on photographic film.

    “I photographed the scene of the accident.” “She snapped a picture of the President.”

    Synonyms: photograph, shoot · A kind of: record · Kinds: retake, x-ray

Snap as a noun

  1. The act of catching an object with the hands.

    “Mays made the catch with his back to the plate.” “He made a grab for the ball before it landed.” “Martin's snatch at the bridle failed and the horse raced away.”

    Synonyms: catch, grab, snatch · A kind of: touch · Kinds: fair catch, interception, reception, rebound, shoestring catch, mesh

  2. A spell of cold weather.

    “A cold snap in the middle of May.”

    A kind of: while

  3. Tender green beans without strings that easily snap into sections.

    Synonyms: snap bean · A kind of: green bean

  4. A crisp round cookie flavored with ginger.

    Synonyms: gingersnap, ginger snap, ginger nut · A kind of: cookie · Kinds: brandysnap

  5. The noise produced by the rapid movement of a finger from the tip to the base of the thumb on the same hand.

    “Servants appeared at the snap of his fingers.”

    A kind of: noise

  6. A sudden sharp noise.

    “The crack of a whip.” “He heard the cracking of the ice.” “He can hear the snap of a twig.”

    Synonyms: crack, cracking · A kind of: noise

  7. A sudden breaking.

    A kind of: break

  8. The tendency of a body to return to its original shape after it has been stretched or compressed.

    “The waistband had lost its snap.”

    Synonyms: elasticity · A kind of: physical property · Kinds: resilience, bounce, give, stretch, temper, elasticity of shear

  9. An informal photograph; usually made with a small hand-held camera.

    “My snapshots haven't been developed yet.” “He tried to get unposed shots of his friends.”

    Synonyms: snapshot, shot · A kind of: photograph

  10. A fastener used on clothing; fastens with a snapping sound.

    “Children can manage snaps better than buttons.”

    Synonyms: snap fastener, press stud · A kind of: fastener

  11. Any undertaking that is easy to do.

    “Marketing this product will be no picnic.”

    Synonyms: cinch, breeze, picnic, duck soup, child's play, pushover, walkover, piece of cake · A kind of: undertaking · Kinds: doddle

  12. The act of snapping the fingers; movement of a finger from the tip to the base of the thumb on the same hand.

    “He gave his fingers a snap.”

    A kind of: motion

  13. (American football) putting the ball in play by passing it (between the legs) to a back.

    “The quarterback fumbled the snap.”

    Synonyms: centering · A kind of: pass

Phrases and compounds with "snap"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • snap at (verb) Bite off with a quick bite.
  • snap up (verb) Get hold of or seize quickly and easily.
  • snap off (verb) Break a piece from a whole.
  • snap pea (noun) Variety of pea plant producing peas having crisp rounded edible pods.
  • cold snap (noun) A spell of cold weather.
  • snap back (verb) Recover quickly.
  • snap bean (noun) Tender green beans without strings that easily snap into sections.
  • snap brim (noun) A brim that can be turned up and down on opposite sides.
  • snap line (noun) A chalked string used in the building trades to make a straight line on a vertical surface.
  • snap ring (noun) An oblong metal ring with a spring clip; used in mountaineering to attach a rope to a piton or to connect two ropes.
  • snap roll (noun) A fast roll.
  • ginger snap (noun) A crisp round cookie flavored with ginger.
  • snap willow (noun) Large willow tree with stiff branches that are easily broken.
  • snap fastener (noun) A fastener used on clothing; fastens with a snapping sound.
  • snap-brim hat (noun) A hat with a snap brim.
  • sugar snap pea (noun) Variety of pea plant producing peas having crisp rounded edible pods.
