Stem: meaning, synonyms and examples

Stem is used as a noun (6 senses) and verb (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: (linguistics) the form of a word after all affixes are removed.

In Hindi
तना
Synonyms
root, root word, base, theme, radical, stalk, shank, bow, fore, prow
Word family
stemmer

Stem as a noun

  1. (linguistics) the form of a word after all affixes are removed.

    “Thematic vowels are part of the stem.”

    Synonyms: root, root word, base, theme, radical · A kind of: form

  2. A slender or elongated structure that supports a plant or fungus or a plant part or plant organ.

    Synonyms: stalk · A kind of: plant organ · Kinds: gynophore, carpophore, cornstalk, filament, funicle, petiolule, cane, sporangiophore

  3. Cylinder forming a long narrow part of something.

    Synonyms: shank · A kind of: cylinder

  4. The tube of a tobacco pipe.

    A kind of: tube

  5. Front part of a vessel or aircraft.

    “He pointed the bow of the boat toward the finish line.”

    Synonyms: bow, fore, prow · A kind of: front

  6. A turn made in skiing; the back of one ski is forced outward and the other ski is brought parallel to it.

    Synonyms: stem turn · A kind of: turn

Stem as a verb

  1. Grow out of, have roots in, originate in.

    “The increase in the national debt stems from the last war.”

    A kind of: originate in

  2. Cause to point inward.

    “Stem your skis.”

    A kind of: orient

  3. Stop the flow of a liquid.

    “Staunch the blood flow.” “Stem the tide.”

    Synonyms: stanch, staunch, halt · A kind of: check

  4. Remove the stem from.

    “For automatic natural language processing, the words must be stemmed.”

    A kind of: remove

Phrases and compounds with "stem"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • blue stem (noun) Tall grass with smooth bluish leaf sheaths grown for hay in the United States.
  • stem cell (noun) An undifferentiated cell whose daughter cells may differentiate into other cell types (such as blood cells).
  • stem turn (noun) A turn made in skiing; the back of one ski is forced outward and the other ski is brought parallel to it.
  • brain stem (noun) The part of the brain continuous with the spinal cord and comprising the medulla oblongata and pons and midbrain and parts of the hypothalamus.
  • brain-stem (noun) The part of the brain continuous with the spinal cord and comprising the medulla oblongata and pons and midbrain and parts of the hypothalamus.
  • onion stem (noun) A white agaric that tends to cluster and has a club-shaped base.
  • stem vowel (noun) A vowel that ends a stem and precedes an inflection.
  • stem blight (noun) A fungous blight attacking the stems of plants.
  • stem canker (noun) Rhizoctinia disease of potatoes.
  • stem ginger (noun) Tropical Asian plant widely cultivated for its pungent root; source of gingerroot and powdered ginger.
  • stem-winder (noun) A watch that is wound by turning a knob at the stem.
  • stem lettuce (noun) Lettuce valued especially for its edible stems.
  • stem-cell research (noun) Research on stem cells and their use in medicine.
  • black-stem spleenwort (noun) Fern of tropical America: from southern United States to West Indies and Mexico to Brazil.
  • hematopoeitic stem cell (noun) Blood forming stem cells in the bone marrow; T cells and B cells arise from these stem cells.
  • embryonic stem-cell research (noun) Biological research on stem cells derived from embryos and on their use in medicine.
