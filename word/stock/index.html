Stock: meaning, synonyms and examples

Stock is used as a noun (14 senses), verb (7 senses) and adjective (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: The capital raised by a corporation through the issue of shares entitling holders to an ownership interest (equity).

In Hindi
भंडार; शेयर
Synonyms
inventory, gunstock, stock certificate, store, fund, lineage, line, line of descent, descent, bloodline
Word family
stockist, stocker, stocking

Stock as a noun

  1. The capital raised by a corporation through the issue of shares entitling holders to an ownership interest (equity).

    “He owns a controlling share of the company's stock.”

    A kind of: capital · Kinds: common stock, no-par-value stock, preferred stock, float, common stock equivalent, control stock, growth stock, hot stock

  2. The merchandise that a shop has on hand.

    “They carried a vast inventory of hardware.” “They stopped selling in exact sizes in order to reduce inventory.”

    Synonyms: inventory · A kind of: merchandise

  3. The handle of a handgun or the butt end of a rifle or shotgun or part of the support of a machine gun or artillery gun.

    “The rifle had been fitted with a special stock.”

    Synonyms: gunstock · A kind of: handle, support · Kinds: pistol grip

  4. A certificate documenting the shareholder's ownership in the corporation.

    “The value of his stocks doubled during the past year.”

    Synonyms: stock certificate · A kind of: security · Kinds: share

  5. A supply of something available for future use.

    “He brought back a large store of Cuban cigars.”

    Synonyms: store, fund · A kind of: accumulation · Kinds: infrastructure, hoard, provision, issue, seed stock

  6. The descendants of one individual.

    “His entire lineage has been warriors.”

    Synonyms: lineage, line, line of descent, descent, bloodline, blood line, blood, pedigree · A kind of: genealogy · Kinds: family, side

  7. A special variety of domesticated animals within a species.

    “He experimented on a particular breed of white rats.” “He created a new strain of sheep.”

    Synonyms: breed, strain · A kind of: variety, animal group · Kinds: bloodstock, pedigree

  8. Liquid in which meat and vegetables are simmered; used as a basis for e.g. soups or sauces.

    “She made gravy with a base of beef stock.”

    Synonyms: broth · A kind of: soup · Kinds: beef broth, chicken broth, stock cube

  9. The reputation and popularity a person has.

    “His stock was so high he could have been elected mayor.”

    A kind of: repute

  10. Persistent thickened stem of a herbaceous perennial plant.

    Synonyms: caudex · A kind of: stalk

  11. A plant or stem onto which a graft is made; especially a plant grown specifically to provide the root part of grafted plants.

    A kind of: plant part · Kinds: rootstock

  12. Any of several Old World plants cultivated for their brightly colored flowers.

    Synonyms: gillyflower · A kind of: flower · Kinds: brompton stock

  13. Any of various ornamental flowering plants of the genus Malcolmia.

    A kind of: flower

  14. Lumber used in the construction of something.

    “They will cut round stock to 1-inch diameter.”

    A kind of: lumber

Stock as a verb

  1. Have on hand.

    “Do you carry kerosene heaters?”

    Synonyms: carry, stockpile · A kind of: have

  2. Equip with a stock.

    “Stock a rifle.”

    A kind of: equip

  3. Supply with fish.

    “Stock a lake.”

    A kind of: supply

  4. Supply with livestock.

    “Stock a farm.”

    A kind of: supply

  5. Amass so as to keep for future use or sale or for a particular occasion or use.

    “Let's stock coffee as long as prices are low.”

    Synonyms: buy in, stock up · A kind of: supply · Kinds: overstock, understock

  6. Provide or furnish with a stock of something.

    “Stock the larder with meat.”

    A kind of: supply · Kinds: plant, restock

  7. Put forth and grow sprouts or shoots.

    “The plant sprouted early this year.”

    Synonyms: sprout · A kind of: grow

Stock as an adjective

  1. Repeated too often; overfamiliar through overuse.

    “Bromidic sermons.” “His remarks were trite and commonplace.” “Hackneyed phrases.”

    Synonyms: banal, commonplace, hackneyed, old-hat, shopworn, threadbare, timeworn, tired · Similar: unoriginal

  2. Routine.

    “A stock answer.”

    Similar: standard

  3. Regularly and widely used or sold.

    “A standard size.” “A stock item.”

    Synonyms: standard · Similar: regular

Phrases and compounds with "stock"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • in stock (adjective) Available for use or sale.
  • stock up (verb) Amass so as to keep for future use or sale or for a particular occasion or use.
  • hot stock (noun) Newly issued stock that is in great public demand.
  • otc stock (noun) Stock that is not listed and traded on an organized exchange.
  • stock car (noun) A car kept in dealers' stock for regular sales.
  • beef stock (noun) A stock made with beef.
  • seed stock (noun) A supply of seeds (or tubers) reserved for planting.
  • stock cube (noun) A cube of dehydrated stock.
  • stock list (noun) A detailed list of all the items in stock.
  • stock room (noun) Storeroom for storing goods and supplies used in a business.
  • stock-take (noun) An instance of stocktaking.
  • take stock (verb) To look at critically or searchingly, or in minute detail.
  • penny stock (noun) A stock selling for less that $1/share.
  • stock index (noun) Index based on a statistical compilation of the share prices of a number of representative stocks.
  • stock issue (noun) (corporation law) the authorization and delivery of shares of stock for sale to the public or the shares thus offered at a particular time.
  • stock power (noun) A power of attorney document to transfer ownership of a registered security from the owner to another party.
