Stone: meaning, synonyms and examples

Stone is used as a noun (13 senses), verb (1 sense) and adjective (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: A lump or mass of hard consolidated mineral matter.

In Hindi
पत्थर
Synonyms
rock, gem, gemstone, pit, endocarp
Word family
stony

Stone as a noun

  1. A lump or mass of hard consolidated mineral matter.

    “He threw a rock at me.”

    Synonyms: rock · A kind of: natural object · Kinds: achondrite, bedrock, boulder, calculus, chondrite, clastic rock, crystal, intrusion

  2. Building material consisting of a piece of rock hewn in a definite shape for a special purpose.

    “He wanted a special stone to mark the site.”

    A kind of: building material · Kinds: ashlar, capstone, cornerstone, cornerstone, foundation stone, gravestone, grindstone, hearthstone

  3. Material consisting of the aggregate of minerals like those making up the Earth's crust.

    “That mountain is solid rock.” “Stone is abundant in New England and there are many quarries.”

    Synonyms: rock · A kind of: material · Kinds: quartzite, road metal, sedimentary rock, sial, sima, metamorphic rock, gravel, caliche

  4. A crystalline rock that can be cut and polished for jewelry.

    “He had the gem set in a ring for his wife.” “She had jewels made of all the rarest stones.”

    Synonyms: gem, gemstone · A kind of: crystal · Kinds: cabochon, opaque gem, transparent gem

  5. An avoirdupois unit used to measure the weight of a human body; equal to 14 pounds.

    “A heavy chap who must have weighed more than twenty stone.”

    A kind of: avoirdupois unit

  6. The hard inner (usually woody) layer of the pericarp of some fruits (as peaches or plums or cherries or olives) that contains the seed.

    “You should remove the stones from prunes before cooking.”

    Synonyms: pit, endocarp · A kind of: pericarp · Kinds: peach pit, cherry stone

  7. United States jurist who was named chief justice of the United States Supreme Court in 1941 by Franklin D. Roosevelt (1872-1946).

  8. United States filmmaker (born in 1946).

  9. United States feminist and suffragist (1818-1893).

  10. United States journalist who advocated liberal causes (1907-1989).

  11. United States jurist who served on the United States Supreme Court as chief justice (1872-1946).

  12. United States architect (1902-1978).

  13. A lack of feeling or expression or movement.

    “He must have a heart of stone.” “Her face was as hard as stone.”

    A kind of: coldness

Stone as a verb

  1. Remove the pits from.

    “Pit plums and cherries.”

    Synonyms: pit · A kind of: remove

Stone as an adjective

  1. Of any of various dull tannish or grey colors.

    Similar: chromatic

Phrases and compounds with "stone"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • stone age (noun) (archeology) the earliest known period of human culture, characterized by the use of stone implements.
  • stone fly (noun) Primitive winged insect with a flattened body; used as bait by fishermen; aquatic gilled larvae are carnivorous and live beneath stones.
  • stone pit (noun) A surface excavation for extracting stone or slate.
  • blue stone (noun) Hydrated blue crystalline form of copper sulfate.
  • lucy stone (noun) United States feminist and suffragist (1818-1893).
  • stone bass (noun) Brown fish of the Atlantic and Mediterranean found around rocks and shipwrecks.
  • stone crab (noun) Pale flesh with delicate texture and flavor; found in Florida but now very rare.
  • stone pine (noun) Medium-sized two-needled pine of southern Europe having a spreading crown; widely cultivated for its sweet seeds that resemble almonds.
  • stone root (noun) Erect perennial strong-scented with serrate pointed leaves and a loose panicle of yellowish flowers; the eastern United States.
  • stone wall (noun) A fence built of rough stones; used to separate fields.
  • stone-cold (adjective) Completely cold.
  • stone-dead (adjective) As lifeless as a stone.
  • stone-deaf (adjective) Totally deaf; unable to hear anything.
  • stone-face (noun) Any plant of the genus Lithops native to Africa having solitary yellow or white flowers and thick leaves that resemble stones.
  • stone-gray (adjective) Of the color of slate or granite.
  • stone-grey (adjective) Of the color of slate or granite.
