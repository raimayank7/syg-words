Stop: meaning, synonyms and examples

Stop is used as a verb (11 senses) and noun (11 senses). Its most common meaning is: Come to a halt, stop moving.

In Hindi
रुकना, रोकना
Synonyms
halt, discontinue, cease, give up, quit, lay off, block, kibosh, stop over, break
Opposites
start
Word family
stoppage, stopper

Stop as a verb

  1. Come to a halt, stop moving.

    “The car stopped.” “She stopped in front of a store window.”

    Synonyms: halt · Opposite: start · Kinds: go off, pull up short, check, check, check, rein, stall, stall

  2. Put an end to a state or an activity.

    “Quit teasing your little brother.”

    Synonyms: discontinue, cease, give up, quit, lay off · Kinds: drop, leave off, sign off, retire, pull the plug, shut off, cheese, call it quits

  3. Stop from happening or developing.

    “Block his election.” “Halt the process.”

    Synonyms: halt, block, kibosh · A kind of: prevent · Kinds: embargo, stay

  4. Interrupt a trip.

    “We stopped at Aunt Mary's house.” “They stopped for three days in Florence.”

    Synonyms: stop over · A kind of: interrupt · Kinds: call, lay over

  5. Cause to stop.

    “Stop a car.” “Stop the thief.”

    Opposite: start · Kinds: check, rein, halt, bring up, cut, flag down, stall, stall

  6. Prevent completion.

    “Stop the project.” “Break off the negotiations.”

    Synonyms: break, break off, discontinue · A kind of: end · Kinds: fracture, bog down, interrupt

  7. Hold back, as of a danger or an enemy; check the expansion or influence of.

    “Arrest the downward trend.” “Check the growth of communism in South East Asia.” “Contain the rebel movement.”

    Synonyms: check, turn back, arrest, contain, hold back · A kind of: defend · Kinds: cut down

  8. Seize on its way.

    “The fighter plane was ordered to intercept an aircraft that had entered the country's airspace.”

    Synonyms: intercept · A kind of: catch · Kinds: cut off

  9. Have an end, in a temporal, spatial, or quantitative sense; either spatial or metaphorical.

    “The bronchioles terminate in a capillary bed.” “Your rights stop where you infringe upon the rights of other.” “My property ends by the bushes.”

    Synonyms: end, finish, terminate, cease · Kinds: pass away, lapse, cut out, go out, adjourn, vanish, culminate, run out

  10. Render unsuitable for passage.

    “Block the way.” “Barricade the streets.” “Stop the busy road.”

    Synonyms: barricade, block, blockade, block off, block up, bar · A kind of: obstruct · Kinds: shut off, close

  11. Stop and wait, as if awaiting further instructions or developments.

    “Hold on a moment!”

    Synonyms: hold on · A kind of: interrupt

Stop as a noun

  1. The event of something ending.

    “It came to a stop at the bottom of the hill.”

    Synonyms: halt · A kind of: ending · Kinds: cessation, stand

  2. The act of stopping something.

    “The third baseman made some remarkable stops.” “His stoppage of the flow resulted in a flood.”

    Synonyms: stoppage · A kind of: act · Kinds: hemostasis

  3. A brief stay in the course of a journey.

    “They made a stopover to visit their friends.”

    Synonyms: stopover, layover · A kind of: stay · Kinds: night-stop, pit stop, pit stop, stand

  4. The state of inactivity following an interruption.

    “The negotiations were in arrest.” “Held them in check.” “During the halt he got some lunch.”

    Synonyms: arrest, check, halt, hitch, stay, stoppage · A kind of: inaction · Kinds: countercheck, logjam

  5. A spot where something halts or pauses.

    “His next stop is Atlanta.”

    A kind of: topographic point · Kinds: bus stop, checkpoint, loading zone, stopover

  6. A consonant produced by stopping the flow of air at some point and suddenly releasing it.

    “His stop consonants are too aspirated.”

    Synonyms: stop consonant, occlusive, plosive consonant, plosive speech sound, plosive · A kind of: obstruent · Kinds: labial stop, glottal stop, suction stop

  7. A punctuation mark (.) placed at the end of a declarative sentence to indicate a full stop or after abbreviations.

    “In England they call a period a stop.”

    Synonyms: period, point, full stop, full point · A kind of: punctuation · Kinds: suspension point

  8. (music) a knob on an organ that is pulled to change the sound quality from the organ pipes.

    “The organist pulled out all the stops.”

    A kind of: knob

  9. A mechanical device in a camera that controls size of aperture of the lens.

    “The new cameras adjust the diaphragm automatically.”

    Synonyms: diaphragm · A kind of: mechanical device · Kinds: iris

  10. A restraint that checks the motion of something.

    “He used a book as a stop to hold the door open.”

    Synonyms: catch · A kind of: restraint · Kinds: bench hook, doorstop, pawl, tripper

  11. An obstruction in a pipe or tube.

    “We had to call a plumber to clear out the blockage in the drainpipe.”

    Synonyms: blockage, block, closure, occlusion, stoppage · A kind of: obstruction · Kinds: breechblock, plug, vapor lock

Phrases and compounds with "stop"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • stop up (verb) Fill or close tightly with or as if with a plug.
  • bus stop (noun) A place on a bus route where buses stop to discharge and take on passengers.
  • pit stop (noun) A stop during an automobile trip for rest and refreshment.
  • flag stop (noun) A small railway station between the principal stations or a station where the train stops only on a signal.
  • flue stop (noun) An organ stop with the tone of a flue pipe.
  • full stop (noun) A punctuation mark (.) placed at the end of a declarative sentence to indicate a full stop or after abbreviations.
  • reed stop (noun) An organ stop with the tone of a reed instrument.
  • rest stop (noun) Designated paved area beside a main road where cars can stop temporarily.
  • stop bath (noun) An acid bath used to stop the action of a developer.
  • stop dead (verb) Stop moving or become immobilized.
  • stop over (verb) Interrupt a journey temporarily, e.g., overnight.
  • night-stop (noun) A break in a journey for the night.
  • organ stop (noun) A graduated set of organ pipes of like tone quality.
  • short-stop (noun) An acid bath used to stop the action of a developer.
  • stop order (noun) An order to a broker to sell (buy) when the price of a security falls (rises) to a designated level.
  • stop press (noun) Late news that is inserted into the newspaper at the last minute.
