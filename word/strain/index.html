Strain: meaning, synonyms and examples

Strain is used as a noun (11 senses) and verb (9 senses). Its most common meaning is: (physics) deformation of a physical body under the action of applied forces.

In Hindi
खिंचाव, तनाव
Synonyms
stress, tune, melody, air, melodic line, line, melodic phrase, mental strain, nervous strain, breed
Opposites
unstrain
Word family
strenuous, straining, strainer

Strain as a noun

  1. (physics) deformation of a physical body under the action of applied forces.

    A kind of: deformation · Kinds: overstrain

  2. Difficulty that causes worry or emotional tension.

    “She endured the stresses and strains of life.” “He presided over the economy during the period of the greatest stress and danger.”

    Synonyms: stress · A kind of: difficulty

  3. A succession of notes forming a distinctive sequence.

    “She was humming an air from Beethoven.”

    Synonyms: tune, melody, air, melodic line, line, melodic phrase · A kind of: music · Kinds: flourish, glissando, roulade, leitmotiv, theme song, signature, theme, part

  4. (psychology) nervousness resulting from mental stress.

    “His responsibilities were a constant strain.” “The mental strain of staying alert hour after hour was too much for him.”

    Synonyms: mental strain, nervous strain · A kind of: nervousness · Kinds: tension

  5. A special variety of domesticated animals within a species.

    “He experimented on a particular breed of white rats.” “He created a new strain of sheep.”

    Synonyms: breed, stock · A kind of: variety, animal group · Kinds: bloodstock, pedigree

  6. (biology) a group of organisms within a species that differ in trivial ways from similar groups.

    “A new strain of microorganisms.”

    Synonyms: form, variant · A kind of: taxonomic group

  7. Injury to a muscle (often caused by overuse); results in swelling and pain.

    A kind of: injury

  8. The general meaning or substance of an utterance.

    “Although I disagreed with him I could follow the tenor of his argument.”

    Synonyms: tenor · A kind of: meaning · Kinds: drift

  9. An effortful attempt to attain a goal.

    Synonyms: striving, nisus, pains · A kind of: attempt

  10. An intense or violent exertion.

    Synonyms: straining · A kind of: effort

  11. The act of singing.

    “With a shout and a song they marched up to the gates.”

    Synonyms: song · A kind of: vocal music · Kinds: carol, lullaby

Strain as a verb

  1. To exert much effort or energy.

    “Straining our ears to hear.”

    Synonyms: strive, reach · A kind of: tug · Kinds: extend oneself, trouble oneself

  2. Test the limits of.

    “You are trying my patience!”

    Synonyms: try, stress · A kind of: afflict · Kinds: rack

  3. Use to the utmost; exert vigorously or to full capacity.

    “He really extended himself when he climbed Kilimanjaro.” “Don't strain your mind too much.”

    Synonyms: extend · A kind of: use · Kinds: overstrain, tax

  4. Separate by passing through a sieve or other straining device to separate out coarser elements.

    “Sift the flour.”

    Synonyms: sift, sieve · A kind of: separate · Kinds: rice, resift, riddle, winnow

  5. Cause to be tense and uneasy or nervous or anxious.

    “He got a phone call from his lawyer that tensed him up.”

    Synonyms: tense, tense up · Opposite: unstrain · A kind of: affect · Kinds: stretch

  6. Become stretched or tense or taut.

    “The bodybuilder's neck muscles tensed;.” “The rope strained when the weight was attached.”

    Synonyms: tense · A kind of: tighten

  7. Remove by passing through a filter.

    “Filter out the impurities.”

    Synonyms: filter, filtrate, separate out, filter out · A kind of: separate

  8. Rub through a strainer or process in an electric blender.

    “Puree the vegetables for the baby.”

    Synonyms: puree · A kind of: rub

  9. Alter the shape of (something) by stress.

    “His body was deformed by leprosy.”

    Synonyms: deform, distort · A kind of: shape · Kinds: jaundice
