Study: meaning, synonyms and examples

Study is used as a noun (10 senses) and verb (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: A detailed critical inspection.

In Hindi
पढ़ाई करना; अध्ययन
Synonyms
survey, work, report, written report, discipline, subject, subject area, subject field, field, field of study
Word family
studious, student, studying

Study as a noun

  1. A detailed critical inspection.

    Synonyms: survey · A kind of: examination · Kinds: resurvey

  2. Applying the mind to learning and understanding a subject (especially by reading).

    “Mastering a second language requires a lot of work.” “No schools offer graduate study in interior design.”

    Synonyms: work · A kind of: learning

  3. A written document describing the findings of some individual or group.

    “This accords with the recent study by Hill and Dale.”

    Synonyms: report, written report · A kind of: document · Kinds: assay, case study, white book, blue book, green paper, progress report, position paper, medical report

  4. A state of deep mental absorption.

    “She is in a deep study.”

    A kind of: concentration

  5. A room used for reading and writing and studying.

    “He knocked lightly on the closed door of the study.”

    A kind of: room

  6. A branch of knowledge.

    “In what discipline is his doctorate?” “Teachers should be well trained in their subject.” “Anthropology is the study of human beings.”

    Synonyms: discipline, subject, subject area, subject field, field, field of study, bailiwick · A kind of: knowledge domain · Kinds: occultism, communications, major, frontier, genealogy, allometry, bibliotics, ology

  7. Preliminary drawing for later elaboration.

    “He made several studies before starting to paint.”

    Synonyms: sketch · A kind of: drawing · Kinds: design, draft, vignette

  8. Attentive consideration and meditation.

    “After much cogitation he rejected the offer.”

    Synonyms: cogitation · A kind of: contemplation · Kinds: lucubration

  9. Someone who memorizes quickly and easily (as the lines for a part in a play).

    “He is a quick study.”

    A kind of: memorizer

  10. A composition intended to develop one aspect of the performer's technique.

    “A study in spiccato bowing.”

    A kind of: musical composition

Study as a verb

  1. Consider in detail and subject to an analysis in order to discover essential features or meaning.

    “Analyze a sonnet by Shakespeare.” “Analyze the evidence in a criminal trial.” “Analyze your real motives.”

    Synonyms: analyze, analyse, examine, canvass, canvas · Kinds: anatomize, diagnose, diagnose, survey, survey, compare, check, assay

  2. Be a student; follow a course of study; be enrolled at an institute of learning.

    Kinds: major

  3. Give careful consideration to.

    “Consider the possibility of moving.”

    Synonyms: consider · A kind of: chew over · Kinds: factor, compare

  4. Be a student of a certain subject.

    “She is reading for the bar exam.”

    Synonyms: learn, read, take · Kinds: audit, train, drill

  5. Learn by reading books.

    “He is studying geology in his room.” “I have an exam next week; I must hit the books now.”

    Synonyms: hit the books · A kind of: learn · Kinds: memorize, cram

  6. Think intently and at length, as for spiritual purposes.

    “He is meditating in his study.”

    Synonyms: meditate, contemplate · A kind of: think

Phrases and compounds with "study"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • case study (noun) A careful study of some social unit (as a corporation or division within a corporation) that attempts to determine what factors led to its success or failure.
  • home study (noun) A course of study carried out at home rather than in a classroom.
  • study hall (noun) A period of time during the school day that is set aside for study.
  • time study (noun) An analysis of a specific job in an effort to find the most efficient method in terms of time and effort.
  • work study (noun) An analysis of a specific job in an effort to find the most efficient method in terms of time and effort.
  • brown study (noun) A state of deep absorption or thoughtfulness.
  • quick study (noun) Someone able to acquire new knowledge and skills rapidly and easily.
  • motion study (noun) An analysis of a specific job in an effort to find the most efficient method in terms of time and effort.
  • nature study (noun) The study of animals and plants in the natural world (usually at an elementary level).
  • directed study (noun) A course of study that is supervised and controlled by a specialist in the subject.
  • field of study (noun) A branch of knowledge.
  • literary study (noun) The humanistic study of literature.
  • course of study (noun) An integrated course of academic studies.
  • time-motion study (noun) An analysis of a specific job in an effort to find the most efficient method in terms of time and effort.
  • double-blind study (noun) An experimental procedure in which neither the subjects of the experiment nor the persons administering the experiment know the critical aspects of the experiment.
  • work-study program (noun) An educational plan in which students alternate between paid employment and formal study.
