Swallow: meaning, synonyms and examples

Swallow is used as a verb (8 senses) and noun (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: Pass through the esophagus as part of eating or drinking.

In Hindi
निगलना; अबाबील
Synonyms
get down, immerse, swallow up, bury, eat up, take back, unsay, withdraw, accept, live with

Swallow as a verb

  1. Pass through the esophagus as part of eating or drinking.

    Synonyms: get down · A kind of: consume · Kinds: bolt

  2. Engulf and destroy.

    “The Nazis swallowed the Baltic countries.”

    A kind of: demolish

  3. Enclose or envelop completely, as if by swallowing.

    “The huge waves swallowed the small boat and it sank shortly thereafter.”

    Synonyms: immerse, swallow up, bury, eat up · A kind of: enclose

  4. Utter indistinctly.

    “She swallowed the last words of her speech.”

    A kind of: talk

  5. Take back what one has said.

    “He swallowed his words.”

    Synonyms: take back, unsay, withdraw · A kind of: disown

  6. Keep from expressing.

    “I swallowed my anger and kept quiet.”

    A kind of: suppress

  7. Tolerate or accommodate oneself to.

    “I shall have to accept these unpleasant working conditions.” “I swallowed the insult.” “She has learned to live with her husband's little idiosyncrasies.”

    Synonyms: accept, live with · A kind of: digest

  8. Believe or accept without questioning or challenge.

    “Am I supposed to swallow that story?”

    A kind of: believe

Swallow as a noun

  1. A small amount of liquid food.

    “A sup of ale.”

    Synonyms: sup

  2. The act of swallowing.

    “One swallow of the liquid was enough.”

    Synonyms: drink, deglutition · A kind of: consumption · Kinds: aerophagia, gulp, sip

  3. Small long-winged songbird noted for swift graceful flight and the regularity of its migrations.

    A kind of: oscine · Kinds: barn swallow, cliff swallow, tree swallow, white-bellied swallow, martin

Phrases and compounds with "swallow"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • swallow up (verb) Enclose or envelop completely, as if by swallowing.
  • sea swallow (noun) Common tern of Eurasia and America having white black and grey plumage.
  • bank swallow (noun) Swallow of the northern hemisphere that nests in tunnels dug in clay or sand banks.
  • barn swallow (noun) Common swallow of North America and Europe that nests in barns etc.
  • swallow dive (noun) A dive in which the diver arches the back with arms outstretched before entering the water.
  • swallow hole (noun) A depression in the ground communicating with a subterranean passage (especially in limestone) and formed by solution or by collapse of a cavern roof.
  • swallow wort (noun) Perennial herb with branched woody stock and bright yellow flowers.
  • tree swallow (noun) Bluish-green-and-white North American swallow; nests in tree cavities.
  • wood swallow (noun) Australasian and Asiatic bird related to the shrikes and resembling a swallow.
  • cliff swallow (noun) North American swallow that lives in colonies and builds bottle-shaped mud nests on cliffs and walls.
  • fairy swallow (noun) Fancy domestic pigeon having blue-and-white plumage and heavily muffed feet.
  • swallow shrike (noun) Australasian and Asiatic bird related to the shrikes and resembling a swallow.
  • swallow-tailed (adjective) (especially of butterflies and birds) having a forked tail like that of a swallow.
  • chimney swallow (noun) American swift that nests in e.g. unused chimneys.
  • swallow-tailed coat (noun) A man's full-dress jacket with two long tapering tails at the back.
  • swallow-tailed hawk (noun) Graceful North American black-and-white kite.
