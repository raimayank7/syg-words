Sweet: meaning, synonyms and examples

Sweet is used as an adjective (10 senses), adverb (1 sense) and noun (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: Having or denoting the characteristic taste of sugar.

In Hindi
मीठा; मिठाई
Synonyms
angelic, angelical, cherubic, seraphic, dulcet, honeyed, mellifluous, mellisonant, gratifying, odoriferous
Opposites
sour
Word family
sweetness

Sweet as an adjective

  1. Having or denoting the characteristic taste of sugar.

    Opposite: sour · Similar: cloying, sweetish

  2. Having a sweet nature befitting an angel or cherub.

    “An angelic smile.” “A cherubic face.” “Looking so seraphic when he slept.”

    Synonyms: angelic, angelical, cherubic, seraphic · Similar: lovable

  3. Pleasing to the ear.

    “The dulcet tones of the cello.”

    Synonyms: dulcet, honeyed, mellifluous, mellisonant · Similar: melodious

  4. Pleasing to the senses.

    “The sweet song of the lark.” “The sweet face of a child.”

    Similar: pleasing

  5. Pleasing to the mind or feeling.

    “Sweet revenge.”

    Synonyms: gratifying · Similar: pleasing

  6. Having a natural fragrance.

    “Odoriferous spices.” “The odorous air of the orchard.” “The perfumed air of June.”

    Synonyms: odoriferous, odorous, perfumed, scented, sweet-scented, sweet-smelling · Similar: fragrant

  7. (used of wines) having a high residual sugar content.

    “Sweet dessert wines.”

  8. Not containing or composed of salt water.

    “Fresh water.”

    Synonyms: fresh

  9. Not soured or preserved.

    “Sweet milk.”

    Synonyms: fresh, unfermented · Similar: unsoured

  10. With sweetening added.

    Synonyms: sugared, sweetened, sweet-flavored · Similar: sugary

Sweet as an adverb

  1. In an affectionate or loving manner (`sweet' is sometimes a poetic or informal variant of `sweetly').

    “Susan Hayward plays the wife sharply and sweetly.” “How sweet the moonlight sleeps upon this bank.” “Talking sweet to each other.”

    Synonyms: sweetly

Sweet as a noun

  1. English phonetician; one of the founders of modern phonetics (1845-1912).

  2. A dish served as the last course of a meal.

    Synonyms: dessert, afters · A kind of: course · Kinds: ambrosia, blancmange, charlotte, compote, dumpling, flan, frozen dessert, junket

  3. A food rich in sugar.

    Synonyms: confection · A kind of: dainty · Kinds: confectionery, confiture, sweetmeat, candy, hardbake, chewing gum, candied apple, center

  4. The taste experience when sugar dissolves in the mouth.

    Synonyms: sweetness, sugariness · A kind of: taste

  5. The property of tasting as if it contains sugar.

    Synonyms: sweetness · A kind of: taste property · Kinds: saccharinity, sugariness

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • sweet pea (noun) Climbing garden plant having fragrant pastel-colored flowers.

  • sweet corn (noun) A corn plant developed in order to have young ears that are sweet and suitable for eating.
  • sweet fern (noun) Mat-forming lithophytic or terrestrial fern with creeping rootstocks and large pinnatifid fronds found throughout North America and Europe and Africa and east Asia.
  • sweet flag (noun) Perennial marsh plant having swordlike leaves and aromatic roots.
  • sweet gale (noun) Bog shrub of north temperate zone having bitter-tasting fragrant leaves.
  • sweet lime (noun) Lemon tree having fruit with a somewhat insipid sweetish pulp.
  • sweet roll (noun) Any of numerous yeast-raised sweet rolls with our without raisins or nuts or spices or a glaze.
  • sweet talk (noun) Flattery designed to gain favor.
  • sweet-talk (verb) Influence or urge by gentle urging, caressing, or flattering.
  • henry sweet (noun) English phonetician; one of the founders of modern phonetics (1845-1912).
  • sweet basil (noun) Annual or perennial of tropical Asia having spikes of small white flowers and aromatic leaves; one of the most important culinary herbs; used in salads, casseroles, sauces and some liqueurs.
  • sweet bells (noun) Bushy deciduous shrub of the eastern United States with long racemes of pinkish flowers.
