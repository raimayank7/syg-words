Switch: meaning, synonyms and examples

Switch is used as a verb (7 senses) and noun (7 senses). Its most common meaning is: Change over, change around, as to a new order or sequence.

In Hindi
स्विच; बदलना
Synonyms
switch over, exchange, trade, swap, swop, shift, change, change over, throw, flip
Word family
switching, switcher

Switch as a verb

  1. Change over, change around, as to a new order or sequence.

    Synonyms: switch over, exchange · A kind of: change by reversal

  2. Exchange or give (something) in exchange for.

    Synonyms: trade, swap, swop · A kind of: exchange

  3. Lay aside, abandon, or leave for another.

    “She switched psychiatrists.” “The car changed lanes.”

    Synonyms: shift, change · Kinds: transition, shift, break, surf, leap, diphthongize, cut, break

  4. Make a shift in or exchange of.

    “First Joe led; then we switched.”

    Synonyms: change over, shift · A kind of: change · Kinds: back, veer

  5. Cause to go on or to be engaged or set in operation.

    “Switch on the light.” “Throw the lever.”

    Synonyms: throw, flip · A kind of: engage · Kinds: switch on, switch off

  6. Flog with or as if with a flexible rod.

    A kind of: flog

  7. Reverse (a direction, attitude, or course of action).

    Synonyms: interchange, tack, alternate, flip, flip-flop · A kind of: change by reversal

Switch as a noun

  1. Control consisting of a mechanical or electrical or electronic device for making or breaking or changing the connections in a circuit.

    Synonyms: electric switch, electrical switch · A kind of: control · Kinds: commutator, cutout, ignition switch, push button, selector, three-way switch, time-switch, toggle switch

  2. An event in which one thing is substituted for another.

    “The replacement of lost blood by a transfusion of donor blood.”

    Synonyms: substitution, permutation, transposition, replacement · A kind of: variation

  3. Hairpiece consisting of a tress of false hair; used by women to give shape to a coiffure.

    A kind of: hairpiece

  4. Railroad track having two movable rails and necessary connections; used to turn a train from one track to another or to store rolling stock.

    A kind of: railroad track

  5. A flexible implement used as an instrument of punishment.

    A kind of: instrument of punishment · Kinds: birch, cane, ferule, rattan

  6. A basketball maneuver; two defensive players shift assignments so that each guards the player usually guarded by the other.

    A kind of: basketball play

  7. The act of changing one thing or position for another.

    Synonyms: switching, shift · A kind of: change · Kinds: switcheroo

Phrases and compounds with "switch"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • switch on (verb) Cause to operate by flipping a switch.
  • dip switch (noun) (computer science) one of a set of small on-off switches mounted in computer hardware; used in place of jumpers to configure the machine for a user.
  • switch off (verb) Cause to stop operating by disengaging a switch.
  • switch-hit (verb) Bat right-handed against a left-handed and left-handed against a right-handed pitcher.
  • switch-ivy (noun) Fast-growing evergreen shrub of southeastern United States having arching interlaced branches and racemes of white flowers.
  • switch cane (noun) Small cane of watery or moist areas in southern United States.
  • switch over (verb) Change over, change around, as to a new order or sequence.
  • time-switch (noun) A switch set to operate at a desired time.
  • switch grass (noun) Grass of western America used for hay.
  • on-off switch (noun) A hinged switch that can assume either of two positions.
  • switch engine (noun) A locomotive for switching rolling stock in a railroad yard.
  • switch-hitter (noun) A baseball player who can bat either right or left handed.
  • toggle switch (noun) A hinged switch that can assume either of two positions.
  • bait and switch (noun) A deceptive way of selling that involves advertising a product at a very low price in order to attract customers who are then persuaded to switch to a more expensive product.
  • electric switch (noun) Control consisting of a mechanical or electrical or electronic device for making or breaking or changing the connections in a circuit.
  • ignition switch (noun) Switch that operates a solenoid that closes a circuit to operate the starter.
