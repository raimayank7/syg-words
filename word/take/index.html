Take: meaning, synonyms and examples

Take is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Carry out.

In Hindi
लेना
Synonyms
occupy, use up, lead, direct, conduct, guide, get hold of, assume, acquire, adopt
Opposites
give
Word family
taking, taker

Take as a verb

  1. Carry out.

    “Take action.” “Take steps.” “Take vengeance.”

    A kind of: act

  2. Require (time or space).

    “It took three hours to get to work this morning.” “This event occupied a very short time.”

    Synonyms: occupy, use up · A kind of: use · Kinds: be

  3. Take somebody somewhere.

    “We lead him to our chief.” “Can you take me to the main entrance?” “He conducted us to the palace.”

    Synonyms: lead, direct, conduct, guide · Kinds: beacon, hand, mislead, usher

  4. Get into one's hands, take physically.

    “Take a cookie!” “Can you take this bag, please.”

    Synonyms: get hold of · Kinds: seize, seize

  5. Take on a certain form, attribute, or aspect.

    “His voice took on a sad tone.” “The story took a new turn.” “He adopted an air of superiority.”

    Synonyms: assume, acquire, adopt, take on · A kind of: change · Kinds: re-assume

  6. Interpret something in a certain way; convey a particular meaning or impression.

    “I read this address as a satire.” “How should I take this message?” “You can't take credit for this!”

    Synonyms: read · A kind of: interpret · Kinds: misread

  7. Take something or somebody with oneself somewhere.

    “Bring me the box from the other room.” “Take these letters to the boss.” “This brings me to the main point.”

    Synonyms: bring, convey · A kind of: transport · Kinds: fetch, transit, ferry, return, tube, whisk, impart, land

  8. Take into one's possession.

    “We are taking an orphan from Romania.” “I'll take three salmon steaks.”

    Opposite: give · Kinds: adopt, take away, take back, collect, impound, sequester, pocket, bear

  9. Travel or go by means of a certain kind of transportation, or a certain route.

    “He takes the bus to work.” “She takes Route 1 to Newark.”

    A kind of: use

  10. Pick out, select, or choose from a number of alternatives.

    “Take any one of these cards.” “Choose a good husband for your daughter.” “She selected a pair of shoes from among the dozen the salesgirl had shown her.”

    Synonyms: choose, select, pick out · A kind of: decide · Kinds: empanel, anoint, field, sieve, draw, dial, plump, pick

  11. Receive willingly something given or offered.

    “The only girl who would have him was the miller's daughter.” “I won't have this dog in my house!” “Please accept my present.”

    Synonyms: accept, have · A kind of: get · Kinds: accept, welcome, honor, adopt

  12. Assume, as of positions or roles.

    “She took the job as director of development.” “He occupies the position of manager.” “The young prince will soon occupy the throne.”

    Synonyms: fill, occupy · A kind of: work

  13. Take into consideration for exemplifying purposes.

    “Take the case of China.” “Consider the following case.”

    Synonyms: consider, deal, look at · A kind of: think about · Kinds: contemplate, dally, abstract, warm to

  14. Require as useful, just, or proper.

    “It takes nerve to do what she did.” “Success usually requires hard work.” “This job asks a lot of patience and skill.”

    Synonyms: necessitate, ask, postulate, need, require, involve, call for, demand · Kinds: claim, govern, draw, cost, cry out for, compel

Take as a noun

  1. The income or profit arising from such transactions as the sale of land or other property.

    “The average return was about 5%.”

    Synonyms: return, issue, takings, proceeds, yield, payoff · A kind of: income · Kinds: economic rent, payback

  2. The act of photographing a scene or part of a scene without interruption.

    A kind of: filming · Kinds: retake

Phrases and compounds with "take"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • take in (verb) Provide with shelter.
  • take on (verb) Take on a certain form, attribute, or aspect.
  • take to (verb) Have a fancy or particular liking or desire for.
  • take up (verb) Pursue or resume.
  • take-in (noun) The act of taking in as by fooling or cheating or swindling someone.
  • take-up (noun) Any of various devices for reducing slack (as in a sewing machine) or taking up motion (as in a loom).
  • take aim (verb) Point or cause to go (blows, weapons, or objects such as photographic equipment) towards.
  • take for (verb) Keep in mind or convey as a conviction or view.
  • take off (verb) Leave.
  • take out (verb) Cause to leave.
  • take ten (verb) Take a ten minute break.
  • take arms (verb) Commence hostilities.
  • take away (verb) Remove from a certain place, environment, or mental or emotional state; transport into a new location or state.
  • take back (verb) Bring back to the point of departure.
  • take care (verb) Be careful, prudent, or watchful.
  • take down (verb) Move something or somebody to a lower position.
