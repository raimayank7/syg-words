Trial: meaning, synonyms and examples

Trial is used as a noun (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: The act of testing something.

In Hindi
मुक़दमा; परीक्षण
Synonyms
test, run, trial run, tryout, tribulation, visitation
Word family
try

Trial as a noun

  1. The act of testing something.

    “In the experimental trials the amount of carbon was measured separately.” “He called each flip of the coin a new trial.”

    Synonyms: test, run · A kind of: attempt · Kinds: double blind, audition, field trial, fitting, pilot project

  2. Trying something to find out about it.

    “A sample for ten days free trial.” “A trial of progesterone failed to relieve the pain.”

    Synonyms: trial run, test, tryout · A kind of: experiment · Kinds: field trial, alpha test, beta test, road test, trial balloon

  3. The act of undergoing testing.

    “He survived the great test of battle.” “Candidates must compete in a trial of skill.”

    Synonyms: test · A kind of: attempt

  4. (law) the determination of a person's innocence or guilt by due process of law.

    “He had a fair trial and the jury found him guilty.” “Most of these complaints are settled before they go to trial.”

    A kind of: proceeding · Kinds: court-martial, ordeal, show trial, mistrial, retrial

  5. (sports) a preliminary competition to determine qualifications.

    “The trials for the semifinals began yesterday.”

    A kind of: contest

  6. An annoying or frustrating or catastrophic event.

    “His mother-in-law's visits were a great trial for him.” “Life is full of tribulations.” “A visitation of the plague.”

    Synonyms: tribulation, visitation · A kind of: affliction · Kinds: fire

Phrases and compounds with "trial"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • on trial (adjective) In the process of being tested or tried.
  • trial run (noun) Trying something to find out about it.
  • show trial (noun) A trial held for show; the guilt of the accused person has been decided in advance.
  • field trial (noun) A test of the performance of some new product under the conditions in which it will be used.
  • trial court (noun) The first court before which the facts of a case are decided.
  • trial judge (noun) A judge in a trial court.
  • scopes trial (noun) A highly publicized trial in 1925 when John Thomas Scopes violated a Tennessee state law by teaching evolution in high school; Scopes was prosecuted by William Jennings Bryan and defended by Clarence Darrow; Scopes was convicted but the verdict was later reversed.
  • trial lawyer (noun) A lawyer who specializes in defending clients before a court of law.
  • trial period (noun) A period of time during which someone or something is tested.
  • trial balance (noun) A balance of debits and credits in double-entry bookkeeping; drawn up to test their equality.
  • trial balloon (noun) A test of public opinion.
  • trial attorney (noun) A lawyer who specializes in defending clients before a court of law.
  • trial and error (noun) Experimenting until a solution is found.
  • trial by ordeal (noun) A primitive method of determining a person's guilt or innocence by subjecting the accused person to dangerous or painful tests believed to be under divine control; escape was usually taken as a sign of innocence.
  • trial-and-error (adjective) Trying out various means or theories until error is satisfactorily reduced or eliminated.
  • trial impression (noun) (printing) an impression made to check for errors.
