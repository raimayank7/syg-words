Year: meaning, synonyms and examples

Year is used as a noun (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: A period of time containing 365 (or 366) days.

In Hindi
साल, वर्ष
Synonyms

Word family
yearly

Year as a noun

  1. A period of time containing 365 (or 366) days.

    “She is 4 years old.” “In the year 1920.”

    Synonyms: twelvemonth, yr · A kind of: time period · Kinds: church year, common year, leap year, off year, off year, calendar year, fiscal year, annum

  2. A period of time occupying a regular part of a calendar year that is used for some particular activity.

    “A school year.”

    A kind of: time period · Kinds: school year

  3. The period of time that it takes for a planet (as, e.g., Earth or Mars) to make a complete revolution around the sun.

    “A Martian year takes 687 of our days.”

    A kind of: time period · Kinds: anomalistic year, solar year, lunar year, sidereal year

  4. A body of students who graduate together.

    “The class of '97.” “She was in my year at Hoehandle High.”

    Synonyms: class · A kind of: gathering · Kinds: graduating class, senior class, junior class, sophomore class, freshman class

Phrases and compounds with "year"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • new year (noun) The calendar year just begun.
  • off year (noun) A year in which productivity is low or inferior.
  • one-year (adjective) Completing its life cycle within a year.
  • per year (adverb) By the year; every year (usually with reference to a sum of money paid or received).
  • two-year (adjective) Having a life cycle lasting two seasons.
  • year dot (noun) As long ago as anyone can remember.
  • year-end (noun) The end of a calendar year.
  • each year (adverb) Without missing a year.
  • holy year (noun) (Roman Catholic Church) a period of remission from sin (usually granted every 25 years).
  • leap year (noun) In the Gregorian calendar: any year divisible by 4 except centenary years divisible by 400.
  • civil year (noun) The year (reckoned from January 1 to December 31) according to Gregorian calendar.
  • every year (adverb) Without missing a year.
  • first-year (adjective) Used of a person in the first year of an experience (especially in United States high school or college).
  • great year (noun) Time required for one complete cycle of the precession of the equinoxes, about 25,800 years.
  • light year (noun) The distance that light travels in a vacuum in 1 year; 5.88 trillion miles or 9.46 trillion kilometers.
  • light-year (noun) The distance that light travels in a vacuum in 1 year; 5.88 trillion miles or 9.46 trillion kilometers.
