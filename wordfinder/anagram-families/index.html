Anagram families: letters that make the most words

Two words are anagrams when they use exactly the same letters. Group every word in a dictionary by its letters and you get anagram families. Most words are alone in their family. In our list of 114,118 words, 16,102 words belong to a family of two or more, and there are 7,092 such families. A few sets of letters are remarkably fertile. The largest family here has 9 members.

Why these letters?

Look at the letters of the big families: A, E, R, S, T, L, P, I, N. They are the most frequent letters in English (see letter frequency), and each can stand in many positions. S is especially generous, since it turns nouns into plurals and gives verbs their -s form. Rare letters such as J, Q, X and Z never appear in a large family, because they fit in so few patterns.

Long anagram pairs

Long words rarely have anagrams, which makes the pairs that exist pleasing. At fifteen letters our list has, among others, algorithmically and logarithmically, where the two halves ALGO-RITHM and LOGA-RITHM are themselves anagrams; and microphotograph and photomicrograph, the same parts in a different order.

What to do with this

  • Puzzle setters avoid the big families when they want a jumble with one answer, and choose them when they want solvers to find several.
  • Word-game players learn the big five-letter families by heart, since those letters are drawn so often.
  • Teachers use a family as a spelling exercise: give the letters, ask for every word.
