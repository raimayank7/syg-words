Words by ending

English builds a large part of its vocabulary by fixing endings (suffixes) to shorter words. Learn what a dozen endings mean and how they are spelt, and you can read, spell and unscramble thousands of words that you have never met as wholes. Each page below explains one ending, gives its spelling rules and lists the everyday words that carry it.

-ing8,637Makes the form of a verb that shows continuing action (i am reading) and the noun for the activity itself (reading is fun).

-er5,720Has two jobs.
-est1,306Makes the superlative of short adjectives and adverbs: the highest degree (tall, tallest; fast, fastest).
-ly4,735Turns an adjective into an adverb that tells how something is done (quick, quickly).

-able1,194Makes an adjective meaning "can be done" (wash, washable; read, readable).

Two kinds of ending

Grammar endings do not make a new word so much as a new form of the same word: walk, walked, walking; tall, taller, tallest. Every regular verb and most short adjectives take them, which is why -ed and -ing are the two largest families in the table.

Word-building endings make a different kind of word: the adjective kind becomes the noun kindness; the verb pay becomes the noun payment; the noun hope becomes the adjectives hopeful and hopeless, and those in turn become the adverbs hopefully and hopelessly. Endings can be stacked, one on another: care, careless, carelessness.

Why endings matter in word puzzles

Spotting an ending is the quickest way to shorten a problem. Seven jumbled letters are hard; four letters plus -ING are easy. Good solvers scan their letters for S, ED, ER, ING and LY before anything else. Our guide on solving anagrams starts with exactly this step.

Spelling changes to watch

  • A silent E is dropped before an ending that starts with a vowel: hope, hoping, hoped.
  • A final consonant after a single short vowel is doubled: stop, stopped, stopping; big, bigger, biggest.
  • A final Y after a consonant becomes I: happy, happier, happiness, happily.
