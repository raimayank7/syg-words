Palindromes: words that read the same both ways

A palindrome is a word that is spelt the same forwards and backwards: level, noon, radar. The name comes from Greek words meaning "running back again". Our word list contains 75 palindromes of three letters or more. The longest are repaper, reviver, rotator.

Palindromes are favourites in quizzes and classrooms because the idea takes a moment to explain and children love hunting for them. Names can be palindromes too (Anna, Hannah, Otto, Bob), though names are not part of our list.

How palindromes are built

A palindrome with an odd number of letters has a middle letter with a mirror image on each side: ra-d-ar, le-v-el. One with an even number is two mirrored halves: no-on, de-ed. That is why so many short palindromes are family or baby words made by repeating a sound, such as dad, mum, mom, pop, pup, nun, tot and pap.

A classroom game

Ask pupils to write every three-letter palindrome they can think of in three minutes, then compare with the list. Follow with the question: which palindromes are still real words when you remove the first and last letters? (Level gives eve; radar gives ada, which is not in our list; rotator gives otato, which is not a word.)

Beyond single words

Whole sentences can be palindromes if spaces and punctuation are ignored. "Was it a car or a cat I saw" is a well-known one. Numbers and dates can be too: 2002 and 12321 read the same both ways. A word that makes a different word backwards, such as stressed and desserts, is sometimes called a semordnilap, which is "palindromes" spelt backwards.
