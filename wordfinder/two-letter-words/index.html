Two-letter words, with meanings

There are 74 two-letter words in our word list. They are the smallest words in English and, in word games and puzzles, among the most useful: they fit where nothing else will, and they let you lay a word alongside another. Half of them you use every day. The rest are mostly sounds people make (ah, eh, um), names of letters and musical notes (em, mu, re, ti), and a few short words from other languages that English dictionaries have taken in (qi, om, ka).

Each word below has a short meaning written by us. Words in bold are everyday words; the others are less common or rare, and may not be accepted in every game, so check the dictionary your game uses. Use the box to search.

How to learn them

Do not try to learn the whole list at once. Take them in families:

  • Sounds: ah, eh, er, ha, hi, ho, oh, oi, ow, sh, uh, um, yo.
  • Musical notes: do, re, mi, fa, so, la, ti, with the older names ut and si.
  • Greek letters: mu, nu, pi, xi.
  • Letter names and printing terms: em, en, es, ex.
  • Family words: ma, pa.
  • The unusual ones: ai, jo, ka, ki, od, oo, pe, qi, wo, xu. These are the words worth writing on a card.

The most valuable to remember

Words that use a rare letter are the ones that rescue a difficult hand: ax, ex, ox, xi and xu for X; qi for Q, the only two-letter Q word here; jo for J. There is no two-letter word with V or Z in our list, and none with C.

A note on what counts

Dictionaries disagree about two-letter words more than about any others. Some lists accept forms that ours leaves out, and some reject a few that ours includes. We removed entries that were only plurals of letter names or abbreviations of units. See about the word list. Next: the three-letter words.
