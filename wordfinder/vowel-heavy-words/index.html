Vowel-heavy words

Every word-game player knows the feeling of a hand full of vowels: A, E, I, O, U and nothing to hang them on. The cure is a stock of words that are almost all vowels. This page lists 138 of them from our word list: everyday and less common words of three to seven letters in which every letter but one is a vowel, together with some rare longer words that are at least three-quarters vowels.

Everyday words are in bold. The rare ones, in italics, may not be accepted in every game.

The most useful

  • Three letters, two vowels: ace, age, ago, aid, aim, air, ape, are, ate, awe, ear, eat, eel, ice, oak, oar, oil, one, our, out, owe, use.
  • Four letters, three vowels: area, aide, aura, auto, ease, easy (if you count Y), idea, oboe, ooze, euro.
  • Five letters, four vowels: audio, adieu, eerie, queue, aerie.

Why some words collect vowels

Many of these came into English from French (adieu, queue, beau), Latin (area, aura, idea) or Italian (audio, oboe), languages that end words with vowels more freely than English does. Native English words tend to wrap their vowels in consonants.

Managing vowels in a game

If you draw letters as you play, try not to hold more than three vowels after a move. Play off doubles first, especially two Is or two Us, which are the hardest to place. The letters A and E are the easiest vowels to keep; U and I the hardest.

The opposite problem: words with no A, E, I, O or U.
