Word checker: is it a word?

Type a word to see whether it is in our list, how common it is, and which other words use the same letters.

What the checker tells you

Type a word and the checker says whether it is in our word list and, if it is, how common the list considers it: an everyday word, a less common word, or a rare or technical one. It also shows any anagrams of the word, and links to every shorter word that can be made from its letters.

What "not found" means

It means only that the word is not in this list. It does not mean the word does not exist. Our list leaves out:

  • names of people, places and products, and all words that need a capital letter;
  • abbreviations, and words with apostrophes or hyphens;
  • very rare, very new and strongly regional words;
  • offensive words, which we removed on purpose.

Also check your spelling. British and American spellings are both included, so "colour" and "color" are each found.

What "found" means

It means the spelling appears in SCOWL, a large open word list made for spelling checkers. It is good evidence that the word is correctly spelt English. It is not a ruling for any game: every word game names its own dictionary, and the two may disagree, especially about rare words, which our results mark as such.

Using it to settle an argument

If a group agrees beforehand to accept this checker as the judge, it does the job well: it gives a clear yes or no and nobody has to leaf through a book. Agree also on the level. A friendly house rule is "everyday and less common words count, rare ones do not", which keeps out most words nobody at the table can define.

Using it for spelling

If you are unsure between two spellings, check both. When only one is found, that is very likely the right one. When both are found, they may be British and American forms, or two different words (practice and practise, affect and effect), and you need a dictionary to choose. If you cannot get close enough to check, the pattern finder lets you type the letters you are sure of.

Meanings

The source list has no definitions, so the checker cannot explain a word. We did write short meanings for every two-letter word, since those are the ones people most often doubt.
