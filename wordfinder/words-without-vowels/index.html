Words without vowels (no A, E, I, O or U)

Strictly speaking, almost no English word is spoken without a vowel sound. But many are written without any of the five vowel letters A, E, I, O and U. In nearly all of them the letter Y does the vowel's work, sounding like a short I (gym, myth) or a long I (fly, sky, why). A few are written sounds (hmm, shh, psst, brr), and one or two came from Welsh, where W is a vowel (cwm, a steep hollow on a mountain; crwth, an old stringed instrument).

Our word list has 80 such words. They are useful in word games when your letters are all consonants, and they are a good spelling challenge: rhythm is one of the most often misspelt words in English.

The ones to remember

  • Two and three letters: by, my, sh; cry, dry, fly, fry, gym, ply, pry, shy, sky, sly, spy, sty, try, why, wry.
  • Four and five: hymn, lynx, myth, cyst; crypt, glyph, lymph, nymph, psych, tryst, shyly, slyly, dryly.
  • Six and more: rhythm, sylphs, rhythms. The word syzygy (the lining-up of three bodies in space, such as the sun, earth and moon) has three Ys and no other vowel.

Is Y a vowel?

Y is a consonant when it starts a syllable with the sound of "yes" and "yard". Everywhere else it stands for a vowel sound: happy, system, type. So these words do have vowels in speech; they simply spell them with Y.
