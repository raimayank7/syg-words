Words to use instead of "very"

"Very" is the easiest word in English and one of the weakest. "Very tired" tells the reader a little; "exhausted" shows them a person who can hardly stand. English usually has a single, stronger word waiting, and using it makes your writing shorter and sharper at the same time.

This list gives 48 common "very + word" pairs and one precise word for each, with its meaning in Hindi. Do not try to learn them all at once. Take the six that match what you write most, such as feelings for a diary or size words for a description, and use each in a sentence of your own today.

A caution: the strong word is not always better. "Very cold" water is cold; "freezing" water is close to ice. If you only mean cold, say cold. Choose the strong word when it is true.

How to use these words well

  1. Do not add "very" back. "Very exhausted" and "very enormous" are mistakes of style: the strong word already contains "very". With such words, use "absolutely" or "completely" if you need emphasis: "absolutely furious".
  2. Match the register. "Famished" and "serene" are slightly literary; "starving" and "calm" are everyday. In a letter to a friend, the everyday word is right.
  3. One strong word per sentence. A sentence loaded with "gorgeous", "stunning" and "dazzling" tires the reader as quickly as three "verys".
  4. Sometimes a detail beats any adjective. Instead of "the room was very dirty" or even "filthy", try "dust lay thick on the table and a plate of old food sat by the bed".

The same advice holds for "really", "so" and "extremely". For the full meaning and other senses of any word here, type it into the word finder.
