Synonyms and antonyms: 70 common words

A synonym is a word with nearly the same meaning as another: big and large. An antonym is a word with the opposite meaning: big and small. Questions on both are a fixture of English papers from primary school to competitive examinations, and a good stock of them makes your own writing less repetitive.

The seventy words below are common ones whose synonyms and antonyms are often asked. Each row gives one clear synonym and one clear antonym. Remember that no two words mean exactly the same thing in every sentence. "Big" and "large" are close, but we say a "big mistake", not a "large mistake". The row gives you the pair; reading and listening teach you where each word fits.

How antonyms are formed

Many antonyms are made by adding a prefix: accurate, inaccurate; honest, dishonest; tidy, untidy; pure, impure. Others swap a suffix: careful, careless; useful, useless. And many are simply different words: begin and end, rich and poor. Which prefix a word takes has to be learnt; see prefixes and suffixes.

How to answer synonym and antonym questions

  1. Fix the part of speech. If the given word is an adjective, the answer must be an adjective. "Beauty" cannot be a synonym of "beautiful".
  2. Read the sentence, if there is one. A word such as "bright" has several meanings: shining (antonym dull) or clever (antonym slow). The sentence tells you which is meant.
  3. Beware of the opposite in a synonym question. Examiners place the antonym among the options because a hurried eye picks any word that feels connected.
  4. Remove what is clearly wrong, then choose between the remaining options by putting each into a sentence in your head.

Shades of meaning

Synonyms differ in strength and in tone. "Glad", "happy", "delighted" and "overjoyed" climb a ladder. "Thin", "slim" and "skinny" describe the same body with a neutral, a kind and an unkind voice. A good writer chooses among synonyms; a weak one treats them as spare parts. Our page on words to use instead of "very" has more such ladders.
