Act: meaning, synonyms and examples

Act is used as a noun (5 senses) and verb (10 senses). Its most common meaning is: A legal document codifying the result of deliberations of a committee or society or legislative body.

In Hindi
काम करना, अभिनय करना; अधिनियम
Synonyms
enactment, deed, human action, human activity, routine, number, turn, bit, move, behave
Opposites
refrain
Word family
actuate, action, actable, actor, acting

Act as a noun

  1. A legal document codifying the result of deliberations of a committee or society or legislative body.

    Synonyms: enactment · A kind of: legal document · Kinds: nullity, decree, legislative act

  2. Something that people do or cause to happen.

    Synonyms: deed, human action, human activity · A kind of: event · Kinds: action, acquiring, causing, delivery, departure, discovery, disposal, implementation

  3. A subdivision of a play or opera or ballet.

    A kind of: dramatic composition

  4. A short theatrical performance that is part of a longer program.

    “He did his act three times every evening.” “She had a catchy little routine.” “It was one of the best numbers he ever did.”

    Synonyms: routine, number, turn, bit · A kind of: performance · Kinds: show-stopper

  5. A manifestation of insincerity.

    “He put on quite an act for her benefit.”

    A kind of: expression

Act as a verb

  1. Perform an action, or work out or perform (an action).

    “Think before you act.” “We must move quickly.” “The governor should act on the new energy bill.”

    Synonyms: move · Opposite: refrain · Kinds: alternate, finish up, rush, interrupt, react, go ahead, force, create

  2. Behave in a certain manner; show a certain behavior; conduct or comport oneself.

    “You should act like an adult.” “Don't behave like a fool.” “What makes her do this way?”

    Synonyms: behave, do · Kinds: act involuntarily, fall over backwards, presume, optimize, quack, menace, make, swagger

  3. Play a role or part.

    “Gielgud played Hamlet.” “She wants to act Lady Macbeth, but she is too young for the role.” “She played the servant to her husband's master.”

    Synonyms: play, represent · A kind of: re-create · Kinds: make, support, enact, act out, impersonate, parody, emote

  4. Discharge one's duties.

    “She acts as the chair.” “In what capacity are you acting?”

    A kind of: serve · Kinds: criticize

  5. Pretend to have certain qualities or state of mind.

    “He acted the idiot.” “She plays deaf when the news are bad.”

    Synonyms: play, act as · A kind of: act

  6. Be suitable for theatrical performance.

    “This scene acts well.”

    A kind of: be

  7. Have an effect or outcome; often the one desired or expected.

    “The voting process doesn't work as well as people thought.” “How does your idea work in practice?” “This method doesn't work.”

    Synonyms: work · A kind of: succeed

  8. Be engaged in an activity, often for no particular purpose other than pleasure.

    Kinds: play, drive around, walk around

  9. Behave unnaturally or affectedly.

    “She's just acting.”

    Synonyms: dissemble, pretend · Kinds: simulate

  10. Perform on a stage or theater.

    “She acts in this play.” “He acted in `Julius Caesar'.” “I played in `A Christmas Carol'.”

    Synonyms: play, roleplay, playact · A kind of: perform · Kinds: stooge, mime, overact, underact

Phrases and compounds with "act"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • act as (verb) Function as or act like.
  • act on (verb) Carry further or advance.
  • act up (verb) Misbehave badly; act in a silly or improper way.
  • act out (verb) Represent an incident, state, or emotion by action, especially on stage.
  • act upon (verb) Have and exert influence or effect.
  • rico act (noun) Law intended to eradicate organized crime by establishing strong sanctions and forfeiture provisions.
  • riot act (noun) A vigorous reprimand.
  • class act (noun) Someone who shows impressive and stylish excellence.
  • stamp act (noun) An act passed by the British Parliament in 1756 that raised revenue from the American Colonies by a duty in the form of a stamp required on all newspapers and legal or commercial documents; opposition by the Colonies resulted in the repeal of the act in 1766.
  • act of god (noun) A natural and unavoidable catastrophe that interrupts the expected course of events.
  • speech act (noun) The use of language to perform some act.
  • special act (noun) A legislative act that applies only to a particular person or particular district.
  • act superior (verb) Act like the master of.
  • enabling act (noun) A provision in a law that confers on appropriate officials the power to implement or enforce the law.
  • fair-trade act (noun) Formerly a state law that protected manufacturers from price-cutting by allowing them to set minimum retail prices for their merchandise; eliminated by the United States Congress in 1975.
  • act reflexively (verb) Act in an uncontrolled manner.
