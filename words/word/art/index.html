Art: meaning, synonyms and examples

Art is used as a noun (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: The products of human creativity; works of art collectively.

Word family

Art as a noun

  1. The products of human creativity; works of art collectively.

    “An art exhibition.” “A fine collection of art.”

    Synonyms: fine art · A kind of: creation · Kinds: artificial flower, commercial art, cyberart, decoupage, diptych, gem, genre, graphic art

  2. The creation of beautiful or significant things.

    “Art does not need to be innovative to be good.” “I was never any good at art.” “He said that architecture is the art of wasting space beautifully.”

    Synonyms: artistic creation, artistic production · A kind of: creation · Kinds: arts and crafts, ceramics, decalcomania, decoupage, drawing, glyptography, gastronomy, origami

  3. A superior skill that you can learn by study and practice and observation.

    “The art of conversation.” “It's quite an art.”

    Synonyms: artistry, prowess · A kind of: superior skill · Kinds: aviation, eristic, falconry, fortification, homiletics, horology, minstrelsy, musicianship

  4. Photographs or other visual representations in a printed publication.

    “The publisher was responsible for all the artwork in the book.”

    Synonyms: artwork, graphics, nontextual matter · A kind of: visual communication · Kinds: illustration, drawing

Phrases and compounds with "art"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • op art (noun) A style of abstractionism popular in the 1960s; produces dramatic visual effects with colors and contrasts that are difficult for the eye to resolve.
  • pop art (noun) A school of art that emerged in the United Kingdom in the 1950s and became prevalent in the United States and the United Kingdom in the 1960s; it imitated the techniques of commercial art (as the soup cans of Andy Warhol) and the styles of popular culture and the mass media.
  • art deco (noun) A style of design that was popular in the 1920s and 1930s; marked by stylized forms and geometric designs adapted to mass production.
  • art form (noun) (architecture) a form of artistic expression (such as writing or painting or architecture).
  • art rock (noun) A style of rock music that emerged in the 1970s; associated with attempts to combine rock with jazz and other forms; intended for listening and not dancing.
  • clip art (noun) Ready-made pieces of computerized graphic art that can be used to decorate a document.
  • fine art (noun) The products of human creativity; works of art collectively.
  • folk art (noun) Genre of art of unknown origin that reflects traditional values of a society.
  • art class (noun) A class in which you learn to draw or paint.
  • art paper (noun) A high-quality paper (usually having a filler of china clay).
  • art tatum (noun) United States jazz pianist who was almost completely blind; his innovations influenced many other jazz musicians (1910-1956).
  • black art (noun) The belief in magical spells that harness occult forces or evil spirits to produce unnatural effects in the world.
  • naive art (noun) A genre of art and outdoor constructions made by untrained artists who do not recognize themselves as artists.
  • art critic (noun) A critic of paintings.
  • art dealer (noun) A dealer in works of art requiring esthetic evaluation.
  • art editor (noun) An editor who is responsible for illustrations and layouts in printed matter.
