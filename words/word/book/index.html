Book: meaning, synonyms and examples

Book is used as a noun (9 senses) and verb (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: A written work or composition that has been published (printed on pages bound together).

In Hindi
किताब, पुस्तक; बुक करना
Synonyms
volume, record, record book, script, playscript, ledger, leger, account book, book of account, rule book
Word family
booklet, booker, booking, bookable

Book as a noun

  1. A written work or composition that has been published (printed on pages bound together).

    “I am reading a good book on economics.”

    A kind of: publication · Kinds: authority, formulary, trade book, bestiary, pop-up book, storybook, tome, booklet

  2. Physical objects consisting of a number of pages bound together.

    “He used a large book as a doorstop.”

    Synonyms: volume · A kind of: product · Kinds: album, coffee-table book, folio, hardback, journal, novel, order book, paperback book

  3. A compilation of the known facts regarding something or someone.

    “Al Smith used to say, `Let's look at the record'.” “His name is in all the record books.”

    Synonyms: record, record book · A kind of: fact · Kinds: logbook, won-lost record, card

  4. A written version of a play or other dramatic composition; used in preparing for a performance.

    Synonyms: script, playscript · A kind of: dramatic composition · Kinds: promptbook, continuity, dialogue, libretto, scenario, screenplay, shooting script

  5. A record in which commercial accounts are recorded.

    “They got a subpoena to examine our books.”

    Synonyms: ledger, leger, account book, book of account · A kind of: record · Kinds: cost ledger, general ledger, subsidiary ledger, daybook

  6. A collection of playing cards satisfying the rules of a card game.

    A kind of: collection

  7. A collection of rules or prescribed standards on the basis of which decisions are made.

    “They run things by the book around here.”

    Synonyms: rule book · A kind of: collection

  8. A major division of a long written composition.

    “The book of Isaiah.”

    A kind of: section

  9. A number of sheets (ticket or stamps etc.) bound together on one edge.

    “He bought a book of stamps.”

    A kind of: product

Book as a verb

  1. Engage for a performance.

    “Her agent had booked her for several concerts in Tokyo.”

    A kind of: schedule

  2. Arrange for and reserve (something for someone else) in advance.

    “Reserve me a seat on a flight.” “The agent booked tickets to the show for the whole family.” “Please hold a table at Maxim's.”

    Synonyms: reserve, hold · A kind of: request · Kinds: keep open

  3. Record a charge in a police register.

    “The policeman booked her when she tried to solicit a man.”

    A kind of: record · Kinds: ticket

  4. Register in a hotel booker.

    A kind of: register

Phrases and compounds with "book"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • book up (verb) Book all available places or tickets.
  • book bag (noun) A bag in which students carry their books.
  • day book (noun) The daily written record of events (as arrests) in a police station.
  • mug book (noun) A file of mug shots (pictures of criminals that are kept on file by the police).
  • bell book (noun) A logbook in which all orders concerning the main engines of a ship are recorded.
  • blue book (noun) A report published by the British government; bound in blue.
  • book fair (noun) Fair organized by publishers or booksellers to promote the sale of books.
  • book lung (noun) Organ in many arachnids containing many thin folds of membrane resembling the leaves of a book.
  • fake book (noun) A fake in the form of an imitation book; used to fill bookcases of people who wish to appear scholarly.
  • leaf-book (noun) An unbound manuscript of some ancient classic (as distinguished from a scroll).
  • rule book (noun) A collection of rules or prescribed standards on the basis of which decisions are made.
  • black book (noun) A list of people who are out of favor.
  • book agent (noun) A book salesman.
  • book louse (noun) Minute wingless psocopterous insects injurious to books and papers.
  • book lover (noun) Someone who loves (and usually collects) books.
  • book token (noun) A gift voucher that can be exchanged for books costing up to an amount given on the voucher.
