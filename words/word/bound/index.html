Bound: meaning, synonyms and examples

Bound is used as an adjective (9 senses), verb (4 senses) and noun (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: Confined by bonds.

In Hindi
बँधा हुआ; सीमा; उछलना
Synonyms
destined, bandaged, apprenticed, articled, indentured, jump, leap, spring, border, restrict
Opposites
unbound, free
Word family
bounder, boundary

Bound as an adjective

  1. Confined by bonds.

    “Bound and gagged hostages.”

    Opposite: unbound · Similar: chained, fettered, furled, pinioned, tethered, trussed

  2. Held with another element, substance or material in chemical or physical union.

    Opposite: free · Similar: conjugate, conjugate

  3. Secured with a cover or binding; often used as a combining form.

    “Bound volumes.” “Leather-bound volumes.”

    Opposite: unbound · Similar: brassbound, cased, half-bound, paperback, well-bound

  4. (usually followed by `to') governed by fate.

    “Bound to happen.” “An old house destined to be demolished.” “He is destined to be famous.”

    Synonyms: destined · Similar: certain

  5. Covered or wrapped with a bandage.

    “The bandaged wound on the back of his head.” “An injury bound in fresh gauze.”

    Synonyms: bandaged · Similar: treated

  6. Headed or intending to head in a certain direction; often used as a combining form as in `college-bound students'.

    “Children bound for school.” “A flight destined for New York.”

    Synonyms: destined · Similar: oriented

  7. Bound by an oath.

    “A bound official.”

    Similar: sworn

  8. Bound by contract.

    Synonyms: apprenticed, articled, indentured · Similar: unfree

  9. Confined in the bowels.

    “He is bound in the belly.”

    Similar: constipated

Bound as a verb

  1. Move forward by leaps and bounds.

    “The horse bounded across the meadow.” “The child leapt across the puddle.” “Can you jump over the fence?”

    Synonyms: jump, leap, spring · A kind of: move · Kinds: pronk, bounce, burst, bounce, capriole, galumph, ski jump, saltate

  2. Form the boundary of; be contiguous to.

    Synonyms: border · A kind of: enclose · Kinds: skirt, verge, shore

  3. Place limits on (extent or access).

    “Restrict the use of this parking lot.” “Limit the time you can spend with your friends.”

    Synonyms: restrict, restrain, trammel, limit, confine, throttle · A kind of: control · Kinds: reduce, tie, gate, draw the line, mark off, rule, baffle, hamper

  4. Spring back; spring away from an impact.

    “The rubber ball bounced.” “These particles do not resile but they unite after they collide.”

    Synonyms: bounce, resile, take a hop, spring, rebound, recoil, reverberate, ricochet · A kind of: jump · Kinds: kick back, skip, carom

Bound as a noun

  1. A line determining the limits of an area.

    Synonyms: boundary, edge · A kind of: line · Kinds: rim, margin, periphery, brink, upper bound, lower bound, thalweg

  2. The line or plane indicating the limit or extent of something.

    Synonyms: boundary, bounds · A kind of: extremity · Kinds: hairline, frontier, heliopause, boundary line, bourn, district line, county line, city line

  3. The greatest possible degree of something.

    “What he did was beyond the bounds of acceptable behavior.” “To the limit of his ability.”

    Synonyms: limit, boundary · A kind of: extent · Kinds: knife-edge, starkness, thermal barrier, utmost, verge

  4. A light, self-propelled movement upwards or forwards.

    Synonyms: leap, leaping, spring, saltation, bounce · A kind of: jump · Kinds: caper, pounce

Phrases and compounds with "bound"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • bound up (adjective) Closely or inseparably connected or associated with.
  • bound off (verb) Bound off one point after another.
  • bound form (noun) A morpheme that occurs only as part of a larger construction; eg an -s at the end of plural nouns.
  • desk-bound (adjective) Restricted to working in an office rather than in an active physical capacity.
  • duty-bound (adjective) Under a moral obligation to do something.
  • half-bound (adjective) (of books) having the back bound in one material and the sides in another.
  • well-bound (adjective) (of books) having a sturdy and attractive binding.
  • frost-bound (adjective) (of the ground) made hard by frost.
  • lower bound (noun) (mathematics) a number equal to or less than any other number in a given set.
  • spell-bound (adjective) Having your attention fixated as though by a spell.
  • upper bound (noun) (mathematics) a number equal to or greater than any other number in a given set.
  • muscle-bound (adjective) Having stiff muscles as the result of excessive exercise.
  • outward-bound (adjective) That is going out or leaving.
  • weather-bound (adjective) Delayed or shut in by bad weather.
  • bound morpheme (noun) A morpheme that occurs only as part of a larger construction; eg an -s at the end of plural nouns.
  • homeward-bound (adjective) Oriented toward home.
