Break: meaning, synonyms and examples

Break is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: Terminate.

In Hindi
तोड़ना; विराम
Synonyms
interrupt, separate, split up, fall apart, come apart, bust, transgress, offend, infract, violate
Opposites
repair, keep, make
Word family
breakable, breakage, breaker

Break as a verb

  1. Terminate.

    “Break a lucky streak.” “Break the cycle of poverty.”

    Synonyms: interrupt · A kind of: end · Kinds: hold on, cut short, freeze

  2. Become separated into pieces or fragments.

    “The figurine broke.” “The freshly baked loaf fell apart.”

    Synonyms: separate, split up, fall apart, come apart · A kind of: change integrity · Kinds: burst, puncture, burst, smash, ladder, snap, break up, crush

  3. Render inoperable or ineffective.

    “You broke the alarm clock when you took it apart!”

    A kind of: damage

  4. Ruin completely.

    “He busted my radio!”

    Synonyms: bust · Opposite: repair · A kind of: destroy · Kinds: break down

  5. Destroy the integrity of; usually by force; cause to separate into pieces or fragments.

    “He broke the glass plate.” “She broke the match.”

    A kind of: separate · Kinds: shatter, fracture, break in, smash

  6. Act in disregard of laws, rules, contracts, or promises.

    “Offend all laws of humanity.” “Violate the basic laws or human civilization.” “Break a law.”

    Synonyms: transgress, offend, infract, violate, go against, breach · Opposite: keep · A kind of: disrespect · Kinds: sin, drop the ball, conflict, trespass, trespass

  7. Move away or escape suddenly.

    “The horses broke from the stable.” “Three inmates broke jail.” “Nobody can break out--this prison is high security.”

    Synonyms: break out, break away · A kind of: escape

  8. Scatter or part.

    “The clouds broke after the heavy downpour.”

    A kind of: disperse

  9. Force out or release suddenly and often violently something pent up.

    “Break into tears.” “Erupt in anger.”

    Synonyms: burst, erupt · A kind of: express emotion

  10. Prevent completion.

    “Stop the project.” “Break off the negotiations.”

    Synonyms: break off, discontinue, stop · A kind of: end · Kinds: fracture, bog down, interrupt

  11. Enter someone's (virtual or real) property in an unauthorized manner, usually with the intent to steal or commit a violent act.

    “Someone broke in while I was on vacation.” “They broke into my car and stole my radio!” “Who broke into my account last night?”

    Synonyms: break in · A kind of: trespass · Kinds: crack

  12. Make submissive, obedient, or useful.

    “The horse was tough to break.” “I broke in the new intern.”

    Synonyms: break in · A kind of: domesticate

  13. Fail to agree with; be in violation of; as of rules or patterns.

    “This sentence violates the rules of syntax.”

    Synonyms: violate, go against · Kinds: fly in the face of

  14. Surpass in excellence.

    “She bettered her own record.” “Break a record.”

    Synonyms: better · A kind of: surpass

Break as a noun

  1. Some abrupt occurrence that interrupts an ongoing activity.

    “The telephone is an annoying interruption.” “There was a break in the action when a player was hurt.”

    Synonyms: interruption · A kind of: happening · Kinds: dislocation, punctuation, suspension, eclipse

  2. An unexpected piece of good luck.

    “He finally got his big break.”

    Synonyms: good luck, happy chance · A kind of: accident

  3. (geology) a crack in the earth's crust resulting from the displacement of one side with respect to the other.

    “They built it right over a geological fault.” “He studied the faulting of the earth's crust.”

    Synonyms: fault, faulting, geological fault, shift, fracture · A kind of: crack · Kinds: inclined fault, strike-slip fault

  4. A personal or social separation (as between opposing factions).

    “They hoped to avoid a break in relations.”

    Synonyms: rupture, breach, severance, rift, falling out · A kind of: separation · Kinds: schism

  5. A pause from doing something (as work).

    “We took a 10-minute break.” “He took time out to recuperate.”

    Synonyms: respite, recess, time out · A kind of: pause · Kinds: spring break

  6. The act of breaking something.

    “The breakage was unavoidable.”

    Synonyms: breakage, breaking · A kind of: change of integrity · Kinds: rupture, smashing, fracture, chip

  7. A time interval during which there is a temporary cessation of something.

    Synonyms: pause, intermission, interruption, suspension · A kind of: time interval · Kinds: lapse, blackout, caesura, dead air, delay, halftime, respite, time-out

  8. Breaking of hard tissue such as bone.

    “It was a nasty fracture.” “The break seems to have been caused by a fall.”

    Synonyms: fracture · A kind of: injury · Kinds: comminuted fracture, complete fracture, compound fracture, compression fracture, depressed fracture, displaced fracture, fatigue fracture, hairline fracture

  9. The occurrence of breaking.

    “The break in the dam threatened the valley.”

    A kind of: separation · Kinds: snap

  10. An abrupt change in the tone or register of the voice (as at puberty or due to emotion).

    “Then there was a break in her voice.”

    A kind of: change

  11. The opening shot that scatters the balls in billiards or pool.

    A kind of: stroke

  12. (tennis) a score consisting of winning a game when your opponent was serving.

    “He was up two breaks in the second set.”

    Synonyms: break of serve · A kind of: score

  13. An act of delaying or interrupting the continuity.

    “It was presented without commercial breaks.” “There was a gap in his account.”

    Synonyms: interruption, disruption, gap · A kind of: delay · Kinds: cut-in, cut-in, interjection, breaking off, heckling

  14. A sudden dash.

    “He made a break for the open door.”

    A kind of: dash · Kinds: fast break

Phrases and compounds with "break"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • break in (verb) Enter someone's (virtual or real) property in an unauthorized manner, usually with the intent to steal or commit a violent act.
  • break up (verb) To cause to separate and go in different directions.
  • break-in (noun) Trespassing for an unlawful purpose; illegal entrance into premises with criminal intent.
  • break off (verb) Interrupt before its natural or planned end.
  • break out (verb) Start abruptly.
  • break-axe (noun) West Indian timber tree having very hard wood.
  • tax break (noun) A tax deduction that is granted in order to encourage a particular type of commercial activity.
  • tea break (noun) A snack taken during a break in the work day.
  • break away (verb) Move away or escape suddenly.
  • break camp (verb) Leave a camp.
  • break down (verb) Make ineffective.
  • break even (verb) Make neither profit nor loss.
  • break into (verb) Express or utter spontaneously.
  • break open (verb) Open with force.
  • break seal (noun) A seal that must be broken when first used and cannot easily be resealed.
  • break wind (verb) Expel intestinal gases through the anus.
