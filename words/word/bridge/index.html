Bridge: meaning, synonyms and examples

Bridge is used as a noun (9 senses) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: A structure that allows people or vehicles to cross an obstacle such as a river or canal or railway etc.

Word family

Bridge as a noun

  1. A structure that allows people or vehicles to cross an obstacle such as a river or canal or railway etc.

    Synonyms: span · A kind of: structure · Kinds: cantilever bridge, cattle guard, covered bridge, drawbridge, footbridge, overpass, pontoon bridge, rope bridge

  2. A circuit consisting of two branches (4 arms arranged in a diamond configuration) across which a meter is connected.

    Synonyms: bridge circuit · A kind of: circuit

  3. Something resembling a bridge in form or function.

    “His letters provided a bridge across the centuries.”

    A kind of: connection

  4. The hard ridge that forms the upper part of the nose.

    “Her glasses left marks on the bridge of her nose.”

    A kind of: structure

  5. Any of various card games based on whist for four players.

    A kind of: card game · Kinds: bridge whist, auction, contract

  6. A wooden support that holds the strings up.

    A kind of: support

  7. A denture anchored to teeth on either side of missing teeth.

    Synonyms: bridgework · A kind of: denture

  8. The link between two lenses; rests on the nose.

    Synonyms: nosepiece · A kind of: link

  9. An upper deck where a ship is steered and the captain stands.

    Synonyms: bridge deck · A kind of: upper deck · Kinds: conning tower, flying bridge

Bridge as a verb

  1. Connect or reduce the distance between.

    Synonyms: bridge over · A kind of: connect

  2. Make a bridge across.

    “Bridge a river.”

    A kind of: join

  3. Cross over on a bridge.

    A kind of: traverse

Phrases and compounds with "bridge"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • fly bridge (noun) The highest navigational bridge on a ship; a small (often open) deck above the pilot house.
  • jet bridge (noun) An extendible bridge for loading passengers onto large commercial airplanes; provides protected access to the plane from the gate.
  • bridge deck (noun) An upper deck where a ship is steered and the captain stands.
  • bridge hand (noun) The cards held in a game of bridge.
  • bridge over (verb) Suffice for a period between two points.
  • lift bridge (noun) A bridge that can be raised to block passage or to allow boats or ships to pass beneath it.
  • rope bridge (noun) A bridge consisting of ropes.
  • toll bridge (noun) A bridge where toll is charged for crossing.
  • bridge agent (noun) An operative who acts as a courier or go-between from a case officer to a secret agent in a hostile area.
  • bridge whist (noun) The earliest form of bridge; the dealer could name the trump suit.
  • truss bridge (noun) A bridge supported by trusses.
  • bailey bridge (noun) A temporary bridge designed for rapid construction.
  • bateau bridge (noun) A temporary bridge built over a series of pontoons.
  • bridge player (noun) A card player in a game of bridge.
  • flying bridge (noun) The highest navigational bridge on a ship; a small (often open) deck above the pilot house.
  • humber bridge (noun) A suspension bridge at Hull, England; 4,626 feet long.
