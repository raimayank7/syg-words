Campaign: meaning, synonyms and examples

Campaign is used as a noun (4 senses) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: A race between candidates for elective office.

In Hindi
अभियान
Synonyms
political campaign, run, cause, crusade, drive, movement, effort, military campaign, hunting expedition, safari
Word family
campaigner

Campaign as a noun

  1. A race between candidates for elective office.

    “I managed his campaign for governor.” “He is raising money for a Senate run.”

    Synonyms: political campaign, run · A kind of: race · Kinds: governor's race, senate campaign

  2. A series of actions advancing a principle or tending toward a particular end.

    “He supported populist campaigns.” “They worked in the cause of world peace.” “The team was ready for a drive toward the pennant.”

    Synonyms: cause, crusade, drive, movement, effort · A kind of: venture · Kinds: advertising campaign, anti-war movement, charm campaign, consumerism, campaigning, fund-raising campaign, feminist movement, lost cause

  3. Several related operations aimed at achieving a particular goal (usually within geographical and temporal constraints).

    Synonyms: military campaign · A kind of: operation · Kinds: expedition

  4. An overland journey by hunters (especially in Africa).

    Synonyms: hunting expedition, safari · A kind of: expedition

Campaign as a verb

  1. Run, stand, or compete for an office or a position.

    “Who's running for treasurer this year?”

    Synonyms: run · A kind of: race · Kinds: stump, rerun, whistlestop, cross-file

  2. Exert oneself continuously, vigorously, or obtrusively to gain an end or engage in a crusade for a certain cause or person; be an advocate for.

    “The liberal party pushed for reforms.” “She is crusading for women's rights.” “The Dean is pushing for his favorite candidate.”

    Synonyms: crusade, fight, press, push, agitate · A kind of: advertise

  3. Go on a campaign; go off to war.

    Synonyms: take the field · Kinds: crusade

Phrases and compounds with "campaign"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • ad campaign (noun) An organized program of advertisements.
  • campaign hat (noun) A broad-brimmed felt hat with a high crown; formerly worn by the United States Army and Marine personnel.
  • charm campaign (noun) A campaign of flattery and friendliness (by a company, politician, etc.) to become more popular and gain support.
  • naval campaign (noun) An operation conducted primarily by naval forces in order to gain or extend or maintain control of the sea.
  • sales campaign (noun) An advertising campaign intended to promote sales.
  • senate campaign (noun) A race for election to the senate.
  • okinawa campaign (noun) A campaign in the closing days of World War II in the Pacific (April to June 1945); in savage close-quarter fighting United States marines and regular army troops took the island from the Japanese; considered the greatest victory of the Pacific campaign for the Americans.
  • military campaign (noun) Several related operations aimed at achieving a particular goal (usually within geographical and temporal constraints).
  • political campaign (noun) A race between candidates for elective office.
  • petersburg campaign (noun) The final campaign of the American Civil War (1864-65); Union forces under Grant besieged and finally defeated Confederate forces under Lee.
  • wilderness campaign (noun) American Civil War; a series of indecisive battles in Grant's campaign (1864) against Lee in which both armies suffered terrible losses.
  • advertising campaign (noun) An organized program of advertisements.
  • dardanelles campaign (noun) The unsuccessful campaign in World War I (1915) by the English and French to open a passage for aid to Russia; defeated by the Turks.
  • front-porch campaign (noun) A campaign in which the candidate makes speeches but does not travel.
  • campaign for governor (noun) A race for election to the governorship.
  • fund-raising campaign (noun) A campaign to raise money for some cause.
