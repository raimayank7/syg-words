Catch: meaning, synonyms and examples

Catch is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (10 senses). Its most common meaning is: Discover or come upon accidentally, suddenly, or unexpectedly; catch somebody doing something or in a certain state.

In Hindi
पकड़ना
Synonyms
pick up, get, grab, take hold of, capture, hitch, arrest, overtake, catch up with, take in
Word family
catcher, catchment, catchy

Catch as a verb

  1. Discover or come upon accidentally, suddenly, or unexpectedly; catch somebody doing something or in a certain state.

    “She caught her son eating candy.” “She was caught shoplifting.”

    A kind of: surprise

  2. Perceive with the senses quickly, suddenly, or momentarily.

    “I caught the aroma of coffee.” “He caught the allusion in her glance.” “Ears open to catch every sound.”

    Synonyms: pick up · A kind of: perceive

  3. Reach with a blow or hit in a particular spot.

    “The rock caught her in the back of the head.” “The blow got him in the back.” “The punch caught him in the stomach.”

    Synonyms: get · A kind of: hit

  4. Take hold of so as to seize or restrain or stop the motion of.

    “Catch the ball!” “Grab the elevator door!”

    Synonyms: grab, take hold of · A kind of: seize · Kinds: harpoon, fish, hook, net, intercept

  5. Succeed in catching or seizing, especially after a chase.

    “We finally got the suspect.” “Did you catch the thief?”

    Synonyms: get, capture · A kind of: seize · Kinds: recapture, lasso

  6. To hook or entangle.

    “One foot caught in the stirrup.”

    Synonyms: hitch · A kind of: attach · Kinds: snag

  7. Attract and fix.

    “His look caught her.” “She caught his eye.” “Catch the attention of the waiter.”

    Synonyms: arrest, get · A kind of: attract

  8. Capture as if by hunting, snaring, or trapping.

    “I caught a rabbit in the trap today.”

    Synonyms: capture · A kind of: get · Kinds: hunt, frog, batfowl, rat, trap

  9. Reach in time.

    “I have to catch a train at 7 o'clock.”

    A kind of: board · Kinds: get

  10. Get or regain something necessary, usually quickly or briefly.

    “Catch some sleep.” “Catch one's breath.”

    A kind of: get

  11. Catch up with and possibly overtake.

    “The Rolls Royce caught us near the exit ramp.”

    Synonyms: overtake, catch up with

  12. Be struck or affected by.

    “Catch fire.” “Catch the mood.”

    A kind of: change

  13. Check oneself during an action.

    “She managed to catch herself before telling her boss what was on her mind.”

    A kind of: control

  14. Hear, usually without the knowledge of the speakers.

    “We overheard the conversation at the next table.”

    Synonyms: take in, overhear · A kind of: hear

Catch as a noun

  1. A drawback or difficulty that is not readily evident.

    “It sounds good but what's the catch?”

    Synonyms: gimmick · A kind of: drawback

  2. The quantity that was caught.

    “The catch was only 10 fish.”

    Synonyms: haul · A kind of: indefinite quantity

  3. A person regarded as a good matrimonial prospect.

    Synonyms: match · A kind of: adult

  4. Anything that is caught (especially if it is worth catching).

    “He shared his catch with the others.”

    A kind of: object

  5. A break or check in the voice (usually a sign of strong emotion).

    A kind of: manner of speaking

  6. A restraint that checks the motion of something.

    “He used a book as a stop to hold the door open.”

    Synonyms: stop · A kind of: restraint · Kinds: bench hook, doorstop, pawl, tripper

  7. A fastener that fastens or locks a door or window.

    A kind of: fastener · Kinds: hasp, hood latch, hook, latch

  8. A cooperative game in which a ball is passed back and forth.

    “He played catch with his son in the backyard.”

    A kind of: game

  9. The act of catching an object with the hands.

    “Mays made the catch with his back to the plate.” “He made a grab for the ball before it landed.” “Martin's snatch at the bridle failed and the horse raced away.”

    Synonyms: grab, snatch, snap · A kind of: touch · Kinds: fair catch, interception, reception, rebound, shoestring catch, mesh

  10. The act of apprehending (especially apprehending a criminal).

    “The policeman on the beat got credit for the collar.”

    Synonyms: apprehension, arrest, collar, pinch, taking into custody · A kind of: capture

Phrases and compounds with "catch"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • by-catch (noun) Unwanted marine creatures that are caught in the nets while fishing for another species.
  • catch it (verb) Receive punishment; be scolded or reprimanded.
  • catch on (verb) Understand, usually after some initial difficulty.
  • catch up (verb) Reach the point where one should be after a delay.
  • catch out (verb) Trap; especially in an error or in a reprehensible act.
  • catch cold (verb) Come down with a cold.
  • catch crop (noun) A crop that grows quickly (e.g. lettuce) and can be planted between two regular crops grown in successive seasons or between two rows of crops in the same season.
  • catch fire (verb) Start to burn or burst into flames.
  • fair catch (noun) (American football) a catch of a punt on the fly by a defensive player who has signalled that he will not run and so should not be tackled.
  • catch sight (verb) See something for a brief time.
  • catch a wink (verb) Take a siesta.
  • catch phrase (noun) A phrase that has become a catchword.
  • safety catch (noun) Guard consisting of a locking device that prevents a weapon from being fired.
  • catch up with (verb) Catch up with and possibly overtake.
  • glottal catch (noun) A stop consonant articulated by releasing pressure at the glottis; as in the sudden onset of a vowel.
  • catch some z's (verb) Be asleep.
