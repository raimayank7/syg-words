Charge: meaning, synonyms and examples

Charge is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: To make a rush at or sudden attack upon, as in battle.

In Hindi
शुल्क; आरोप; चार्ज करना
Synonyms
bear down, accuse, bill, tear, shoot, shoot down, buck, appoint, lodge, file
Opposites
discharge, pay cash
Word family
charger

Charge as a verb

  1. To make a rush at or sudden attack upon, as in battle.

    “He saw Jess charging at him with a pitchfork.”

    Synonyms: bear down · A kind of: rush

  2. Blame for, make a claim of wrongdoing or misbehavior against.

    “He charged the director with indifference.”

    Synonyms: accuse · Kinds: blame, accuse, defame, indict

  3. Demand payment.

    “Will I get charged for this service?” “We were billed for 4 nights in the hotel, although we stayed only 3 nights.”

    Synonyms: bill · A kind of: account · Kinds: levy, tithe, assess, undercharge, surcharge, invoice

  4. Move quickly and violently.

    “The car tore down the street.” “He came charging into my office.”

    Synonyms: tear, shoot, shoot down, buck · A kind of: rush · Kinds: rip

  5. Assign a duty, responsibility or obligation to.

    “He was appointed deputy manager.” “She was charged with supervising the creation of a concordance.”

    Synonyms: appoint · Kinds: delegate, depute, name, empower, accredit, create

  6. File a formal charge against.

    Synonyms: lodge, file · A kind of: accuse · Kinds: impeach

  7. Make an accusatory claim.

    “The defense attorney charged that the jurors were biased.”

    A kind of: claim · Kinds: tax, complain

  8. Fill or load to capacity.

    “Charge the wagon with hay.”

    Opposite: discharge · A kind of: change · Kinds: burden, freight

  9. Enter a certain amount as a charge.

    “He charged me $15.”

    A kind of: debit

  10. Cause to be admitted; of persons to an institution.

    “After the second episode, she had to be committed.” “He was committed to prison.”

    Synonyms: commit, institutionalize, institutionalise, send · A kind of: transfer · Kinds: hospitalize

  11. Give over to another for care or safekeeping.

    “Consign your baggage.”

    Synonyms: consign · A kind of: entrust · Kinds: pledge, pawn, check, check

  12. Pay with a credit card; pay with plastic money; postpone payment by recording a purchase as a debt.

    “Will you pay cash or charge the purchase?”

    Opposite: pay cash · A kind of: pay

  13. Lie down on command, of hunting dogs.

    A kind of: lie down

  14. Cause to be agitated, excited, or roused.

    “The speaker charged up the crowd with his inflammatory remarks.”

    Synonyms: agitate, rouse, turn on, commove, excite, charge up · A kind of: disturb · Kinds: hype up, bother, pother, electrify

Charge as a noun

  1. An impetuous rush toward someone or something.

    “The wrestler's charge carried him past his adversary.” “The battle began with a cavalry charge.”

    Kinds: countercharge

  2. (criminal law) a pleading describing some wrong or offense.

    “He was arrested on a charge of larceny.”

    Synonyms: complaint · A kind of: pleading · Kinds: accusation

  3. The price charged for some article or service.

    “The admission charge.”

    A kind of: cost · Kinds: carrying charge, depreciation charge, undercharge, overcharge, fare, fixed charge, agio, demurrage

  4. The quantity of unbalanced electricity in a body (either positive or negative) and construed as an excess or deficiency of electrons.

    “The battery needed a fresh charge.”

    Synonyms: electric charge · A kind of: electrical phenomenon · Kinds: electrostatic charge, positive charge, negative charge

  5. Attention and management implying responsibility for safety.

    “He is in the care of a bodyguard.”

    Synonyms: care, tutelage, guardianship · A kind of: protection · Kinds: due care, foster care, great care, providence, slight care

  6. A special assignment that is given to a person or group.

    “A confidential mission to London.” “His charge was deliver a message.”

    Synonyms: mission, commission · A kind of: assignment · Kinds: fool's errand, mission impossible

  7. A person committed to your care.

    “The teacher led her charges across the street.”

    A kind of: dependant

  8. Financial liabilities (such as a tax).

    “The charges against the estate.”

    A kind of: liabilities · Kinds: levy, encumbrance, assessment

  9. (psychoanalysis) the libidinal energy invested in some idea or person or object.

    “Freud thought of cathexis as a psychic analog of an electrical charge.”

    Synonyms: cathexis · A kind of: libidinal energy

  10. The swift release of a store of affective force.

    “They got a great bang out of it.” “What a boot!” “He does it for kicks.”

    Synonyms: bang, boot, rush, flush, thrill, kick · A kind of: exhilaration

  11. Request for payment of a debt.

    “They submitted their charges at the end of each month.”

    Synonyms: billing · A kind of: request · Kinds: presentment

  12. A formal statement of a command or injunction to do something.

    “The judge's charge to the jury.”

    Synonyms: commission, direction · A kind of: command · Kinds: misdirection

  13. An assertion that someone is guilty of a fault or offence.

    Synonyms: accusation · A kind of: assertion · Kinds: countercharge

  14. Heraldry consisting of a design or image depicted on a shield.

    Synonyms: bearing, heraldic bearing, armorial bearing · A kind of: heraldry · Kinds: annulet, chevron, fleur-de-lis, ordinary

Phrases and compounds with "charge"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • charge up (verb) Cause to be agitated, excited, or roused.
  • charge card (noun) A card (usually plastic) that assures a seller that the person using it has a satisfactory credit rating and that the issuer will see to it that the seller receives payment for the merchandise delivered.
  • charge unit (noun) A measure of the quantity of electricity (determined by the amount of an electric current and the time for which it flows).
  • take charge (verb) Assume control.
  • charge plate (noun) A card (usually plastic) that assures a seller that the person using it has a satisfactory credit rating and that the issuer will see to it that the seller receives payment for the merchandise delivered.
  • charge sheet (noun) The daily written record of events (as arrests) in a police station.
  • cover charge (noun) A fixed charge by a restaurant or nightclub over and above the charge for food and drink.
  • fixed charge (noun) A periodic charge that does not vary with business volume (as insurance or rent or mortgage payments etc.).
  • ionic charge (noun) The charge on an ion is equal to a constant charge e multiplied by an integer from 1 to 15.
  • banzai charge (noun) A mass attack of troops without concern for casualties; originated by Japanese who accompanied it with yells of `banzai'.
  • charge account (noun) Credit extended by a business to a customer.
  • free of charge (adverb) Without payment.
  • service charge (noun) A percentage of a bill (as at a hotel or restaurant) added in payment for service.
  • take in charge (verb) Accept as a charge.
  • bursting charge (noun) A quantity of explosive to be set off at one time.
  • carrying charge (noun) Charge made for carrying an account or for merchandise sold on an installment plan.
