Clean: meaning, synonyms and examples

Clean is used as a verb (9 senses), adjective (14 senses), noun (1 sense) and adverb (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Make clean by removing dirt, filth, or unwanted substances from.

In Hindi
साफ़; साफ़ करना
Synonyms
make clean, pick, houseclean, clean house, cleanse, strip, scavenge, clear, light, unclouded
Opposites
dirty, unclean
Word family
cleaner, cleaning, cleanness

Clean as a verb

  1. Make clean by removing dirt, filth, or unwanted substances from.

    “Clean the stove!” “The dentist cleaned my teeth.”

    Synonyms: make clean · Opposite: dirty · A kind of: change · Kinds: disinfect, brush, dust, vacuum, sanitize, bream, steam, preen

  2. Remove unwanted substances from, such as feathers or pits.

    “Clean the turkey.”

    Synonyms: pick · A kind of: remove

  3. Clean and tidy up the house.

    “She housecleans every week.”

    Synonyms: houseclean, clean house · A kind of: tidy · Kinds: spring-clean

  4. Clean one's body or parts thereof, as by washing.

    “Clean up before you see your grandparents.” “Clean your fingernails before dinner.”

    Synonyms: cleanse · A kind of: groom · Kinds: bathe, wash, wash, soap, bathe, douche, floss, decontaminate

  5. Be cleanable.

    “This stove cleans easily.”

    A kind of: be

  6. Remove all contents or possession from, or empty completely.

    “The boys cleaned the sandwich platters.” “The trees were cleaned of apples by the storm.”

    Synonyms: strip · A kind of: remove

  7. Remove while making clean.

    “Clean the spots off the rug.”

    A kind of: remove

  8. Remove unwanted substances from.

    Synonyms: scavenge · A kind of: remove

  9. Remove shells or husks from.

    “Clean grain before milling it.”

    A kind of: remove

Clean as an adjective

  1. Free from dirt or impurities; or having clean habits.

    “Children with clean shining faces.” “Clean white shirts.” “Clean dishes.”

    Opposite: dirty · Similar: cleanable, cleanly, dry-cleaned, fresh, immaculate, pristine

  2. Free of restrictions or qualifications.

    “A clean bill of health.” “A clear winner.”

    Synonyms: clear · Similar: unqualified

  3. (of sound or color) free from anything that dulls or dims.

    “Efforts to obtain a clean bass in orchestral recordings.” “Clear laughter like a waterfall.” “Clear reds and blues.”

    Synonyms: clear, light, unclouded · Similar: pure

  4. Free from impurities.

    “Clean water.” “Fresh air.”

    Synonyms: fresh · Similar: pure

  5. (of a record) having no marks of discredit or offense.

    “A clean voting record.” “A clean driver's license.”

    Similar: perfect

  6. Ritually clean or pure.

    Opposite: unclean · Similar: halal, kosher

  7. Not spreading pollution or contamination; especially radioactive contamination.

    “A clean fuel.” “Cleaner and more efficient engines.”

    Synonyms: uncontaminating · Opposite: dirty

  8. (of behavior or especially language) free from objectionable elements; fit for all observers.

    “Good clean fun.” “A clean joke.”

    Synonyms: unobjectionable · Similar: antiseptic

  9. Free from sepsis or infection.

    “A clean (or uninfected) wound.”

    Synonyms: uninfected · Similar: antiseptic

  10. Morally pure.

    “Led a clean life.”

    Synonyms: clean-living · Similar: moral

  11. (of a manuscript) having few alterations or corrections.

    “Fair copy.” “A clean manuscript.”

    Synonyms: fair · Similar: legible

  12. (of a surface) not written or printed on.

    “Blank pages.” “Fill in the blank spaces.” “A clean page.”

    Synonyms: blank, white · Similar: empty

  13. Exhibiting or calling for sportsmanship or fair play.

    “A clean fight.” “A sporting solution of the disagreement.” “Sportsmanlike conduct.”

    Synonyms: sporting, sporty, sportsmanlike · Similar: fair

  14. Without difficulties or problems.

    “A clean test flight.”

    Similar: easy

Clean as a noun

  1. A weightlift in which the barbell is lifted to shoulder height and then jerked overhead.

    Synonyms: clean and jerk · A kind of: weightlift

Clean as an adverb

  1. Completely; used as intensifiers.

    “Clean forgot the appointment.” “I'm plumb (or plum) tuckered out.”

    Synonyms: plumb, plum

  2. In conformity with the rules or laws and without fraud or cheating.

    “They played fairly.”

    Synonyms: fairly, fair

Phrases and compounds with "clean"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • clean up (verb) Put (things or places) in order.
  • clean out (verb) Empty completely.
  • clean-cut (adjective) Neat and smart in appearance.
  • dry clean (verb) Clean with chemical agents.
  • clean room (noun) A room that is virtually free of dust or bacteria; used in laboratory work and in assembly or repair of precision equipment.
  • make clean (verb) Make clean by removing dirt, filth, or unwanted substances from.
  • clean house (verb) Clean and tidy up the house.
  • clean slate (noun) An opportunity to start over without prejudice.
  • steam clean (verb) Clean by means of steaming.
  • clean-handed (adjective) Free from evil or guilt.
  • clean-limbed (adjective) Having well-proportioned limbs.
  • clean-living (adjective) Morally pure.
  • clean-shaven (adjective) Closely shaved recently.
  • spring-clean (verb) Thoroughly clean the entire house, often done only once a year.
  • vacuum-clean (verb) Clean with a vacuum cleaner.
  • clean-burning (adjective) Leaving little contamination while consuming fuel.
