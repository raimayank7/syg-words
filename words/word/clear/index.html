Clear: meaning, synonyms and examples

Clear is used as an adjective (14 senses), verb (14 senses), adverb (2 senses) and noun (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Readily apparent to the mind.

In Hindi
साफ़, स्पष्ट
Synonyms
open, clean, light, unclouded, unmortgaged, clean-cut, clear-cut, well-defined, decipherable, readable
Opposites
unclear, opaque, cloudy, bounce
Word family
clarity, clearness, clearing, clearance

Clear as an adjective

  1. Readily apparent to the mind.

    “A clear and present danger.” “A clear explanation.” “A clear indication that she was angry.”

    Opposite: unclear · Similar: broad, clear-cut, limpid, prima facie, unmistakable, vivid

  2. Free from confusion or doubt.

    “A complex problem requiring a clear head.” “Not clear about what is expected of us.”

    Similar: clearheaded

  3. Affording free passage or view.

    “A clear view.” “A clear path to victory.” “Open waters.”

    Synonyms: open · Similar: unobstructed

  4. Allowing light to pass through.

    “Clear water.” “Clear plastic bags.” “Clear glass.”

    Opposite: opaque · Similar: crystalline, hyaline, liquid, translucent, unclouded, unfrosted

  5. Free from contact or proximity or connection.

    “We were clear of the danger.” “The ship was clear of the reef.”

    Similar: free

  6. Characterized by freedom from troubling thoughts (especially guilt).

    “A clear conscience.” “Regarded her questioner with clear untroubled eyes.”

    Similar: untroubled

  7. (of sound or color) free from anything that dulls or dims.

    “Efforts to obtain a clean bass in orchestral recordings.” “Clear laughter like a waterfall.” “Clear reds and blues.”

    Synonyms: clean, light, unclouded · Similar: pure

  8. (especially of a title) free from any encumbrance or limitation that presents a question of fact or law.

    “I have clear title to this property.”

    Synonyms: unmortgaged · Similar: unencumbered

  9. Clear and distinct to the senses; easily perceptible.

    “As clear as a whistle.” “Clear footprints in the snow.” “The letter brought back a clear image of his grandfather.”

    Synonyms: clean-cut, clear-cut · Similar: distinct

  10. Accurately stated or described.

    “A set of well-defined values.”

    Synonyms: well-defined

  11. Free from clouds or mist or haze.

    “On a clear day.”

    Opposite: cloudy · Similar: cloudless, fair, serene

  12. Free of restrictions or qualifications.

    “A clean bill of health.” “A clear winner.”

    Synonyms: clean · Similar: unqualified

  13. Free from flaw or blemish or impurity.

    “A clear perfect diamond.” “The clear complexion of a healthy young woman.”

    Similar: perfect

  14. Clear of charges or deductions.

    “A clear profit.”

    Similar: net

Clear as a verb

  1. Rid of obstructions.

    “Clear your desk.”

    Synonyms: unclutter · A kind of: change

  2. Make a way or path by removing objects.

    “Clear a path through the dense forest.”

    A kind of: make

  3. Become clear.

    “The sky cleared after the storm.”

    Synonyms: clear up, light up, brighten

  4. Grant authorization or clearance for.

    “Clear the manuscript for publication.” “The rock star never authorized this slanderous biography.”

    Synonyms: authorize, authorise, pass · A kind of: permit · Kinds: approbate, certificate, approve, declare, license, commission, validate

  5. Remove.

    “Clear the leaves from the lawn.” “Clear snow from the road.”

    A kind of: remove · Kinds: denude, clear-cut, stump, deforest

  6. Go unchallenged; be approved.

    “The bill cleared the House.”

    Synonyms: pass · A kind of: succeed · Kinds: clear

  7. Be debited and credited to the proper bank accounts.

    “The check will clear within 2 business days.”

    Opposite: bounce

  8. Go away or disappear.

    “The fog cleared in the afternoon.”

    A kind of: vanish

  9. Pass by, over, or under without making contact.

    “The balloon cleared the tree tops.”

    Synonyms: top · A kind of: pass · Kinds: hop

  10. Make free from confusion or ambiguity; make clear.

    “Could you clarify these remarks?” “Clear up the question of who is at fault.”

    Synonyms: clear up, shed light on, crystallize, crystallise, crystalize, crystalise, straighten out, sort out · A kind of: clarify

  11. Free from payment of customs duties, as of a shipment.

    “Clear the ship and let it dock.”

    A kind of: free

  12. Clear from impurities, blemishes, pollution, etc.

    A kind of: rid

  13. Yield as a net profit.

    “This sale netted me $1 million.”

    Synonyms: net · A kind of: yield

  14. Make as a net profit.

    “The company cleared $1 million.”

    Synonyms: net, sack, sack up · A kind of: profit

Clear as an adverb

  1. Completely.

    “Read the book clear to the end.” “Slept clear through the night.” “There were open fields clear to the horizon.”

    Synonyms: all the way

  2. In an easily perceptible manner.

    “Could be seen clearly under the microscope.” “She cried loud and clear.”

    Synonyms: clearly

Clear as a noun

  1. The state of being free of suspicion.

    “Investigation showed that he was in the clear.”

    A kind of: innocence

  2. A clear or unobstructed space or expanse of land or water.

    “Finally broke out of the forest into the open.”

    Synonyms: open · A kind of: area

Phrases and compounds with "clear"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • clear up (verb) Make clear and (more) comprehensible.
  • all clear (noun) A signal (usually a siren) that danger is over.
  • clear off (verb) Remove from sight.
  • clear out (verb) Move out and leave nothing behind.
  • clear-cut (adjective) Clearly or sharply defined to the mind.
  • clear away (verb) Remove from sight.
  • clear-eyed (adjective) Mentally acute or penetratingly discerning.
  • clear sailing (noun) Easy unobstructed progress.
  • clear the air (verb) Dispel differences or negative emotions.
  • clear-sighted (adjective) Having sharp clear vision.
  • crystal clear (adjective) Transmitting light; able to be seen through with clarity.
  • clear-thinking (adjective) Not mentally confused; able to think clearly and act intelligently.
  • clear the throat (verb) Clear mucus or food from one's throat.
  • clear liquid diet (noun) A diet of fluids with minimal residues (fat-free broth or strained fruit juices or gelatin); cannot be used for more than one day postoperative.
  • clear-air turbulence (noun) Strong turbulence in an otherwise cloudless region that subjects aircraft to violent updrafts or downdrafts.
  • clear and present danger (noun) A standard for judging when freedom of speech can be abridged.
