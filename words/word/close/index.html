Close: meaning, synonyms and examples

Close is used as a verb (14 senses), adjective (14 senses), adverb (2 senses) and noun (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: Move so that an opening or passage is obstructed; make shut.

In Hindi
बंद करना; पास, क़रीब
Synonyms
shut, close up, fold, shut down, close down, conclude, come together, fill up, near, nigh
Opposites
open, distant
Word family
closure, closing, closer, closeness

Close as a verb

  1. Move so that an opening or passage is obstructed; make shut.

    “Close the door.” “Shut the window.”

    Synonyms: shut · Opposite: open · Kinds: snap, slat, slam, shutter, draw, roll up, bung, seal

  2. Become closed.

    “The windows closed with a loud bang.”

    Synonyms: shut · Opposite: open · A kind of: change state

  3. Cease to operate or cause to cease operating.

    “The owners decided to move and to close the factory.” “My business closes every night at 8 P.M.” “Close up the shop.”

    Synonyms: close up, fold, shut down, close down · Opposite: open · Kinds: adjourn

  4. Finish or terminate (meetings, speeches, etc.).

    “The meeting was closed with a charge by the chairman of the board.”

    Opposite: open · A kind of: end

  5. Come to a close.

    “The concert closed with a nocturne by Chopin.”

    Synonyms: conclude · A kind of: end

  6. Complete a business deal, negotiation, or an agreement.

    “We closed on the house on Friday.” “They closed the deal on the building.”

    A kind of: end

  7. Be priced or listed when trading stops.

    “The stock market closed high this Friday.” “My new stocks closed at $59 last night.”

    A kind of: trade

  8. Engage at close quarters.

    “Close with the enemy.”

    A kind of: prosecute

  9. Cause a window or an application to disappear on a computer desktop.

    Opposite: open · A kind of: end

  10. Change one's body stance so that the forward shoulder and foot are closer to the intended point of impact.

    A kind of: move

  11. Come together, as if in an embrace.

    “Her arms closed around her long lost relative.”

    Synonyms: come together · A kind of: move

  12. Draw near.

    “The probe closed with the space station.”

    A kind of: approach

  13. Bring together all the elements or parts of.

    “Management closed ranks.”

    A kind of: join

  14. Bar access to.

    “Due to the accident, the road had to be closed for several hours.”

    A kind of: barricade

Close as an adjective

  1. At or within a short distance in space or time or having elements near each other.

    “Close to noon.” “How close are we to town?” “A close formation of ships.”

    Opposite: distant · Similar: adjacent, ambient, appressed, approximate, at hand, at hand

  2. Close in relevance or relationship.

    “A close family.” “We are all...in close sympathy with...” “Close kin.”

    Opposite: distant · Similar: approximate, boon, chummy, close-knit, confidential, cozy

  3. Not far distant in time or space or degree or circumstances.

    “Near neighbors.” “In the near future.” “They are near equals.”

    Synonyms: near, nigh · Similar: adjacent, nearby, warm, hot

  4. Rigorously attentive; strict and thorough.

    “Close supervision.” “Paid close attention.” “A close study.”

    Similar: careful

  5. Marked by fidelity to an original.

    “A close translation.” “A faithful copy of the portrait.” “A faithful rendering of the observed facts.”

    Synonyms: faithful · Similar: accurate

  6. (of a contest or contestants) evenly matched.

    “A close contest.” “A close election.” “A tight game.”

    Synonyms: tight · Similar: equal

  7. Crowded.

    “Close quarters.”

    Synonyms: confining · Similar: confined

  8. Lacking fresh air.

    “A dusty airless attic.” “The dreadfully close atmosphere.” “Hot and stuffy and the air was blue with smoke.”

    Synonyms: airless, stuffy, unaired · Similar: unventilated

  9. Of textiles.

    “A close weave.” “Smooth percale with a very tight weave.”

    Synonyms: tight · Similar: fine

  10. Strictly confined or guarded.

    “Kept under close custody.”

    Similar: restrained

  11. Confined to specific persons.

    “A close secret.”

    Similar: private

  12. Fitting closely but comfortably.

    “A close fit.”

    Synonyms: snug, close-fitting · Similar: tight

  13. Used of hair or haircuts.

    “A close military haircut.”

    Similar: short

  14. Giving or spending with reluctance.

    “Our cheeseparing administration.” “Very close (or near) with his money.” “A penny-pinching miserly old man.”

    Synonyms: cheeseparing, near, penny-pinching, skinny · Similar: stingy

Close as an adverb

  1. Near in time or place or relationship.

    “As the wedding day drew near.” “Stood near the door.” “Don't shoot until they come near.”

    Synonyms: near, nigh

  2. In an attentive manner.

    “He remained close on his guard.”

    Synonyms: closely, tight

Close as a noun

  1. The temporal end; the concluding time.

    “The stopping point of each round was signaled by a bell.” “The market was up at the finish.” “They were playing better at the close of the season.”

    Synonyms: stopping point, finale, finis, finish, last, conclusion · A kind of: end

  2. The last section of a communication.

    “In conclusion I want to say...”

    Synonyms: conclusion, end, closing, ending · A kind of: section · Kinds: anticlimax, epilogue, epilogue, peroration, finale

  3. The concluding part of any performance.

    Synonyms: finale, closing curtain, finis · A kind of: finish

Phrases and compounds with "close"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • close in (verb) Advance or converge on.
  • close to (adverb) (of quantities) imprecise but fairly close to correct.
  • close up (adverb) Very close.
  • close off (verb) Stem the flow of.
  • close out (verb) Make impossible, especially beforehand.
  • close set (adjective) Set close together.
  • close-set (adjective) Set close together.
  • close call (noun) Something achieved (or escaped) by a narrow margin.
  • close down (verb) Cease to operate or cause to cease operating.
  • close-knit (adjective) Held together as by social or cultural ties.
  • come close (verb) Nearly do something.
  • draw close (verb) Move or arrange oneself in a comfortable and cozy position.
  • hold close (verb) Hold firmly, usually with one's hands.
  • close order (noun) A military formation for drill or marching.
  • close shave (noun) Something achieved (or escaped) by a narrow margin.
  • close-hauled (adjective) Having the sails trimmed for sailing as close to the wind as possible.
