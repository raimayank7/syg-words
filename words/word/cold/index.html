Cold: meaning, synonyms and examples

Cold is used as an adjective (11 senses) and noun (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: Having a low or inadequate temperature or feeling a sensation of coldness or having been made cold by e.g. ice or refrigeration.

In Hindi

Synonyms

Opposites
hot
Word family
coldness

Cold as an adjective

  1. Having a low or inadequate temperature or feeling a sensation of coldness or having been made cold by e.g. ice or refrigeration.

    “A cold climate.” “A cold room.” “Dinner has gotten cold.”

    Opposite: hot · Similar: acold, algid, arctic, bleak, chilly, crisp

  2. Extended meanings; especially of psychological coldness; without human warmth or emotion.

    “A cold unfriendly nod.” “A cold and unaffectionate person.” “A cold impersonal manner.”

    Opposite: hot · Similar: emotionless, frigid

  3. Having lost freshness through passage of time.

    “A cold trail.” “Dogs attempting to catch a cold scent.”

    Similar: stale

  4. (color) giving no sensation of warmth.

    “A cold bluish grey.”

    Similar: cool

  5. Marked by errorless familiarity.

    “Had her lines cold before rehearsals started.”

    Similar: perfect

  6. Lacking originality or spontaneity; no longer new.

    “Moth-eaten theories about race.” “Stale news.”

    Synonyms: stale, dusty, moth-eaten · Similar: unoriginal

  7. So intense as to be almost uncontrollable.

    “Cold fury gripped him.”

    Similar: intense

  8. Without compunction or human feeling.

    “In cold blood.” “Insensate destruction.”

    Synonyms: cold-blooded, inhuman, insensate · Similar: inhumane

  9. Feeling or showing no enthusiasm.

    “A cold audience.” “A cold response to the new play.”

    Similar: unenthusiastic

  10. Of a seeker; far from the object sought.

    Similar: far

  11. Lacking the warmth of life.

    “Cold in his grave.”

    Similar: dead

Cold as a noun

  1. A mild viral infection involving the nose and respiratory passages (but not the lungs).

    “Will they never find a cure for the common cold?”

    Synonyms: common cold · A kind of: respiratory disease, communicable disease · Kinds: head cold

  2. The absence of heat.

    “The coldness made our breath visible.” “Come in out of the cold.” “Cold is a vasoconstrictor.”

    Synonyms: coldness, low temperature, frigidity, frigidness · A kind of: temperature · Kinds: chill, chilliness, frostiness, cool

  3. The sensation produced by low temperatures.

    “He shivered from the cold.” “The cold helped clear his head.”

    Synonyms: coldness · A kind of: temperature

Phrases and compounds with "cold"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • cold war (noun) A state of political hostility between countries using means short of armed warfare.
  • ice-cold (adjective) As cold as ice.
  • cold cash (noun) Money in the form of cash that is readily available.
  • cold cuts (noun) Sliced assorted cold meats.
  • cold feet (noun) Timidity that prevents the continuation of a course of action.
  • cold fish (noun) An aloof unemotional person.
  • cold snap (noun) A spell of cold weather.
  • cold sore (noun) Caused by herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1).
  • cold wave (noun) A wave of unusually cold weather.
  • cold work (verb) Shape (metal) without heat.
  • cold-eyed (adjective) Unaffected by strong emotion or prejudice.
  • head cold (noun) A common cold affecting the nasal passages and resulting in congestion and sneezing and headache.
  • catch cold (verb) Come down with a cold.
  • cold cream (noun) A cream used cosmetically (mostly by women) for softening and cleaning the skin.
  • cold frame (noun) Protective covering consisting of a wooden frame with a glass top in which small plants are protected from the cold.
  • cold front (noun) The front of an advancing mass of colder air.
