Count: meaning, synonyms and examples

Count is used as a verb (9 senses) and noun (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: Determine the number or amount of.

In Hindi
गिनना; गिनती
Synonyms
number, enumerate, numerate, matter, weigh, consider, depend, look, calculate, reckon
Word family
countable, counter, counting

Count as a verb

  1. Determine the number or amount of.

    “Can you count the books on your shelf?” “Count your change.”

    Synonyms: number, enumerate, numerate · A kind of: determine · Kinds: recount, miscount, census, total

  2. Have weight; have import, carry weight.

    “It does not matter much.”

    Synonyms: matter, weigh · A kind of: be · Kinds: weigh

  3. Show consideration for; take into account.

    “You must consider her age.” “The judge considered the offender's youth and was lenient.”

    Synonyms: consider, weigh

  4. Name or recite the numbers in ascending order.

    “The toddler could count to 100.”

    A kind of: recite

  5. Put into a group.

    “The academy counts several Nobel Prize winners among its members.”

    Synonyms: number · A kind of: classify

  6. Include as if by counting.

    “I can count my colleagues in the opposition.”

    A kind of: include

  7. Have a certain value or carry a certain weight.

    “Each answer counts as three points.”

    A kind of: be

  8. Have faith or confidence in.

    “You can count on me to help you any time.” “Look to your friends for support.” “Depend on your family in times of crisis.”

    Synonyms: depend, look, calculate, reckon · A kind of: trust

  9. Take account of.

    “You have to reckon with our opponents.” “Count on the monsoon.”

    Synonyms: reckon · A kind of: estimate

Count as a noun

  1. The total number counted.

    “A blood count.”

    A kind of: number · Kinds: complement, blood count, circulation, circulation, head count, pollen count, sperm count

  2. The act of counting; reciting numbers in ascending order.

    “The counting continued for several hours.”

    Synonyms: counting, numeration, enumeration, reckoning, tally · A kind of: investigation · Kinds: blood count, census, countdown, miscount, poll, recount, sperm count

  3. A nobleman (in various countries) having rank equal to a British earl.

    Kinds: count palatine, landgrave

Phrases and compounds with "count"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • count on (verb) Judge to be probable.
  • no-count (adjective) Without merit.
  • count off (verb) Call in turn from right to left or from back to front numbers that determine some position or function.
  • count out (verb) Declare the loser.
  • count noun (noun) A noun that forms plurals.
  • head count (noun) Number of people in a particular group.
  • nose count (noun) A periodic count of the population.
  • blood count (noun) The number of red and white corpuscles in a blood sample.
  • count fleet (noun) Thoroughbred that won the triple crown in 1943.
  • sperm count (noun) The number of sperm in an ejaculate.
  • pollen count (noun) The number of pollen grains (usually ragweed) in a standard volume of air over a twenty-four hour period and a specified time and place.
  • count rumford (noun) English physicist (born in America) who studied heat and friction; experiments convinced him that heat is caused by moving particles (1753-1814).
  • count palatine (noun) A count who exercised royal authority in his own domain.
  • take the count (verb) Be counted out; remain down while the referee counts to ten.
  • count per minute (noun) Frequency per minute.
  • complete blood count (noun) Counting the number of white and red blood cells and the number of platelets in 1 cubic millimeter of blood.
