Crack: meaning, synonyms and examples

Crack is used as a verb (13 senses), noun (9 senses) and adjective (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: Become fractured; break or crack on the surface only.

In Hindi
दरार; चटकना
Synonyms
check, break, snap, break through, crack up, crock up, break up, collapse, cleft, crevice
Word family
cracker, cracking

Crack as a verb

  1. Become fractured; break or crack on the surface only.

    “The glass cracked when it was heated.”

    Synonyms: check, break · A kind of: change · Kinds: crack

  2. Make a very sharp explosive sound.

    “His gun cracked.”

    A kind of: sound

  3. Make a sharp sound.

    “His fingers snapped.”

    Synonyms: snap · A kind of: sound

  4. Hit forcefully; deal a hard blow, making a cracking noise.

    “The teacher cracked him across the face with a ruler.”

    A kind of: hit

  5. Pass through (a barrier).

    “Registrations cracked through the 30,000 mark in the county.”

    Synonyms: break through · A kind of: pass

  6. Break partially but keep its integrity.

    “The glass cracked.”

    A kind of: crack · Kinds: chap, craze, alligator

  7. Break suddenly and abruptly, as under tension.

    “The pipe snapped.”

    Synonyms: snap · A kind of: break

  8. Gain unauthorized access computers with malicious intentions.

    “She cracked my password.” “Crack a safe.”

    A kind of: break in

  9. Suffer a nervous breakdown.

    Synonyms: crack up, crock up, break up, collapse · A kind of: suffer

  10. Tell spontaneously.

    “Crack a joke.”

    A kind of: tell

  11. Cause to become cracked.

    “Heat and light cracked the back of the leather chair.”

    A kind of: change · Kinds: check, fissure

  12. Reduce (petroleum) to a simpler compound by cracking.

    A kind of: decompose

  13. Break into simpler molecules by means of heat.

    “The petroleum cracked.”

    A kind of: change

Crack as a noun

  1. A long narrow opening.

    Synonyms: cleft, crevice, fissure, scissure · A kind of: opening · Kinds: chap, chink, crevasse, fatigue crack, fault, rift, slit, split

  2. A narrow opening.

    “He opened the window a crack.”

    Synonyms: gap · A kind of: opening · Kinds: lacuna, spark gap

  3. A long narrow depression in a surface.

    Synonyms: crevice, cranny, fissure, chap · A kind of: depression

  4. A sudden sharp noise.

    “The crack of a whip.” “He heard the cracking of the ice.” “He can hear the snap of a twig.”

    Synonyms: cracking, snap · A kind of: noise

  5. A chance to do something.

    “He wanted a shot at the champion.”

    Synonyms: shot · A kind of: opportunity

  6. Witty remark.

    Synonyms: wisecrack, sally, quip · A kind of: remark

  7. A blemish resulting from a break without complete separation of the parts.

    “There was a crack in the mirror.”

    A kind of: blemish · Kinds: craze

  8. A usually brief attempt.

    “He took a crack at it.” “I gave it a whirl.”

    Synonyms: fling, go, pass, whirl, offer · A kind of: attempt

  9. The act of cracking something.

    Synonyms: fracture, cracking · A kind of: breakage

Crack as an adjective

  1. Of the highest quality.

    “An ace reporter.” “A crack shot.” “A first-rate golfer.”

    Synonyms: ace, first-rate, super, tiptop, topnotch, top-notch, tops · Similar: superior

Phrases and compounds with "crack"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • crack up (verb) Suffer a nervous breakdown.
  • crack-up (noun) A mental or physical breakdown.
  • toe crack (noun) A crack on the forepart of a horse's hindfoot.
  • crack down (verb) Repress or suppress (something regarded as undesirable).
  • crack shot (noun) Someone skilled in shooting.
  • sand crack (noun) A fissure in the wall of a horse's hoof often causing lameness.
  • crack willow (noun) Large willow tree with stiff branches that are easily broken.
  • crack of doom (noun) (New Testament) day at the end of time following Armageddon when God will decree the fates of all individual humans according to the good and evil of their earthly lives.
  • fatigue crack (noun) A crack in metal resulting from metal fatigue.
  • quarter crack (noun) A crack on the inside of a horse's forefoot.
