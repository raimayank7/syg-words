Dark: meaning, synonyms and examples

Dark is used as an adjective (11 senses) and noun (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: Devoid of or deficient in light or brightness; shadowed or black.

In Hindi
अँधेरा; गहरा (रंग)
Synonyms
black, sinister, dour, glowering, glum, moody, morose, saturnine, sour, sullen
Opposites
light
Word family
darkness

Dark as an adjective

  1. Devoid of or deficient in light or brightness; shadowed or black.

    “Sitting in a dark corner.” “A dark day.” “Dark shadows.”

    Opposite: light · Similar: aphotic, black, caliginous, crepuscular, darkened, darkening

  2. (used of color) having a dark hue.

    “Dark green.” “Dark glasses.”

    Opposite: light · Similar: darkish

  3. Brunet (used of hair or skin or eyes).

    “Dark eyes.”

    Similar: brunet

  4. Stemming from evil characteristics or forces; wicked or dishonorable.

    “Black deeds.” “A black lie.” “His black heart has concocted yet another black deed.”

    Synonyms: black, sinister · Similar: evil

  5. Secret.

    “Keep it dark.”

    Similar: concealed

  6. Showing a brooding ill humor.

    “A dark scowl.” “The proverbially dour New England Puritan.” “A glum, hopeless shrug.”

    Synonyms: dour, glowering, glum, moody, morose, saturnine, sour, sullen · Similar: ill-natured

  7. Lacking enlightenment or knowledge or culture.

    “This benighted country.” “Benighted ages of barbarism and superstition.” “The dark ages.”

    Synonyms: benighted · Similar: unenlightened

  8. Marked by difficulty of style or expression.

    “Much that was dark is now quite clear to me.” “Those who do not appreciate Kafka's work say his style is obscure.”

    Synonyms: obscure · Similar: incomprehensible

  9. Causing dejection.

    “A blue day.” “The dark days of the war.” “A week of rainy depressing weather.”

    Synonyms: blue, dingy, disconsolate, dismal, gloomy, grim, sorry, drab · Similar: depressing

  10. Having skin rich in melanin pigments.

    “National Association for the Advancement of Colored People.” “Dark-skinned peoples.”

    Synonyms: colored, coloured, dark-skinned, non-white · Similar: black

  11. Not giving performances; closed.

    “The theater is dark on Mondays.”

    Similar: inactive

Dark as a noun

  1. Absence of light or illumination.

    Synonyms: darkness · Opposite: light · A kind of: illumination · Kinds: night, total darkness, blackout, semidarkness

  2. Absence of moral or spiritual values.

    “The powers of darkness.”

    Synonyms: iniquity, wickedness, darkness · A kind of: condition · Kinds: foulness

  3. An unilluminated area.

    “He moved off into the darkness.”

    Synonyms: darkness, shadow · A kind of: scene

  4. The time after sunset and before sunrise while it is dark outside.

    Synonyms: night, nighttime · A kind of: time period · Kinds: weeknight, wedding night

  5. An unenlightened state.

    “He was in the dark concerning their intentions.” “His lectures dispelled the darkness.”

    Synonyms: darkness · A kind of: unenlightenment

Phrases and compounds with "dark"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • dark red (noun) A red color that reflects little light.
  • dark ages (noun) The period of history between classical antiquity and the Italian Renaissance.
  • dark blue (noun) A dark shade of blue.
  • dark meat (noun) The flesh of the legs of fowl used as food.
  • dark-blue (adjective) Of a dark shade of blue.
  • dark-gray (adjective) Of a dark shade of grey.
  • dark-grey (adjective) Of a dark shade of grey.
  • dark bread (noun) Bread made with whole wheat flour.
  • dark horse (noun) A political candidate who is not well known but could win unexpectedly.
  • dark-brown (adjective) Of a color similar to that of wood or earth.
  • dark-green (adjective) Of the color between blue and yellow in the color spectrum; similar to the color of fresh grass.
  • pitch-dark (adjective) Extremely dark.
  • dark comedy (noun) A comedy characterized by grim or satiric humor; a comedy having gloomy or disturbing elements.
  • dark matter (noun) (cosmology) a hypothetical form of matter that is believed to make up 90 percent of the universe; it is invisible (does not absorb or emit light) and does not collide with atomic particles but exerts gravitational force.
  • dark-coated (adjective) Covered with dark hair.
  • dark-haired (adjective) Having hair of a dark color.
