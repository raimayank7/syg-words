Deep: meaning, synonyms and examples

Deep is used as an adjective (14 senses), adverb (3 senses) and noun (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: Relatively deep or strong; affecting one deeply.

In Hindi
गहरा
Synonyms
bass, rich, thick, cryptic, cryptical, inscrutable, mysterious, mystifying, abstruse, recondite
Opposites
shallow
Word family
deepness

Deep as an adjective

  1. Relatively deep or strong; affecting one deeply.

    “A deep breath.” “A deep sigh.” “Deep concentration.”

    Opposite: shallow · Similar: heavy, profound

  2. Marked by depth of thinking.

    “Deep thoughts.” “A deep allegory.”

    Similar: profound

  3. Having great spatial extension or penetration downward or inward from an outer surface or backward or laterally or outward from a center; sometimes used in combination.

    “A deep well.” “A deep dive.” “Deep water.”

    Opposite: shallow · Similar: abysmal, bottomless, deep-water, profound, walk-in

  4. Very distant in time or space.

    “Deep in the past.” “Deep in enemy territory.” “Deep in the woods.”

    Similar: distant

  5. Extreme.

    “In deep trouble.” “Deep happiness.”

    Similar: intense

  6. Having or denoting a low vocal or instrumental range.

    “A deep voice.” “A bass voice is lower than a baritone voice.” “A bass clarinet.”

    Synonyms: bass · Similar: low

  7. Strong; intense.

    “Deep purple.” “A rich red.”

    Synonyms: rich · Similar: colorful

  8. Relatively thick from top to bottom.

    “Deep carpets.” “Deep snow.”

    Similar: thick

  9. Extending relatively far inward.

    “A deep border.”

    Similar: wide

  10. (of darkness) very intense.

    “Thick night.” “Thick darkness.” “A face in deep shadow.”

    Synonyms: thick · Similar: intense

  11. Large in quantity or size.

    “Deep cuts in the budget.”

    Similar: large

  12. With head or back bent low.

    “A deep bow.”

    Similar: low

  13. Of an obscure nature.

    “The new insurance policy is written without cryptic or mysterious terms.” “A deep dark secret.” “The inscrutable workings of Providence.”

    Synonyms: cryptic, cryptical, inscrutable, mysterious, mystifying · Similar: inexplicable

  14. Difficult to penetrate; incomprehensible to one of ordinary understanding or knowledge.

    “The professor's lectures were so abstruse that students tended to avoid them.” “A deep metaphysical theory.” “Some recondite problem in historiography.”

    Synonyms: abstruse, recondite · Similar: esoteric

Deep as an adverb

  1. To a great depth;far down.

    “Dived deeply.” “Dug deep.”

    Synonyms: deeply

  2. To an advanced time.

    “Deep into the night.” “Talked late into the evening.”

    Synonyms: late

  3. To a great distance.

    “Penetrated deep into enemy territory.” “Went deep into the woods.”

Deep as a noun

  1. The central and most intense or profound part.

    “In the deep of night.” “In the deep of winter.”

    A kind of: middle

  2. A long steep-sided depression in the ocean floor.

    Synonyms: trench, oceanic abyss · A kind of: natural depression

  3. Literary term for an ocean.

    “Denizens of the deep.”

    A kind of: ocean

Phrases and compounds with "deep"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • go deep (verb) Extend in importance or range.
  • deep red (noun) A deep and vivid red color.
  • deep-dye (verb) Dye thoroughly.
  • deep-fry (verb) Cook by immersing in fat.
  • deep-sea (adjective) Of or taking place in the deeper parts of the sea.
  • deep-set (adjective) Having a sunken area.
  • deep-six (verb) Toss out; get rid of.
  • deep down (adverb) In reality.
  • deep kiss (noun) An openmouthed kiss in which your tongue is inserted into the other's mouth.
  • deep-eyed (adjective) Characteristic of the bony face of a cadaver.
  • deep-laid (adjective) Secretly and carefully planned.
  • deep-pink (adjective) Of a deep shade of pink.
  • knee-deep (adjective) Coming only to the ankle or knee.
  • neck-deep (adjective) Deeply involved.
  • skin-deep (adjective) Penetrating no deeper than the skin: "her beauty is only skin-deep".
  • ankle-deep (adjective) Coming only to the ankle or knee.
