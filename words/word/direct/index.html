Direct: meaning, synonyms and examples

Direct is used as a verb (13 senses), adjective (10 senses) and adverb (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: Command with authority.

In Hindi
सीधा; निर्देश देना
Synonyms
target, aim, place, point, lead, take, conduct, guide, send, train
Opposites
indirect, retrograde, inverse, alternating
Word family
directive, director, directness

Direct as a verb

  1. Command with authority.

    “He directed the children to do their homework.”

    A kind of: order · Kinds: stet

  2. Intend (something) to move towards a certain goal.

    “He aimed his fists towards his opponent's face.” “Criticism directed at her superior.” “Direct your anger towards others, not towards yourself.”

    Synonyms: target, aim, place, point · A kind of: aim · Kinds: address, range in

  3. Guide the actors in (plays and films).

    A kind of: make · Kinds: stage direct

  4. Be in charge of.

    A kind of: manage · Kinds: hold, guide, head, operate, administer

  5. Take somebody somewhere.

    “We lead him to our chief.” “Can you take me to the main entrance?” “He conducted us to the palace.”

    Synonyms: lead, take, conduct, guide · Kinds: beacon, hand, mislead, usher

  6. Cause to go somewhere.

    “The explosion sent the car flying in the air.” “She sent her children to camp.” “He directed all his energies into his dissertation.”

    Synonyms: send · A kind of: move · Kinds: project, turn, turn, divert, route, refer, redirect, blow

  7. Point or cause to go (blows, weapons, or objects such as photographic equipment) towards.

    “Please don't aim at your little brother!” “He trained his gun on the burglar.” “Don't train your camera on the women.”

    Synonyms: aim, take, train, take aim · A kind of: position · Kinds: target, draw a bead on, hold, turn, swing, charge, level, sight

  8. Lead, as in the performance of a composition.

    “Conduct an orchestra; Barenboim conducted the Chicago symphony for years.”

    Synonyms: conduct, lead · A kind of: perform

  9. Give directions to; point somebody into a certain direction.

    “I directed them towards the town hall.”

    A kind of: instruct · Kinds: talk down, point the way

  10. Specifically design a product, event, or activity for a certain public.

    Synonyms: calculate, aim · A kind of: intend

  11. Direct the course; determine the direction of travelling.

    Synonyms: steer, maneuver, manoeuver, manoeuvre, point, head, guide, channelize · A kind of: control · Kinds: dock, sheer, pull over, helm, crab, navigate, stand out, starboard

  12. Put an address on (an envelope).

    Synonyms: address · A kind of: label · Kinds: misdirect, instrument, re-address

  13. Plan and direct (a complex undertaking).

    “He masterminded the robbery.”

    Synonyms: mastermind, engineer, organize, organise, orchestrate · A kind of: plan · Kinds: choreograph

Direct as an adjective

  1. Direct in spatial dimensions; proceeding without deviation or interruption; straight and short.

    “A direct route.” “A direct flight.” “A direct hit.”

    Opposite: indirect · Similar: door-to-door, nonstop, point-blank, straightforward, undeviating, through

  2. Having no intervening persons, agents, conditions.

    “In direct sunlight.” “In direct contact with the voters.” “Direct exposure to the disease.”

    Synonyms: unmediated · Similar: immediate

  3. Straightforward in means or manner or behavior or language or action.

    “A direct question.” “A direct response.” “A direct approach.”

    Opposite: indirect · Similar: bluff, blunt, brutal, flat-footed, man-to-man, no-nonsense

  4. In a straight unbroken line of descent from parent to child.

    “Lineal ancestors.” “Lineal heirs.” “A direct descendant of the king.”

    Synonyms: lineal · Similar: matrilineal, patrilineal, unilateral

  5. Moving from west to east on the celestial sphere; or--for planets--around the sun in the same direction as the Earth.

    Opposite: retrograde

  6. Similar in nature or effect or relation to another quantity.

    “A term is in direct proportion to another term if it increases (or decreases) as the other increases (or decreases).”

    Opposite: inverse

  7. (of a current) flowing in one direction only.

    “Direct current.”

    Opposite: alternating

  8. Being an immediate result or consequence.

    “A direct result of the accident.”

    Similar: primary

  9. In precisely the same words used by a writer or speaker.

    “A direct quotation.” “Repeated their dialog verbatim.”

    Synonyms: verbatim · Similar: exact

  10. Lacking compromising or mitigating elements; exact.

    “The direct opposite.”

    Similar: absolute

Direct as an adverb

  1. Without deviation.

    “The path leads directly to the lake.” “Went direct to the office.”

    Synonyms: directly, straight

Phrases and compounds with "direct"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • direct dye (noun) Dye with a high affinity for cellulose fibers (cotton or rayon etc.).
  • direct sum (noun) A union of two disjoint sets in which every element is the sum of an element from each of the disjoint sets.
  • direct tax (noun) A tax paid directly by the person or organization on whom it is levied.
  • direct fire (noun) Fire delivered on a target that is visible to the person aiming it.
  • direct loan (noun) A loan by a lender to a customer without the use of a third party; direct lending gives the lender greater discretion in making loans.
  • direct mail (noun) Advertising sent directly to prospective customers via the mail.
  • direct tide (noun) The occurrence of high tide on one side of the earth coinciding with high tide on the opposite side.
  • direct trust (noun) A trust created by the free and deliberate act of the parties involved (usually on the basis of written documentation).
  • stage direct (verb) Direct for the stage.
  • direct action (noun) A protest action by labor or minority groups to obtain their demands.
  • direct flight (noun) A flight with one or more intermediate stops but no change of aircraft.
  • direct mailer (noun) A distributor who uses direct mail to sell merchandise.
  • direct object (noun) The object that receives the direct action of the verb.
  • direct antonym (noun) Antonyms that are commonly associated (e.g., `wet' and `dry').
  • direct current (noun) An electric current that flows in one direction steadily.
  • direct primary (noun) A primary where voters directly select the candidates who will run for office.
