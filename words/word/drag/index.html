Drag: meaning, synonyms and examples

Drag is used as a verb (11 senses) and noun (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: Pull, as against a resistance.

In Hindi
घसीटना
Synonyms
haul, hale, cart, embroil, tangle, sweep, sweep up, drag in, trail, get behind
Word family
dragger

Drag as a verb

  1. Pull, as against a resistance.

    “He dragged the big suitcase behind him.” “These worries were dragging at him.”

    A kind of: pull · Kinds: shlep, trail

  2. Draw slowly or heavily.

    “Haul stones.” “Haul nets.”

    Synonyms: haul, hale, cart · A kind of: pull · Kinds: bowse

  3. Force into some kind of situation, condition, or course of action.

    “They were swept up by the events.” “Don't drag me into this business.”

    Synonyms: embroil, tangle, sweep, sweep up, drag in · A kind of: involve

  4. Move slowly and as if with great effort.

    A kind of: travel

  5. To lag or linger behind.

    “But in so many other areas we still are dragging.”

    Synonyms: trail, get behind, hang back, drop behind, drop back · A kind of: lag

  6. Suck in or take (air).

    “Draw a deep breath.” “Draw on a cigarette.”

    Synonyms: puff, draw · A kind of: inhale

  7. Use a computer mouse to move icons on the screen and select commands from a menu.

    “Drag this icon to the lower right hand corner of the screen.”

    A kind of: move

  8. Walk without lifting the feet.

    Synonyms: scuff · A kind of: shuffle

  9. Search (as the bottom of a body of water) for something valuable or lost.

    Synonyms: dredge · A kind of: search

  10. Persuade to come away from something attractive or interesting.

    “He dragged me away from the television set.”

    A kind of: persuade

  11. Proceed for an extended period of time.

    “The speech dragged on for two hours.”

    Synonyms: drag on, drag out · A kind of: proceed

Drag as a noun

  1. The phenomenon of resistance to motion through a fluid.

    Synonyms: retarding force · A kind of: resistance · Kinds: sonic barrier, windage

  2. Something that slows or delays progress.

    “Taxation is a drag on the economy.” “Too many laws are a drag on the use of new land.”

    A kind of: hindrance

  3. Something tedious and boring.

    “Peeling potatoes is a drag.”

    A kind of: tediousness

  4. A slow inhalation (as of tobacco smoke).

    “He took a puff on his pipe.” “He took a drag on his cigarette and expelled the smoke slowly.”

    Synonyms: puff, pull · A kind of: inhalation

  5. The act of dragging (pulling with force).

    “The drag up the hill exhausted him.”

    A kind of: pull

Phrases and compounds with "drag"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • drag in (verb) Force into some kind of situation, condition, or course of action.
  • drag on (verb) Last unnecessarily long.
  • drag up (verb) Mention something unpleasant from the past.
  • drag out (verb) Last unnecessarily long.
  • drag down (verb) Exert a force with a heavy weight.
  • main drag (noun) The main street of a town or city.
  • drag a bunt (verb) Hit a ball in such a way so as to make it go a short distance.
  • drag one's feet (verb) Postpone doing what one should be doing.
  • drag coefficient (noun) The ratio of the drag on a body moving through air to the product of the velocity and the surface area of the body.
  • drag one's heels (verb) Postpone doing what one should be doing.
  • coefficient of drag (noun) The ratio of the drag on a body moving through air to the product of the velocity and the surface area of the body.
  • drag through the mud (verb) Speak unfavorably about.
  • knock-down-and-drag-out (adjective) Extremely violent.
