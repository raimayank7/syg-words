Effect: meaning, synonyms and examples

Effect is used as a noun (5 senses) and verb (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: A phenomenon that follows and is caused by some previous phenomenon.

In Hindi
असर, प्रभाव
Synonyms
consequence, outcome, result, event, issue, upshot, impression, essence, burden, core
Word family
effectuate, effective, effecter, effector

Effect as a noun

  1. A phenomenon that follows and is caused by some previous phenomenon.

    “The magnetic effect was greater when the rod was lengthwise.” “His decision had depressing consequences for business.” “He acted very wise after the event.”

    Synonyms: consequence, outcome, result, event, issue, upshot · A kind of: phenomenon · Kinds: offspring, aftereffect, aftermath, bandwagon effect, brisance, butterfly effect, by-product, change

  2. An outward appearance.

    “He made a good impression.” “I wanted to create an impression of success.” “She retained that bold effect in her reproductions of the original painting.”

    Synonyms: impression · A kind of: appearance · Kinds: figure, image, mark, tout ensemble

  3. An impression (especially one that is artificial or contrived).

    “He just did it for effect.”

    A kind of: impression · Kinds: sound effect, special effect

  4. The central meaning or theme of a speech or literary work.

    Synonyms: essence, burden, core, gist · A kind of: meaning

  5. (of a law) having legal validity.

    “The law is still in effect.”

    Synonyms: force · A kind of: validity

Effect as a verb

  1. Produce.

    “The scientists set up a shock wave.”

    Synonyms: effectuate, set up · A kind of: cause · Kinds: carry through, draw, precipitate, induce, serve

  2. Act so as to bring into existence.

    “Effect a change.”

    A kind of: act · Kinds: bring to bear, carry, backdate

Phrases and compounds with "effect"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • in effect (adverb) In actuality or reality or fact.
  • skin effect (noun) The tendency of high-frequency alternating current to distribute near the surface of a conductor.
  • take effect (verb) Go into effect or become effective or operative.
  • piezo effect (noun) Electricity produced by mechanical pressure on certain crystals (notably quartz or Rochelle salt); alternatively, electrostatic stress produces a change in the linear dimensions of the crystal.
  • sound effect (noun) An effect that imitates a sound called for in the script of a play.
  • stage effect (noun) A special effect created on the stage.
  • domino effect (noun) The consequence of one event setting off a chain of similar events (like a falling domino causing a whole row of upended dominos to fall).
  • ground effect (noun) Apparent increase in aerodynamic lift experienced by an aircraft flying close to the ground.
  • law of effect (noun) (psychology) the principle that behaviors are selected by their consequences; behavior having good consequences tends to be repeated whereas behavior that leads to bad consequences is not repeated.
  • doppler effect (noun) Change in the apparent frequency of a wave as observer and source move toward or away from each other.
  • special effect (noun) An effect used to produce scenes that cannot be achieved by normal techniques (especially on film).
  • to that effect (adverb) With that general meaning.
  • tyndall effect (noun) The phenomenon in which light is scattered by very small particles in its path; it makes a beam of light visible; the scattered light is mainly blue.
  • coriolis effect (noun) (physics) an effect whereby a body moving in a rotating frame of reference experiences the Coriolis force acting perpendicular to the direction of motion and to the axis of rotation; on Earth the Coriolis effect deflects moving bodies to the right in the northern hemisphere and to the left in the southern hemisphere.
  • knock-on effect (noun) A secondary or incidental effect.
  • position effect (noun) (genetics) the effect on the expression of a gene that is produced by changing its location in a chromosome.
