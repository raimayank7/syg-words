Escape: meaning, synonyms and examples

Escape is used as a verb (7 senses) and noun (8 senses). Its most common meaning is: Run away from confinement.

In Hindi
बच निकलना, भागना
Synonyms
get away, break loose, miss, get off, get by, get out, elude, scat, run, scarper
Word family
escapee, escapist

Escape as a verb

  1. Run away from confinement.

    Synonyms: get away, break loose · A kind of: flee · Kinds: break, shake, elude, slip, run away, escape

  2. Fail to experience.

    “Fortunately, I missed the hurricane.”

    Synonyms: miss · A kind of: avoid

  3. Escape potentially unpleasant consequences; get away with a forbidden action.

    “I couldn't get out from under these responsibilities.”

    Synonyms: get off, get away, get by, get out · A kind of: avoid · Kinds: evade

  4. Be incomprehensible to; escape understanding by.

    “What you are seeing in him eludes me.”

    Synonyms: elude · A kind of: perplex · Kinds: defy

  5. Remove oneself from a familiar environment, usually for pleasure or diversion.

    “We escaped to our summer house for a few days.” “The president of the company never manages to get away during the summer.”

    Synonyms: get away · A kind of: escape

  6. Flee; take to one's heels; cut and run.

    “If you see this man, run!” “The burglars escaped before the police showed up.”

    Synonyms: scat, run, scarper, turn tail, lam, run away, hightail it, bunk · A kind of: leave · Kinds: flee, skedaddle

  7. Issue or leak, as from a small opening.

    “Gas escaped into the bedroom.”

    A kind of: issue

Escape as a noun

  1. The act of escaping physically.

    “He made his escape from the mental hospital.” “The canary escaped from its cage.” “His flight was an indication of his guilt.”

    Synonyms: flight · A kind of: running away · Kinds: evasion, break, getaway, exodus, skedaddle

  2. An inclination to retreat from unpleasant realities through diversion or fantasy.

    “Romantic novels were her escape from the stress of daily life.”

    Synonyms: escapism · A kind of: diversion

  3. Nonperformance of something distasteful (as by deceit or trickery) that you are supposed to do.

    “His evasion of his clear duty was reprehensible.” “That escape from the consequences is possible but unattractive.”

    Synonyms: evasion, dodging · A kind of: negligence · Kinds: escape mechanism, malingering, shirking, circumvention

  4. An avoidance of danger or difficulty.

    “That was a narrow escape.”

    A kind of: avoidance

  5. A means or way of escaping.

    “Hard work was his escape from worry.” “They installed a second hatch as an escape.” “Their escape route.”

    A kind of: means

  6. A plant originally cultivated but now growing wild.

    A kind of: plant

  7. The discharge of a fluid from some container.

    “They tried to stop the escape of gas from the damaged pipe.” “He had to clean up the leak.”

    Synonyms: leak, leakage, outflow · A kind of: discharge

  8. A valve in a container in which pressure can build up (as a steam boiler); it opens automatically when the pressure reaches a dangerous level.

    Synonyms: safety valve, relief valve, escape valve, escape cock · A kind of: valve, regulator

Phrases and compounds with "escape"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • escape cock (noun) A valve in a container in which pressure can build up (as a steam boiler); it opens automatically when the pressure reaches a dangerous level.
  • escape from (verb) Get rid of.
  • fire escape (noun) A stairway (often on the outside of a building) that permits exit in the case of fire or other emergency.
  • escape hatch (noun) Hatchway that provides a means of escape in an emergency.
  • escape valve (noun) A valve in a container in which pressure can build up (as a steam boiler); it opens automatically when the pressure reaches a dangerous level.
  • escape wheel (noun) Gear that engages a rocking lever.
  • escape expert (noun) An entertainer who is expert in the art of escaping.
  • narrow escape (noun) Something achieved (or escaped) by a narrow margin.
  • escape velocity (noun) The minimum velocity needed to escape a gravitational field.
  • escape mechanism (noun) A form of behavior that evades unpleasant realities.
