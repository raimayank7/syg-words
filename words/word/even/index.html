Even: meaning, synonyms and examples

Even is used as an adverb (4 senses), adjective (6 senses), noun (1 sense) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: Used as an intensive especially to indicate something unexpected.

In Hindi
भी; समतल, बराबर
Synonyms
yet, still, fifty-fifty, regular, tied, level, evening, eve, eventide, flush
Opposites
odd, uneven
Word family
evenness

Even as an adverb

  1. Used as an intensive especially to indicate something unexpected.

    “Even an idiot knows that.” “Declined even to consider the idea.” “I don't have even a dollar!”

  2. In spite of; notwithstanding.

    “Even when he is sick, he works.” “Even with his head start she caught up with him.”

  3. To a greater degree or extent; used with comparisons.

    “Looked sick and felt even worse.” “An even (or still) more interesting problem.” “Still another problem must be solved.”

    Synonyms: yet, still

  4. To the full extent.

    “Loyal even unto death.”

Even as an adjective

  1. Divisible by two.

    Opposite: odd

  2. Equal in degree or extent or amount; or equally matched or balanced.

    “Even amounts of butter and sugar.” “On even terms.” “It was a fifty-fifty (or even) split.”

    Synonyms: fifty-fifty · Similar: equal

  3. Being level or straight or regular and without variation as e.g. in shape or texture; or being in the same plane or at the same height as something else (i.e. even with).

    “An even application of varnish.” “An even floor.” “The road was not very even.”

    Opposite: uneven · Similar: flat, flatbottom, flush, justified, lap-jointed, straight-grained

  4. Symmetrically arranged.

    “Even features.” “Regular features.” “A regular polygon.”

    Synonyms: regular · Similar: symmetrical

  5. Occurring at fixed intervals.

    “A regular beat.” “The even rhythm of his breathing.”

    Synonyms: regular · Similar: steady

  6. Of the score in a contest.

    “The score is tied.”

    Synonyms: tied, level · Similar: equal

Even as a noun

  1. The latter part of the day (the period of decreasing daylight from late afternoon until nightfall).

    “He enjoyed the evening light across the lake.”

    Synonyms: evening, eve, eventide · A kind of: day · Kinds: guest night

Even as a verb

  1. Make level or straight.

    “Level the ground.”

    Synonyms: flush, level, even out · A kind of: change surface · Kinds: grade, strickle, strickle

  2. Become even or more even.

    “Even out the surface.”

    Synonyms: even out · A kind of: change

  3. Make even or more even.

    Synonyms: even out · A kind of: regularize

Phrases and compounds with "even"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • even as (adverb) At the same time as.
  • even so (adverb) Despite anything to the contrary (usually following a concession).
  • even up (verb) Adjust for.
  • even off (verb) Adjust for.
  • even out (verb) Adjust for.
  • get even (verb) Compensate; make the score equal.
  • even-toed (adjective) Of or relating to or belonging to mammals of the order Artiodactyla.
  • break even (verb) Make neither profit nor loss.
  • even chance (noun) An unpredictable phenomenon.
  • even spacing (noun) Regularity of spacing.
  • even-pinnate (adjective) (of a leaf shape) pinnate with a pair of leaflets at the apex.
  • getting even (noun) A reciprocal group action.
  • even a little (adverb) To any extent at all.
  • even-tempered (adjective) Not easily irritated.
  • even-textured (adjective) Having an even texture.
  • odd-even check (noun) A system of checking for errors in computer functioning.
