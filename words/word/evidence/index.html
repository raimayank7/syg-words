Evidence: meaning, synonyms and examples

Evidence is used as a noun (3 senses) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: Your basis for belief or disbelief; knowledge on which to base belief.

In Hindi
सबूत, प्रमाण
Synonyms
grounds, attest, certify, manifest, demonstrate, testify, bear witness, prove, show, tell
Word family
evidential, evidentiary, evident

Evidence as a noun

  1. Your basis for belief or disbelief; knowledge on which to base belief.

    “The evidence that smoking causes lung cancer is very compelling.”

    Synonyms: grounds · A kind of: information · Kinds: probable cause, proof, disproof, lead, symptom, sign

  2. An indication that makes something evident.

    “His trembling was evidence of his fear.”

    A kind of: indication · Kinds: smoking gun, clue, footprint evidence, record, testimony, argument, attestation, identification

  3. (law) all the means by which any alleged matter of fact whose truth is investigated at judicial trial is established or disproved.

    A kind of: information · Kinds: exhibit, testimony, corpus delicti, direct evidence, circumstantial evidence, corroborating evidence, hearsay evidence, state's evidence

Evidence as a verb

  1. Provide evidence for; stand as proof of; show by one's behavior, attitude, or external attributes.

    “His high fever attested to his illness.” “The buildings in Rome manifest a high level of architectural sophistication.” “This decision demonstrates his sense of fairness.”

    Synonyms: attest, certify, manifest, demonstrate · A kind of: testify · Kinds: authenticate, reflect, reflect, notarize, condemn

  2. Provide evidence for.

    “The blood test showed that he was the father.” “Her behavior testified to her incompetence.”

    Synonyms: testify, bear witness, prove, show · A kind of: inform · Kinds: attest, presume, adduce

  3. Give evidence.

    “He was telling on all his former colleague.”

    Synonyms: tell · A kind of: inform

Phrases and compounds with "evidence"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • in evidence (adjective) Clearly to be seen.
  • cogent evidence (noun) Any factual evidence that helps to establish the truth of something.
  • direct evidence (noun) Evidence (usually the testimony of a witness) directly related to the fact in dispute.
  • hearsay evidence (noun) Evidence based on what someone has told the witness and not of direct knowledge.
  • rule of evidence (noun) (law) a rule of law whereby any alleged matter of fact that is submitted for investigation at a judicial trial is established or disproved.
  • state's evidence (noun) Evidence for the prosecution in criminal proceedings.
  • indirect evidence (noun) Evidence providing only a basis for inference about the fact in dispute.
  • best evidence rule (noun) A rule of evidence requiring that to prove the content of a writing or recording or photograph the original is required.
  • footprint evidence (noun) Evidence in the form of footprints.
  • parol evidence rule (noun) A rule that oral evidence cannot be used to contradict the terms of a written contract.
  • corroborating evidence (noun) Additional evidence or evidence of different kind that supports a proof already offered in a proceeding.
  • circumstantial evidence (noun) Evidence providing only a basis for inference about the fact in dispute.
