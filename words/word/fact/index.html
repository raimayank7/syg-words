Fact: meaning, synonyms and examples

Fact is used as a noun (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: A piece of information about circumstances that exist or events that have occurred.

In Hindi
तथ्य, सच्चाई

Fact as a noun

  1. A piece of information about circumstances that exist or events that have occurred.

    “First you must collect all the facts of the case.”

    A kind of: information · Kinds: case, detail, particular, general, matter of fact, observation, reason, score

  2. A statement or assertion of verified information about something that is the case or has happened.

    “He supported his argument with an impressive array of facts.”

    A kind of: information · Kinds: record, basics, index

  3. An event known to have happened or something known to have existed.

    “Your fears have no basis in fact.” “How much of the story is fact and how much fiction is hard to tell.”

    A kind of: reality

  4. A concept whose truth can be proved.

    “Scientific hypotheses are not facts.”

    A kind of: concept

Phrases and compounds with "fact"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • in fact (adverb) In reality or actuality.
  • fact mood (noun) A mood (grammatically unmarked) that represents the act or state as an objective fact.
  • fact-finding (adjective) Designed to find information or ascertain facts.
  • fraud in fact (noun) Actual deceit; concealing something or making a false representation with an evil intent to cause injury to another.
  • matter of fact (noun) A disputed factual contention that is generally left for a jury to decide.
  • matter-of-fact (adjective) Not fanciful or imaginative.
  • finding of fact (noun) (law) the findings of a jury on issues of fact submitted to it for decision; can be used in formulating a judgment.
  • scientific fact (noun) An observation that has been confirmed repeatedly and is accepted as true (although its truth is never final).
  • contrary to fact (adjective) Going counter to the facts (usually as a hypothesis).
  • in point of fact (adverb) In reality or actuality.
  • question of fact (noun) A disputed factual contention that is generally left for a jury to decide.
  • accomplished fact (noun) An irreversible accomplishment.
  • as a matter of fact (adverb) In reality or actuality.
  • accessory after the fact (noun) A person who gives assistance or comfort to someone known to be a felon or known to be sought in connection with the commission of a felony.
  • accessory before the fact (noun) A person who procures or advises or commands the commission of a felony but who is not present at its perpetration.
  • accessory during the fact (noun) A person who witnesses a crime but does not try to prevent it.
