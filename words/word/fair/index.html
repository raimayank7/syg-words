Fair: meaning, synonyms and examples

Fair is used as an adjective (10 senses), noun (4 senses), verb (1 sense) and adverb (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Free from favoritism or self-interest or bias or deception; conforming with established standards or rules.

In Hindi

Synonyms

Opposites
unfair, foul
Word family

Fair as an adjective

  1. Free from favoritism or self-interest or bias or deception; conforming with established standards or rules.

    “A fair referee.” “Fair deal.” “On a fair footing.”

    Synonyms: just · Opposite: unfair · Similar: antimonopoly, clean, fair-minded, fair-and-square

  2. Not excessive or extreme.

    “A fairish income.” “Reasonable prices.”

    Synonyms: fairish, reasonable · Similar: moderate

  3. Very pleasing to the eye.

    “My bonny lass.” “There's a bonny bay beyond.” “A comely face.”

    Synonyms: bonny, bonnie, comely, sightly · Similar: beautiful

  4. (of a baseball) hit between the foul lines.

    “He hit a fair ball over the third base bag.”

    Opposite: foul · Similar: in-bounds

  5. Lacking exceptional quality or ability.

    “A novel of average merit.” “Only a fair performance of the sonata.” “In fair health.”

    Synonyms: average, mediocre, middling · Similar: ordinary

  6. Attractively feminine.

    Similar: feminine

  7. (of a manuscript) having few alterations or corrections.

    “Fair copy.” “A clean manuscript.”

    Synonyms: clean · Similar: legible

  8. Gained or earned without cheating or stealing.

    “An honest wage.” “An fair penny.”

    Synonyms: honest · Similar: equitable

  9. Free of clouds or rain.

    “Today will be fair and warm.”

    Similar: clear

  10. (used of hair or skin) pale or light-colored.

    “A fair complexion.”

    Synonyms: fairish · Similar: blond

Fair as a noun

  1. A traveling show; having sideshows and rides and games of skill etc.

    Synonyms: carnival, funfair · A kind of: show

  2. Gathering of producers to promote business.

    “World fair.” “Trade fair.” “Book fair.”

    A kind of: gathering · Kinds: book fair

  3. A competitive exhibition of farm products.

    “She won a blue ribbon for her baking at the county fair.”

    A kind of: exhibition

  4. A sale of miscellany; often for charity.

    “The church bazaar.”

    Synonyms: bazaar · A kind of: sale · Kinds: book fair, craft fair

Fair as a verb

  1. Join so that the external surfaces blend smoothly.

    A kind of: join

Fair as an adverb

  1. In conformity with the rules or laws and without fraud or cheating.

    “They played fairly.”

    Synonyms: fairly, clean

  2. Without favoring one party, in a fair evenhanded manner.

    “Deal fairly with one another.”

    Synonyms: fairly, evenhandedly

Phrases and compounds with "fair"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • fair use (noun) The conditions under which you can use material that is copyrighted by someone else without paying royalties.
  • book fair (noun) Fair organized by publishers or booksellers to promote the sale of books.
  • fair ball (noun) (baseball) a ball struck with the bat so that it stays between the lines (the foul lines) that define the width of the playing field.
  • fair copy (noun) A clean copy of a corrected draft.
  • fair deal (noun) Fair treatment.
  • fair game (noun) A person who is the aim of an attack (especially a victim of ridicule or exploitation) by some hostile person or influence.
  • craft fair (noun) A fair at which objects made by craftsmen are offered for sale.
  • fair catch (noun) (American football) a catch of a punt on the fly by a defensive player who has signalled that he will not run and so should not be tackled.
  • fair trade (noun) Trade that satisfies certain criteria on the supply chain of the goods involved, usually including fair payment for producers; often with other social and environmental considerations.
  • fair chance (noun) A reasonable probability of success.
  • fair-haired (adjective) Favorite.
  • fair-minded (adjective) Of a person; just and impartial; not prejudiced.
  • vanity fair (noun) A vain and frivolous lifestyle especially in large cities.
  • fair hearing (noun) A hearing that is granted in extraordinary situations where the normal judicial process would be inadequate to secure due process because the person would be harmed or denied their rights before a judicial remedy became available (as in deportation or loss of welfare benefits).
  • fair weather (noun) Moderate weather; suitable for outdoor activities.
  • fair-trade act (noun) Formerly a state law that protected manufacturers from price-cutting by allowing them to set minimum retail prices for their merchandise; eliminated by the United States Congress in 1975.
