Fast: meaning, synonyms and examples

Fast is used as an adjective (10 senses), adverb (2 senses), noun (1 sense) and verb (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Acting or moving or capable of acting or moving quickly.

In Hindi
तेज़; उपवास
Synonyms
debauched, degenerate, degraded, dissipated, dissolute, libertine, profligate, riotous, flying, quick
Opposites
slow
Word family
fastness, fasting

Fast as an adjective

  1. Acting or moving or capable of acting or moving quickly.

    “Fast film.” “On the fast track in school.” “Set a fast pace.”

    Opposite: slow · Similar: accelerated, alacritous, blistering, double-quick, express, fast-breaking

  2. (used of timepieces) indicating a time ahead of or later than the correct time.

    “My watch is fast.”

    Opposite: slow

  3. At a rapid tempo.

    “The band played a fast fox trot.”

    Opposite: slow · Similar: allegro, allegretto, andantino, presto, prestissimo, vivace

  4. (of surfaces) conducive to rapid speeds.

    “A fast road.” “Grass courts are faster than clay.”

    Similar: smooth

  5. Resistant to destruction or fading.

    “Fast colors.”

    Similar: impervious

  6. Unrestrained by convention or morality.

    “Congreve draws a debauched aristocratic society.” “Deplorably dissipated and degraded.” “Riotous living.”

    Synonyms: debauched, degenerate, degraded, dissipated, dissolute, libertine, profligate, riotous · Similar: immoral

  7. Hurried and brief.

    “Paid a flying visit.” “Took a flying glance at the book.” “A quick inspection.”

    Synonyms: flying, quick · Similar: hurried

  8. Securely fixed in place.

    “The post was still firm after being hit by the car.”

    Synonyms: firm, immobile · Similar: fixed

  9. Unwavering in devotion to friend or vow or cause.

    “A firm ally.” “Loyal supporters.” “The true-hearted soldier...of Tippecanoe.”

    Synonyms: firm, loyal, truehearted · Similar: faithful

  10. (of a photographic lens or emulsion) causing a shortening of exposure time.

    “A fast lens.”

    Similar: causative

Fast as an adverb

  1. Quickly or rapidly (often used as a combining form).

    “How fast can he get here?” “Ran as fast as he could.” “Needs medical help fast.”

  2. Firmly or closely.

    “Held fast to the rope.” “Her foot was stuck fast.” “Held tight.”

    Synonyms: tight

Fast as a noun

  1. Abstaining from food.

    Synonyms: fasting · A kind of: abstinence · Kinds: diet, hunger strike

Fast as a verb

  1. Abstain from certain foods, as for religious or medical reasons.

    “Catholics sometimes fast during Lent.”

    A kind of: abstain · Kinds: diet

  2. Abstain from eating.

    “Before the medical exam, you must fast.”

    A kind of: abstain · Kinds: diet

Phrases and compounds with "fast"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • fast day (noun) A day designated for fasting.
  • fast dye (verb) Dye with fast colors.
  • fast one (noun) A cunning or deceitful action or device.
  • acid-fast (adjective) Not easily decolorized by acid solutions; pertains to micro-organisms (especially the tubercle bacillus that causes tuberculosis).
  • fast buck (noun) Quick or easy earnings, "they are traders out to make a fast buck".
  • fast food (noun) Inexpensive food (hamburgers or chicken or milkshakes) prepared and served quickly.
  • fast lane (noun) A hectic and pressured lifestyle often characterized by recklessness or dissipation.
  • hold fast (verb) Stick to firmly.
  • very fast (adverb) In a relatively short time.
  • fast break (noun) (basketball) a rapid dash to get a shot as soon as possible after taking possession of the ball.
  • fast track (noun) A rapid means of achieving a goal.
  • fast-paced (adjective) Of communication that proceeds rapidly.
  • stand fast (verb) Refuse to abandon one's opinion or belief.
  • fast asleep (adjective) Sleeping deeply.
  • fast-flying (adjective) Moving swiftly.
  • fast-footed (adjective) Having rapidly moving feet.
