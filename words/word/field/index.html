Field: meaning, synonyms and examples

Field is used as a noun (14 senses) and verb (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: A piece of land cleared of trees and usually enclosed.

In Hindi
खेत, मैदान; क्षेत्र
Synonyms
battlefield, battleground, field of battle, field of honor, discipline, subject, subject area, subject field, field of study, study
Word family
fielder, fielding

Field as a noun

  1. A piece of land cleared of trees and usually enclosed.

    “He planted a field of wheat.”

    A kind of: tract · Kinds: yard, campus, firebreak, grainfield, lawn, paddy

  2. A region where a battle is being (or has been) fought.

    “They made a tour of Civil War battlefields.”

    Synonyms: battlefield, battleground, field of battle, field of honor · A kind of: tract

  3. Somewhere (away from a studio or office or library or laboratory) where practical work is done or data is collected.

    “Anthropologists do much of their work in the field.”

    A kind of: region

  4. A branch of knowledge.

    “In what discipline is his doctorate?” “Teachers should be well trained in their subject.” “Anthropology is the study of human beings.”

    Synonyms: discipline, subject, subject area, subject field, field of study, study, bailiwick · A kind of: knowledge domain · Kinds: occultism, communications, major, frontier, genealogy, allometry, bibliotics, ology

  5. The space around a radiating body within which its electromagnetic oscillations can exert force on another similar body not in contact with it.

    Synonyms: field of force, force field · A kind of: physical phenomenon · Kinds: electric field, gravitational field, magnetic field, radiation field

  6. A particular kind of commercial enterprise.

    “They are outstanding in their field.”

    Synonyms: field of operation, line of business · A kind of: commercial enterprise

  7. A particular environment or walk of life.

    “His social sphere is limited.” “It was a closed area of employment.” “He's out of my orbit.”

    Synonyms: sphere, domain, area, orbit, arena · A kind of: environment · Kinds: distaff, front, kingdom, lap, political arena, preserve, province

  8. A piece of land prepared for playing a game.

    “The home crowd cheered when Princeton took the field.”

    Synonyms: playing field, athletic field, playing area · A kind of: tract · Kinds: arena, ball field, court, football field, palestra, bowling green

  9. Extensive tract of level open land.

    “They emerged from the woods onto a vast open plain.” “He longed for the fields of his youth.”

    Synonyms: plain, champaign · A kind of: land · Kinds: flat, floodplain, llano, moor, peneplain, snowfield, steppe, tundra

  10. (mathematics) a set of elements such that addition and multiplication are commutative and associative and multiplication is distributive over addition and there are two elements 0 and 1.

    “The set of all rational numbers is a field.”

    A kind of: set · Kinds: scalar field

  11. A region in which active military operations are in progress.

    “The army was in the field awaiting action.” “He served in the Vietnam theater for three years.”

    Synonyms: field of operations, theater, theater of operations, theatre, theatre of operations · A kind of: region

  12. All of the horses in a particular horse race.

    A kind of: set

  13. All the competitors in a particular contest or sporting event.

    A kind of: set

  14. A geographic region (land or sea) under which something valuable is found.

    “The diamond fields of South Africa.”

    A kind of: geographical area · Kinds: coalfield, gasfield, oilfield

Field as a verb

  1. Catch or pick up (balls) in baseball or cricket.

    A kind of: handle

  2. Play as a fielder.

    A kind of: play

  3. Answer adequately or successfully.

    “The lawyer fielded all questions from the press.”

    A kind of: answer

  4. Select (a team or individual player) for a game.

    “The Buckeyes fielded a young new quarterback for the Rose Bowl.”

    A kind of: choose

Phrases and compounds with "field"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • bit field (noun) A field containing only binary characters.
  • field day (noun) (military) a day for military exercises and display.
  • field gun (noun) Movable artillery (other than antiaircraft) used by armies in the field (especially for direct support of front-line troops).
  • field hut (noun) Temporary military shelter.
  • field pea (noun) Seed of the field pea plant.
  • hop field (noun) A garden where hops are grown.
  • ice field (noun) A large flat mass of ice (larger than an ice floe) floating at sea.
  • ball field (noun) The baseball playing field.
  • corn field (noun) A field planted with corn.
  • field balm (noun) Trailing European aromatic plant of the mint family having rounded leaves and small purplish flowers often grown in hanging baskets; naturalized in North America; sometimes placed in genus Nepeta.
  • field bean (noun) Old World upright plant grown especially for its large flat edible seeds but also as fodder.
  • field coil (noun) The electric coil around a field magnet that produces the magneto motive force to set up the flux in an electric machine.
  • field corn (noun) Corn grown primarily for animal feed or market grain.
  • field crop (noun) A crop (other than fruits or vegetables) that is grown for agricultural purposes.
  • field game (noun) An outdoor game played on a field of specified dimensions.
  • field goal (noun) A score in American football; a score made by kicking the ball between the opponents' goal posts.
