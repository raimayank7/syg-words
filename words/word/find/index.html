Find: meaning, synonyms and examples

Find is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Come upon, as if by accident; meet with.

In Hindi
ढूँढ़ना, पाना
Synonyms
happen, chance, bump, encounter, detect, observe, discover, notice, regain, determine
Opposites
lose
Word family
finder, finding

Find as a verb

  1. Come upon, as if by accident; meet with.

    “We find this idea in Plato.” “I happened upon the most wonderful bakery not very far from here.” “She chanced upon an interesting book in the bookstore the other day.”

    Synonyms: happen, chance, bump, encounter

  2. Discover or determine the existence, presence, or fact of.

    “She detected high levels of lead in her drinking water.” “We found traces of lead in the paint.”

    Synonyms: detect, observe, discover, notice · A kind of: spy · Kinds: find out, sense, instantiate, trace, see

  3. Come upon after searching; find the location of something that was missed or lost.

    “Did you find your glasses?” “I cannot find my gloves!”

    Synonyms: regain · Opposite: lose · A kind of: get · Kinds: feel, locate, fall upon, rout up

  4. Establish after a calculation, investigation, experiment, survey, or study.

    “Find the product of two numbers.” “The physicist who found the elusive particle won the Nobel Prize.”

    Synonyms: determine, find out, ascertain · Kinds: gauge, translate, rectify, redetermine, sequence, refract, count, admeasure

  5. Come to believe on the basis of emotion, intuitions, or indefinite grounds.

    “I feel that he doesn't like me.” “I find him to be obnoxious.” “I found the movie rather entertaining.”

    Synonyms: feel · A kind of: reason

  6. Perceive or be contemporaneous with.

    “We found Republicans winning the offices.” “You'll see a lot of cheating in this school.” “The 1960's saw the rebellion of the younger generation against established traditions.”

    Synonyms: witness, see · A kind of: experience · Kinds: catch

  7. Get something or somebody for a specific purpose.

    “I found this gadget that will serve as a bottle opener.” “I got hold of these tools to fix our plumbing.” “The chairman got hold of a secretary on Friday night to type the urgent letter.”

    Synonyms: line up, get hold, come up · A kind of: get

  8. Make a discovery, make a new finding.

    “Roentgen discovered X-rays.” “Physicists believe they found a new elementary particle.”

    Synonyms: discover · A kind of: gestate

  9. Make a discovery.

    “She found that he had lied to her.” “The story is false, so far as I can discover.”

    Synonyms: discover · A kind of: learn · Kinds: rake up, ferret out

  10. Obtain through effort or management.

    “She found the time and energy to take care of her aging parents.” “We found the money to send our sons to college.”

    A kind of: get

  11. Decide on and make a declaration about.

    “Find someone guilty.”

    Synonyms: rule · A kind of: pronounce

  12. Receive a specified treatment (abstract).

    “These aspects of civilization do not find expression or receive an interpretation.” “His movie received a good review.” “I got nothing but trouble for my good intentions.”

    Synonyms: receive, get, obtain, incur · A kind of: change · Kinds: take

  13. Perceive oneself to be in a certain condition or place.

    “I found myself in a difficult situation.” “When he woke up, he found himself in a hospital room.”

    A kind of: perceive

  14. Get or find back; recover the use of.

    “She regained control of herself.” “She found her voice and replied quickly.”

    Synonyms: recover, retrieve, regain · A kind of: get · Kinds: access

Find as a noun

  1. A productive insight.

    Synonyms: discovery, breakthrough · A kind of: insight

  2. The act of discovering something.

    Synonyms: discovery, uncovering · A kind of: act · Kinds: tracing, detection, self-discovery, breakthrough, determination, rediscovery

Phrases and compounds with "find"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • find out (verb) Establish after a calculation, investigation, experiment, survey, or study.
  • find fault (verb) Harass with constant criticism.
  • find oneself (verb) Accept and make use of one's personality, abilities, and situation.
