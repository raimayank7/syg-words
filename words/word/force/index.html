Force: meaning, synonyms and examples

Force is used as a noun (10 senses) and verb (9 senses). Its most common meaning is: A powerful effect or influence.

In Hindi
बल, ताक़त; मजबूर करना
Synonyms
forcefulness, strength, personnel, military unit, military force, military group, violence, power, effect, force out
Word family
forcible

Force as a noun

  1. A powerful effect or influence.

    “The force of his eloquence easily persuaded them.”

    A kind of: influence · Kinds: pressure, duress, heartbeat, lifeblood, wheel

  2. (physics) the influence that produces a change in a physical quantity.

    “Force equals mass times acceleration.”

    A kind of: physical phenomenon · Kinds: aerodynamic force, affinity, attraction, repulsion, centrifugal force, centripetal force, cohesion, drift

  3. Physical energy or intensity.

    “He hit with all the force he could muster.” “It was destroyed by the strength of the gale.” “A government has not the vitality and forcefulness of a living man.”

    Synonyms: forcefulness, strength · A kind of: intensity · Kinds: brunt, momentum, energy

  4. Group of people willing to obey orders.

    “A public force is necessary to give security to the rights of citizens.”

    Synonyms: personnel · A kind of: organization · Kinds: guerrilla force, military service, military, paramilitary, police, security force, military police, work force

  5. A unit that is part of some military service.

    “He sent Caesar a force of six thousand men.”

    Synonyms: military unit, military force, military group · A kind of: unit · Kinds: trip wire, command, enemy, task force, army unit, naval unit, air unit, armor

  6. An act of aggression (as one against a person who resists).

    “He may accomplish by craft in the long run what he cannot do by force and violence in the short one.”

    Synonyms: violence · A kind of: aggression · Kinds: domestic violence, road rage, riot

  7. One possessing or exercising power or influence or authority.

    “The mysterious presence of an evil power.” “May the force be with you.” “The forces of evil.”

    Synonyms: power · A kind of: causal agent · Kinds: juggernaut, influence

  8. A group of people having the power of effective action.

    “He joined forces with a band of adventurers.”

    A kind of: social group

  9. (of a law) having legal validity.

    “The law is still in effect.”

    Synonyms: effect · A kind of: validity

  10. A putout of a base runner who is required to run; the putout is accomplished by holding the ball while touching the base to which the runner must advance before the runner reaches that base.

    “The shortstop got the runner at second on a force.”

    Synonyms: force out, force-out, force play · A kind of: putout

Force as a verb

  1. To cause to do through pressure or necessity, by physical, moral or intellectual means :"She forced him to take a job in the city".

    “She forced him to take a job in the city.” “He squeezed her for information.”

    Synonyms: coerce, hale, squeeze, pressure · A kind of: compel · Kinds: turn up the heat, drive, bludgeon, steamroller, squeeze for, dragoon, bring oneself

  2. Urge or force (a person) to an action; constrain or motivate.

    Synonyms: impel · A kind of: cause

  3. Move with force, "He pushed the table into a corner".

    “He pushed the table into a corner.”

    Synonyms: push · A kind of: move · Kinds: nudge, press, repel, shove, jerk, jostle, nose, push out

  4. Impose urgently, importunately, or inexorably.

    “She forced her diet fads on him.”

    Synonyms: thrust · A kind of: compel · Kinds: stick

  5. Squeeze like a wedge into a tight space.

    “I squeezed myself into the corner.”

    Synonyms: wedge, squeeze · A kind of: move · Kinds: impact, compress

  6. Force into or from an action or state, either physically or metaphorically.

    “She rammed her mind into focus.” “He drives me mad.”

    Synonyms: drive, ram · A kind of: thrust · Kinds: toe

  7. Cause to move by pulling.

    “Draw a wagon.” “Pull a sled.”

    Synonyms: pull, draw · A kind of: move · Kinds: twitch, pull back, adduct, abduct, stretch, pluck, tug, drag

  8. Do forcibly; exert force.

    “Don't force it!”

    A kind of: act · Kinds: pull

  9. Take by force.

    “Storm the fort.”

    Synonyms: storm · A kind of: penetrate

Phrases and compounds with "force"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • g-force (noun) A unit of force equal to the force exerted by gravity; used to indicate the force to which a body is subjected when it is accelerated.
  • in force (adjective) Exerting force or influence.
  • air force (noun) The airborne branch of a country's armed forces.
  • force out (noun) A putout of a base runner who is required to run; the putout is accomplished by holding the ball while touching the base to which the runner must advance before the runner reaches that base.
  • force-out (noun) A putout of a base runner who is required to run; the putout is accomplished by holding the ball while touching the base to which the runner must advance before the runner reaches that base.
  • force back (verb) Cause to move back by force or influence.
  • force feed (noun) Mechanical system of lubricating internal combustion engines in which a pump forces oil into the engine bearings.
  • force play (noun) A putout of a base runner who is required to run; the putout is accomplished by holding the ball while touching the base to which the runner must advance before the runner reaches that base.
  • force pump (noun) Pump used to force a liquid up and expel it under pressure.
  • force unit (noun) A unit of measurement of physical force.
  • force-feed (verb) Feed someone who will not or cannot eat.
  • force-land (verb) Make a forced landing.
  • life force (noun) (biology) a hypothetical force (not physical or chemical) once thought by Henri Bergson to cause the evolution and development of organisms.
  • task force (noun) A temporary military unit formed to accomplish a particular objective.
  • weak force (noun) (physics) an interaction between elementary particles involving neutrinos or antineutrinos that is responsible for certain kinds of radioactive decay; mediated by intermediate vector bosons.
  • work force (noun) The force of workers available.
