Form: meaning, synonyms and examples

Form is used as a noun (14 senses) and verb (7 senses). Its most common meaning is: The phonological or orthographic sound or appearance of a word that can be used to describe or identify something.

In Hindi
रूप, आकार; फ़ॉर्म
Synonyms
word form, signifier, descriptor, kind, sort, variety, shape, pattern, configuration, contour
Word family
formation, formative

Form as a noun

  1. The phonological or orthographic sound or appearance of a word that can be used to describe or identify something.

    “The inflected forms of a word can be represented by a stem and a list of inflections to be attached.”

    Synonyms: word form, signifier, descriptor · A kind of: word · Kinds: plural, singular, ghost word, root, etymon, citation form, abbreviation, acronym

  2. A category of things distinguished by some common characteristic or quality.

    “Sculpture is a form of art.” “What kinds of desserts are there?”

    Synonyms: kind, sort, variety · A kind of: category · Kinds: description, type, antitype, art form, style, flavor, color, species

  3. A perceptual structure.

    “The composition presents problems for students of musical form.” “A visual pattern must include not only objects but the spaces between them.”

    Synonyms: shape, pattern · A kind of: structure · Kinds: fractal, gestalt, grid, kaleidoscope, mosaic, strand

  4. Any spatial attributes (especially as defined by outline).

    “He could barely make out their shapes.”

    Synonyms: shape, configuration, contour, conformation · A kind of: spatial property · Kinds: sharpness, dullness, topography, lobularity, concavity, convexity, angularity, narrowing

  5. Alternative names for the body of a human being.

    “Leonardo studied the human body.” “He has a strong physique.” “The spirit is willing but the flesh is weak.”

    Synonyms: human body, physical body, material body, soma, build, figure, physique, anatomy · A kind of: body · Kinds: person, juvenile body, adult body, male body, female body

  6. The spatial arrangement of something as distinct from its substance.

    “Geometry is the mathematical science of shape.”

    Synonyms: shape · A kind of: attribute · Kinds: solid, plane, natural shape, flare, figure, line, angular shape, round shape

  7. The visual appearance of something or someone.

    “The delicate cast of his features.”

    Synonyms: shape, cast · A kind of: appearance

  8. A printed document with spaces in which to write.

    “He filled out his tax form.”

    A kind of: document · Kinds: application form, claim form, order form, questionnaire, requisition, tax form, telegraph form

  9. (biology) a group of organisms within a species that differ in trivial ways from similar groups.

    “A new strain of microorganisms.”

    Synonyms: variant, strain · A kind of: taxonomic group

  10. An arrangement of the elements in a composition or discourse.

    “The essay was in the form of a dialogue.” “He first sketches the plot in outline form.”

    A kind of: writing style · Kinds: versification

  11. A particular mode in which something is manifested.

    “His resentment took the form of extreme hostility.”

    A kind of: manner

  12. (physical chemistry) a distinct state of matter in a system; matter that is identical in chemical composition and physical state and separated from other material by the phase boundary.

    “The reaction occurs in the liquid phase of the system.”

    Synonyms: phase · A kind of: state of matter · Kinds: dispersed phase, dispersing phase

  13. A body of students who are taught together.

    “Early morning classes are always sleepy.”

    Synonyms: class, grade, course · A kind of: gathering · Kinds: master class, section

  14. An ability to perform well.

    “He was at the top of his form.” “The team was off form last night.”

    A kind of: ability

Form as a verb

  1. Create (as an entity).

    “Social groups form everywhere.” “They formed a company.”

    Synonyms: organize, organise · A kind of: make · Kinds: reorganize, choose up, draw up, regiment, syndicate

  2. To compose or represent:"This wall forms the background of the stage setting".

    “This wall forms the background of the stage setting.” “The branches made a roof.” “This makes a fine introduction.”

    Synonyms: constitute, make · A kind of: constitute · Kinds: chelate, add

  3. Develop into a distinctive entity.

    “Our plans began to take shape.”

    Synonyms: take form, take shape, spring · A kind of: become · Kinds: regenerate

  4. Give shape or form to.

    “Shape the dough.” “Form the young child's character.”

    Synonyms: shape · A kind of: change · Kinds: individuate, tie, terrace, fork, tabulate, dimension, roll, draw

  5. Make something, usually for a specific function.

    “She molded the rice balls carefully.” “Form cylinders from the dough.” “Shape a figure.”

    Synonyms: shape, work, mold, mould, forge · A kind of: create from raw material · Kinds: carve, swage, chip, layer, cut out, machine, grind, stamp

  6. Establish or impress firmly in the mind.

    “We imprint our ideas onto our children.”

    Synonyms: imprint · A kind of: influence

  7. Assume a form or shape.

    “The water formed little beads.”

    A kind of: change · Kinds: constellate, culminate, granulate, conglobate, bunch, brecciate, reticulate, flake

Phrases and compounds with "form"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • art form (noun) (architecture) a form of artistic expression (such as writing or painting or architecture).
  • tax form (noun) A form to use when paying your taxes.
  • form-only (adjective) Being a matter of form only; lacking substance.
  • free form (noun) A morpheme that can occur alone.
  • good form (noun) Behavior that conforms to social conventions of the time.
  • leaf form (noun) Any of the various shape that leaves of plants can assume.
  • life form (noun) The characteristic bodily form of a mature organism.
  • take form (verb) Develop into a distinctive entity.
  • wave form (noun) The shape of a wave illustrated graphically by plotting the values of the period quantity against time.
  • word form (noun) The phonological or orthographic sound or appearance of a word that can be used to describe or identify something.
  • bound form (noun) A morpheme that occurs only as part of a larger construction; eg an -s at the end of plural nouns.
  • claim form (noun) A form to use when filing a claim.
  • form class (noun) One of the traditional categories of words intended to reflect their functions in a grammatical context.
  • form genus (noun) (biology) an artificial taxonomic category for organisms of which the true relationships are obscure.
  • order form (noun) A form to use when placing an order.
  • verse form (noun) A composition written in metrical feet forming rhythmical lines.
