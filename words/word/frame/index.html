Frame: meaning, synonyms and examples

Frame is used as a noun (11 senses) and verb (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: The framework for a pair of eyeglasses.

In Hindi
चौखटा, ढाँचा
Synonyms
human body, physical body, material body, soma, build, figure, physique, anatomy, shape, bod
Word family
framing, framer

Frame as a noun

  1. The framework for a pair of eyeglasses.

    A kind of: framework · Kinds: chase

  2. A single one of a series of still transparent pictures forming a cinema, television or video film.

    A kind of: photograph

  3. Alternative names for the body of a human being.

    “Leonardo studied the human body.” “He has a strong physique.” “The spirit is willing but the flesh is weak.”

    Synonyms: human body, physical body, material body, soma, build, figure, physique, anatomy · A kind of: body · Kinds: person, juvenile body, adult body, male body, female body

  4. (baseball) one of nine divisions of play during which each team has a turn at bat.

    Synonyms: inning · A kind of: part

  5. A single drawing in a comic_strip.

    A kind of: drawing

  6. An application that divides the user's display into two or more windows that can be scrolled independently.

    A kind of: application

  7. A system of assumptions and standards that sanction behavior and give it meaning.

    Synonyms: frame of reference · A kind of: system · Kinds: vocabulary

  8. The hard structure (bones and cartilages) that provides a frame for the body of an animal.

    Synonyms: skeletal system, skeleton, systema skeletale · A kind of: system · Kinds: endoskeleton, exoskeleton

  9. The internal supporting structure that gives an artifact its shape.

    “The building has a steel skeleton.”

    Synonyms: skeleton, skeletal frame, underframe · A kind of: supporting structure · Kinds: chassis, hoop

  10. A framework that supports and protects a picture or a mirror.

    “The frame enhances but is not itself the subject of attention.” “The frame was much more valuable than the miror it held.”

    Synonyms: framing · A kind of: framework

  11. One of the ten divisions into which bowling is divided.

    A kind of: part

Frame as a verb

  1. Enclose in or as if in a frame.

    “Frame a picture.”

    Synonyms: frame in, border · A kind of: enclose

  2. Enclose in a frame, as of a picture.

    A kind of: enclose

  3. Take or catch as if in a snare or trap.

    “I was set up!” “The innocent man was framed by the police.”

    Synonyms: ensnare, entrap, set up · A kind of: deceive

  4. Formulate in a particular style or language.

    “I wouldn't put it that way.” “She cast her request in very polite language.”

    Synonyms: redact, cast, put, couch · A kind of: give voice

  5. Make up plans or basic details for.

    “Frame a policy.”

    Synonyms: compose, draw up · A kind of: plan

  6. Construct by fitting or uniting parts together.

    Synonyms: frame up · A kind of: construct

Phrases and compounds with "frame"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • frame in (verb) Enclose in or as if in a frame.
  • frame up (verb) Construct by fitting or uniting parts together.
  • frame-up (noun) An act that incriminates someone on a false charge.
  • cold frame (noun) Protective covering consisting of a wooden frame with a glass top in which small plants are protected from the cold.
  • open frame (noun) Any frame in which a bowler fails to make a strike or spare.
  • time frame (noun) A time period during which something occurs or is expected to occur.
  • frame buffer (noun) (computer science) a buffer that stores the contents of an image pixel by pixel.
  • window frame (noun) The framework that supports a window.
  • zimmer frame (noun) A light enclosing framework (trade name Zimmer) with rubber castors or wheels and handles; helps invalids or the handicapped or the aged to walk.
  • frame of mind (noun) A temporary psychological state.
  • picture frame (noun) A framework in which a picture is mounted.
  • climbing frame (noun) A framework of bars or logs for children to climb on.
  • inertial frame (noun) A coordinate system in which Newton's first law of motion is valid.
  • skeletal frame (noun) The internal supporting structure that gives an artifact its shape.
  • spinning frame (noun) Spinning machine that draws, twists, and winds yarn.
  • reference frame (noun) A system that uses coordinates to establish position.
