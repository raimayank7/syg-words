Gain: meaning, synonyms and examples

Gain is used as a verb (9 senses) and noun (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: Obtain.

In Hindi
पाना; लाभ
Synonyms
derive, acquire, win, profit, benefit, reach, make, attain, hit, arrive at
Opposites
fall back, reduce, loss
Word family
gainer

Gain as a verb

  1. Obtain.

    “Derive pleasure from one's garden.”

    Synonyms: derive · A kind of: obtain · Kinds: reap

  2. Win something through one's efforts.

    “I acquired a passing knowledge of Chinese.” “Gain an understanding of international finance.”

    Synonyms: acquire, win · A kind of: get · Kinds: cozen

  3. Derive a benefit from.

    “She profited from his vast experience.”

    Synonyms: profit, benefit · A kind of: get · Kinds: cash in on, profiteer, capitalize, pyramid, net

  4. Reach a destination, either real or abstract.

    “We hit Detroit by noon.” “The water reached the doorstep.” “We barely made it to the finish line.”

    Synonyms: reach, make, attain, hit, arrive at · Kinds: catch up, scale, access, bottom out, top out, summit, top, make

  5. Obtain advantages, such as points, etc.

    “The home team was gaining ground.” “After defeating the Knicks, the Blazers pulled ahead of the Lakers in the battle for the number-one playoff berth in the Western Conference.”

    Synonyms: advance, win, pull ahead, make headway, get ahead, gain ground · Opposite: fall back · Kinds: steal, score

  6. Rise in rate or price.

    “The stock market gained 24 points today.”

    Synonyms: advance · A kind of: wax

  7. Increase or develop.

    “The peace movement gained momentum.” “The car gathers speed.”

    Synonyms: gather · A kind of: increase

  8. Earn on some commercial or business transaction; earn as salary or wages.

    “How much do you make a month in your new job?” “She earns a lot in her new job.” “This merger brought in lots of money.”

    Synonyms: take in, clear, make, earn, realize, realise, pull in, bring in · A kind of: get · Kinds: eke out, profit, rake off, take home, rake in, gross, yield

  9. Increase (one's body weight).

    “She gained 20 pounds when she stopped exercising.”

    Synonyms: put on · Opposite: reduce · A kind of: change state · Kinds: round, pack on

Gain as a noun

  1. A quantity that is added.

    “There was an addition to property taxes this year.” “They recorded the cattle's gain in weight over a period of weeks.”

    Synonyms: addition, increase · A kind of: indefinite quantity · Kinds: accretion

  2. The advantageous quality of being beneficial.

    Synonyms: profit · A kind of: advantage · Kinds: account, profitableness

  3. The amount of increase in signal power or voltage or current expressed as the ratio of output to input.

    Synonyms: amplification · A kind of: increase · Kinds: loop gain

  4. The amount by which the revenue of a business exceeds its cost of operating.

    Opposite: loss · A kind of: sum · Kinds: financial gain, paper profit

Phrases and compounds with "gain"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • loop gain (noun) (telecommunication) the gain of a feedback amplifier or system as a function of how much output is fed back to the input.
  • gain vigor (verb) Gain or regain energy.
  • gain ground (verb) Obtain advantages, such as points, etc.
  • capital gain (noun) The amount by which the selling price of an asset exceeds the purchase price; the gain is realized when the asset is sold.
  • financial gain (noun) The amount of monetary gain.
