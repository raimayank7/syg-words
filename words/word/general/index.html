General: meaning, synonyms and examples

General is used as an adjective (6 senses), noun (3 senses) and verb (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: Applying to all or most members of a category or group.

In Hindi
सामान्य, आम; जनरल
Synonyms
cosmopolitan, ecumenical, oecumenical, universal, worldwide, world-wide, full general, superior general
Opposites
specific, local, particular
Word family
generality, generalship

General as an adjective

  1. Applying to all or most members of a category or group.

    “The general public.” “General assistance.” “A general rule.”

    Opposite: specific · Similar: broad, general-purpose, generic, gross, overall, pandemic

  2. Not specialized or limited to one class of things.

    “General studies.” “General knowledge.”

    Similar: undiversified

  3. Prevailing among and common to the general public.

    “The general discontent.”

    Similar: common

  4. Affecting the entire body.

    “A general anesthetic.” “General symptoms.”

    Opposite: local · Similar: systemic

  5. Somewhat indefinite.

    “Bearing a general resemblance to the original.” “A general description of the merchandise.”

    Similar: imprecise

  6. Of worldwide scope or applicability.

    “An issue of cosmopolitan import.” “The shrewdest political and ecumenical comment of our time.” “Universal experience.”

    Synonyms: cosmopolitan, ecumenical, oecumenical, universal, worldwide, world-wide · Similar: comprehensive

General as a noun

  1. A general officer of the highest rank.

    Synonyms: full general · A kind of: general officer

  2. The head of a religious order or congregation.

    Synonyms: superior general · A kind of: head

  3. A fact about the whole (as opposed to particular).

    “He discussed the general but neglected the particular.”

    Opposite: particular, specific · A kind of: fact

General as a verb

  1. Command as a general.

    “We are generaled by an incompetent!”

    A kind of: command

Phrases and compounds with "general"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • in general (adverb) Without distinction of one from others.
  • full general (noun) A general officer of the highest rank.
  • general lien (noun) A lien on all the property owned by a debtor and not just a specific property.
  • field general (noun) (football) the person who plays quarterback.
  • general agent (noun) Someone authorized to transact every kind of business for the principal.
  • general staff (noun) Military officers assigned to assist a senior officer in planning military policy.
  • general store (noun) A retail store serving a sparsely populated region; usually stocked with a wide variety of merchandise.
  • major-general (noun) A general officer ranking above a brigadier general and below a lieutenant general.
  • vicar-general (noun) (Roman Catholic Church) an administrative deputy who assists a bishop.
  • general franco (noun) Spanish general whose armies took control of Spain in 1939 and who ruled as a dictator until his death (1892-1975).
  • general ledger (noun) The ledger that contains all of the financial accounts of a business; contains offsetting debit and credit accounts (including control accounts).
  • states general (noun) Assembly of the estates of an entire country especially the sovereign body of the Dutch republic from 16th to 18th centuries.
  • estates general (noun) Assembly of the estates of all France; last meeting in 1789.
  • general anatomy (noun) The branch of morphology that deals with the structure of animals.
  • general baptist (noun) Group of Baptist congregations believing the teachings of the Dutch theologian Jacobus Arminius (who opposed the doctrine of strict predestination of the Calvinists).
  • general damages (noun) (law) compensation for losses that can readily be proven to have occurred and for which the injured party has the right to be compensated.
