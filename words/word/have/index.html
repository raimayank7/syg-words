Have: meaning, synonyms and examples

Have is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: Have or possess, either in a concrete or an abstract sense.

In Hindi
रखना, पास होना
Synonyms
have got, hold, feature, experience, receive, get, own, possess, let, consume
Opposites
lack

Have as a verb

  1. Have or possess, either in a concrete or an abstract sense.

    “She has $1,000 in the bank.” “He has got two beautiful daughters.” “She holds a Master's degree from Harvard.”

    Synonyms: have got, hold · Kinds: sustain, keep, keep, keep, keep, monopolize, wield, stock

  2. Have as a feature.

    “This restaurant features the most famous chefs in France.”

    Synonyms: feature · Opposite: lack · Kinds: wear, wear, carry, carry, bear, give off, unite, star

  3. Go through (mental or physical states or experiences).

    “Get an idea.” “Experience vertigo.” “Get nauseous.”

    Synonyms: experience, receive, get · A kind of: undergo · Kinds: suffer, take, horripilate

  4. Have ownership or possession of.

    “He owns three houses in Florida.” “How many cars does she have?”

    Synonyms: own, possess · Kinds: prepossess

  5. Cause to move; cause to be in a certain position or condition.

    “He got his squad on the ball.” “This let me in for a big surprise.” “He got a girl into trouble.”

    Synonyms: get, let · A kind of: make

  6. Serve oneself to, or consume regularly.

    “Have another bowl of chicken soup!” “I don't take sugar in my coffee.”

    Synonyms: consume, ingest, take in, take · Kinds: hit, cannibalize, use, eat, eat, drink, partake, feed

  7. Have a personal or business relationship with someone.

    “Have a postdoc.” “Have an assistant.” “Have a lover.”

    A kind of: interact

  8. Organize or be responsible for.

    “Hold a reception.” “Have, throw, or make a party.” “Give a course.”

    Synonyms: hold, throw, make, give · A kind of: direct

  9. Have left.

    “I have two years left.” “I don't have any money left.” “They have two more years before they retire.”

    Kinds: leave

  10. Be confronted with.

    “What do we have here?” “Now we have a fine mess.”

  11. Undergo.

    “The stocks had a fast run-up.”

    Synonyms: experience · A kind of: change · Kinds: meet

  12. Suffer from; be ill with.

    “She has arthritis.”

    A kind of: suffer

  13. Cause to do; cause to act in a specified manner.

    “The ads induced me to buy a VCR.” “My children finally got me to buy a computer.” “My wife made me buy a new sofa.”

    Synonyms: induce, stimulate, cause, get, make · Kinds: decide, persuade, bring, solicit, encourage, let, lead, prompt

  14. Receive willingly something given or offered.

    “The only girl who would have him was the miller's daughter.” “I won't have this dog in my house!” “Please accept my present.”

    Synonyms: accept, take · A kind of: get · Kinds: accept, welcome, honor, adopt

Have as a noun

  1. A person who possesses great material wealth.

    Synonyms: rich person, wealthy person · A kind of: person · Kinds: affluent, fat cat, man of means, millionairess, plutocrat, millionaire, billionaire, multi-billionaire

Phrases and compounds with "have"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • have on (verb) Be dressed in.
  • have got (verb) Have or possess, either in a concrete or an abstract sense.
  • have-not (noun) A person with few or no possessions.
  • have a go (verb) Make an attempt at something.
  • have down (verb) Have (something) mastered.
  • have a fit (verb) Get very angry and fly into a rage.
  • have words (verb) Censure severely or angrily.
  • have young (verb) Birth.
  • have a ball (verb) Enjoy oneself greatly.
  • have a look (verb) Look at with attention.
  • have in mind (verb) Intend to refer to.
  • have kittens (verb) Get very angry and fly into a rage.
  • have the best (verb) Overcome, usually through no fault or weakness of the person that is overcome.
  • have it coming (verb) Deserve (either good or bad).
  • might-have-been (noun) An event that could have occurred but never did.
  • have a good time (verb) Enjoy oneself greatly.

How to use this page

First decide whether "have" is working as a verb or a noun in your sentence, and read only that section. Then go down the numbered senses until one fits; the example in quotation marks shows the word in action. Take a synonym only from the sense you mean. More help is in how to read a word page.
