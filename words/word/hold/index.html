Hold: meaning, synonyms and examples

Hold is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (9 senses). Its most common meaning is: Keep in a certain state, position, or activity; e.g., "keep clean".

In Hindi
पकड़ना, थामना
Synonyms
keep, maintain, take hold, throw, have, make, give, have got, deem, view as
Opposites
let go of
Word family
holding, holder

Hold as a verb

  1. Keep in a certain state, position, or activity; e.g., "keep clean".

    “Keep clean.” “Hold in place.” “She always held herself as a lady.”

    Synonyms: keep, maintain · Kinds: pressurize, continue, hold over, conserve, preserve, distance, housekeep

  2. Have or hold in one's hands or grip.

    “Hold this bowl for a moment, please.” “A crazy idea took hold of him.”

    Synonyms: take hold · Opposite: let go of · Kinds: grasp, cling to, cradle, clinch, lock, trap, cradle

  3. Organize or be responsible for.

    “Hold a reception.” “Have, throw, or make a party.” “Give a course.”

    Synonyms: throw, have, make, give · A kind of: direct

  4. Have or possess, either in a concrete or an abstract sense.

    “She has $1,000 in the bank.” “He has got two beautiful daughters.” “She holds a Master's degree from Harvard.”

    Synonyms: have, have got · Kinds: sustain, keep, keep, keep, keep, monopolize, wield, stock

  5. Keep in mind or convey as a conviction or view.

    “Take for granted.” “View as important.” “Hold these truths to be self-evident.”

    Synonyms: deem, view as, take for · A kind of: see

  6. Maintain (a theory, thoughts, or feelings).

    “Bear a grudge.” “Entertain interesting notions.” “Harbor a resentment.”

    Synonyms: harbor, harbour, entertain, nurse · A kind of: feel

  7. To close within bounds, limit or hold back from movement.

    “This holds the local until the express passengers change trains.” “About a dozen animals were held inside the stockade.” “The illegal immigrants were held at a detention center.”

    Synonyms: restrain, confine · A kind of: disable · Kinds: tie down, fetter, enchain, pinion, impound, pound, pen up, ground

  8. Secure and keep for possible future use or application.

    “The landlord retained the security deposit.” “I reserve the right to disagree.”

    Synonyms: retain, keep back, hold back · A kind of: keep · Kinds: hold down

  9. Have rightfully; of rights, titles, and offices.

    “She bears the title of Duchess.” “He held the governorship for almost a decade.”

    Synonyms: bear · A kind of: have

  10. Be the physical support of; carry the weight of.

    “The beam holds up the roof.” “He supported me with one hand while I balanced on the beam.” “What's holding that mirror?”

    Synonyms: support, sustain, hold up · Kinds: scaffold, block, carry, chock, buoy, pole, bracket, underpin

  11. Contain or hold; have within.

    “The canteen holds fresh water.” “This can contains water.”

    Synonyms: bear, carry, contain · A kind of: include · Kinds: retain, enclose

  12. Have room for; hold without crowding.

    “This hotel can accommodate 250 guests.” “The theater admits 300 people.” “The auditorium can't hold more than 500 people.”

    Synonyms: accommodate, admit · Kinds: sleep, house, seat

  13. Remain in a certain state, position, or condition.

    “The weather held.” “They held on the road and kept marching.”

    A kind of: continue

  14. Support or hold in a certain manner.

    “She holds her head high.” “He carried himself upright.”

    Synonyms: carry, bear · Kinds: sling, stoop, piggyback, poise, behave

Hold as a noun

  1. The act of grasping.

    “He released his clasp on my arm.” “He has a strong grip for an old man.” “She kept a firm hold on the railing.”

    Synonyms: clasp, clench, clutch, clutches, grasp, grip · A kind of: grasping · Kinds: chokehold, embrace, wrestling hold

  2. Understanding of the nature or meaning or quality or magnitude of something.

    “He has a good grasp of accounting practices.”

    Synonyms: appreciation, grasp · A kind of: understanding · Kinds: sense

  3. Power by which something or someone is affected or dominated.

    “He has a hold over them.”

    A kind of: control

  4. Time during which some action is awaited.

    “Instant replay caused too long a delay.” “He ordered a hold in the action.”

    Synonyms: delay, time lag, postponement, wait · A kind of: pause · Kinds: extension, moratorium, retardation

  5. A state of being confined (usually for a short time).

    “His detention was politically motivated.” “The prisoner is on hold.” “He is in the custody of police.”

    Synonyms: detention, detainment, custody · A kind of: confinement

  6. A stronghold.

    A kind of: stronghold

  7. A cell in a jail or prison.

    Synonyms: keep · A kind of: cell

  8. The appendage to an object that is designed to be held in order to use or move it.

    “He grabbed the hammer by the handle.” “It was an old briefcase but it still had a good grip.”

    Synonyms: handle, grip, handgrip · A kind of: appendage · Kinds: ax handle, broomstick, crop, haft, hilt, hoe handle, knob, mop handle

  9. The space in a ship or aircraft for storing cargo.

    Synonyms: cargo area, cargo deck, cargo hold, storage area · A kind of: enclosure

Phrases and compounds with "hold"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • hold in (verb) Close in; darkness enclosed him".
  • hold on (verb) Hold firmly.
  • hold up (verb) Be the physical support of; carry the weight of.
  • get hold (verb) Get something or somebody for a specific purpose.
  • hold off (verb) Resist and fight to a standoff.
  • hold out (verb) Thrust or extend out.
  • hold back (verb) Hold back, as of a danger or an enemy; check the expansion or influence of.
  • hold dear (verb) Be fond of; be attached to.
  • hold down (verb) Keep.
  • hold fast (verb) Stick to firmly.
  • hold firm (verb) Refuse to abandon one's opinion or belief.
  • hold open (verb) Retain rights to.
  • hold over (verb) Intimidate somebody (with a threat).
  • hold sway (verb) Be master; reign or rule.
  • hold-down (noun) A limitation or constraint.
  • take hold (verb) Assume control.
