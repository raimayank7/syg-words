Hot: meaning, synonyms and examples

Hot is used as an adjective (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: Used of physical heat; having a high or higher than desirable temperature or giving off heat or feeling or causing a sensation of heat or burning.

In Hindi
गरम; तीखा
Synonyms
raging, blistering, red-hot, spicy, live
Opposites
cold
Word family
hotness

Hot as an adjective

  1. Used of physical heat; having a high or higher than desirable temperature or giving off heat or feeling or causing a sensation of heat or burning.

    “Hot stove.” “Hot water.” “A hot August day.”

    Opposite: cold · Similar: baking, blistering, calefacient, calefactory, calorifacient, calorific

  2. Characterized by violent and forceful activity or movement; very intense.

    “The fighting became hot and heavy.” “A hot engagement.” “A raging battle.”

    Synonyms: raging · Similar: violent

  3. Extended meanings; especially of psychological heat; marked by intensity or vehemence especially of passion or enthusiasm.

    “A hot temper.” “A hot topic.” “A hot new book.”

    Opposite: cold · Similar: fiery, heated, red-hot, torrid, white-hot

  4. (color) bold and intense.

    “Hot pink.”

    Similar: warm

  5. Recently stolen or smuggled.

    “Hot merchandise.” “A hot car.”

    Similar: illegal

  6. Very fast; capable of quick response and great speed.

    “A hot sports car.” “A blistering pace.” “Got off to a hot start.”

    Synonyms: blistering, red-hot · Similar: fast

  7. Wanted by the police.

    “A hot suspect.”

    Similar: wanted

  8. Producing a burning sensation on the taste nerves.

    “Hot salsa.” “Jalapeno peppers are very hot.”

    Synonyms: spicy · Similar: tasty

  9. Performed or performing with unusually great skill and daring and energy.

    “A hot drummer.” “He's hot tonight.”

    Similar: skilled

  10. Very popular or successful.

    “One of the hot young talents.” “Cabbage patch dolls were hot last season.”

    Similar: popular

  11. Very unpleasant or even dangerous.

    “Make it hot for him.” “In the hot seat.” “In hot water.”

    Similar: unpleasant

  12. Newest or most recent.

    “News hot off the press.” “Red-hot information.”

    Synonyms: red-hot · Similar: new

  13. Having or bringing unusually good luck.

    “Hot at craps.” “The dice are hot tonight.”

    Similar: lucky

  14. Very good; often used in the negative.

    “He's hot at math but not so hot at history.”

    Similar: good

Phrases and compounds with "hot"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • hot up (verb) Gain heat or get hot.
  • hot air (noun) Air that has been heated and tends to rise.
  • hot dog (noun) Someone who performs dangerous stunts to attract attention to himself.
  • hot pad (noun) A pad for use under a hot dish to protect a table.
  • hot pot (noun) A stew of meat and potatoes cooked in a tightly covered pot.
  • hot rod (noun) A car modified to increase its speed and acceleration.
  • hot tub (noun) A very large tub (large enough for more than one bather) filled with hot water.
  • hot war (noun) Actual fighting between the warring parties.
  • hot-dog (verb) Perform intricate maneuvers while skiing.
  • hot-rod (noun) A car modified to increase its speed and acceleration.
  • red hot (noun) A frankfurter served hot on a bun.
  • hot cake (noun) A flat cake of thin batter fried on both sides on a griddle.
  • hot jazz (noun) Jazz that is emotionally charged and intense and marked by strong rhythms and improvisation.
  • hot line (noun) A direct telephone line between two officials.
  • hot seat (noun) An instrument of execution by electrocution; resembles an ordinary seat for one person.
  • hot spot (noun) A place of political unrest and potential violence.
