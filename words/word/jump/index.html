Jump: meaning, synonyms and examples

Jump is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: Move forward by leaps and bounds.

In Hindi
कूदना
Synonyms
leap, bound, spring, startle, start, leap out, jump out, stand out, stick out, rise
Word family
jumping, jumpy

Jump as a verb

  1. Move forward by leaps and bounds.

    “The horse bounded across the meadow.” “The child leapt across the puddle.” “Can you jump over the fence?”

    Synonyms: leap, bound, spring · A kind of: move · Kinds: pronk, bounce, burst, bounce, capriole, galumph, ski jump, saltate

  2. Move or jump suddenly, as if in surprise or alarm.

    “She startled when I walked into the room.”

    Synonyms: startle, start · A kind of: move · Kinds: shy, boggle, rear back, jackrabbit

  3. Make a sudden physical attack on.

    “The muggers jumped the woman in the fur coat.”

    A kind of: assail

  4. Increase suddenly and significantly.

    “Prices jumped overnight.”

    A kind of: wax

  5. Be highly noticeable.

    Synonyms: leap out, jump out, stand out, stick out · A kind of: look

  6. Enter eagerly into.

    “He jumped into the game.”

    A kind of: enter

  7. Rise in rank or status.

    “Her new novel jumped high on the bestseller list.”

    Synonyms: rise, climb up · A kind of: change

  8. Jump down from an elevated point.

    “The parachutist didn't want to jump.” “Every year, hundreds of people jump off the Golden Gate bridge.” “The widow leapt into the funeral pyre.”

    Synonyms: leap, jump off · A kind of: move

  9. Run off or leave the rails.

    “The train derailed because a cow was standing on the tracks.”

    Synonyms: derail · A kind of: travel

  10. Jump from an airplane and descend with a parachute.

    Synonyms: chute, parachute · A kind of: dive · Kinds: sky dive

  11. Cause to jump or leap.

    “The trainer jumped the tiger through the hoop.”

    Synonyms: leap

  12. Start (a car engine whose battery is dead) by connecting it to another car's battery.

    Synonyms: jumpstart, jump-start · A kind of: start

  13. Bypass.

    “He skipped a row in the text and so the sentence was incomprehensible.”

    Synonyms: pass over, skip, skip over · A kind of: neglect

  14. Pass abruptly from one state or topic to another.

    “Leap into fame.” “Jump to a conclusion.” “Jump from one thing to another.”

    Synonyms: leap · A kind of: switch

Jump as a noun

  1. A sudden and decisive increase.

    “A jump in attendance.”

    Synonyms: leap · A kind of: increase · Kinds: quantum leap

  2. An abrupt transition.

    “A successful leap from college to the major leagues.”

    Synonyms: leap, saltation · A kind of: transition · Kinds: quantum jump

  3. (film) an abrupt transition from one scene to another.

    A kind of: transition

  4. A sudden involuntary movement.

    “He awoke with a start.”

    Synonyms: startle, start · A kind of: reflex · Kinds: startle response, startle reflex, wince

  5. Descent with a parachute.

    “He had done a lot of parachuting in the army.”

    Synonyms: parachuting · A kind of: descent · Kinds: skydiving

  6. The act of jumping; propelling yourself off the ground.

    “He advanced in a series of jumps.” “The jumping was unexpected.”

    Synonyms: jumping · A kind of: propulsion · Kinds: header, hop, leap, vault, jumping up and down, capriole

Phrases and compounds with "jump"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • jump on (verb) Get up on the back of.
  • jump cut (noun) An immediate transition from one scene to another.
  • jump off (verb) Set off quickly, usually with success.
  • jump out (verb) Be highly noticeable.
  • ski jump (noun) A steep downward ramp from which skiers jump.
  • high jump (noun) A competition that involves jumping as high as possible over a horizontal bar.
  • jump ball (noun) (basketball) the way play begins or resumes when possession is disputed; an official tosses the ball up between two players who jump in an effort to tap it to a teammate.
  • jump rope (noun) A length of rope (usually with handles on each end) that is swung around while someone jumps over it.
  • jump seat (noun) A folding seat in an automobile.
  • jump shot (noun) (basketball) a player releases the basketball at the high point of a jump.
  • jump suit (noun) One-piece garment fashioned after a parachutist's uniform.
  • long jump (noun) A competition that involves jumping as far as possible from a running start.
  • pole jump (noun) A competition that involves jumping over a high crossbar with the aid of a long pole.
  • broad jump (noun) A competition that involves jumping as far as possible from a running start.
  • jump-start (noun) Starting an automobile engine that has a weak battery by means of jumper cables to another car.
  • water jump (noun) A pool or stream in a steeplechase or similar contest.
