Key: meaning, synonyms and examples

Key is used as a noun (14 senses), adjective (1 sense) and verb (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: Metal device shaped in such a way that when it is inserted into the appropriate lock the lock's mechanism can be rotated.

In Hindi
चाबी; मुख्य
Synonyms
tonality, samara, key fruit, cay, paint, winder, keystone, headstone, cardinal, central

Key as a noun

  1. Metal device shaped in such a way that when it is inserted into the appropriate lock the lock's mechanism can be rotated.

    A kind of: device · Kinds: ignition key, latchkey, passkey

  2. Something crucial for explaining.

    “The key to development is economic integration.”

    A kind of: explanation

  3. Pitch of the voice.

    “He spoke in a low key.”

    A kind of: pitch

  4. Any of 24 major or minor diatonic scales that provide the tonal framework for a piece of music.

    Synonyms: tonality · A kind of: musical notation · Kinds: major key, minor key, tonic key

  5. A winged often one-seed indehiscent fruit as of the ash or elm or maple.

    Synonyms: samara, key fruit · A kind of: achene

  6. United States lawyer and poet who wrote a poem after witnessing the British attack on Baltimore during the War of 1812; the poem was later set to music and entitled `The Star-Spangled Banner' (1779-1843).

  7. A coral reef off the southern coast of Florida.

    Synonyms: cay · A kind of: coral reef

  8. (basketball) a space (including the foul line) in front of the basket at each end of a basketball court; usually painted a different color from the rest of the court.

    “He hit a jump shot from the top of the key.” “He dominates play in the paint.”

    Synonyms: paint · A kind of: space

  9. A list of answers to a test.

    “Some students had stolen the key to the final exam.”

    A kind of: list

  10. A list of words or phrases that explain symbols or abbreviations.

    A kind of: list · Kinds: key word

  11. A generic term for any device whose possession entitles the holder to a means of access.

    “A safe-deposit box usually requires two keys to open it.”

    A kind of: positive identification

  12. Mechanical device used to wind another device that is driven by a spring (as a clock).

    Synonyms: winder · A kind of: mechanical device · Kinds: watch key

  13. The central building block at the top of an arch or vault.

    Synonyms: keystone, headstone · A kind of: building block · Kinds: quoin

  14. A lever (as in a keyboard) that actuates a mechanism when depressed.

    A kind of: lever · Kinds: backspace key, control key, return key, shift key, space bar, tab key, telegraph key

Key as an adjective

  1. Serving as an essential component.

    “A cardinal rule.” “The central cause of the problem.” “An example that was fundamental to the argument.”

    Synonyms: cardinal, central, fundamental, primal · Similar: important

Key as a verb

  1. Identify as in botany or biology, for example.

    Synonyms: identify, discover, key out, distinguish, describe, name

  2. Provide with a key.

    “We were keyed after the locks were changed in the building.”

    A kind of: supply

  3. Vandalize a car by scratching the sides with a key.

    “His new Mercedes was keyed last night in the parking lot.”

    A kind of: vandalize

  4. Regulate the musical pitch of.

    A kind of: harmonize

  5. Harmonize with or adjust to.

    “Key one's actions to the voters' prevailing attitude.”

    A kind of: harmonize

Phrases and compounds with "key"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • on-key (adjective) In tune; accurate in pitch.
  • ash-key (noun) Winged seed of the ash tree.
  • key out (verb) Identify as in botany or biology, for example.
  • low-key (adjective) Restrained in style or quality.
  • off-key (adjective) Inaccurate in pitch.
  • tab key (noun) The key on a typewriter or a word processor that causes a tabulation.
  • home key (noun) The basic key in which a piece of music is written.
  • key lime (noun) Small yellow-green limes of southern Florida.
  • key palm (noun) Small stocky fan palm of southern Florida and Cuba.
  • key ring (noun) A circular ring of metal for holding keys.
  • key west (noun) A town on the westernmost of the Florida keys in the Gulf of Mexico.
  • key word (noun) A word that is used as a pattern to decode an encrypted message.
  • greek key (noun) An ornamental pattern consisting of repeated vertical and horizontal lines (often in relief).
  • key fruit (noun) A winged often one-seed indehiscent fruit as of the ash or elm or maple.
  • major key (noun) A key whose harmony is based on the major scale.
  • minor key (noun) A key based on the minor scale.
