Labor: meaning, synonyms and examples

Labor is used as a noun (6 senses) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: A social class comprising those who do manual labor or work for wages.

In Hindi
श्रम, मज़दूरी
Synonyms
labour, working class, proletariat, toil, labor movement, trade union movement, undertaking, project, task, tug
Word family
laborious, laborer

Labor as a noun

  1. A social class comprising those who do manual labor or work for wages.

    “There is a shortage of skilled labor in this field.”

    Synonyms: labour, working class, proletariat · A kind of: class · Kinds: labor force, lumpenproletariat, organized labor

  2. Productive work (especially physical work done for wages).

    “His labor did not require a great deal of skill.”

    Synonyms: labour, toil · A kind of: work · Kinds: roping, corvee, drudgery, effort, hackwork, haymaking, manual labor, overwork

  3. An organized attempt by workers to improve their status by united action (particularly via labor unions) or the leaders of this movement.

    Synonyms: labor movement, trade union movement · A kind of: reform movement · Kinds: unionism

  4. A political party formed in Great Britain in 1900; characterized by the promotion of labor's interests and formerly the socialization of key industries.

    A kind of: labor party

  5. The federal department responsible for promoting the working conditions of wage earners in the United States; created in 1913.

    A kind of: executive department

  6. Any piece of work that is undertaken or attempted.

    “He prepared for great undertakings.”

    Synonyms: undertaking, project, task · A kind of: work · Kinds: cinch, adventure, assignment, baby, enterprise, labor of love, marathon, no-brainer

Labor as a verb

  1. Strive and make an effort to reach a goal.

    “She tugged for years to make a decent living.” “We have to push a little to make the deadline!” “She is driving away at her doctoral thesis.”

    Synonyms: tug, labour, push, drive · A kind of: fight · Kinds: strive

  2. Work hard.

    “She was digging away at her math homework.” “Lexicographers drudge all day long.”

    Synonyms: labour, toil, fag, travail, grind, drudge, dig, moil · A kind of: work

  3. Undergo the efforts of childbirth.

    Synonyms: labour · A kind of: undergo

Phrases and compounds with "labor"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • labor day (noun) First Monday in September in the United States and Canada.
  • labor camp (noun) A penal institution for political prisoners who are used as forced labor.
  • labor pain (noun) Pain and discomfort associated with contractions of the uterus during labor.
  • labor pool (noun) The source of trained people from which workers can be hired.
  • labor coach (noun) An assistant (often the father of the soon-to-be-born child) who provides support for a woman in labor by encouraging her to use techniques learned in childbirth-preparation classes.
  • labor force (noun) The source of trained people from which workers can be hired.
  • labor pains (noun) A regularly recurrent spasm of pain that is characteristic of childbirth.
  • labor party (noun) A left-of-center political party formed to represent the interest of ordinary working people.
  • labor union (noun) An organization of employees formed to bargain with the employer.
  • bonded labor (noun) A practice in which employers give high-interest loans to workers whose entire families then labor at low wages to pay off the debt; the practice is illegal in the United States.
  • labor leader (noun) A leader of a labor movement.
  • labor market (noun) The market in which workers compete for jobs and employers compete for workers.
  • manual labor (noun) Labor done with the hands.
  • labor of love (noun) Productive work performed voluntarily without material reward or compensation.
  • labor contract (noun) Contract between labor and management governing wages and benefits and working conditions.
  • labor movement (noun) An organized attempt by workers to improve their status by united action (particularly via labor unions) or the leaders of this movement.
