Level: meaning, synonyms and examples

Level is used as a noun (8 senses), verb (6 senses) and adjective (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: A position on a scale of intensity or amount or quality.

In Hindi
स्तर; समतल
Synonyms
degree, grade, tier, stage, point, spirit level, horizontal surface, layer, stratum, floor
Opposites
raise
Word family
leveller, leveler

Level as a noun

  1. A position on a scale of intensity or amount or quality.

    “A moderate grade of intelligence.” “A high level of care is required.” “It is all a matter of degree.”

    Synonyms: degree, grade · A kind of: property · Kinds: quality, intensity, grind, depth, highness, high, low, lowness

  2. A relative position or degree of value in a graded group.

    “Lumber of the highest grade.”

    Synonyms: grade, tier · A kind of: rank · Kinds: biosafety level, college level

  3. A specific identifiable position in a continuum or series or especially in a process.

    “A remarkable degree of frankness.” “At what stage are the social sciences?”

    Synonyms: degree, stage, point · A kind of: state · Kinds: ladder, acme, extent, resultant, standard of living, plane, state of the art, ultimacy

  4. Height above ground.

    “The water reached ankle level.” “The pictures were at the same level.”

    A kind of: altitude

  5. Indicator that establishes the horizontal when a bubble is centered in a tube of liquid.

    Synonyms: spirit level · A kind of: indicator · Kinds: mason's level

  6. A flat surface at right angles to a plumb line.

    “Park the car on the level.”

    Synonyms: horizontal surface · A kind of: surface · Kinds: floor, paved surface, platform

  7. An abstract place usually conceived as having depth.

    “A good actor communicates on several levels.” “A simile has at least two layers of meaning.” “The mind functions on many strata simultaneously.”

    Synonyms: layer, stratum · A kind of: place

  8. A structure consisting of a room or set of rooms at a single position along a vertical scale.

    “What level is the office on?”

    Synonyms: floor, storey, story · A kind of: structure · Kinds: basement, ground floor, loft, loft, mezzanine

Level as a verb

  1. Aim at.

    “Level criticism or charges at somebody.”

    A kind of: aim

  2. Tear down so as to make flat with the ground.

    “The building was levelled.”

    Synonyms: raze, rase, dismantle, tear down, take down, pull down · Opposite: raise · A kind of: destroy · Kinds: bulldoze

  3. Make level or straight.

    “Level the ground.”

    Synonyms: flush, even out, even · A kind of: change surface · Kinds: grade, strickle, strickle

  4. Direct into a position for use.

    “Point a gun.” “He charged his weapon at me.”

    Synonyms: charge, point · A kind of: aim

  5. Talk frankly with; lay it on the line.

    “I have to level with you.”

    A kind of: talk

  6. Become level or even.

    “The ground levelled off.”

    Synonyms: level off · A kind of: change surface

Level as an adjective

  1. Having a surface without slope, tilt in which no part is higher or lower than another.

    “A flat desk.” “Acres of level farmland.” “A plane surface.”

    Synonyms: flat, plane · Similar: even

  2. Not showing abrupt variations.

    “Spoke in a level voice.” “She gave him a level look.”

    Synonyms: unwavering · Similar: steady

  3. Being on a precise horizontal plane.

    “A billiard table must be level.”

    Similar: horizontal

  4. Oriented at right angles to the plumb.

    “The picture is level.”

    Similar: even

  5. Of the score in a contest.

    “The score is tied.”

    Synonyms: tied, even · Similar: equal

Phrases and compounds with "level"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • a level (noun) The advanced level of a subject taken in school (usually two years after O level).
  • o level (noun) The basic level of a subject taken in school.
  • level off (verb) Become level or even.
  • low-level (adjective) Not intense.
  • sea level (noun) Level of the ocean's surface (especially that halfway between mean high and low tide); used as a standard in reckoning land elevation or sea depth.
  • sea-level (adjective) Lying below the normal level.
  • fuel level (noun) The amount of fuel remaining.
  • high-level (adjective) At an elevated level in rank or importance.
  • level best (noun) The greatest possible degree.
  • abney level (noun) A surveying instrument consisting of a spirit level and a sighting tube; used to measure the angle of inclination of a line from the observer to the target.
  • dumpy level (noun) A surveyor's level having a short telescope fixed to a horizontally rotating table and a spirit level.
  • noise level (noun) The amplitude level of the undesired background noise.
  • plumb level (noun) A carpenter's level with a plumb line at right angles to it.
  • price level (noun) An index that traces the relative changes in the price of an individual good (or a market basket of goods) over time.
  • upper-level (adjective) At an elevated level in rank or importance.
  • water level (noun) The level of the surface of a body of water.
