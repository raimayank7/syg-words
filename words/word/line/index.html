Line: meaning, synonyms and examples

Line is used as a noun (14 senses) and verb (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: A formation of people or things one beside another.

In Hindi
रेखा, पंक्ति
Synonyms
argumentation, logical argument, argument, line of reasoning, cable, transmission line, course, wrinkle, furrow, crease
Word family
lineal, liner, lining, lineation

Line as a noun

  1. A formation of people or things one beside another.

    “The line of soldiers advanced with their bayonets fixed.” “They were arrayed in line of battle.” “The cast stood in line for the curtain call.”

    A kind of: formation · Kinds: rivet line, chorus, diagonal, linemen, lineup, row, rank

  2. A mark that is long relative to its width.

    “He drew a line on the chart.”

    A kind of: mark · Kinds: cross hair, dotted line, ascender, bar line, descender, squiggle, spectrum line, trend line

  3. A formation of people or things one behind another.

    “The line stretched clear around the corner.” “You must wait in a long line at the checkout counter.”

    A kind of: formation · Kinds: diagonal, file, line of march, line of succession, picket line, conga line, trap line, queue

  4. A length (straight or curved) without breadth or thickness; the trace of a moving point.

    A kind of: shape · Kinds: curve, curve, straight line, geodesic, perimeter, centerline, boundary

  5. Text consisting of a row of words written across a page or computer screen.

    “The letter consisted of three short lines.” “There are six lines in every stanza.”

    A kind of: text · Kinds: opening line, heading, line of poetry, by-line, dateline, verse, punch line, actor's line

  6. A single frequency (or very narrow band) of radiation in a spectrum.

    A kind of: electromagnetic radiation

  7. A fortified position (especially one marking the most forward position of troops).

    “They attacked the enemy's line.”

    A kind of: military position · Kinds: firing line, battlefront, line of battle, battle line

  8. A course of reasoning aimed at demonstrating a truth or falsehood; the methodical process of logical reasoning.

    “I can't follow your line of reasoning.”

    Synonyms: argumentation, logical argument, argument, line of reasoning · A kind of: reasoning · Kinds: line of inquiry, casuistry, policy

  9. A conductor for transmitting electrical or optical signals or electric power.

    Synonyms: cable, transmission line · A kind of: conductor · Kinds: coaxial cable, electrical cable, fiber optic cable, power line, printer cable

  10. A connected series of events or actions or developments.

    “The government took a firm course.” “Historians can only point out those lines for which evidence is available.”

    Synonyms: course · A kind of: series · Kinds: stream

  11. A spatial location defined by a real or imaginary unidimensional extent.

    A kind of: location · Kinds: part, axis, aclinic line, agonic line, isogonic line, crest, timber line, snow line

  12. A slight depression in the smoothness of a surface.

    “His face has many lines.” “Ironing gets rid of most wrinkles.”

    Synonyms: wrinkle, furrow, crease, crinkle, seam · A kind of: depression · Kinds: crow's foot, dermatoglyphic, frown line, line of life, line of heart, line of fate

  13. A pipe used to transport liquids or gases.

    “A pipeline runs from the wells to the seaport.”

    Synonyms: pipeline · A kind of: pipe · Kinds: gas line, oil pipeline

  14. The road consisting of railroad track and roadbed.

    Synonyms: railway line, rail line · A kind of: road · Kinds: branch line, trunk line

Line as a verb

  1. Be in line with; form a line along.

    “Trees line the riverbank.”

    Synonyms: run along · A kind of: lie · Kinds: rim

  2. Cover the interior of.

    “Line the gloves.” “Line a chimney.”

    A kind of: cover · Kinds: reline, face, crib, babbitt

  3. Make a mark or lines on a surface.

    “Draw a line.” “Trace the outline of a figure in the sand.”

    Synonyms: trace, draw, describe, delineate · A kind of: mark · Kinds: construct, inscribe, circumscribe, circumscribe, write

  4. Mark with lines.

    “Sorrow had lined his face.”

    A kind of: score · Kinds: reline, hatch, crisscross

  5. Fill plentifully.

    “Line one's pockets.”

    A kind of: fill

  6. Reinforce with fabric.

    “Lined books are more enduring.”

    A kind of: reinforce

Phrases and compounds with "line"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • a-line (noun) Women's clothing that has a fitted top and a flared skirt that is widest at the hemline.
  • by-line (noun) A line giving the name of the writer of a story or article.
  • in line (adjective) Being next in a line of succession.
  • line up (verb) Form a line.
  • on-line (adjective) On a regular route of a railroad or bus or airline system.
  • bar line (noun) A vertical line before the accented beat marking the boundary between musical bars.
  • bus line (noun) An organization responsible for operating a bus transportation system.
  • gag line (noun) The point of a joke or humorous story.
  • gas line (noun) A queue of vehicles waiting to purchase gasoline.
  • hot line (noun) A direct telephone line between two officials.
  • log line (noun) A knotted cord that runs out from a reel to a piece of wood that is attached to it.
  • new line (noun) The operation that prepares for the next character to be printed or displayed as the first character on the next line.
  • off-line (adjective) Not on a regular route of a transportation system.
  • old-line (adjective) Adhering to conservative or reactionary principles.
  • red line (noun) A line that is colored red and that bisects an ice hockey rink.
  • tag line (noun) The point of a joke or humorous story.
