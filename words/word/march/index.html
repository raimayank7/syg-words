March: meaning, synonyms and examples

March is used as a verb (7 senses) and noun (7 senses). Its most common meaning is: March in a procession.

In Hindi
मार्च करना, कूच; मार्च (महीना)
Synonyms
process, demonstrate, parade, exhibit, border, adjoin, edge, abut, butt, butt against
Word family
marching, marcher

March as a verb

  1. March in a procession.

    “They processed into the dining room.”

    Synonyms: process · A kind of: walk · Kinds: file, parade, goose step, countermarch, debouch

  2. Force to march.

    “The Japanese marched their prisoners through Manchuria.”

    A kind of: walk · Kinds: frogmarch

  3. Walk fast, with regular or measured steps; walk with a stride.

    “He marched into the classroom and announced the exam.” “The soldiers marched across the border.”

    A kind of: walk · Kinds: troop

  4. March in protest; take part in a demonstration.

    “Thousands demonstrated against globalization during the meeting of the most powerful economic nations in Seattle.”

    Synonyms: demonstrate · A kind of: protest · Kinds: picket

  5. Walk ostentatiously.

    “She parades her new husband around town.”

    Synonyms: parade, exhibit · A kind of: walk

  6. Cause to march or go at a marching pace.

    “They marched the mules into the desert.”

    A kind of: walk

  7. Lie adjacent to another or share a boundary.

    “Canada adjoins the U.S.” “England marches with Scotland.”

    Synonyms: border, adjoin, edge, abut, butt, butt against, butt on · A kind of: touch · Kinds: neighbor

March as a noun

  1. The month following February and preceding April.

  2. The act of marching; walking with regular steps (especially in a procession of some kind).

    “It was a long march.” “We heard the sound of marching.”

    Synonyms: marching · A kind of: walk · Kinds: countermarch, goose step, lockstep, promenade, quick march, routemarch

  3. A steady advance.

    “The march of science.” “The march of time.”

    A kind of: progress

  4. A procession of people walking together.

    “The march went up Fifth Avenue.”

    A kind of: procession · Kinds: hunger march

  5. District consisting of the area on either side of a border or boundary of a country or an area.

    “The Welsh marches between England and Wales.”

    Synonyms: borderland, border district, marchland · A kind of: district

  6. Genre of music written for marching.

    “Sousa wrote the best marches.”

    Synonyms: marching music · A kind of: music genre · Kinds: military march, processional march

  7. A degree granted for the successful completion of advanced study of architecture.

    A kind of: master's degree

Phrases and compounds with "march"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • march on (verb) Move forward, also in the metaphorical sense.
  • march out (verb) March out (as from a defile) into open ground.
  • mid-march (noun) The middle part of March.
  • dead march (noun) A slow march to be played for funeral processions.
  • march king (noun) A United States bandmaster and composer of military marches (1854-1932).
  • peace march (noun) A protest march against (a particular) war and in favor of peace.
  • quick march (noun) Marching at quick time.
  • hunger march (noun) A march of protest or demonstration by the unemployed.
  • funeral march (noun) A slow march to be played for funeral processions.
  • line of march (noun) The route along which a column advances.
  • march equinox (noun) March 21.
  • protest march (noun) Occasion when you can express opposition by marching (usually on some government institution) without a license.
  • wedding march (noun) A march to be played for a wedding procession.
  • military march (noun) Brisk marching music suitable for troops marching in a military parade.
  • recessional march (noun) A march to be played for processions.
  • processional march (noun) A march to be played for processions.
