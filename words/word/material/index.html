Material: meaning, synonyms and examples

Material is used as a noun (5 senses) and adjective (6 senses). Its most common meaning is: The tangible substance that goes into the makeup of a physical object.

In Hindi
सामग्री; भौतिक
Synonyms
stuff, fabric, cloth, textile, corporeal, substantial, real
Opposites
immaterial
Word family
materiality

Material as a noun

  1. The tangible substance that goes into the makeup of a physical object.

    “Coal is a hard black material.” “Wheat is the stuff they use to make bread.”

    Synonyms: stuff · A kind of: substance · Kinds: ballast, bedding material, rind, precursor, atom, ammunition, floccule, aggregate

  2. Information (data or ideas or observations) that can be used or reworked into a finished form.

    “The archives provided rich material for a definitive biography.”

    A kind of: information · Kinds: rehash, copy

  3. Artifact made by weaving or felting or knitting or crocheting natural or synthetic fibers.

    “The fabric in the curtains was light and semitransparent.” “Woven cloth originated in Mesopotamia around 5000 BC.” “She measured off enough material for a dress.”

    Synonyms: fabric, cloth, textile · A kind of: artifact · Kinds: aba, acrylic, alpaca, baize, basket weave, batik, batiste, belting

  4. Things needed for doing or making something.

    “Writing materials.” “Useful teaching materials.”

    A kind of: equipment · Kinds: packaging, railing, roofing

  5. A person judged suitable for admission or employment.

    “He was university material.” “She was vice-presidential material.”

    A kind of: applicant

Material as an adjective

  1. Concerned with worldly rather than spiritual interests.

    “Material possessions.” “Material wealth.” “Material comforts.”

    Similar: worldly

  2. Derived from or composed of matter.

    “The material universe.”

    Opposite: immaterial · Similar: physical, physical

  3. Directly relevant to a matter especially a law case.

    “His support made a material difference.” “Evidence material to the issue at hand.” “Facts likely to influence the judgment are called material facts.”

    Opposite: immaterial · Similar: crucial

  4. Concerned with or affecting physical as distinct from intellectual or psychological well-being.

    “Material needs.” “The moral and material welfare of all good citizens.”

    Similar: physical

  5. Having material or physical form or substance.

    “That which is created is of necessity corporeal and visible and tangible.”

    Synonyms: corporeal · Similar: bodily, bodied, reincarnate

  6. Having substance or capable of being treated as fact; not imaginary.

    “The substantial world.” “A mere dream, neither substantial nor practical.” “Most ponderous and substantial things.”

    Synonyms: substantial, real

Phrases and compounds with "material"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • raw material (noun) Material suitable for manufacture or use or finishing.
  • material body (noun) Alternative names for the body of a human being.
  • plant material (noun) Material derived from plants.
  • waste material (noun) Any materials unused and rejected as worthless or unwanted.
  • animal material (noun) Material derived from animals.
  • material breach (noun) A breach serious enough to destroy the value of the contract and to give a basis for an action for breach of contract.
  • paving material (noun) Material used to pave an area.
  • bedding material (noun) Material used to provide a bed for animals.
  • fencing material (noun) Material for building fences.
  • material witness (noun) A witness whose testimony is both relevant to the matter at issue and required in order to resolve the matter.
  • packing material (noun) Any material used especially to protect something.
  • reading material (noun) Written material intended to be read.
  • roofing material (noun) Building material used in constructing roofs.
  • sealing material (noun) Any substance used to seal joints or fill cracks in a porous surface.
  • sorbent material (noun) A material that sorbs another substance; i.e. that has the capacity or tendency to take it up by either absorption or adsorption.
  • written material (noun) The work of a writer; anything expressed in letters of the alphabet (especially when considered from the point of view of style and effect).
