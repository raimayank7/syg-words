Modern: meaning, synonyms and examples

Modern is used as an adjective (5 senses) and noun (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Belonging to the modern era; since the Middle Ages.

In Hindi
आधुनिक
Synonyms
mod, modernistic, advanced, forward-looking, innovative, modern font
Opposites
nonmodern, old style
Word family
modernity, modernness

Modern as an adjective

  1. Belonging to the modern era; since the Middle Ages.

    “Modern art.” “Modern furniture.” “Modern history.”

    Opposite: nonmodern · Similar: contemporary, neo, red-brick, ultramodern, moderne

  2. Relating to a recently developed fashion or style.

    “Their offices are in a modern skyscraper.” “Tables in modernistic designs.”

    Synonyms: mod, modernistic · Similar: fashionable

  3. Characteristic of present-day art and music and literature and architecture.

    Similar: nonclassical

  4. Ahead of the times.

    “The advanced teaching methods.” “Had advanced views on the subject.” “A forward-looking corporation.”

    Synonyms: advanced, forward-looking, innovative · Similar: progressive

  5. Used of a living language; being the current stage in its development.

    “Modern English.” “New Hebrew is Israeli Hebrew.”

    Similar: late

Modern as a noun

  1. A contemporary person.

    A kind of: person

  2. A typeface (based on an 18th century design by Gianbattista Bodoni) distinguished by regular shape and hairline serifs and heavy downstrokes.

    Synonyms: modern font · Opposite: old style · A kind of: proportional font

Phrases and compounds with "modern"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • modern era (noun) The present or recent times.
  • modern man (noun) Subspecies of Homo sapiens; includes all modern races.
  • modern-day (adjective) Characteristic of the present.
  • modern font (noun) A typeface (based on an 18th century design by Gianbattista Bodoni) distinguished by regular shape and hairline serifs and heavy downstrokes.
  • modern jazz (noun) Any of various styles of jazz that appeared after 1940.
  • modern dance (noun) A style of theatrical dancing that is not as restricted as classical ballet; movements are expressive of feelings.
  • modern greek (noun) The Greek language as spoken and written today.
  • modern times (noun) The circumstances and ideas of the present age.
  • modern world (noun) The circumstances and ideas of the present age.
  • modern ballet (noun) A style of ballet that admits a wider variety of movements.
  • modern hebrew (noun) Hebrew used in Israel today; revived from ancient Hebrew.
  • modern english (noun) English since about 1450.
  • secondary modern school (noun) A former British secondary school emphasizing practical rather than academic education.
