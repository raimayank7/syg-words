Motion: meaning, synonyms and examples

Motion is used as a noun (7 senses) and verb (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: The use of movements (especially of the hands) to communicate familiar or prearranged signals.

In Hindi
गति; प्रस्ताव
Synonyms
gesture, movement, move, motility, question, apparent motion, apparent movement, gesticulate
Opposites
motionlessness
Word family
move, motional

Motion as a noun

  1. The use of movements (especially of the hands) to communicate familiar or prearranged signals.

    Synonyms: gesture · A kind of: visual communication · Kinds: sign, gesticulation, beck, facial expression, flourish, high-five, previous question, shrug

  2. A natural event that involves a change in the position or location of something.

    Synonyms: movement · A kind of: happening · Kinds: crustal movement, approach, passing, deflection, bending, change of location, wave, jitter

  3. A change of position that does not entail a change of location.

    “The reflex motion of his eyebrows revealed his surprise.” “Movement is a sign of life.” “An impatient move of his hand.”

    Synonyms: movement, move, motility · A kind of: change · Kinds: abduction, adduction, agitation, circumduction, disturbance, flit, gesture, headshake

  4. A state of change.

    “They were in a state of steady motion.”

    Opposite: motionlessness · A kind of: state · Kinds: shaking, perpetual motion, precession, stream

  5. A formal proposal for action made to a deliberative assembly for discussion and vote.

    “He made a motion to adjourn.” “She called for the question.”

    Synonyms: question · A kind of: proposal

  6. The act of changing location from one place to another.

    “Police controlled the motion of the crowd.” “The movement of people from the farms to the cities.” “His move put him directly in my path.”

    Synonyms: movement, move · A kind of: change · Kinds: approach, progress, locomotion, lurch, travel, pursuit, rise, descent

  7. An optical illusion of motion produced by viewing a rapid succession of still pictures of a moving object.

    “The cinema relies on apparent motion.” “The succession of flashing lights gave an illusion of movement.”

    Synonyms: apparent motion, apparent movement, movement · A kind of: optical illusion

Motion as a verb

  1. Show, express or direct through movement.

    “He gestured his desire to leave.”

    Synonyms: gesticulate, gesture · A kind of: communicate · Kinds: wink, exsert, shrug, clap, applaud, bless, nod, cross oneself

Phrases and compounds with "motion"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • slow motion (noun) A movie that apparently takes place at a slower than normal speed; achieved by taking the film at a faster rate.
  • axial motion (noun) Rotary motion of an object around its own axis.
  • motion study (noun) An analysis of a specific job in an effort to find the most efficient method in terms of time and effort.
  • law of motion (noun) One of three basic laws of classical mechanics.
  • make a motion (verb) Propose formally; in a debate or parliamentary meeting.
  • onward motion (noun) The act of moving forward (as toward a goal).
  • rotary motion (noun) The act of rotating as if on an axis.
  • set in motion (verb) Get going; give impetus to.
  • forward motion (noun) The act of moving forward (as toward a goal).
  • motion picture (noun) A form of entertainment that enacts a story by sound and a sequence of images giving the illusion of continuous movement.
  • orbital motion (noun) Motion of an object in an orbit around a fixed point.
  • apparent motion (noun) An optical illusion of motion produced by viewing a rapid succession of still pictures of a moving object.
  • brownian motion (noun) The random motion of small particles suspended in a gas or liquid.
  • harmonic motion (noun) A periodic motion in which the displacement is either symmetrical about a point or is the sum of such motions.
  • motion sickness (noun) The state of being dizzy or nauseated because of the motions that occur while traveling in or on a moving vehicle.
  • periodic motion (noun) Motion that recurs over and over and the period of time required for each recurrence remains the same.
