Move: meaning, synonyms and examples

Move is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: Change location; move, travel, or proceed, also metaphorically.

In Hindi
हिलना, चलना; कदम
Synonyms
travel, go, locomote, displace, proceed, be active, act, affect, impress, strike
Opposites
stand still, stay
Word family
motion, movement, mover, moveable, movable

Move as a verb

  1. Change location; move, travel, or proceed, also metaphorically.

    “How fast does your new car go?” “We travelled from Rome to Naples by bus.” “The policemen went from door to door looking for the suspect.”

    Synonyms: travel, go, locomote · Kinds: go around, carry, ease, whish, float, swap, seek, whine

  2. Cause to move or shift into a new position or place, both in a concrete and in an abstract sense.

    “Move those boxes into the corner, please.” “I'm moving my money to another bank.” “The director moved more responsibilities onto his new assistant.”

    Synonyms: displace · Kinds: mobilize, relocate, dislocate, translate, station, agitate, transmit, funnel

  3. Move so as to change position, perform a nontranslational motion.

    “He moved his hand slightly to the right.”

    Opposite: stand still · Kinds: move involuntarily, quicken, stretch, snap, flex, spread, reach, putter

  4. Change residence, affiliation, or place of employment.

    “We moved from Idaho to Nebraska.” “The basketball player moved from one team to another.”

    Opposite: stay · Kinds: relocate, move in, move out, evacuate, migrate, migrate

  5. Follow a procedure or take a course.

    “We should go farther in this matter.” “She went through a lot of trouble.” “Go about the world in a certain manner.”

    Synonyms: go, proceed · A kind of: act · Kinds: work, venture, steamroller

  6. Be in a state of action.

    “She is always moving.”

    Synonyms: be active · Kinds: bestir

  7. Go or proceed from one point to another.

    “The debate moved from family values to the economy.”

    A kind of: change · Kinds: step, scroll, sink

  8. Perform an action, or work out or perform (an action).

    “Think before you act.” “We must move quickly.” “The governor should act on the new energy bill.”

    Synonyms: act · Kinds: alternate, finish up, rush, interrupt, react, go ahead, force, create

  9. Have an emotional or cognitive impact upon.

    “This child impressed me as unusually mature.” “This behavior struck me as odd.”

    Synonyms: affect, impress, strike · Kinds: infect, surprise, impress, awaken, engrave, strike dumb, zap, jar

  10. Give an incentive for action.

    “This moved me to sacrifice my career.”

    Synonyms: motivate, actuate, propel, prompt, incite · A kind of: cause

  11. Arouse sympathy or compassion in.

    “Her fate moved us all.”

    A kind of: affect

  12. Dispose of by selling.

    “The chairman of the company told the salesmen to move the computers.”

    A kind of: sell

  13. Progress by being changed.

    “The speech has to go through several more drafts.” “Run through your presentation before the meeting.”

    Synonyms: go, run · A kind of: change

  14. Live one's life in a specified environment.

    “She moves in certain circles only.”

    A kind of: live

Move as a noun

  1. The act of deciding to do something.

    “He didn't make a move to help.” “His first move was to hire a lawyer.”

    A kind of: decision · Kinds: demarche, maneuver

  2. The act of changing your residence or place of business.

    “They say that three moves equal one fire.”

    Synonyms: relocation · A kind of: change · Kinds: flit, overspill

  3. A change of position that does not entail a change of location.

    “The reflex motion of his eyebrows revealed his surprise.” “Movement is a sign of life.” “An impatient move of his hand.”

    Synonyms: motion, movement, motility · A kind of: change · Kinds: abduction, adduction, agitation, circumduction, disturbance, flit, gesture, headshake

  4. The act of changing location from one place to another.

    “Police controlled the motion of the crowd.” “The movement of people from the farms to the cities.” “His move put him directly in my path.”

    Synonyms: motion, movement · A kind of: change · Kinds: approach, progress, locomotion, lurch, travel, pursuit, rise, descent

  5. (game) a player's turn to take some action permitted by the rules of the game.

    A kind of: turn · Kinds: chess move

Phrases and compounds with "move"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • move in (verb) Occupy a place.
  • move on (verb) Move forward, also in the metaphorical sense.
  • move up (verb) Move to a better position in life or to a better job.
  • move out (verb) Cause to leave.
  • move back (verb) Pull back or move away or backward.
  • move into (verb) To come or go into.
  • move over (verb) Move in order to make room for someone for something.
  • chess move (noun) The act of moving a chess piece.
  • move in on (verb) Make intrusive advances towards.
  • move around (verb) Pass to the other side of.
  • move through (verb) Make a passage or journey from one place to another.
  • opening move (noun) The first of a series of actions.
  • move reflexively (verb) Move in an uncontrolled manner.
  • move involuntarily (verb) Move in an uncontrolled manner.
  • move back and forth (verb) Move in one direction and then into the opposite direction.
