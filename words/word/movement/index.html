Movement: meaning, synonyms and examples

Movement is used as a noun (10 senses). Its most common meaning is: A change of position that does not entail a change of location.

In Hindi
गति, हरकत; आंदोलन
Synonyms
motion, move, motility, social movement, front, campaign, cause, crusade, drive, effort
Word family
move

Movement as a noun

  1. A change of position that does not entail a change of location.

    “The reflex motion of his eyebrows revealed his surprise.” “Movement is a sign of life.” “An impatient move of his hand.”

    Synonyms: motion, move, motility · A kind of: change · Kinds: abduction, adduction, agitation, circumduction, disturbance, flit, gesture, headshake

  2. The act of changing location from one place to another.

    “Police controlled the motion of the crowd.” “The movement of people from the farms to the cities.” “His move put him directly in my path.”

    Synonyms: motion, move · A kind of: change · Kinds: approach, progress, locomotion, lurch, travel, pursuit, rise, descent

  3. A natural event that involves a change in the position or location of something.

    Synonyms: motion · A kind of: happening · Kinds: crustal movement, approach, passing, deflection, bending, change of location, wave, jitter

  4. A group of people with a common ideology who try together to achieve certain general goals.

    “He was a charter member of the movement.” “Politicians have to respect a mass movement.” “He led the national liberation front.”

    Synonyms: social movement, front · A kind of: social group · Kinds: artistic movement, common front, cultural movement, falun gong, political movement, reform movement, religious movement

  5. A major self-contained part of a symphony or sonata.

    “The second movement is slow and melodic.”

    A kind of: musical composition · Kinds: intermezzo, scherzo

  6. A series of actions advancing a principle or tending toward a particular end.

    “He supported populist campaigns.” “They worked in the cause of world peace.” “The team was ready for a drive toward the pennant.”

    Synonyms: campaign, cause, crusade, drive, effort · A kind of: venture · Kinds: advertising campaign, anti-war movement, charm campaign, consumerism, campaigning, fund-raising campaign, feminist movement, lost cause

  7. An optical illusion of motion produced by viewing a rapid succession of still pictures of a moving object.

    “The cinema relies on apparent motion.” “The succession of flashing lights gave an illusion of movement.”

    Synonyms: apparent motion, motion, apparent movement · A kind of: optical illusion

  8. A general tendency to change (as of opinion).

    “Not openly liberal but that is the trend of the book.” “A broad movement of the electorate to the right.”

    Synonyms: drift, trend · A kind of: inclination · Kinds: evolutionary trend, gravitation

  9. The driving and regulating parts of a mechanism (as of a watch or clock).

    “It was an expensive watch with a diamond movement.”

    A kind of: action

  10. The act of changing the location of something.

    “The movement of cargo onto the vessel.”

    A kind of: change · Kinds: displacement, transportation, insertion, lowering, transplant, troop movement

Phrases and compounds with "movement"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • art movement (noun) A group of artists who agree on general principles.
  • eye movement (noun) The movement of the eyes.
  • labor movement (noun) An organized attempt by workers to improve their status by united action (particularly via labor unions) or the leaders of this movement.
  • troop movement (noun) Movement of military units to a new location.
  • youth movement (noun) Political or religious or social reform movement or agitation consisting chiefly of young people.
  • oxford movement (noun) 19th-century movement in the Church of England opposing liberal tendencies.
  • reform movement (noun) A movement intended to bring about social and humanitarian reforms.
  • social movement (noun) A group of people with a common ideology who try together to achieve certain general goals.
  • crustal movement (noun) Movement resulting from or causing deformation of the earth's crust.
  • anti-war movement (noun) A campaign against entering or continuing a war.
  • apparent movement (noun) An optical illusion of motion produced by viewing a rapid succession of still pictures of a moving object.
  • artistic movement (noun) A group of artists who agree on general principles.
  • brownian movement (noun) The random motion of small particles suspended in a gas or liquid.
  • cultural movement (noun) A group of people working together to advance certain cultural goals.
  • feminist movement (noun) The movement aimed at equal rights for women.
  • periodic movement (noun) Motion that recurs over and over and the period of time required for each recurrence remains the same.
