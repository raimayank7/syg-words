Off: meaning, synonyms and examples

Off is used as an adverb (3 senses) and adjective (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: From a particular thing or place or position (`forth' is obsolete).

In Hindi
बंद; दूर
Synonyms
away, forth, cancelled, sour, turned
Opposites
on

Off as an adverb

  1. From a particular thing or place or position (`forth' is obsolete).

    “Ran away from the lion.” “Wanted to get away from there.” “Sent the children away to boarding school.”

    Synonyms: away, forth

  2. At a distance in space or time.

    “The boat was 5 miles off (or away).” “The party is still 2 weeks off (or away).” “Away back in the 18th century.”

    Synonyms: away

  3. No longer on or in contact or attached.

    “Clean off the dirt.” “He shaved off his mustache.”

Off as an adjective

  1. Not in operation or operational.

    “The oven is off.” “The lights are off.”

    Opposite: on · Similar: disconnected

  2. Below a satisfactory level.

    “An off year for tennis.” “His performance was off.”

    Similar: unsatisfactory

  3. (of events) no longer planned or scheduled.

    “The wedding is definitely off.”

    Synonyms: cancelled · Opposite: on

  4. In an unpalatable state.

    “Sour milk.”

    Synonyms: sour, turned · Similar: soured

  5. Not performing or scheduled for duties.

    “He's off every Tuesday.”

    Similar: inactive

Phrases and compounds with "off"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • go off (verb) Run away; usually includes taking something or somebody along.
  • beg off (verb) Ask for permission to be released from an engagement.
  • buy off (verb) Pay someone with influence in order to receive a favor.
  • cap off (verb) Finish or complete, as with some decisive action.
  • cut off (verb) Make a break in.
  • day off (noun) A day when you are not required to work.
  • die off (verb) Become extinct.
  • ebb off (verb) Flow back or recede.
  • far-off (adjective) Very far away in space or time.
  • fob off (verb) Sell as genuine, sell with the intention to deceive.
  • get off (verb) Leave a vehicle, aircraft, etc.
  • lay off (verb) Put an end to a state or an activity.
  • let off (verb) Grant exemption or release to.
  • lip off (verb) Speak spontaneously and without restraint.
  • log off (verb) Exit a computer.
  • lop off (verb) Remove by or as if by cutting.
