Old: meaning, synonyms and examples

Old is used as an adjective (8 senses) and noun (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: (used especially of persons) having lived for a relatively long time or attained a specific age.

In Hindi
पुराना, बूढ़ा
Synonyms

Opposites
young, new
Word family
oldness

Old as an adjective

  1. (used especially of persons) having lived for a relatively long time or attained a specific age.

    “His mother is very old.” “A ripe old age.” “How old are you?”

    Opposite: young · Similar: aged, aged, aging, ancient, anile, centenarian

  2. Of long duration; not new.

    “Old tradition.” “Old house.” “Old country.”

    Opposite: new · Similar: age-old, antediluvian, antique, auld, hand-me-down, hoary

  3. (used for emphasis) very familiar.

    “Good old boy.” “Same old story.”

    Similar: familiar

  4. Skilled through long experience.

    “An old offender.” “The older soldiers.”

    Synonyms: older · Similar: experienced

  5. Belonging to some prior time.

    “Erstwhile friend.” “Our former glory.” “The once capital of the state.”

    Synonyms: erstwhile, former, onetime, one-time, quondam, sometime · Similar: past

  6. (used informally especially for emphasis).

    “A real honest-to-god live cowboy.” “Had us a high old time.” “Went upriver to look at a sure-enough fish wheel.”

    Synonyms: honest-to-god, honest-to-goodness, sure-enough · Similar: genuine

  7. Of a very early stage in development.

    “Old English is also called Anglo Saxon.” “Old High German is High German from the middle of the 9th to the end of the 11th century.”

    Similar: early

  8. Just preceding something else in time or order.

    “The previous owner.” “My old house was larger.”

    Synonyms: previous · Similar: preceding

Old as a noun

  1. Past times (especially in the phrase `in days of old').

    A kind of: past

Phrases and compounds with "old"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • age-old (adjective) Belonging to or lasting from times long ago.
  • day-old (adjective) Not fresh today.
  • old age (noun) A late time of life.
  • old bag (noun) An ugly or ill-tempered woman.
  • old boy (noun) A familiar term of address for a man.
  • old man (noun) A man who is very old.
  • old-hat (adjective) Repeated too often; overfamiliar through overuse.
  • old gold (noun) A dark yellow.
  • old hand (noun) An experienced person who has been through many battles; someone who has given long service.
  • old lady (noun) Your own wife.
  • old maid (noun) An elderly unmarried woman.
  • old rose (noun) A greyish-pink color.
  • old salt (noun) A man who serves as a sailor.
  • old-line (adjective) Adhering to conservative or reactionary principles.
  • old-time (adjective) Attractively old-fashioned (but not necessarily authentic).
  • old delhi (noun) A city in north central India.
