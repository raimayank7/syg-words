Order: meaning, synonyms and examples

Order is used as a noun (14 senses) and verb (9 senses). Its most common meaning is: (often plural) a command given by a superior (e.g., a military or law enforcement officer) that must be obeyed.

In Hindi
आदेश; क्रम; ऑर्डर
Synonyms
order of magnitude, ordering, ordination, orderliness, decree, edict, fiat, rescript, purchase order, club
Opposites
disorder
Word family
orderly, orderer, ordering

Order as a noun

  1. (often plural) a command given by a superior (e.g., a military or law enforcement officer) that must be obeyed.

    “The British ships dropped anchor and waited for orders from London.”

    A kind of: command · Kinds: marching orders, summons, word

  2. A degree in a continuum of size or quantity.

    “It was on the order of a mile.” “An explosion of a low order of magnitude.”

    Synonyms: order of magnitude · A kind of: magnitude

  3. Established customary state (especially of society).

    “Order ruled in the streets.” “Law and order.”

    Opposite: disorder · A kind of: state · Kinds: civil order, rule of law, tranquillity, harmony, stability, peace

  4. Logical or comprehensible arrangement of separate elements.

    “We shall consider these questions in the inverse order of their presentation.”

    Synonyms: ordering, ordination · A kind of: arrangement · Kinds: bacteria order, word order, genetic code, genome, series

  5. A condition of regular or proper arrangement.

    “He put his desk in order.” “The machine is now in working order.”

    Synonyms: orderliness · Opposite: disorder · A kind of: condition · Kinds: spit and polish, kilter, tidiness

  6. A legally binding command or decision entered on the court record (as if issued by a court or judge).

    “A friend in New Mexico said that the order caused no trouble out there.”

    Synonyms: decree, edict, fiat, rescript · A kind of: act · Kinds: consent decree, curfew, decree nisi, imperial decree, legal separation, programma, prohibition, stay

  7. A commercial document used to request someone to supply something in return for payment and providing specifications and quantities.

    “IBM received an order for a hundred computers.”

    Synonyms: purchase order · A kind of: commercial document · Kinds: credit order, indent, market order, production order, reorder, stop order, stop payment, mail order

  8. A formal association of people with similar interests.

    “He joined a golf club.” “They formed a small lunch society.” “Men from the fraternal order will staff the soup kitchen today.”

    Synonyms: club, social club, society, guild, gild, lodge · A kind of: association · Kinds: athenaeum, bookclub, chapter, chess club, country club, fraternity, glee club, golf club

  9. A body of rules followed by an assembly.

    Synonyms: rules of order, parliamentary law, parliamentary procedure · A kind of: rule · Kinds: interpellation, standing order, closure, point of order, previous question

  10. A group of person living under a religious rule.

    “The order of Saint Benedict.”

    Synonyms: monastic order · A kind of: sect

  11. (biology) taxonomic group containing one or more families.

    A kind of: taxonomic group · Kinds: animal order, protoctist order, plant order, fungus order

  12. A request for something to be made, supplied, or served.

    “I gave the waiter my order.” “The company's products were in such demand that they got more orders than their call center could handle.”

    A kind of: request · Kinds: short order

  13. (architecture) one of original three styles of Greek architecture distinguished by the type of column and entablature used or a style developed from the original three by the Romans.

    A kind of: artistic style

  14. The act of putting things in a sequential arrangement.

    “There were mistakes in the ordering of items on the list.”

    Synonyms: ordering · A kind of: organization · Kinds: rank order, scaling, succession, layout, alphabetization

Order as a verb

  1. Give instructions to or direct somebody to do something with authority.

    “I said to him to go home.” “She ordered him to do the shopping.” “The mother told the child to get dressed.”

    Synonyms: tell, enjoin, say · A kind of: request · Kinds: direct, instruct, command, call, warn

  2. Make a request for something.

    “Order me some flowers.” “Order a work stoppage.”

    A kind of: request · Kinds: reorder, place, call, wish, commission

  3. Issue commands or orders for.

    Synonyms: prescribe, dictate · A kind of: inflict · Kinds: mandate

  4. Bring into conformity with rules or principles or usage; impose regulations.

    “We cannot regulate the way people dress.” “This town likes to regulate.”

    Synonyms: regulate, regularize, regularise, govern · A kind of: decide · Kinds: standardize, zone

  5. Bring order to or into.

    “Order these files.”

    Opposite: disorder · A kind of: arrange · Kinds: tidy, systematize, collate, disentangle

  6. Place in a certain order.

    “Order the photos chronologically.”

    A kind of: arrange

  7. Appoint to a clerical posts.

    “He was ordained in the Church.”

    Synonyms: ordain, consecrate, ordinate · A kind of: invest

  8. Arrange thoughts, ideas, temporal events.

    “Arrange my schedule.” “Set up one's life.” “I put these memories with those of bygone times.”

    Synonyms: arrange, set up, put · A kind of: organize · Kinds: synchronize, phrase

  9. Assign a rank or rating to.

    “How would you rank these students?” “The restaurant is rated highly in the food guide.”

    Synonyms: rate, rank, range, grade, place · A kind of: evaluate · Kinds: superordinate, shortlist, seed, reorder, subordinate, prioritize, sequence, downgrade

Phrases and compounds with "order"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • in order (adjective) In a state of proper readiness or preparation or arrangement.
  • to order (adverb) To specification.
  • gag order (noun) A court order restricting information or comment by the participants involved in a lawsuit.
  • mail order (noun) A purchase negotiated by mail.
  • open order (noun) A military formation leaving enough space between ranks to allow an inspecting officer to pass.
  • order arms (noun) A position in the manual of arms; the rifle is held vertically on the right side with the butt on the ground; often used as a command.
  • order book (noun) A printed copy of the order of the day.
  • order code (noun) The portion of a set of operation descriptions that specifies the operation to be performed; the set of operations in a computer.
  • order form (noun) A form to use when placing an order.
  • rank order (noun) An arrangement according to rank.
  • side order (noun) A dish that is served with, but is subordinate to, a main course.
  • stop order (noun) An order to a broker to sell (buy) when the price of a security falls (rises) to a designated level.
  • tall order (noun) A formidable task or requirement.
  • word order (noun) The order of words in a text.
  • civil order (noun) The form of government of a social organization.
  • close order (noun) A military formation for drill or marching.
