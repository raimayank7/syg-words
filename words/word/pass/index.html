Pass: meaning, synonyms and examples

Pass is used as a verb (14 senses), noun (14 senses) and adjective (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: Go across or through.

In Hindi
गुज़रना, पास होना; दर्रा
Synonyms
go through, go across, travel by, pass by, surpass, go past, go by, legislate, elapse, lapse
Opposites
fail
Word family
passer, passage, passing

Pass as a verb

  1. Go across or through.

    “We passed the point where the police car had parked.” “A terrible thought went through his mind.”

    Synonyms: go through, go across · A kind of: travel · Kinds: break through, squeak by, transit, traverse, infiltrate, run, transit, cut

  2. Move past.

    “A black limousine passed by when she looked out the window.” “He passed his professor in the hall.” “One line of soldiers surpassed the other.”

    Synonyms: travel by, pass by, surpass, go past, go by · A kind of: travel · Kinds: skirt, run by, fly by, zip by

  3. Make laws, bills, etc. or bring into effect by legislation.

    “They passed the amendment.” “We cannot legislate how people spend their free time.”

    Synonyms: legislate · A kind of: ordain

  4. Pass by.

    “Three years elapsed.”

    Synonyms: elapse, lapse, slip by, glide by, slip away, go by, slide by, go along · A kind of: advance · Kinds: fly

  5. Place into the hands or custody of.

    “Hand me the spoon, please.” “Turn the files over to me, please.” “He turned over the prisoner to his lawyers.”

    Synonyms: hand, reach, pass on, turn over, give · A kind of: transfer · Kinds: give, slip, deal, hand over, release, entrust, entrust

  6. Stretch out over a distance, space, time, or scope; run or extend between two points or beyond a certain point.

    “Service runs all the way to Cranbury.” “His knowledge doesn't go very far.” “My memory extends back to my fourth year of life.”

    Synonyms: run, go, lead, extend · A kind of: be · Kinds: come, radiate, go far

  7. Travel past.

    “The sports car passed all the trucks.”

    Synonyms: overtake, overhaul · A kind of: advance · Kinds: get by, clear

  8. Come to pass.

    “What is happening?” “The meeting took place off without an incidence.” “Nothing occurred that seemed important.”

    Synonyms: happen, hap, go on, pass off, occur, fall out, come about, take place · Kinds: break, arise, result, intervene, transpire, give, operate, supervene

  9. Go unchallenged; be approved.

    “The bill cleared the House.”

    Synonyms: clear · A kind of: succeed · Kinds: clear

  10. Pass time in a specific way.

    “How are you spending your summer vacation?”

    Synonyms: spend · Kinds: soldier, slum, weekend, vacation, serve, while away, sojourn, winter

  11. Pass over, across, or through.

    “He ran his eyes over her body.” “She ran her fingers along the carved figurine.” “He drew her hair through his fingers.”

    Synonyms: guide, run, draw · Kinds: rub, thread, thread

  12. Transmit information.

    “Please communicate this message to all employees.” “Pass along the good news.”

    Synonyms: communicate, pass on, pass along, put across · A kind of: convey · Kinds: plant, send a message, relay, get across, request, acknowledge, carry, render

  13. Disappear gradually.

    “The pain eventually passed off.”

    Synonyms: evanesce, fade, blow over, pass off, fleet · A kind of: disappear

  14. Go successfully through a test or a selection process.

    “She passed the new Jersey Bar Exam and can practice law now.”

    Synonyms: make it · Opposite: fail · A kind of: succeed · Kinds: breeze through

Pass as a noun

  1. (baseball) an advance to first base by a batter who receives four balls.

    “He worked the pitcher for a base on balls.”

    Synonyms: base on balls, walk · A kind of: accomplishment

  2. (military) a written leave of absence.

    “He had a pass for three days.”

    A kind of: leave

  3. (American football) a play that involves one player throwing the ball to a teammate.

    “The coach sent in a passing play on third and long.”

    Synonyms: passing play, passing game, passing · Kinds: forward pass, lateral pass, spot pass

  4. The location in a range of mountains of a geological formation that is lower than the surrounding peaks.

    “We got through the pass before it started to snow.”

    Synonyms: mountain pass, notch · A kind of: location · Kinds: col, defile, saddleback

  5. Any authorization to pass or go somewhere.

    “The pass to visit had a strict time limit.”

    Synonyms: passport · A kind of: permission · Kinds: safe-conduct

  6. A document indicating permission to do something without restrictions.

    “The media representatives had special passes.”

    Synonyms: laissez passer · A kind of: permission · Kinds: boarding card, hall pass, ticket-of-leave

  7. A flight or run by an aircraft over a target.

    “The plane turned to make a second pass.”

    A kind of: flight

  8. A bad or difficult situation or state of affairs.

    Synonyms: strait, straits · A kind of: situation · Kinds: desperate straits

  9. A difficult juncture.

    “A pretty pass.” “Matters came to a head yesterday.”

    Synonyms: head, straits · A kind of: juncture

  10. One complete cycle of operations (as by a computer).

    “It was not possible to complete the computation in a single pass.”

    A kind of: cycle

  11. You advance to the next round in a tournament without playing an opponent.

    “He had a bye in the first round.”

    Synonyms: bye · A kind of: concession

  12. A permit to enter or leave a military installation.

    “He had to show his pass in order to get out.”

    Synonyms: liberty chit · A kind of: license

  13. A complimentary ticket.

    “The star got passes for his family.”

    A kind of: ticket

  14. A usually brief attempt.

    “He took a crack at it.” “I gave it a whirl.”

    Synonyms: crack, fling, go, whirl, offer · A kind of: attempt

Pass as an adjective

  1. Of advancing the ball by throwing it.

    “A team with a good passing attack.” “A pass play.”

    Synonyms: passing

Phrases and compounds with "pass"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • pass by (verb) Move past.
  • pass on (verb) Place into the hands or custody of.
  • pass up (verb) Refuse to accept.
  • pass off (verb) Be accepted as something or somebody in a false character or identity.
  • pass out (verb) Pass out from weakness, physical or emotional distress due to a loss of blood supply to the brain.
  • hall pass (noun) Written permission from a teacher for a student to be out the classroom and in the halls of the school.
  • make pass (verb) Cause to pass.
  • pass away (verb) Pass from physical life and lose all bodily attributes and functions necessary to sustain life.
  • pass over (verb) Bypass.
  • spot pass (noun) A pass to a designated spot on the field; the receiver should arrive at that spot the same time the ball does.
  • flare pass (noun) A short forward pass to a back who is running toward the sidelines.
  • pass along (verb) Transmit information.
  • pass water (verb) Eliminate urine.
  • donner pass (noun) A mountain pass in northeastern California near Lake Tahoe; site where in 1844 some members of an emigrant party survived by eating those who had died.
  • pass across (verb) Make a passage or journey from one place to another.
  • pass around (verb) Cause be distributed.
