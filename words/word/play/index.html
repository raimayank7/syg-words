Play: meaning, synonyms and examples

Play is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: Participate in games or sport.

In Hindi
खेलना; नाटक
Synonyms
act, represent, spiel, act as, recreate, toy, run, fiddle, diddle, dally
Word family
player, playing, playlet

Play as a verb

  1. Participate in games or sport.

    “We played hockey all afternoon.” “Play cards.” “Pele played for the Brazilian teams in many important matches.”

    A kind of: compete · Kinds: line up, curl, snooker, revoke, develop, develop, die, misplay

  2. Act or have an effect in a specified way or with a specific effect or outcome.

    “This factor played only a minor part in his decision.” “This development played into her hands.” “I played no role in your dismissal.”

    A kind of: act

  3. Play on an instrument.

    “The band played all night long.”

    A kind of: perform · Kinds: sound off, swing, symphonize, play along, bow, busk

  4. Play a role or part.

    “Gielgud played Hamlet.” “She wants to act Lady Macbeth, but she is too young for the role.” “She played the servant to her husband's master.”

    Synonyms: act, represent · A kind of: re-create · Kinds: make, support, enact, act out, impersonate, parody, emote

  5. Be at play; be engaged in playful activity; amuse oneself in a way characteristic of children.

    “The kids were playing outside all day.” “I used to play with trucks as a little girl.”

    A kind of: act · Kinds: roughhouse, horse around, frolic, dabble

  6. Replay (as a melody).

    “Play it again, Sam.” “She played the third movement very beautifully.”

    Synonyms: spiel · A kind of: re-create · Kinds: riff, misplay, fiddle, replay, prelude, jazz, rag, bugle

  7. Perform music on (a musical instrument).

    “He plays the flute.” “Can you play on this old recorder?”

    A kind of: sound · Kinds: register, skirl, beat, tweedle, chord, pipe, drum, harp

  8. Pretend to have certain qualities or state of mind.

    “He acted the idiot.” “She plays deaf when the news are bad.”

    Synonyms: act, act as · A kind of: act

  9. Move or seem to move quickly, lightly, or irregularly.

    “The spotlights played on the politicians.”

    A kind of: travel

  10. Engage in recreational activities rather than work; occupy oneself in a diversion.

    “On weekends I play.” “The students all recreate alike.”

    Synonyms: recreate

  11. Pretend to be somebody in the framework of a game or playful activity.

    “Let's play like I am mommy.” “Play cowboy and Indians.”

    A kind of: simulate

  12. Emit recorded sound.

    “The tape was playing for hours.” “The stereo was playing Beethoven when I entered.”

    A kind of: sound

  13. Perform on a certain location.

    “The prodigy played Carnegie Hall at the age of 16.” “She has been playing on Broadway for years.”

    A kind of: perform

  14. Put (a card or piece) into play during a game, or act strategically as if in a card game.

    “He is playing his cards close to his chest.” “The Democrats still have some cards to play before they will concede the electoral victory.”

    A kind of: deploy · Kinds: pitch, cover, declare

Play as a noun

  1. A dramatic work intended for performance by actors on a stage.

    “He wrote several plays but only one was produced on Broadway.”

    Synonyms: drama, dramatic play · A kind of: dramatic composition · Kinds: theater of the absurd, playlet, miracle play, morality play, mystery play, satyr play

  2. A theatrical performance of a drama.

    “The play lasted two hours.”

    A kind of: show · Kinds: musical, curtain raiser

  3. A preset plan of action in team sports.

    “The coach drew up the plays for her team.”

    A kind of: plan of action · Kinds: knock on, power play, razzle-dazzle, basketball play

  4. A deliberate coordinated movement requiring dexterity and skill.

    “He made a great maneuver.” “The runner was out on a play by the shortstop.”

    Synonyms: maneuver, manoeuvre · A kind of: motion · Kinds: takeaway, figure, completion, ball hawking, assist, icing, jugglery, obstruction

  5. A state in which action is feasible.

    “The ball was still in play.” “Insiders said the company's stock was in play.”

    A kind of: action

  6. Utilization or exercise.

    “The play of the imagination.”

    A kind of: use

  7. An attempt to get something.

    “They made a futile play for power.” “He made a bid to gain attention.”

    Synonyms: bid · A kind of: attempt

  8. Activity by children that is guided more by imagination than by fixed rules.

    “Freud believed in the utility of play to a small child.”

    Synonyms: child's play · A kind of: diversion · Kinds: house, doctor, fireman

  9. (in games or plays or other performances) the time during which play proceeds.

    “Rain stopped play in the 4th inning.”

    Synonyms: playing period, period of play · A kind of: measure · Kinds: hole, set

  10. The removal of constraints.

    “He gave free rein to his impulses.” “They gave full play to the artist's talent.”

    Synonyms: free rein · A kind of: freedom

  11. A weak and tremulous light.

    “The shimmer of colors on iridescent feathers.” “The play of light on the water.”

    Synonyms: shimmer · A kind of: change

  12. Verbal wit or mockery (often at another's expense but not to be taken seriously).

    “He became a figure of fun.” “He said it in sport.”

    Synonyms: fun, sport · A kind of: wit · Kinds: jocosity, waggery, drollery, pun

  13. Movement or space for movement.

    “There was too much play in the steering wheel.”

    Synonyms: looseness · A kind of: movability · Kinds: wiggliness, slack

  14. (game) the activity of doing something in an agreed succession.

    “It is my turn.” “It is still my play.”

    Synonyms: turn · A kind of: activity · Kinds: move, start, innings, down, bat, lead, ruff, trick

Phrases and compounds with "play"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • in play (adjective) Of a ball.
  • play up (verb) Move into the foreground to make more visible or prominent.
  • play off (verb) Set into opposition or rivalry.
  • play out (verb) Deplete.
  • play-box (noun) A box for a child's toys and personal things (especially at a boarding school).
  • draw play (noun) (American football) the quarterback moves back as if to pass and then hands the ball to the fullback who is running toward the line of scrimmage.
  • foul play (noun) Unfair or dishonest behavior (especially involving violence).
  • long-play (adjective) (used of records) playing at a slower speed and for a longer time than earlier records.
  • play back (verb) Reproduce (a recording) on a recorder.
  • play down (verb) Understate the importance or quality of.
  • play list (noun) A list of musical selections for performance or for broadcast by radio.
  • trap play (noun) (American football) a play in which a defensive player is allowed to cross the line of scrimmage and then blocked off as the runner goes through the place the lineman vacated.
  • word play (noun) Playing on words or speech sounds.
  • force play (noun) A putout of a base runner who is required to run; the putout is accomplished by holding the ball while touching the base to which the runner must advance before the runner reaches that base.
  • match play (noun) Golf scoring by holes won.
  • medal play (noun) Golf scoring by total strokes taken.
