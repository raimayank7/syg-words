Point: meaning, synonyms and examples

Point is used as a noun (14 senses) and verb (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: A geometric element that has position but no extension.

In Hindi
बिंदु; मुद्दा; इशारा करना
Synonyms
detail, item, degree, level, stage, point in time, tip, peak, dot, spot
Word family
pointer

Point as a noun

  1. A geometric element that has position but no extension.

    “A point is defined by its coordinates.”

    A kind of: component · Kinds: attractor, intersection, intercept

  2. The precise location of something; a spatially limited location.

    “She walked to a point where she could survey the whole street.”

    A kind of: location · Kinds: punctum, blind spot, navel, node, antinode, beginning, celestial point, center

  3. A brief version of the essential meaning of something.

    “Get to the point.” “He missed the point of the joke.” “Life has lost its point.”

    A kind of: meaning · Kinds: bottom line, crux, rallying point, talking point

  4. An isolated fact that is considered separately from the whole.

    “Several of the details are similar.” “A point of information.”

    Synonyms: detail, item · A kind of: fact · Kinds: minutia, nook and cranny, respect, sticking point, technicality

  5. A specific identifiable position in a continuum or series or especially in a process.

    “A remarkable degree of frankness.” “At what stage are the social sciences?”

    Synonyms: degree, level, stage · A kind of: state · Kinds: ladder, acme, extent, resultant, standard of living, plane, state of the art, ultimacy

  6. An instant of time.

    “At that point I had to leave.”

    Synonyms: point in time · A kind of: measure · Kinds: distance, date, deadline, arrival time, departure time, midterm, term, midterm

  7. The object of an activity.

    “What is the point of discussing it?”

    A kind of: aim

  8. A V shape.

    “The cannibal's teeth were filed to sharp points.”

    Synonyms: tip, peak · A kind of: convex shape · Kinds: widow's peak, cusp, head

  9. A very small circular shape.

    “A row of points.” “Draw lines between the dots.”

    Synonyms: dot · A kind of: disk

  10. The unit of counting in scoring a game or contest.

    “He scored 20 points in the first half.” “A touchdown counts 6 points.”

    A kind of: unit of measurement · Kinds: point after, advantage, set point, match point

  11. A promontory extending out into a large body of water.

    “They sailed south around the point.”

    A kind of: promontory

  12. A distinct part that can be specified separately in a group of things that could be enumerated on a list.

    “He noticed an item in the New York Times.” “She had several items on her shopping list.” “The main point on the agenda was taken up first.”

    Synonyms: item · A kind of: part · Kinds: agenda item, incidental, inventory item, line item, news item, place

  13. A style in speech or writing that arrests attention and has a penetrating or convincing quality or effect.

    A kind of: relevance

  14. An outstanding characteristic.

    “His acting was one of the high points of the movie.”

    Synonyms: spot · A kind of: characteristic

Point as a verb

  1. Indicate a place, direction, person, or thing; either spatially or figuratively.

    “I showed the customer the glove section.” “He pointed to the empty parking space.” “He indicated his opponents.”

    Synonyms: indicate, designate, show · A kind of: inform · Kinds: point, finger, signalize, reflect

  2. Be oriented.

    “The weather vane points North.” “The dancers toes pointed outward.”

    Synonyms: orient · A kind of: lie

  3. Direct into a position for use.

    “Point a gun.” “He charged his weapon at me.”

    Synonyms: charge, level · A kind of: aim

  4. Direct the course; determine the direction of travelling.

    Synonyms: steer, maneuver, manoeuver, manoeuvre, direct, head, guide, channelize · A kind of: control · Kinds: dock, sheer, pull over, helm, crab, navigate, stand out, starboard

  5. Be a signal for or a symptom of.

    “These symptoms indicate a serious illness.” “Her behavior points to a severe neurosis.” “The economic indicators signal that the euro is undervalued.”

    Synonyms: bespeak, betoken, indicate, signal · A kind of: tell · Kinds: bode, mark

  6. Sail close to the wind.

    Synonyms: luff · A kind of: sail

  7. Mark (Hebrew words) with diacritics.

    A kind of: tag

  8. Mark with diacritics.

    “Point the letter.”

    A kind of: tag

  9. Mark (a psalm text) to indicate the points at which the music changes.

    A kind of: tag

  10. Be positionable in a specified manner.

    “The gun points with ease.”

    A kind of: be

  11. Intend (something) to move towards a certain goal.

    “He aimed his fists towards his opponent's face.” “Criticism directed at her superior.” “Direct your anger towards others, not towards yourself.”

    Synonyms: target, aim, place, direct · A kind of: aim · Kinds: address, range in

  12. Indicate the presence of (game) by standing and pointing with the muzzle.

    “The dog pointed the dead duck.”

    A kind of: indicate

  13. Give a point to.

    “The candles are tapered.”

    Synonyms: sharpen, taper · A kind of: change shape · Kinds: acuminate

  14. Repair the joints of bricks.

    “Point a chimney.”

    Synonyms: repoint · A kind of: repair

Phrases and compounds with "point"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • point up (verb) Emphasize, especially by identification.
  • dew point (noun) The temperature at which the water vapor in the air becomes saturated and condensation begins.
  • dry point (noun) A print produced by dry point engraving.
  • end point (noun) A place where something ends or is complete.
  • point jam (verb) Jam a narrow band of frequencies.
  • point man (noun) Someone who is the forefront of an important enterprise.
  • point out (verb) Make or write a comment on.
  • set point (noun) (tennis) the final point needed to win a set in tennis.
  • blue point (noun) Oysters originally from Long Island Sound but now from anywhere along the northeastern seacoast; usually eaten raw.
  • data point (noun) An item of factual information derived from measurement or research.
  • full point (noun) A punctuation mark (.) placed at the end of a declarative sentence to indicate a full stop or after abbreviations.
  • gros point (noun) A needlepoint stitch covering two horizontal and two vertical threads.
  • high point (noun) The most enjoyable part of a given experience.
  • point duty (noun) The control of traffic by a policeman stationed at an intersection.
  • point lace (noun) Lace worked with a needle in a buttonhole stitch on a paper pattern.
  • weak point (noun) An attribute that is inadequate or deficient.
