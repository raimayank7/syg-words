Pressure: meaning, synonyms and examples

Pressure is used as a noun (7 senses) and verb (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: The force applied to a unit area of surface; measured in pascals (SI unit) or in dynes (cgs unit).

Synonyms
pressure level, force per unit area, press, pressing, imperativeness, insistence, insistency, pressure sensation, atmospheric pressure, air pressure
Word family
press, pressurize, pressurise

Pressure as a noun

  1. The force applied to a unit area of surface; measured in pascals (SI unit) or in dynes (cgs unit).

    “The compressed gas exerts an increased pressure.”

    Synonyms: pressure level, force per unit area · A kind of: physical phenomenon · Kinds: blood pressure, gas pressure, head, hydrostatic head, intraocular pressure, oil pressure, osmotic pressure, radiation pressure

  2. A force that compels.

    “The public brought pressure to bear on the government.”

    A kind of: force

  3. The act of pressing; the exertion of pressure.

    “He gave the button a press.” “He used pressure to stop the bleeding.” “At the pressing of a button.”

    Synonyms: press, pressing · A kind of: push · Kinds: impression, compression

  4. The state of demanding notice or attention.

    “The insistence of their hunger.” “The press of business matters.”

    Synonyms: imperativeness, insistence, insistency, press · A kind of: urgency

  5. The somatic sensation that results from applying force to an area of skin.

    “The sensitivity of his skin to pressure and temperature was normal.”

    Synonyms: pressure sensation · A kind of: somesthesia

  6. An oppressive condition of physical or mental or social or economic distress.

    A kind of: distress

  7. The pressure exerted by the atmosphere.

    Synonyms: atmospheric pressure, air pressure · A kind of: gas pressure · Kinds: barometric pressure, compartment pressure, overpressure, sea-level pressure

Pressure as a verb

  1. To cause to do through pressure or necessity, by physical, moral or intellectual means :"She forced him to take a job in the city".

    “She forced him to take a job in the city.” “He squeezed her for information.”

    Synonyms: coerce, hale, squeeze, force · A kind of: compel · Kinds: turn up the heat, drive, bludgeon, steamroller, squeeze for, dragoon, bring oneself

  2. Exert pressure on someone through threats.

    Synonyms: blackmail, blackjack · A kind of: influence

Phrases and compounds with "pressure"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • air pressure (noun) The pressure exerted by the atmosphere.
  • gas pressure (noun) The pressure exerted by a gas.
  • low-pressure (adjective) Not forceful.
  • oil pressure (noun) Pressure that keeps oil on the moving parts of an internal-combustion engine.
  • high-pressure (adjective) Aggressively and persistently persuasive.
  • pressure dome (noun) A dome-shaped building that is pressurized.
  • pressure feed (noun) Mechanical system of lubricating internal combustion engines in which a pump forces oil into the engine bearings.
  • pressure gage (noun) Gauge for measuring and indicating fluid pressure.
  • pressure sore (noun) A chronic ulcer of the skin caused by prolonged pressure on it (as in bedridden patients).
  • pressure suit (noun) Protective garment consisting of an inflatable suit for space or high altitude flying.
  • pressure unit (noun) A unit measuring force per unit area.
  • pressure-cook (verb) Cook in a pressure cooker.
  • pressure-wash (verb) Wash before painting to remove old paint and mildew.
  • blood pressure (noun) The pressure of the circulating blood against the walls of the blood vessels; results from the systole of the left ventricle of the heart; sometimes measured for a quick evaluation of a person's health.
  • pressure cabin (noun) Cabin consisting of the pressurized section of an aircraft or spacecraft.
  • pressure gauge (noun) Gauge for measuring and indicating fluid pressure.
