Price: meaning, synonyms and examples

Price is used as a noun (7 senses) and verb (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: The property of having material worth (often indicated by the amount of money something would bring if sold).

In Hindi
दाम, क़ीमत
Synonyms
monetary value, cost, terms, damage, toll
Word family
pricy, pricey, pricing

Price as a noun

  1. The property of having material worth (often indicated by the amount of money something would bring if sold).

    “The fluctuating monetary value of gold and silver.” “He puts a high price on his services.” “He couldn't calculate the cost of the collection.”

    Synonyms: monetary value, cost · A kind of: value · Kinds: average cost, marginal cost, expensiveness, assessment, inexpensiveness

  2. The amount of money needed to purchase something.

    “The price of gasoline.” “He got his new car on excellent terms.” “How much is the damage?”

    Synonyms: terms, damage · A kind of: cost · Kinds: asking price, bid price, closing price, factory price, highway robbery, purchase price, spot price, support level

  3. Value measured by what must be given or done or undergone to obtain something.

    “The cost in human life was enormous.” “The price of success is hard work.” “What price glory?”

    Synonyms: cost, toll · A kind of: value · Kinds: death toll

  4. The high value or worth of something.

    “Her price is far above rubies.”

    A kind of: worth

  5. A monetary reward for helping to catch a criminal.

    “The cattle thief has a price on his head.”

    A kind of: reward

  6. Cost of bribing someone.

    “They say that every politician has a price.”

    A kind of: cost

  7. United States operatic soprano (born 1927).

Price as a verb

  1. Determine the price of.

    “The grocer priced his wares high.”

    A kind of: determine · Kinds: mark up, rig, overprice, underquote

  2. Ascertain or learn the price of.

    “Have you priced personal computers lately?”

    A kind of: ascertain

Phrases and compounds with "price"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • bid price (noun) (stock market) the price at which a broker is willing to buy a certain security.
  • cut price (noun) A price below the standard price.
  • cut-price (adjective) Costing less than standard price.
  • price cut (noun) Cutting the price of merchandise to one lower than the usual or advertised price.
  • price tag (noun) A tag showing the price of the article it is attached to.
  • price war (noun) Intense competition in which competitors cut retail prices to gain business.
  • cash price (noun) The current delivery price of a commodity traded in the spot market.
  • half-price (adverb) For half the price.
  • list price (noun) The selling price of something as stated in a catalogue or price list; often subject to discounts.
  • price list (noun) A listing of prices for different goods or services.
  • spot price (noun) The current delivery price of a commodity traded in the spot market.
  • bride price (noun) Money or property given (in some societies) by the bridegroom to the family of his bride.
  • offer price (noun) (stock market) the price at which a broker is willing to sell a certain security.
  • price floor (noun) Floor below which prices are not allowed to fall.
  • price index (noun) An index that traces the relative changes in the price of an individual good (or a market basket of goods) over time.
  • price level (noun) An index that traces the relative changes in the price of an individual good (or a market basket of goods) over time.
