Pull: meaning, synonyms and examples

Pull is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (7 senses). Its most common meaning is: Cause to move by pulling.

In Hindi
खींचना
Synonyms
draw, force, attract, pull in, draw in, perpetrate, commit, pull out, get out, take out
Opposites
push
Word family
puller, pulling

Pull as a verb

  1. Cause to move by pulling.

    “Draw a wagon.” “Pull a sled.”

    Synonyms: draw, force · Opposite: push · A kind of: move · Kinds: twitch, pull back, adduct, abduct, stretch, pluck, tug, drag

  2. Direct toward itself or oneself by means of some psychological power or physical attributes.

    “Her good looks attract the stares of many men.” “The ad pulled in many potential customers.” “This pianist pulls huge crowds.”

    Synonyms: attract, pull in, draw, draw in · A kind of: pull · Kinds: tug, catch, draw in, bring

  3. Move into a certain direction.

    “The car pulls to the right.”

    A kind of: drive · Kinds: cut in

  4. Apply force so as to cause motion towards the source of the motion.

    “Pull the rope.” “Pull the handle towards you.” “Pull the string gently.”

    A kind of: force · Kinds: wrench, gather, attract, hike up, pick at, retract, draw close

  5. Perform an act, usually with a negative connotation.

    “Perpetrate a crime.” “Pull a bank robbery.”

    Synonyms: perpetrate, commit · A kind of: act · Kinds: make, recommit

  6. Bring, take, or pull out of a container or from under a cover.

    “Draw a weapon.” “Pull out a gun.” “The mugger pulled a knife on his victim.”

    Synonyms: draw, pull out, get out, take out · A kind of: remove · Kinds: unsheathe

  7. Steer into a certain direction.

    “Pull one's horse to a stand.” “Pull the car over.”

    A kind of: drive

  8. Strain abnormally.

    “I pulled a muscle in my leg when I jumped up.” “The athlete pulled a tendon in the competition.”

    Synonyms: overstretch · A kind of: injure

  9. Cause to move in a certain direction by exerting a force upon, either physically or in an abstract sense.

    “A declining dollar pulled down the export figures for the last quarter.”

    Synonyms: draw · A kind of: move

  10. Operate when rowing a boat.

    “Pull the oars.”

    A kind of: row

  11. Rein in to keep from winning a race.

    “Pull a horse.”

    A kind of: rein

  12. Tear or be torn violently.

    “The curtain ripped from top to bottom.” “Pull the cooked chicken into strips.”

    Synonyms: rend, rip, rive · A kind of: tear

  13. Hit in the direction that the player is facing when carrying through the swing.

    “Pull the ball.”

    A kind of: hit

  14. Strip of feathers.

    “Pull a chicken.” “Pluck the capon.”

    Synonyms: pluck, tear, deplume, deplumate, displume · A kind of: strip

Pull as a noun

  1. The act of pulling; applying force to move something toward or with you.

    “The pull up the hill had him breathing harder.” “His strenuous pulling strained his back.”

    Synonyms: pulling · A kind of: propulsion · Kinds: drag, draw, tug, draft, extirpation, pluck, traction

  2. The force used in pulling.

    “The pull of the moon.” “The pull of the current.”

    A kind of: force

  3. Special advantage or influence.

    “The chairman's nephew has a lot of pull.”

    Synonyms: clout · A kind of: advantage

  4. A device used for pulling something.

    “He grabbed the pull and opened the drawer.”

    A kind of: device · Kinds: bellpull, pull chain

  5. A sharp strain on muscles or ligaments.

    “The wrench to his knee occurred as he fell.” “He was sidelined with a hamstring pull.”

    Synonyms: wrench, twist · A kind of: injury · Kinds: sprain

  6. A slow inhalation (as of tobacco smoke).

    “He took a puff on his pipe.” “He took a drag on his cigarette and expelled the smoke slowly.”

    Synonyms: puff, drag · A kind of: inhalation

  7. A sustained effort.

    “It was a long pull but we made it.”

    A kind of: effort

Phrases and compounds with "pull"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • pull at (verb) Pluck or pull at with the fingers.
  • pull in (verb) Direct toward itself or oneself by means of some psychological power or physical attributes.
  • pull up (verb) Come to a halt after driving somewhere.
  • pull-in (noun) A roadside cafe especially for lorry drivers.
  • pull-up (noun) A roadside cafe especially for lorry drivers.
  • leg-pull (noun) As a joke: trying to make somebody believe something that is not true.
  • pull off (verb) Pull or pull out sharply.
  • pull out (verb) Move out or away.
  • pull-off (noun) Designated paved area beside a main road where cars can stop temporarily.
  • pull away (verb) Pull back or move away or backward.
  • pull back (verb) Pull back or move away or backward.
  • pull down (verb) Tear down so as to make flat with the ground.
  • pull over (verb) Steer a vehicle to the side of the road.
  • pull ahead (verb) Obtain advantages, such as points, etc.
  • pull along (verb) Pull along heavily, like a heavy load against a resistance.
  • pull chain (noun) A chain (usually with a handle at the end) that is pulled in order to operate some mechanism (e.g. to flush a toilet).
