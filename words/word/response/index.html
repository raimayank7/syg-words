Response: meaning, synonyms and examples

Response is used as a noun (7 senses). Its most common meaning is: A result.

In Hindi
जवाब, प्रतिक्रिया
Synonyms
reaction, answer, reply, reception

Response as a noun

  1. A result.

    “This situation developed in response to events in Africa.”

    A kind of: consequence · Kinds: reaction

  2. A bodily process occurring due to the effect of some antecedent stimulus or agent.

    “A bad reaction to the medicine.” “His responses have slowed with age.”

    Synonyms: reaction · A kind of: bodily process · Kinds: automatism, rebound, overreaction, galvanic skin response, immune response, tropism, taxis, kinesis

  3. A statement (either spoken or written) that is made to reply to a question or request or criticism or accusation.

    “I waited several days for his answer.” “He wrote replies to several of his critics.”

    Synonyms: answer, reply · A kind of: statement · Kinds: rescript, feedback

  4. The manner in which something is greeted.

    “She did not expect the cold reception she received from her superiors.”

    Synonyms: reception · A kind of: greeting

  5. A phrase recited or sung by the congregation following a versicle by the priest or minister.

    A kind of: phrase

  6. The speech act of continuing a conversational exchange.

    “He growled his reply.”

    Synonyms: reply · A kind of: speech act · Kinds: counterblast, non sequitur, rejoinder, echo, answer

  7. The manner in which an electrical or mechanical device responds to an input signal or a range of input signals.

    A kind of: manner

Phrases and compounds with "response"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • response time (noun) The time that elapses between a stimulus and the response to it.
  • immune response (noun) A bodily defense reaction that recognizes an invading substance (an antigen: such as a virus or fungus or bacteria or transplanted organ) and produces antibodies specific against that antigen.
  • reflex response (noun) An automatic instinctive unlearned reaction to a stimulus.
  • learned response (noun) A reaction that has been acquired by learning.
  • startle response (noun) A complicated involuntary reaction to a sudden unexpected stimulus (especially a loud noise); involves flexion of most skeletal muscles and a variety of visceral reactions.
  • frequency response (noun) (electronics) a curve representing the output-to-input ratio of a transducer as a function of frequency.
  • anamnestic response (noun) Renewed rapid production of an antibody on the second (or subsequent) encounter with the same antigen.
  • conditional response (noun) An acquired response that is under the control of (conditional on the occurrence of) a stimulus.
  • conditioned response (noun) An acquired response that is under the control of (conditional on the occurrence of) a stimulus.
  • immunologic response (noun) A bodily defense reaction that recognizes an invading substance (an antigen: such as a virus or fungus or bacteria or transplanted organ) and produces antibodies specific against that antigen.
  • electrodermal response (noun) A change in the electrical properties of the skin in response to stress or anxiety; can be measured either by recording the electrical resistance of the skin or by recording weak currents generated by the body.
  • galvanic skin response (noun) A change in the electrical properties of the skin in response to stress or anxiety; can be measured either by recording the electrical resistance of the skin or by recording weak currents generated by the body.
  • humoral immune response (noun) An immune response (chiefly against bacterial invasion) that is mediated by B cells.
  • psychogalvanic response (noun) A change in the electrical properties of the skin in response to stress or anxiety; can be measured either by recording the electrical resistance of the skin or by recording weak currents generated by the body.
  • electrical skin response (noun) A change in the electrical properties of the skin in response to stress or anxiety; can be measured either by recording the electrical resistance of the skin or by recording weak currents generated by the body.
  • frequency-response curve (noun) (electronics) a graph of frequency response with signal amplitude or gain plotted against frequency.
