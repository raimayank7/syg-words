Right: meaning, synonyms and examples

Right is used as a noun (8 senses), adjective (14 senses), adverb (10 senses) and verb (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: An abstract idea of that which is due to a person or governmental body by law or tradition or nature.

In Hindi
सही; दायाँ; अधिकार
Synonyms
right field, rightfield, right wing, right hand, rightfulness, correct, proper, right-hand, good, ripe
Opposites
left, wrong, center, wrongly
Word family
rightness

Right as a noun

  1. An abstract idea of that which is due to a person or governmental body by law or tradition or nature.

    “They are endowed by their Creator with certain unalienable Rights.” “Certain rights can never be granted to the government but must be kept in the hands of the people.” “A right is not something that somebody gives you; it is something that nobody can take away.”

    A kind of: abstraction · Kinds: access, advowson, cabotage, claim, due, entree, floor, grant

  2. Location near or direction toward the right side; i.e. the side to the south when a person or object faces east.

    “He stood on the right.”

    Opposite: left · A kind of: position · Kinds: stage right

  3. The piece of ground in the outfield on the catcher's right.

    Synonyms: right field, rightfield · A kind of: tract

  4. Those who support political or social or economic conservatism; those who believe that things are better left unchanged.

    Synonyms: right wing · A kind of: faction · Kinds: religious right, hard right

  5. The hand that is on the right side of the body.

    “He writes with his right hand but pitches with his left.” “Hit him with quick rights to the body.”

    Synonyms: right hand · A kind of: hand

  6. A turn toward the side of the body that is on the south when the person is facing east.

    “Take a right at the corner.”

    A kind of: turn

  7. Anything in accord with principles of justice.

    “He feels he is in the right.” “The rightfulness of his claim.”

    Synonyms: rightfulness · Opposite: wrong · A kind of: justice

  8. (frequently plural) the interest possessed by law or custom in some intangible thing.

    “Mineral rights.” “Film rights.”

    A kind of: interest

Right as an adjective

  1. Being or located on or directed toward the side of the body to the east when facing north.

    “My right hand.” “Right center field.” “A right-hand turn.”

    Opposite: left · Similar: far, rightmost, right-hand, starboard

  2. Free from error; especially conforming to fact or truth.

    “The correct answer.” “The correct version.” “The right answer.”

    Synonyms: correct · Opposite: wrong · Similar: accurate, letter-perfect, straight

  3. Socially right or correct.

    “It isn't right to leave the party without saying goodbye.” “Correct behavior.”

    Synonyms: correct · Similar: proper

  4. In conformance with justice or law or morality.

    “Do the right thing and confess.”

    Opposite: wrong · Similar: ethical

  5. Correct in opinion or judgment.

    “Time proved him right.”

    Synonyms: correct · Opposite: wrong · Similar: right-minded

  6. Appropriate for a condition or purpose or occasion or a person's character, needs.

    “Everything in its proper place.” “The right man for the job.” “She is not suitable for the position.”

    Synonyms: proper · Similar: appropriate

  7. Of or belonging to the political or intellectual right.

    Opposite: left, center · Similar: conservative, oldline, reactionary, rightish, rightist

  8. In or into a satisfactory condition.

    “Things are right again now.” “Put things right.”

    Similar: satisfactory

  9. Intended for the right hand.

    “A right-hand glove.”

    Synonyms: right-hand · Similar: right-handed

  10. In accord with accepted standards of usage or procedure.

    “What's the right word for this?” “The right way to open oysters.”

    Synonyms: correct · Similar: proper

  11. Having the axis perpendicular to the base.

    “A right angle.”

    Similar: perpendicular

  12. (of the side of cloth or clothing) facing or intended to face outward.

    “The right side of the cloth showed the pattern.” “Be sure your shirt is right side out.”

    Similar: outside

  13. Most suitable or right for a particular purpose.

    “A good time to plant tomatoes.” “The right time to act.” “The time is ripe for great sociological changes.”

    Synonyms: good, ripe · Similar: opportune

  14. Precisely accurate.

    “A veracious account.”

    Synonyms: veracious · Similar: accurate

Right as an adverb

  1. Precisely, exactly.

    “Stand right here!”

  2. Immediately.

    “She called right after dinner.”

  3. Exactly.

    “He fell flop on his face.”

    Synonyms: flop

  4. Toward or on the right; also used figuratively.

    “He looked right and left.” “The party has moved right.”

    Opposite: left

  5. In the right manner.

    “Please do your job properly!” “Can't you carry me decent?”

    Synonyms: properly, decently, decent, in good order, the right way

  6. An interjection expressing agreement.

    Synonyms: right on

  7. Completely.

    “She felt right at home.” “He fell right into the trap.”

  8. (Southern regional intensive) very; to a great degree.

    “The baby is mighty cute.” “He's mighty tired.” “It is powerful humid.”

    Synonyms: mighty, mightily, powerful

  9. In accordance with moral or social standards.

    “That serves him right.” “Do right by him.”

    Synonyms: justly

  10. In an accurate manner.

    “The flower had been correctly depicted by his son.” “He guessed right.”

    Synonyms: correctly, aright · Opposite: wrongly

Right as a verb

  1. Make reparations or amends for.

    “Right a wrongs done to the victims of the Holocaust.”

    Synonyms: compensate, redress, correct · Opposite: wrong · A kind of: change · Kinds: over-correct, expiate

  2. Put in or restore to an upright position.

    “They righted the sailboat that had capsized.”

    A kind of: change

  3. Regain an upright or proper position.

    “The capsized boat righted again.”

    A kind of: change posture

  4. Make right or correct.

    “Correct the mistakes.” “Rectify the calculation.”

    Synonyms: correct, rectify · A kind of: change by reversal · Kinds: rectify, debug

Phrases and compounds with "right"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • right on (adverb) An interjection expressing agreement.
  • all right (adverb) An expression of agreement normally occurring at the beginning of a sentence.
  • far-right (adjective) Extremely conservative.
  • own right (noun) By title vested in yourself or by virtue of qualifications that you have achieved.
  • put right (verb) Settle or put right.
  • hard right (noun) The extreme right wing.
  • just right (adverb) In every detail.
  • right away (adverb) Without delay or hesitation; with no time intervening.
  • right bank (noun) The region of Paris on the north bank of the Seine.
  • right hand (noun) The hand that is on the right side of the body.
  • right wing (noun) Those who support political or social or economic conservatism; those who believe that things are better left unchanged.
  • right-down (adjective) Complete and without restriction or qualification; sometimes used informally as intensifiers.
  • right-eyed (adjective) Having only the right eye.
  • right-hand (adjective) Located on or directed toward the right.
  • right-wing (adjective) Believing in or supporting tenets of the political right.
  • civil right (noun) Right or rights belonging to a person by reason of citizenship including especially the fundamental freedoms and privileges guaranteed by the 13th and 14th amendments and subsequent acts of Congress including the right to legal and social and economic equality.
