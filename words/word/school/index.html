School: meaning, synonyms and examples

School is used as a noun (7 senses) and verb (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: An educational institution.

In Hindi
स्कूल, विद्यालय
Synonyms
schoolhouse, schooling, schooltime, school day, shoal, educate, train, cultivate, civilize, civilise
Word family
scholastic, schooling

School as a noun

  1. An educational institution.

    “The school was founded in 1900.”

    A kind of: educational institution · Kinds: academy, correspondence school, crammer, dancing school, direct-grant school, driving school, finishing school, flying school

  2. A building where young people receive education.

    “The school was built in 1932.” “He walked to school every morning.”

    Synonyms: schoolhouse · A kind of: building · Kinds: conservatory, day school

  3. The process of being formally educated at a school.

    “What will you do when you finish school?”

    Synonyms: schooling · A kind of: education

  4. A body of creative artists or writers or thinkers linked by a similar style or by similar teachers.

    “The Venetian school of painting.”

    A kind of: body · Kinds: deconstructivism, historical school, pointillism, art nouveau, lake poets, secession

  5. The period of instruction in a school; the time period when school is in session.

    “Stay after school.” “He didn't miss a single day of school.” “When the school day was done we would walk home together.”

    Synonyms: schooltime, school day · A kind of: time period

  6. An educational institution's faculty and students.

    “The school keeps parents informed.” “The whole school turned out for the game.”

    A kind of: educational institution

  7. A large group of fish.

    “A school of small glittering fish swam by.”

    Synonyms: shoal · A kind of: animal group

School as a verb

  1. Educate in or as if in a school.

    “The children are schooled at great cost to their parents in private institutions.”

    A kind of: educate · Kinds: home-school

  2. Teach or refine to be discriminative in taste or judgment.

    “Cultivate your musical taste.” “Train your tastebuds.” “She is well schooled in poetry.”

    Synonyms: educate, train, cultivate, civilize, civilise · A kind of: polish · Kinds: sophisticate

  3. Swim in or form a large group of fish.

    “A cluster of schooling fish was attracted to the bait.”

    A kind of: swim

Phrases and compounds with "school"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • art school (noun) A school specializing in art.
  • day school (noun) A private school taking day students only.
  • law school (noun) A graduate school offering study leading to a law degree.
  • old school (noun) A class of people favoring traditional ideas.
  • school bus (noun) A bus used to transport children to or from school.
  • school day (noun) Any day on which school is in session.
  • grad school (noun) A school in a university offering study leading to degrees beyond the bachelor's degree.
  • high school (noun) A public secondary school usually including grades 9 through 12.
  • home-school (verb) Educate (one's children) at home instead of sending (them) to a school.
  • prep school (noun) A private secondary school.
  • school bell (noun) A bell rung to announce beginning or ending of class.
  • school ship (noun) A ship used to train students as sailors.
  • school term (noun) The time during which a school holds classes.
  • school text (noun) A book prepared for use in schools or colleges.
  • school year (noun) The period of time each year when the school is open and people are studying.
  • after-school (adjective) Outside regular school hours.
