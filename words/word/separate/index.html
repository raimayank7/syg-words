Separate: meaning, synonyms and examples

Separate is used as a verb (12 senses), adjective (3 senses) and noun (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Act as a barrier between; stand between.

In Hindi
अलग; अलग करना
Synonyms
divide, disunite, part, distinguish, differentiate, secern, secernate, severalize, severalise, tell
Opposites
joint
Word family
separation, separator, separative, separatist, separateness

Separate as a verb

  1. Act as a barrier between; stand between.

    “The mountain range divides the two countries.”

    Synonyms: divide

  2. Force, take, or pull apart.

    “He separated the fighting children.” “Moses parted the Red Sea.”

    Synonyms: disunite, divide, part · A kind of: move · Kinds: compartmentalize, polarize, sequester, disjoin, disjoint, disconnect, cut, tear

  3. Mark as different.

    “We distinguish several kinds of maple.”

    Synonyms: distinguish, differentiate, secern, secernate, severalize, severalise, tell, tell apart · A kind of: identify · Kinds: know, discriminate, label, label, individualize, contrast, severalize, contradistinguish

  4. Separate into parts or portions.

    “Divide the cake into three equal parts.” “The British carved up the Ottoman Empire after World War I.”

    Synonyms: divide, split, split up, dissever, carve up · A kind of: change integrity · Kinds: subdivide, format, sectionalize, triangulate, unitize, lot, parcel, sliver

  5. Divide into components or constituents.

    “Separate the wheat from the chaff.”

    A kind of: change integrity · Kinds: decompose, dialyse, peptize, macerate, tease, filter, extract, fractionate

  6. Arrange or order by classes or categories.

    “How would you classify these pottery shards--are they prehistoric?”

    Synonyms: classify, class, sort, assort, sort out · A kind of: categorize · Kinds: unitize, catalogue, isolate, refer, reclassify, size, dichotomize, pigeonhole

  7. Make a division or separation.

    Synonyms: divide · Kinds: partition, break, disperse, rail, detach, shut off

  8. Discontinue an association or relation; go different ways.

    “The business partners broke over a tax question.” “The couple separated after 25 years of marriage.” “My friend and I split up.”

    Synonyms: part, split up, split, break, break up · Kinds: give the axe, disunify, disassociate, break with, divorce, secede, break

  9. Go one's own way; move apart.

    “The friends separated after the party.”

    Synonyms: part, split · A kind of: move · Kinds: disperse, break up, diffract

  10. Become separated into pieces or fragments.

    “The figurine broke.” “The freshly baked loaf fell apart.”

    Synonyms: break, split up, fall apart, come apart · A kind of: change integrity · Kinds: burst, puncture, burst, smash, ladder, snap, break up, crush

  11. Come apart.

    “The two pieces that we had glued separated.”

    Synonyms: divide, part · A kind of: change · Kinds: subdivide, polarize, calve, chip, disjoin, detach, segregate, segment

  12. Divide into two or more branches so as to form a fork.

    “The road forks.”

    Synonyms: branch, ramify, fork, furcate · A kind of: diverge · Kinds: arborize, twig, bifurcate, trifurcate

Separate as an adjective

  1. Independent; not united or joint.

    “A problem consisting of two separate issues.” “They went their separate ways.” “Formed a separate church.”

    Opposite: joint · Similar: apart, asunder, detached, discrete, disjoint, disjunct

  2. Standing apart; not attached to or supported by anything.

    “A freestanding bell tower.” “A house with a separate garage.”

    Synonyms: freestanding · Similar: detached

  3. Have the connection undone; having become separate.

    Synonyms: disjoined · Similar: unconnected

Separate as a noun

  1. A separately printed article that originally appeared in a larger publication.

    Synonyms: offprint, reprint · A kind of: article

  2. A garment that can be purchased separately and worn in combinations with other garments.

    A kind of: garment

Phrases and compounds with "separate"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • separate off (verb) Partition by means of a divider, such as a screen.
  • separate out (verb) Remove by passing through a filter.
