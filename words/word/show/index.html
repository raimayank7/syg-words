Show: meaning, synonyms and examples

Show is used as a verb (12 senses) and noun (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: Give an exhibition of to an interested audience.

In Hindi
दिखाना; प्रदर्शन
Synonyms
demo, exhibit, present, demonstrate, prove, establish, shew, testify, bear witness, evidence
Opposites
hide
Word family
showing, showy

Show as a verb

  1. Give an exhibition of to an interested audience.

    “She shows her dogs frequently.” “We will demo the new software in Washington.”

    Synonyms: demo, exhibit, present, demonstrate · A kind of: show · Kinds: bring home

  2. Establish the validity of something, as by an example, explanation or experiment.

    “The experiment demonstrated the instability of the compound.” “The mathematician showed the validity of the conjecture.”

    Synonyms: prove, demonstrate, establish, shew · A kind of: confirm · Kinds: prove oneself, prove, negate, stultify

  3. Provide evidence for.

    “The blood test showed that he was the father.” “Her behavior testified to her incompetence.”

    Synonyms: testify, bear witness, prove, evidence · A kind of: inform · Kinds: attest, presume, adduce

  4. Make visible or noticeable.

    “She showed her talent for cooking.” “Show me your etchings, please.”

    Opposite: hide · Kinds: give, reflect, peep, project, do justice, flash, screen, expose

  5. Show in, or as in, a picture.

    “This scene depicts country life.” “The face of the child is rendered with much tenderness in this painting.”

    Synonyms: picture, depict, render · A kind of: represent · Kinds: illustrate, map

  6. Give expression to.

    “She showed her disappointment.”

    Synonyms: express, evince · A kind of: convey · Kinds: sneer, imply, burst out, suggest, imply, give, exude, vent

  7. Indicate a place, direction, person, or thing; either spatially or figuratively.

    “I showed the customer the glove section.” “He pointed to the empty parking space.” “He indicated his opponents.”

    Synonyms: indicate, point, designate · A kind of: inform · Kinds: point, finger, signalize, reflect

  8. Be or become visible or noticeable.

    “His good upbringing really shows.” “The dirty side will show.”

    Synonyms: show up · A kind of: appear · Kinds: register

  9. Indicate a certain reading; of gauges and instruments.

    “The thermometer showed thirteen degrees below zero.” “The gauge read `empty'.”

    Synonyms: read, register, record · A kind of: indicate · Kinds: say, show, strike

  10. Give evidence of, as of records.

    “The diary shows his distress that evening.”

    A kind of: read · Kinds: attest, surcharge

  11. Take (someone) to their seats, as in theaters or auditoriums.

    “The usher showed us to our seats.”

    Synonyms: usher · A kind of: lead · Kinds: marshal

  12. Finish third or better in a horse or dog race.

    A kind of: race

Show as a noun

  1. The act of publicly exhibiting or entertaining.

    “A remarkable show of skill.”

    A kind of: entertainment · Kinds: carnival, dog show, horseshow, raree-show, circus, cabaret, ice show, interlude

  2. Something intended to communicate a particular impression.

    “Made a display of strength.” “A show of impatience.” “A good show of looking interested.”

    Synonyms: display · A kind of: demonstration · Kinds: big stick, gaudery

  3. A social event involving a public performance or entertainment.

    “They wanted to see some of the shows on Broadway.”

    A kind of: social event · Kinds: stage dancing, movie, attraction, broadcast, performance, burlesque, play, galanty show

  4. Pretending that something is the case in order to make a good impression.

    “They try to keep up appearances.” “That ceremony is just for show.”

    Synonyms: appearance · A kind of: pretense

Phrases and compounds with "show"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • no-show (noun) A guest who fails to notify a hotel or restaurant when canceling a reservation.
  • show up (verb) Appear or become visible; make a showing.
  • tv show (noun) A program broadcast by television.
  • dog show (noun) A competitive exhibition of dogs.
  • ice show (noun) Any entertainment performed by ice skaters.
  • show biz (noun) Those involved in providing entertainment: radio and television and films and theater.
  • show off (verb) Display proudly; act ostentatiously or pretentiously.
  • show-off (noun) Someone who deliberately behaves in such a way as to attract attention.
  • chat show (noun) A program during which well-known people discuss a topic or answer questions telephoned in by the audience.
  • dumb show (noun) A performance using gestures and body movements without words.
  • game show (noun) A television or radio program in which contestants compete for awards.
  • news show (noun) A program devoted to current events, often using interviews and commentary.
  • road show (noun) A show on tour.
  • show bill (noun) A poster advertising a show or play.
  • show card (noun) A poster advertising a show or play.
  • show time (noun) The point in time at which an entertainment (a movie or television show etc.) is scheduled to begin.

Word family

Words built from the same root as "show": showing, showy. Learning a family together is quicker than learning each word alone.

How to use this page
