Slip: meaning, synonyms and examples

Slip is used as a verb (11 senses) and noun (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: Move stealthily.

In Hindi
फिसलना; पर्ची
Synonyms
steal, skid, slue, slew, slide, drop off, drop away, fall away, err, mistake
Word family
slippage, slippy

Slip as a verb

  1. Move stealthily.

    “The ship slipped away in the darkness.”

    Synonyms: steal · A kind of: move

  2. Insert inconspicuously or quickly or quietly.

    “He slipped some money into the waiter's hand.”

    A kind of: insert

  3. Move obliquely or sideways, usually in an uncontrolled manner.

    “The wheels skidded against the sidewalk.”

    Synonyms: skid, slue, slew, slide · A kind of: glide · Kinds: submarine, skid, side-slip

  4. Get worse.

    “My grades are slipping.”

    Synonyms: drop off, drop away, fall away · A kind of: worsen · Kinds: lapse

  5. Move smoothly and easily.

    “The bolt slipped into place.” “Water slipped from the polished marble.”

    A kind of: escape

  6. To make a mistake or be incorrect.

    Synonyms: err, mistake · Kinds: misremember, stumble, misjudge, fall for

  7. Pass on stealthily.

    “He slipped me the key when nobody was looking.”

    Synonyms: sneak · A kind of: pass

  8. Move easily.

    “Slip into something comfortable.”

    A kind of: move

  9. Cause to move with a smooth or sliding motion.

    “He slipped the bolt into place.”

    A kind of: move

  10. Pass out of one's memory.

    Synonyms: slip one's mind · A kind of: forget

  11. Move out of position.

    “Dislocate joints.” “The artificial hip joint luxated and had to be put back surgically.”

    Synonyms: dislocate, luxate, splay · A kind of: move

Slip as a noun

  1. A socially awkward or tactless act.

    Synonyms: faux pas, gaffe, solecism, gaucherie · A kind of: blunder

  2. A minor inadvertent mistake usually observed in speech or writing or in small accidents or memory lapses etc.

    Synonyms: slip-up, miscue, parapraxis · A kind of: mistake

  3. Potter's clay that is thinned and used for coating or decorating ceramics.

    A kind of: potter's clay

  4. A part (sometimes a root or leaf or bud) removed from a plant to propagate a new plant through rooting or grafting.

    Synonyms: cutting · A kind of: stalk · Kinds: quickset

  5. A young and slender person.

    “He's a mere slip of a lad.”

    A kind of: young person

  6. A place where a craft can be made fast.

    Synonyms: mooring, moorage, berth · A kind of: anchorage

  7. An accidental misstep threatening (or causing) a fall.

    “He blamed his slip on the ice.” “The jolt caused many slips and a few spills.”

    Synonyms: trip · A kind of: mishap · Kinds: spill

  8. A slippery smoothness.

    “He could feel the slickness of the tiller.”

    Synonyms: slickness, slick, slipperiness · A kind of: smoothness

  9. Artifact consisting of a narrow flat piece of material.

    Synonyms: strip · A kind of: artifact · Kinds: band, band, band, cramp, reef, lead, mullion, ribbon

  10. A small sheet of paper.

    “A receipt slip.”

    Synonyms: slip of paper · A kind of: sheet

  11. A woman's sleeveless undergarment.

    Synonyms: chemise, shimmy, shift, teddy · A kind of: undergarment

  12. Bed linen consisting of a cover for a pillow.

    “The burglar carried his loot in a pillowcase.”

    Synonyms: case, pillowcase, pillow slip · A kind of: bed linen

  13. An unexpected slide.

    Synonyms: skid, sideslip · A kind of: slide

  14. A flight maneuver; aircraft slides sideways in the air.

    Synonyms: sideslip · A kind of: flight maneuver

Phrases and compounds with "slip"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • slip by (verb) Pass by.
  • slip in (verb) Insert casually.
  • slip on (verb) Put on with ease or speed.
  • slip up (verb) Make an error.
  • slip-on (noun) An article of clothing (garment or shoe) that is easily slipped on or off.
  • slip-up (noun) A minor inadvertent mistake usually observed in speech or writing or in small accidents or memory lapses etc.
  • slip off (verb) Take off with ease or speed.
  • half-slip (noun) Undergarment worn under a skirt.
  • pink slip (noun) Official notice that you have been fired from your job.
  • side-slip (verb) Slide sideways through the air in a downward direction in an airplane along an inclined lateral axis.
  • slip away (verb) Leave furtively and stealthily.
  • slip ring (noun) Connection consisting of a metal ring on a rotating part of a machine; provides a continuous electrical connection through brushes on stationary contacts.
  • slip road (noun) A short road giving access to an expressway.
  • cover slip (noun) A small and very thin piece of glass used to cover the specimen on a microscope slide.
  • slip coach (noun) A railway car at the end of the train; it can be detached without stopping the train.
  • slip noose (noun) A loop formed in a cord or rope by means of a slipknot; it binds tighter as the cord or rope is pulled.
