Spring: meaning, synonyms and examples

Spring is used as a noun (6 senses) and verb (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: The season of growth.

In Hindi
वसंत; स्प्रिंग; झरना
Synonyms
springtime, fountain, outflow, outpouring, natural spring, give, springiness, leap, leaping, saltation

Spring as a noun

  1. The season of growth.

    “The emerging buds were a sure sign of spring.” “He will hold office until the spring of next year.”

    Synonyms: springtime · A kind of: season

  2. A metal elastic device that returns to its shape or position when pushed or pulled or pressed.

    “The spring was broken.”

    A kind of: elastic device · Kinds: bedspring, coil spring, leaf spring, mainspring, spiral spring

  3. A natural flow of ground water.

    Synonyms: fountain, outflow, outpouring, natural spring · A kind of: geological formation · Kinds: geyser, hot spring

  4. A point at which water issues forth.

    A kind of: beginning

  5. The elasticity of something that can be stretched and returns to its original length.

    Synonyms: give, springiness · A kind of: elasticity

  6. A light, self-propelled movement upwards or forwards.

    Synonyms: leap, leaping, saltation, bound, bounce · A kind of: jump · Kinds: caper, pounce

Spring as a verb

  1. Move forward by leaps and bounds.

    “The horse bounded across the meadow.” “The child leapt across the puddle.” “Can you jump over the fence?”

    Synonyms: jump, leap, bound · A kind of: move · Kinds: pronk, bounce, burst, bounce, capriole, galumph, ski jump, saltate

  2. Develop into a distinctive entity.

    “Our plans began to take shape.”

    Synonyms: form, take form, take shape · A kind of: become · Kinds: regenerate

  3. Spring back; spring away from an impact.

    “The rubber ball bounced.” “These particles do not resile but they unite after they collide.”

    Synonyms: bounce, resile, take a hop, bound, rebound, recoil, reverberate, ricochet · A kind of: jump · Kinds: kick back, skip, carom

  4. Develop suddenly.

    “The tire sprang a leak.”

    A kind of: grow

  5. Produce or disclose suddenly or unexpectedly.

    “He sprang these news on me just as I was leaving.”

    A kind of: unwrap

Phrases and compounds with "spring"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • spring up (verb) Come into existence; take on form or shape.
  • air spring (noun) A mechanical device using confined air to absorb the shock of motion.
  • box spring (noun) A coiled bedspring in a frame that is covered with cloth.
  • hot spring (noun) A natural spring of water at a temperature of 70 F or above.
  • spring gun (noun) A gun that is set to fire on any intruder that comes in contact with the wire that sets it off.
  • coil spring (noun) A spring in the shape of a coil.
  • leaf spring (noun) Long narrow spring consisting of several layers of metal springs bracketed together.
  • spring frog (noun) Similar to bullfrog; found in or near marshes and ponds; of United States and Canada.
  • spring roll (noun) Minced vegetables and meat wrapped in a pancake and fried.
  • spring break (noun) A week or more of recess during the spring term at school.
  • spring cress (noun) Small white-flowered cress common in wet places in eastern North America.
  • spring heath (noun) Dwarf European shrub with very early blooming bell-shaped red flowers.
  • spring onion (noun) A young onion before the bulb has enlarged; eaten in salads.
  • spring scale (noun) A balance that measure weight by the tension on a helical spring.
  • spring vetch (noun) Herbaceous climbing plant valuable as fodder and for soil-building.
  • spring water (noun) Underground water that is held in the soil and in pervious rocks.
