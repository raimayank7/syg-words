Stand: meaning, synonyms and examples

Stand is used as a verb (12 senses) and noun (12 senses). Its most common meaning is: Be standing; be upright.

In Hindi
खड़ा होना; स्टैंड
Synonyms
stand up, remain firm, digest, endure, stick out, stomach, bear, tolerate, support, brook
Opposites
sit, lie, yield
Word family
standee, stander, standing, stance, standard

Stand as a verb

  1. Be standing; be upright.

    “We had to stand for the entire performance!”

    Synonyms: stand up · Opposite: sit, lie · A kind of: rest · Kinds: ramp, stand back, line up

  2. Be in some specified state or condition.

    “I stand corrected.”

    A kind of: be

  3. Occupy a place or location, also metaphorically.

    “We stand on common ground.”

    A kind of: be

  4. Hold one's ground; maintain a position; be steadfast or upright.

    “I am standing my ground and won't give in!”

    Synonyms: remain firm · Opposite: yield · A kind of: resist

  5. Put up with something or somebody unpleasant.

    “I cannot bear his constant criticism.” “The new secretary had to endure a lot of unprofessional remarks.” “He learned to tolerate the heat.”

    Synonyms: digest, endure, stick out, stomach, bear, tolerate, support, brook · A kind of: permit · Kinds: accept, stand for, bear up, take lying down, take a joke, sit out, pay

  6. Have or maintain a position or stand on an issue.

    “Where do you stand on the War?”

    A kind of: evaluate · Kinds: align

  7. Remain inactive or immobile.

    “Standing water.”

    A kind of: stay

  8. Be in effect; be or remain in force.

    “The law stands!”

    A kind of: continue · Kinds: wash

  9. Be tall; have a height of; copula.

    “She stands 6 feet tall.”

    A kind of: measure

  10. Put into an upright position.

    “Can you stand the bookshelf up?”

    Synonyms: stand up, place upright · A kind of: put

  11. Withstand the force of something.

    “The trees resisted her.” “Stand the test of time.” “The mountain climbers had to fend against the ice and snow.”

    Synonyms: resist, fend · A kind of: fight

  12. Be available for stud services.

    “Male domestic animals such as stallions serve selected females.”

    A kind of: serve

Stand as a noun

  1. A support or foundation.

    “The base of the lamp.”

    Synonyms: base, pedestal · A kind of: support · Kinds: brass monkey, staddle, trivet, trivet

  2. The position where a thing or person stands.

    A kind of: position · Kinds: cabstand

  3. A growth of similar plants (usually trees) in a particular area.

    “They cut down a stand of trees.”

    A kind of: vegetation

  4. A small table for holding articles of various kinds.

    “A bedside stand.”

    A kind of: table · Kinds: lectern

  5. A support for displaying various articles.

    “The newspapers were arranged on a rack.”

    Synonyms: rack · A kind of: support · Kinds: cruet-stand, dress rack, magazine rack, music stand, spice rack, tripod

  6. An interruption of normal activity.

    Synonyms: standstill, tie-up · A kind of: stop

  7. A mental position from which things are viewed.

    “We should consider this problem from the viewpoint of the Russians.” “Teaching history gave him a special point of view toward current events.”

    Synonyms: point of view, viewpoint, standpoint · A kind of: position · Kinds: cityscape, landscape, slant, complexion

  8. A booth where articles are displayed for sale.

    Synonyms: stall, sales booth · A kind of: booth · Kinds: coffee stall, newsstand

  9. A stop made by a touring musical or theatrical group to give a performance.

    “A one-night stand.”

    A kind of: stop

  10. Tiered seats consisting of a structure (often made of wood) where people can sit to watch an event (game or parade).

    A kind of: tiered seat · Kinds: bleachers, grandstand, reviewing stand

  11. A platform where a (brass) band can play in the open air.

    Synonyms: bandstand, outdoor stage · A kind of: platform

  12. A defensive effort.

    “The army made a final stand at the Rhone.”

    A kind of: defense · Kinds: repulsion

Phrases and compounds with "stand"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • stand by (verb) Not act or do anything.
  • stand in (verb) Be a substitute.
  • stand up (verb) Rise to one's feet.
  • stand-in (noun) Someone who takes the place of another (as when things get dangerous or difficult).
  • stand-up (adjective) Requiring a standing position.
  • stand for (verb) Express indirectly by an image, form, or model; be a symbol.
  • stand oil (noun) A thick oil comprised of linseed, tung, or soya oils which have been heated to over 300 C.
  • stand out (verb) Be highly noticeable.
  • stand pat (verb) Refuse to abandon one's opinion or belief.
  • coat stand (noun) An upright pole with pegs or hooks on which to hang clothing.
  • home stand (noun) A series of successive games played at a team's home field or court.
  • stand back (verb) Stay clear of, avoid.
  • stand fast (verb) Refuse to abandon one's opinion or belief.
  • stand firm (verb) Stand up or offer resistance to somebody or something.
  • stand-down (noun) A suspension and relaxation from an alert state or a state of readiness.
  • cruet-stand (noun) A stand for cruets containing various condiments.
