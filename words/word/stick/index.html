Stick: meaning, synonyms and examples

Stick is used as a noun (8 senses) and verb (14 senses). Its most common meaning is: An implement consisting of a length of wood.

In Hindi
छड़ी; चिपकना
Synonyms
control stick, joystick, pin, peg, lodge, wedge, deposit, stay, stick around, stay put
Word family
sticker

Stick as a noun

  1. An implement consisting of a length of wood.

    “He collected dry sticks for a campfire.” “The kid had a candied apple on a stick.”

    A kind of: implement · Kinds: bow, club, divining rod, drumstick, linstock, matchstick, maulstick, singlestick

  2. A small thin branch of a tree.

    A kind of: limb

  3. A lever used by a pilot to control the ailerons and elevators of an airplane.

    Synonyms: control stick, joystick · A kind of: lever

  4. A rectangular quarter pound block of butter or margarine.

    A kind of: butter, margarine

  5. Informal terms for the leg.

    “Fever left him weak on his sticks.”

    Synonyms: pin, peg · A kind of: leg

  6. A long implement (usually made of wood) that is shaped so that hockey or polo players can hit a puck or ball.

    A kind of: sports equipment · Kinds: hockey stick, polo mallet

  7. A long thin implement resembling a length of wood.

    “Cinnamon sticks.” “A stick of dynamite.”

    A kind of: implement

  8. Threat of a penalty.

    “The policy so far is all stick and no carrot.”

    A kind of: punishment

Stick as a verb

  1. Put, fix, force, or implant.

    “Lodge a bullet in the table.” “Stick your thumb in the crack.”

    Synonyms: lodge, wedge, deposit · A kind of: fasten · Kinds: redeposit

  2. Stay put (in a certain place).

    “We are staying in Detroit; we are not moving to Cincinnati.” “Stay put in the corner here!” “Stick around and you will learn something!”

    Synonyms: stay, stick around, stay put · A kind of: stay in place

  3. Stick to firmly.

    “Will this wallpaper adhere to the wall?”

    Synonyms: adhere, hold fast, bond, bind, stick to · A kind of: attach · Kinds: bind

  4. Be or become fixed.

    “The door sticks--we will have to plane it.”

    A kind of: be

  5. Endure.

    “The label stuck to her for the rest of her life.”

    A kind of: persist

  6. Be a devoted follower or supporter.

    “The residents of this village adhered to Catholicism.” “She sticks to her principles.”

    Synonyms: adhere · A kind of: adopt

  7. Be loyal to.

    “She stood by her husband in times of trouble.” “The friends stuck together through the war.”

    Synonyms: stand by, stick by, adhere · A kind of: be

  8. Cover and decorate with objects that pierce the surface.

    “Stick some feathers in the turkey before you serve it.”

    A kind of: decorate

  9. Fasten with an adhesive material like glue.

    “Stick the poster onto the wall.”

    A kind of: fasten

  10. Fasten with or as with pins or nails.

    “Stick the photo onto the corkboard.”

    A kind of: fasten

  11. Fasten into place by fixing an end or point into something.

    “Stick the corner of the sheet under the mattress.”

    A kind of: fasten

  12. Pierce with a thrust using a pointed instrument.

    “He stuck the cloth with the needle.”

    A kind of: pierce

  13. Pierce or penetrate or puncture with something pointed.

    “He stuck the needle into his finger.”

    A kind of: pierce

  14. Come or be in close contact with; stick or hold together and resist separation.

    “The dress clings to her body.” “The label stuck to the box.” “The sushi rice grains cohere.”

    Synonyms: cling, cleave, adhere, cohere · A kind of: touch · Kinds: mold, conglutinate, agglutinate

Phrases and compounds with "stick"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • stick by (verb) Be loyal to.
  • stick in (verb) Insert casually.
  • stick on (verb) Apply a heavy coat to.
  • stick to (verb) Stick to firmly.
  • stick up (verb) Rob at gunpoint or by means of some other threat.
  • stick-on (adjective) Of something (a paper label or postage stamp) gummed in advance.
  • big stick (noun) A display of force or power.
  • cue stick (noun) Sports implement consisting of a tapering rod used to strike a cue ball in pool or billiards.
  • stick lac (noun) Lac in its natural state as scraped off twigs and dried.
  • stick out (verb) Extend out or project in space.
  • fish stick (noun) A long fillet of fish breaded and fried.
  • joss stick (noun) A slender stick of incense burned before a joss by the Chinese.
  • pogo stick (noun) Plaything consisting of a pole with foot rests and a strong spring; propelled by jumping.
  • polo stick (noun) A mallet used to strike the ball in polo.
  • pool stick (noun) Sports implement consisting of a tapering rod used to strike a cue ball in pool or billiards.
  • rain stick (noun) A percussion instrument that is made from a dried cactus branch that is hollowed out and filled with small pebbles and capped at both ends; makes the sound of falling rain when tilted; origin was in Chile where tribesmen used it in ceremonies to bring rain.
