Stretch: meaning, synonyms and examples

Stretch is used as a verb (11 senses), noun (7 senses) and adjective (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Occupy a large, elongated area.

In Hindi
खींचना, फैलाना
Synonyms
stretch along, extend, unfold, stretch out, elongate, load, adulterate, dilute, debase, reach
Opposites
shrink
Word family
stretching, stretcher, stretchy

Stretch as a verb

  1. Occupy a large, elongated area.

    “The park stretched beneath the train line.”

    Synonyms: stretch along · A kind of: be

  2. Extend one's limbs or muscles, or the entire body.

    “Stretch your legs!” “Extend your right arm above your head.”

    Synonyms: extend · A kind of: tense · Kinds: spread-eagle, crane

  3. Extend or stretch out to a greater or the full length.

    “Unfold the newspaper.” “Stretch out that piece of cloth.” “Extend the TV antenna.”

    Synonyms: unfold, stretch out, extend · A kind of: change shape

  4. Become longer by being stretched and pulled.

    “The fabric stretches.”

    Opposite: shrink · A kind of: grow · Kinds: give

  5. Make long or longer by pulling and stretching.

    “Stretch the fabric.”

    Synonyms: elongate · A kind of: lengthen · Kinds: tree

  6. Lie down comfortably.

    “To enjoy the picnic, we stretched out on the grass.”

    Synonyms: stretch out · A kind of: lie down

  7. Pull in opposite directions.

    “During the Inquisition, the torturers would stretch their victims on a rack.”

    A kind of: pull · Kinds: pull back

  8. Extend the scope or meaning of; often unduly.

    “Stretch the limits.” “Stretch my patience.” “Stretch the imagination.”

    A kind of: widen

  9. Corrupt, debase, or make impure by adding a foreign or inferior substance; often by replacing valuable ingredients with inferior ones.

    Synonyms: load, adulterate, dilute, debase · A kind of: corrupt · Kinds: water down, sophisticate

  10. Increase in quantity or bulk by adding a cheaper substance.

    “Stretch the soup by adding some more cream.” “Extend the casserole with a little rice.”

    Synonyms: extend · A kind of: increase

  11. Extend one's body or limbs.

    “Let's stretch for a minute--we've been sitting here for over 3 hours.”

    Synonyms: stretch out · A kind of: move

Stretch as a noun

  1. A large and unbroken expanse or distance.

    “A stretch of highway.” “A stretch of clear water.”

    A kind of: expanse · Kinds: slack

  2. The act of physically reaching or thrusting out.

    Synonyms: reach, reaching · A kind of: motion · Kinds: outreach

  3. A straightaway section of a racetrack.

    A kind of: straightaway · Kinds: homestretch

  4. Exercise designed to extend the limbs and muscles to their full extent.

    Synonyms: stretching · A kind of: exercise · Kinds: pandiculation

  5. Extension to or beyond the ordinary limit.

    “Running at full stretch.” “By no stretch of the imagination.” “Beyond any stretch of his understanding.”

    A kind of: extension

  6. An unbroken period of time during which you do something.

    “There were stretches of boredom.” “He did a stretch in the federal penitentiary.”

    Synonyms: stint · A kind of: duration

  7. The capacity for being stretched.

    Synonyms: stretchiness, stretchability · A kind of: elasticity

Stretch as an adjective

  1. Having an elongated seating area.

    “A stretch limousine.”

    Similar: long

  2. Easily stretched.

    “Stretch hosiery.”

    Similar: elastic

Phrases and compounds with "stretch"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • stretch out (verb) Extend or stretch out to a greater or the full length.
  • stretch along (verb) Occupy a large, elongated area.
  • stretch forth (verb) Thrust or extend out.
  • stretch pants (noun) Trousers made of a stretchy fabric.
  • stretch reflex (noun) Reflex contraction of a muscle when an attached tendon is pulled; important in maintaining erect posture.
  • stretch receptor (noun) A receptor in a muscle that responds to stretching of the muscle tissue.
