Swing: meaning, synonyms and examples

Swing is used as a verb (12 senses) and noun (9 senses). Its most common meaning is: Move in a curve or arc, usually with the intent of hitting.

In Hindi
झूलना; झूला
Synonyms
sway, swing over, sweep, swing out, dangle, drop, get around, swinging, vacillation, swing music
Word family
swinger, swinging, swingy

Swing as a verb

  1. Move in a curve or arc, usually with the intent of hitting.

    “He swung his left fist.” “Swing a bat.”

    A kind of: move · Kinds: wind up

  2. Move or walk in a swinging or swaying manner.

    “He swung back.”

    Synonyms: sway · A kind of: move back and forth · Kinds: waver, lash, oscillate, brachiate

  3. Change direction with a swinging motion; turn.

    “Swing back.” “Swing forward.”

    A kind of: travel

  4. Influence decisively.

    “This action swung many votes over to his side.”

    Synonyms: swing over · A kind of: influence

  5. Make a big sweeping gesture or movement.

    Synonyms: sweep, swing out · A kind of: wield

  6. Hang freely.

    “The ornaments dangled from the tree.” “The light dropped from the ceiling.”

    Synonyms: dangle, drop · A kind of: hang · Kinds: droop

  7. Hit or aim at with a sweeping arm movement.

    “The soccer player began to swing at the referee.”

    A kind of: aim

  8. Alternate dramatically between high and low values.

    “His mood swings.” “The market is swinging up and down.”

    A kind of: change · Kinds: fluctuate

  9. Live in a lively, modern, and relaxed style.

    “The Woodstock generation attempted to swing freely.”

    A kind of: live

  10. Have a certain musical rhythm.

    “The music has to swing.”

    A kind of: be

  11. Be a social swinger; socialize a lot.

    Synonyms: get around · A kind of: socialize

  12. Play with a subtle and intuitively felt sense of rhythm.

    A kind of: play

Swing as a noun

  1. A state of steady vigorous action that is characteristic of an activity.

    “The party went with a swing.” “It took time to get into the swing of things.”

    A kind of: action

  2. Mechanical device used as a plaything to support someone swinging back and forth.

    A kind of: mechanical device, plaything · Kinds: trapeze

  3. A sweeping blow or stroke.

    “He took a wild swing at my head.”

    A kind of: blow

  4. Changing location by moving back and forth.

    Synonyms: swinging, vacillation · A kind of: motion

  5. A style of jazz played by big bands popular in the 1930s; flowing rhythms but less complex than later styles of jazz.

    Synonyms: swing music, jive · A kind of: jazz

  6. A jaunty rhythm in music.

    Synonyms: lilt · A kind of: rhythmicity

  7. The act of swinging a golf club at a golf ball and (usually) hitting it.

    Synonyms: golf stroke, golf shot · A kind of: stroke · Kinds: downswing, slice, hook, drive, explosion, putt, approach, sclaff

  8. In baseball; a batter's attempt to hit a pitched ball.

    “He took a vicious cut at the ball.”

    Synonyms: baseball swing, cut · A kind of: stroke

  9. A square dance figure; a pair of dancers join hands and dance around a point between them.

    A kind of: country-dance

Phrases and compounds with "swing"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • swing out (verb) Make a big sweeping gesture or movement.
  • swing door (noun) A door that swings on a double hinge; opens in either direction.
  • swing over (verb) Influence decisively.
  • swing about (verb) Turn abruptly and face the other way, either physically or metaphorically.
  • swing music (noun) A style of jazz played by big bands popular in the 1930s; flowing rhythms but less complex than later styles of jazz.
  • swing shift (noun) The work shift during the evening (as 4 p.m. to midnight).
  • swing voter (noun) A voter who has no allegiance to any political party and whose unpredictable decisions can swing the outcome of an election one way or the other.
  • swing around (verb) Turn abruptly and face the other way, either physically or metaphorically.
  • in full swing (adverb) Proceeding with full vigor.
  • king of swing (noun) United States clarinetist who in 1934 formed a big band (including black as well as white musicians) and introduced a kind of jazz known as swing (1909-1986).
  • baseball swing (noun) In baseball; a batter's attempt to hit a pitched ball.
