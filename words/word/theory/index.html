Theory: meaning, synonyms and examples

Theory is used as a noun (3 senses). Its most common meaning is: A well-substantiated explanation of some aspect of the natural world; an organized system of accepted knowledge that applies in a variety of circumstances to explain a specific set of phenomena.

In Hindi
सिद्धांत
Synonyms
hypothesis, possibility
Word family
theorist, theoretic, theoretician, theorize, theorise

Theory as a noun

  1. A well-substantiated explanation of some aspect of the natural world; an organized system of accepted knowledge that applies in a variety of circumstances to explain a specific set of phenomena.

    “Theories can incorporate facts and laws and tested hypotheses.” “True in fact and theory.”

    A kind of: explanation · Kinds: reductionism, blastogenesis, preformation, scientific theory, field theory, economic theory, atomism, holism

  2. A tentative insight into the natural world; a concept that is not yet verified but that if true would explain certain facts or phenomena.

    “A scientific hypothesis that survives experimental testing becomes a scientific theory.” “He proposed a fresh theory of alkalis that later was accepted in chemical practices.”

    Synonyms: hypothesis, possibility · A kind of: concept · Kinds: hypothetical, gemmule, model, speculation, assumption, historicism

  3. A belief that can guide behavior.

    “The architect has a theory that more is less.”

    A kind of: belief · Kinds: egoism, patchwork

Phrases and compounds with "theory"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • m-theory (noun) (particle physics) a theory that involves an eleven-dimensional universe in which the weak and strong forces and gravity are unified and to which all the string theories belong.
  • in theory (adverb) With regard to fundamentals although not concerning details.
  • set theory (noun) The branch of pure mathematics that deals with the nature and relations of sets.
  • bohr theory (noun) (physics) a theory of atomic structure that combined Rutherford's model with the quantum theory; electrons orbiting a nucleus can only be in certain stationary energy states and light is emitted when electrons jump from one energy state to another.
  • cell theory (noun) (biology) the theory that cells form the fundamental structural and functional units of all living organisms; proposed in 1838 by Matthias Schleiden and by Theodor Schwann.
  • game theory (noun) (economics) a theory of competition stated in terms of gains and losses among opposing players.
  • germ theory (noun) (medicine) the theory that all contagious diseases are caused by microorganisms.
  • wave theory (noun) (physics) the theory that light is transmitted as waves.
  • field theory (noun) (physics) a theory that explains a physical phenomenon in terms of a field and the manner in which it interacts with matter or with other fields.
  • group theory (noun) The branch of mathematics dealing with groups.
  • theory-based (adjective) Based in theory rather than experiment.
  • atomic theory (noun) A theory of the structure of the atom.
  • domino theory (noun) The political theory that if one nation comes under communist control then neighboring nations will also come under communist control.
  • galois theory (noun) Group theory applied to the solution of algebraic equations.
  • string theory (noun) (particle physics) a theory that postulates that subatomic particles are one-dimensional strings.
  • atomist theory (noun) (chemistry) any theory in which all matter is composed of tiny discrete finite indivisible indestructible particles.
