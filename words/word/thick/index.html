Thick: meaning, synonyms and examples

Thick is used as an adjective (10 senses), noun (1 sense) and adverb (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Not thin; of a specific thickness or of relatively great extent from one surface to the opposite usually in the smallest of the three solid dimensions.

Thick as an adjective

  1. Not thin; of a specific thickness or of relatively great extent from one surface to the opposite usually in the smallest of the three solid dimensions.

    “An inch thick.” “A thick board.” “A thick sandwich.”

    Opposite: thin · Similar: deep, deep-chested, fat, four-ply, heavy, heavy

  2. Having component parts closely crowded together.

    “A compact shopping center.” “A dense population.” “Thick crowds.”

    Similar: concentrated

  3. Relatively dense in consistency.

    “Thick cream.” “Thick soup.” “Thick smoke.”

    Opposite: thin · Similar: clogged, coagulable, coagulate, creamy, dense, gelatinous

  4. Spoken as if with a thick tongue.

    “His words were slurred.”

    Synonyms: slurred · Similar: unintelligible

  5. Having a short and solid form or stature.

    “A wrestler of compact build.” “He was tall and heavyset.” “Stocky legs.”

    Synonyms: compact, heavyset, stocky, thickset · Similar: short

  6. Hard to pass through because of dense growth.

    “Dense vegetation.” “Thick woods.”

    Synonyms: dense · Similar: impenetrable

  7. (of darkness) very intense.

    “Thick night.” “Thick darkness.” “A face in deep shadow.”

    Synonyms: deep · Similar: intense

  8. (used informally) associated on close terms.

    “A close friend.” “The bartender was chummy with the regular customers.” “The two were thick as thieves for months.”

    Synonyms: chummy, buddy-buddy · Similar: close

  9. (used informally) stupid.

    Synonyms: blockheaded, boneheaded, duncical, duncish, fatheaded, loggerheaded, thickheaded, thick-skulled · Similar: stupid

  10. Abounding; having a lot of.

    “The top was thick with dust.”

    Similar: abundant

Thick as a noun

  1. The location of something surrounded by other things.

    “In the midst of the crowd.”

    Synonyms: midst · A kind of: inside

Thick as an adverb

  1. With a thick consistency.

    “The blood was flowing thick.”

    Synonyms: thickly

  2. In quick succession.

    “Misfortunes come fast and thick.”

    Synonyms: thickly

Phrases and compounds with "thick"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • thick skin (noun) Skin that is very thick (as an elephant or rhinoceros).
  • thick-knee (noun) Large-headed large-eyed crepuscular or nocturnal shorebird of the Old World and tropical America having a thickened knee joint.
  • thick-billed (adjective) Having a thick beak.
  • thick-bodied (adjective) Having a thick body.
  • thick-haired (adjective) Having thick hair.
  • thick-lipped (adjective) Having thick lips.
  • thick-skinned (adjective) Insensitive to criticism.
  • thick-skulled (adjective) (used informally) stupid.
  • thick-stemmed (adjective) Having a thick stem.
  • thick-branched (adjective) Having thick branches.
  • thick-billed murre (noun) A variety of murre.
  • thick-footed morel (noun) A delicious morel with a conic fertile portion having deep and irregular pits.
