Throw: meaning, synonyms and examples

Throw is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: Propel through the air.

In Hindi
फेंकना
Synonyms
shed, cast, cast off, shake off, throw off, throw away, drop, thrust, give, flip
Word family
thrower

Throw as a verb

  1. Propel through the air.

    “Throw a frisbee.”

    A kind of: propel · Kinds: slam, ground, prostrate, hurl, heave, pelt, defenestrate, deliver

  2. Move violently, energetically, or carelessly.

    “She threw herself forwards.”

    A kind of: move

  3. Get rid of.

    “He shed his image as a pushy boss.” “Shed your clothes.”

    Synonyms: shed, cast, cast off, shake off, throw off, throw away, drop · A kind of: remove · Kinds: shed, abscise, exfoliate, autotomize

  4. Place or put with great energy.

    “She threw the blanket around the child.” “Thrust the money in the hands of the beggar.”

    Synonyms: thrust · A kind of: put · Kinds: pop

  5. Convey or communicate; of a smile, a look, a physical gesture.

    “Throw a glance.” “She gave me a dirty look.”

    Synonyms: give · A kind of: communicate

  6. Cause to go on or to be engaged or set in operation.

    “Switch on the light.” “Throw the lever.”

    Synonyms: flip, switch · A kind of: engage · Kinds: switch on, switch off

  7. Put or send forth.

    “She threw the flashlight beam into the corner.” “The setting sun threw long shadows.” “Cast a spell.”

    Synonyms: project, cast, contrive · A kind of: send · Kinds: shoot

  8. To put into a state or activity hastily, suddenly, or carelessly.

    “Jane threw dinner together.” “Throw the car into reverse.”

    A kind of: put

  9. Cause to be confused emotionally.

    Synonyms: bewilder, bemuse, discombobulate · A kind of: upset

  10. Utter with force; utter vehemently.

    “Hurl insults.” “Throw accusations at someone.”

    Synonyms: hurl · A kind of: express

  11. Organize or be responsible for.

    “Hold a reception.” “Have, throw, or make a party.” “Give a course.”

    Synonyms: hold, have, make, give · A kind of: direct

  12. Make on a potter's wheel.

    “She threw a beautiful teapot.”

    A kind of: shape

  13. Cause to fall off.

    “The horse threw its inexperienced rider.”

    A kind of: dislodge

  14. Throw (a die) out onto a flat surface.

    “Throw a six.”

    A kind of: turn · Kinds: shoot

Throw as a noun

  1. The act of throwing (propelling something with a rapid movement of the arm and wrist).

    “The catcher made a good throw to second base.”

    A kind of: propulsion · Kinds: bowling, fling, heave, hurl, leaner, pass, pitch, pitch

  2. A single chance or instance.

    “He couldn't afford $50 a throw.”

    A kind of: opportunity

  3. The maximum movement available to a pivoted or reciprocating piece by a cam.

    Synonyms: stroke, cam stroke · A kind of: movement · Kinds: instroke, outstroke

  4. Bedclothes consisting of a lightweight cloth covering (an afghan or bedspread) that is casually thrown over something.

    A kind of: bedclothes

  5. Casting an object in order to determine an outcome randomly.

    “He risked his fortune on a throw of the dice.”

    Kinds: cast, flip

Phrases and compounds with "throw"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • throw in (verb) Add as an extra or as a gratuity.
  • throw up (verb) Eject the contents of the stomach through the mouth.
  • throw-in (noun) (rugby) an act or instance of throwing a ball in to put it into play.
  • throw off (verb) Get rid of.
  • throw out (verb) Force to leave or move out.
  • throw rug (noun) A small rug; several can be used in a room.
  • free throw (noun) An unhindered basketball shot from the foul line; given to penalize the other team for committing a foul.
  • throw away (verb) Throw or cast away.
  • throw back (verb) Throw back with a quick, light motion.
  • throw a fit (verb) Get very angry and fly into a rage.
  • throw stick (noun) A curved piece of wood; when properly thrown will return to thrower.
  • hammer throw (noun) An athletic competition in which a heavy metal ball that is attached to a flexible wire is hurled as far as possible.
  • throw pillow (noun) A small cushion that is used for decorative purposes.
  • throw-weight (noun) The weight of the payload of a missile (not including the weight of the rocket).
  • charity throw (noun) An unhindered basketball shot from the foul line; given to penalize the other team for committing a foul.
  • stone's throw (noun) A short distance.
