Touch: meaning, synonyms and examples

Touch is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (12 senses). Its most common meaning is: Make physical contact with, come in contact with.

In Hindi
छूना; स्पर्श
Synonyms
stir, refer, pertain, relate, concern, come to, bear on, touch on, have-to doe with, adjoin
Word family
toucher, touching

Touch as a verb

  1. Make physical contact with, come in contact with.

    “Touch the stone for good luck.” “She never touched her husband.”

    Kinds: engage, touch, toe, pick up, strike, finger, palpate, handle

  2. Perceive via the tactile sense.

    “Helen Keller felt the physical world by touching people and objects around her.”

    A kind of: perceive

  3. Affect emotionally.

    “A stirring movie.” “I was touched by your kind letter of sympathy.”

    Synonyms: stir · A kind of: affect · Kinds: get

  4. Be relevant to.

    “There were lots of questions referring to her talk.” “My remark pertained to your earlier comments.”

    Synonyms: refer, pertain, relate, concern, come to, bear on, touch on, have-to doe with · Kinds: focus on, apply, involve, matter to

  5. Be in direct physical contact with; make contact.

    “The two buildings touch.” “Their hands touched.” “The wire must not contact the metal cover.”

    Synonyms: adjoin, meet, contact · Kinds: cover, cling, rub, attach, hug, border, surround, lean on

  6. Have an effect upon.

    “Will the new rules affect me?”

    Synonyms: affect, impact, bear upon, bear on, touch on · A kind of: change · Kinds: strike a blow, repercuss, tell on, redound, stimulate, process, hydrolize, tinge

  7. Deal with; usually used with a form of negation.

    “I wouldn't touch her with a ten-foot pole.”

    A kind of: manage

  8. Cause to be in brief contact with.

    “He touched his toes to the horse's flanks.”

    A kind of: touch

  9. To extend as far as.

    “The sunlight reached the wall.” “Can he reach?” “The chair must not touch the wall.”

    Synonyms: reach, extend to · A kind of: be · Kinds: reach into

  10. Be equal to in quality or ability.

    “Nothing can rival cotton for durability.” “Your performance doesn't even touch that of your colleagues.” “Her persistence and ambition only matches that of her parents.”

    Synonyms: equal, rival, match · A kind of: compete

  11. Tamper with.

    “Don't touch my CDs!”

    Synonyms: disturb · A kind of: change · Kinds: upset, violate

  12. Make a more or less disguised reference to.

    “He alluded to the problem but did not mention it.”

    Synonyms: allude, advert · A kind of: hint

  13. Comprehend.

    “He could not touch the meaning of the poem.”

    A kind of: understand

  14. Consume.

    “She didn't touch her food all night.”

    Synonyms: partake · A kind of: consume · Kinds: receive

Touch as a noun

  1. The event of something coming in contact with the body.

    “He longed for the touch of her hand.” “The cooling touch of the night air.”

    Synonyms: touching · A kind of: contact · Kinds: brush, stroke

  2. The faculty by which external objects or forces are perceived through contact with the body (especially the hands).

    “Only sight and touch enable us to locate objects in the space around us.”

    Synonyms: sense of touch, skin senses, touch modality, cutaneous senses · A kind of: somatosense, exteroception

  3. A suggestion of some quality.

    “There was a touch of sarcasm in his tone.” “He detected a ghost of a smile on her face.”

    Synonyms: trace, ghost · A kind of: suggestion

  4. A distinguishing style.

    “This room needs a woman's touch.”

    Synonyms: signature · A kind of: manner · Kinds: common touch

  5. The act of putting two things together with no space between them.

    “At his touch the room filled with lights.”

    Synonyms: touching · A kind of: act · Kinds: contact, tap, hit, dig, kiss, kiss, catch, handling

  6. A slight but appreciable amount.

    “This dish could use a touch of garlic.”

    Synonyms: hint, tinge, mite, pinch, jot, speck, soupcon · A kind of: small indefinite quantity · Kinds: snuff

  7. A communicative interaction.

    “The pilot made contact with the base.” “He got in touch with his colleagues.”

    Synonyms: contact · A kind of: communication

  8. A slight attack of illness.

    “He has a touch of rheumatism.”

    Synonyms: spot · A kind of: attack

  9. The act of soliciting money (as a gift or loan).

    “He watched the beggar trying to make a touch.”

    A kind of: solicitation

  10. The sensation produced by pressure receptors in the skin.

    “She likes the touch of silk on her skin.” “The surface had a greasy feeling.”

    Synonyms: touch sensation, tactual sensation, tactile sensation, feeling · A kind of: perception · Kinds: creepiness, cutaneous sensation

  11. Deftness in handling matters.

    “He has a master's touch.”

    A kind of: adeptness · Kinds: finishing touch

  12. The feel of mechanical action.

    “This piano has a wonderful touch.”

    A kind of: tactile property

Phrases and compounds with "touch"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • touch on (verb) Refer to or discuss briefly.
  • touch up (verb) Alter so as to produce a more desirable appearance.
  • touch off (verb) Put in motion or move to act.
  • soft touch (noun) A person who is gullible and easy to take advantage of.
  • touch base (verb) Establish communication with someone.
  • touch down (verb) Come or bring (a plane) to a landing.
  • touch-type (verb) Type without looking at the keyboard.
  • light touch (noun) Momentary contact.
  • midas touch (noun) An ability to make and manage large amounts of money.
  • common touch (noun) The property of appealing to people in general (usually by appearing to have qualities in common with them).
  • get in touch (verb) Establish communication with someone.
  • touch screen (noun) A computer display that enables the user to interact with the computer by touching areas on the screen.
  • touch system (noun) Typewriting in which the fingers are trained to hit particular keys; typist can read and type at the same time.
  • touch typing (noun) Typewriting in which the fingers are trained to hit particular keys; typist can read and type at the same time.
  • touch-and-go (adjective) Fraught with danger.
  • touch-me-not (noun) North American annual plant with usually yellow or orange flowers; grows chiefly on wet rather acid soil.
