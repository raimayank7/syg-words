Tree: meaning, synonyms and examples

Tree is used as a noun (3 senses) and verb (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: A tall perennial woody plant having a main trunk and branches forming a distinct elevated crown; includes both gymnosperms and angiosperms.

In Hindi
पेड़
Synonyms
tree diagram, corner, shoetree
Word family
treelet

Tree as a noun

  1. A tall perennial woody plant having a main trunk and branches forming a distinct elevated crown; includes both gymnosperms and angiosperms.

    A kind of: woody plant · Kinds: yellowwood, lancewood, anise tree, winter's bark, zebrawood, granadilla tree, acacia, coralwood

  2. A figure that branches from a single root.

    “Genealogical tree.”

    Synonyms: tree diagram · A kind of: plane figure · Kinds: cladogram, stemma

  3. English actor and theatrical producer noted for his lavish productions of Shakespeare (1853-1917).

Tree as a verb

  1. Force a person or an animal into a position from which he cannot escape.

    Synonyms: corner · A kind of: steer

  2. Plant with trees.

    “This lot should be treed so that the house will be shaded in summer.”

    A kind of: plant

  3. Chase an animal up a tree.

    “Her dog likes to tree squirrels.”

    A kind of: chase

  4. Stretch (a shoe) on a shoetree.

    Synonyms: shoetree · A kind of: elongate

Phrases and compounds with "tree"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • bo tree (noun) Fig tree of India noted for great size and longevity; lacks the prop roots of the banyan; regarded as sacred by Buddhists.
  • ash tree (noun) Any of various deciduous pinnate-leaved ornamental or timber trees of the genus Fraxinus.
  • bay tree (noun) Small Mediterranean evergreen tree with small blackish berries and glossy aromatic leaves used for flavoring in cooking; also used by ancient Greeks to crown victors.
  • big tree (noun) Extremely lofty evergreen of southern end of western foothills of Sierra Nevada in California; largest living organism.
  • elm tree (noun) Any of various trees of the genus Ulmus: important timber or shade trees.
  • fig tree (noun) Any moraceous tree of the tropical genus Ficus; produces a closed pear-shaped receptacle that becomes fleshy and edible when mature.
  • fir tree (noun) Any of various evergreen trees of the genus Abies; chiefly of upland areas.
  • god tree (noun) Massive tropical tree with deep ridges on its massive trunk and bearing large pods of seeds covered with silky floss; source of the silky kapok fiber.
  • gum tree (noun) Any of various trees of the genera Eucalyptus or Liquidambar or Nyssa that are sources of gum.
  • nim tree (noun) Large semi-evergreen tree of the East Indies; trunk exudes a tenacious gum; bitter bark used as a tonic; seeds yield an aromatic oil; sometimes placed in genus Melia.
  • nut tree (noun) Tree bearing edible nuts.
  • oak tree (noun) A deciduous tree of the genus Quercus; has acorns and lobed leaves.
  • pea tree (noun) Any plant of the genus Caragana having even-pinnate leaves and mostly yellow flowers followed by seeds in a linear pod.
  • wig tree (noun) Old World shrub having large plumes of yellowish feathery flowers resembling puffs of smoke.
  • akee tree (noun) Widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions for its fragrant flowers and colorful fruits; introduced in Jamaica by William Bligh.
  • bead tree (noun) Small tree of West Indies and northeastern Venezuela having large oblong pointed leaflets and panicles of purple flowers; seeds are black or scarlet with black spots.
