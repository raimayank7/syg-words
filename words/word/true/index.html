True: meaning, synonyms and examples

True is used as an adjective (12 senses), adverb (1 sense), noun (1 sense) and verb (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: Consistent with fact or reality; not false.

True as an adjective

  1. Consistent with fact or reality; not false.

    “The story is true.” “It is undesirable to believe a proposition when there is no ground whatever for supposing it true.” “The true meaning of the statement.”

    Opposite: false · Similar: actual, apodictic, truthful, sure

  2. Accurately placed or thrown.

    “His aim was true.” “He was dead on target.”

    Synonyms: dead on target · Similar: accurate

  3. Devoted (sometimes fanatically) to a cause or concept or truth.

    “True believers bonded together against all who disagreed with them.”

    Similar: faithful

  4. Expressing or given to expressing the truth.

    “A true statement.” “Gave truthful testimony.” “A truthful person.”

    Synonyms: truthful · Similar: honest, veracious

  5. Conforming to definitive criteria.

    “The horseshoe crab is not a true crab.” “Pythagoras was the first true mathematician.”

    Similar: typical

  6. Worthy of being depended on.

    “A dependable worker.” “An honest working stiff.” “A reliable sourcSFLe of information.”

    Synonyms: dependable, honest, reliable · Similar: trustworthy

  7. Not pretended; sincerely felt or expressed.

    “Genuine emotion.” “Her interest in people was unfeigned.” “True grief.”

    Synonyms: genuine, unfeigned · Similar: sincere

  8. Rightly so called.

    “True courage.” “A spirit which true men have always admired.” “A true friend.”

    Similar: real

  9. Determined with reference to the earth's axis rather than the magnetic poles.

    “True north is geographic north.”

    Similar: geographic

  10. Having a legally established claim.

    “The legitimate heir.” “The true and lawful king.”

    Synonyms: lawful, rightful · Similar: legitimate

  11. In tune; accurate in pitch.

    “A true note.”

    Synonyms: on-key · Similar: harmonious

  12. Accurately fitted; level.

    “The window frame isn't quite true.”

    Synonyms: straight · Similar: even

True as an adverb

  1. As acknowledged.

    “True, she is the smartest in her class.”

    Synonyms: admittedly, avowedly, confessedly

True as a noun

  1. Proper alignment; the property possessed by something that is in correct or proper alignment.

    “Out of true.”

    A kind of: alignment

True as a verb

  1. Make level, square, balanced, or concentric.

    “True up the cylinder of an engine.”

    Synonyms: true up · A kind of: align

Phrases and compounds with "true"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • true up (verb) Make level, square, balanced, or concentric.
  • the true (noun) Conformity to reality or actuality.
  • true bug (noun) Any of various insects of the order Hemiptera and especially of the suborder Heteroptera.
  • true cat (noun) Feline mammal usually having thick soft fur and no ability to roar: domestic cats; wildcats.
  • true fir (noun) Any of various evergreen trees of the genus Abies; chiefly of upland areas.
  • true rib (noun) One of the first seven ribs in a human being which attach to the sternum.
  • true bill (noun) An indictment endorsed by a grand jury.
  • true frog (noun) Insectivorous usually semiaquatic web-footed amphibian with smooth moist skin and long hind legs.
  • true pine (noun) A coniferous tree.
  • true seal (noun) Any of several seals lacking external ear flaps and having a stiff hairlike coat with hind limbs reduced to swimming flippers.
  • true toad (noun) Tailless amphibian similar to a frog but more terrestrial and having drier warty skin.
  • true-blue (adjective) Marked by unswerving loyalty.
  • true cedar (noun) Any cedar of the genus Cedrus.
  • true guava (noun) Small tropical American shrubby tree; widely cultivated in warm regions for its sweet globular yellow fruit.
  • true heath (noun) Any plant of the genus Erica.
  • true senna (noun) Erect shrub having racemes of tawny yellow flowers; the dried leaves are used medicinally as a cathartic; sometimes placed in genus Cassia.
