Tube: meaning, synonyms and examples

Tube is used as a noun (5 senses) and verb (4 senses). Its most common meaning is: Conduit consisting of a long hollow object (usually cylindrical) used to hold and conduct objects or liquids or gases.

In Hindi
नली, ट्यूब
Synonyms
tubing, vacuum tube, thermionic vacuum tube, thermionic tube, electron tube, thermionic valve, pipe, tube-shaped structure, metro, underground
Word family
tubular, tubal, tubing

Tube as a noun

  1. Conduit consisting of a long hollow object (usually cylindrical) used to hold and conduct objects or liquids or gases.

    Synonyms: tubing · A kind of: conduit · Kinds: barrel, blowgun, blowtube, cannula, capillary, catheter, cigarette holder, coil

  2. Electronic device consisting of a system of electrodes arranged in an evacuated glass or metal envelope.

    Synonyms: vacuum tube, thermionic vacuum tube, thermionic tube, electron tube, thermionic valve · A kind of: electronic device · Kinds: acorn tube, diode, electron multiplier, gas-discharge tube, klystron, magnetron, pentode, television-camera tube

  3. A hollow cylindrical shape.

    Synonyms: pipe · A kind of: cylinder

  4. (anatomy) any hollow cylindrical body structure.

    Synonyms: tube-shaped structure · A kind of: structure · Kinds: tubule, salpinx, cochlea, vessel

  5. An electric railway operating below the surface of the ground (usually in a city).

    “In Paris the subway system is called the `metro' and in London it is called the `tube' or the `underground'.”

    Synonyms: metro, underground, subway system, subway · A kind of: railway

Tube as a verb

  1. Provide with a tube or insert a tube into.

    A kind of: supply

  2. Convey in a tube.

    “Inside Paris, they used to tube mail.”

    A kind of: bring

  3. Ride or float on an inflated tube.

    “We tubed down the river on a hot summer day.”

    A kind of: ride

  4. Place or enclose in a tube.

    A kind of: envelop

Phrases and compounds with "tube"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • blow tube (noun) A tube that directs air or gas into a flame to concentrate heat.
  • boob tube (noun) An electronic device that receives television signals and displays them on a screen.
  • germ tube (noun) (botany) a slender tubular outgrowth from a spore in germination.
  • glow tube (noun) A gas-discharge tube consisting of a cold cathode and a diode in a tube filled with gas; the color of the glow depends on the particular gas.
  • neon tube (noun) A lamp consisting of a small gas-discharge tube containing neon at low pressure; luminescence is produced by the action of currents at high frequencies that are wrapped a few turns around the tube.
  • test tube (noun) Glass tube closed at one end.
  • tube foot (noun) Tentacular tubular process of most echinoderms (starfish and sea urchins and holothurians) having a sucker at the end and used for e.g. locomotion and respiration.
  • tube well (noun) A well made by driving a tube into the earth to a stratum that bears water.
  • acorn tube (noun) A small vacuum tube; used at high frequencies.
  • calyx tube (noun) The cuplike or ringlike or tubular structure of a flower which bears the sepals and stamens and calyx (as in Rosaceae).
  • color tube (noun) A television tube that displays images in full color.
  • inner tube (noun) An inflatable rubber tube that fits inside the casing of a pneumatic tire.
  • pitot tube (noun) Measuring instrument consisting of a right-angled tube with an open end that is directed in opposition to the flow of a fluid and used to measure the velocity of fluid flow.
  • sieve tube (noun) Tube formed by cells joined end-to-end through which nutrients flow in flowering plants and brown algae.
  • tube-nosed (adjective) Having a tubular nose.
  • x-ray tube (noun) A vacuum tube containing a metal target onto which a beam of electrons is directed at high energy for the generation of X rays.
