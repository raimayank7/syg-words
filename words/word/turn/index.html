Turn: meaning, synonyms and examples

Turn is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (11 senses). Its most common meaning is: Change orientation or direction, also in the abstract sense.

In Hindi
मुड़ना, घुमाना; बारी
Synonyms
change state, become, change by reversal, reverse, move around, grow, release, turn over, plow, plough
Word family
turning, turner, turnery

Turn as a verb

  1. Change orientation or direction, also in the abstract sense.

    “Turn towards me.” “The mugger turned and fled before I could see his face.” “She turned from herself and learned to listen to others' needs.”

    A kind of: move · Kinds: twist, flip, turn on a dime, roll, roll over, swing around, flip, port

  2. Undergo a transformation or a change of position or action.

    “We turned from Socialism to Capitalism.” “The people turned against the President when he stole the election.”

    Synonyms: change state · A kind of: change · Kinds: wake up, zonk out, come to, tense, relax, reduce, gain, dress

  3. Undergo a change or development.

    “The water turned into ice.” “Her former friend became her worst enemy.” “He turned traitor.”

    Synonyms: become · A kind of: transform · Kinds: reduce, suffocate, nucleate, come

  4. Cause to move around or rotate.

    “Turn a key.” “Turn your palm this way.”

    A kind of: move · Kinds: reorient, supinate, port

  5. Change to the contrary.

    “The trend was reversed.” “The tides turned against him.” “Public opinion turned when it was revealed that the president had an affair with a White House intern.”

    Synonyms: change by reversal, reverse · A kind of: change · Kinds: interchange, turn the tables, commutate, switch over, correct, falsify, permute, metamorphose

  6. Pass to the other side of.

    “Turn the corner.” “Move around the obstacle.”

    Synonyms: move around · A kind of: travel

  7. Pass into a condition gradually, take on a specific property or attribute; become.

    “The weather turned nasty.” “She grew angry.”

    Synonyms: grow · A kind of: change · Kinds: bald, turn

  8. Let (something) fall or spill from a container.

    “Turn the flour onto a plate.”

    Synonyms: release · A kind of: transmit · Kinds: deflate, throw

  9. Move around an axis or a center.

    “The wheels are turning.”

    A kind of: move · Kinds: revolve

  10. Cause to move around a center so as to show another side of.

    “Turn a page of a book.”

    Synonyms: turn over · A kind of: move · Kinds: evert, leaf

  11. To send or let go.

    “They turned away the crowd at the gate of the governor's mansion.”

    A kind of: send

  12. To break and turn over earth especially with a plow.

    “Farmer Jones plowed his east field last week.” “Turn the earth in the Spring.”

    Synonyms: plow, plough · A kind of: till · Kinds: ridge, harrow

  13. Shape by rotating on a lathe or cutting device or a wheel.

    “Turn the legs of the table.” “Turn the clay on the wheel.”

    A kind of: shape

  14. Change color.

    “In Vermont, the leaves turn early.”

    A kind of: turn, discolor

Turn as a noun

  1. A circular segment of a curve.

    “A bend in the road.” “A crook in the path.”

    Synonyms: bend, crook, twist · A kind of: curve · Kinds: bight

  2. The act of changing or reversing the direction of the course.

    “He took a turn to the right.”

    Synonyms: turning · A kind of: change of course · Kinds: diversion, right, left, kick turn, stem turn, telemark, swerve, three-point turn

  3. (game) the activity of doing something in an agreed succession.

    “It is my turn.” “It is still my play.”

    Synonyms: play · A kind of: activity · Kinds: move, start, innings, down, bat, lead, ruff, trick

  4. An unforeseen development.

    “Events suddenly took an awkward turn.”

    Synonyms: turn of events, twist · A kind of: development

  5. A movement in a new direction.

    “The turning of the wind.”

    Synonyms: turning · A kind of: movement · Kinds: reversal, yaw, rotation, return, volution

  6. The act of turning away or in the opposite direction.

    “He made an abrupt turn away from her.”

    A kind of: change of direction

  7. Turning or twisting around (in place).

    “With a quick twist of his head he surveyed the room.”

    Synonyms: twist · A kind of: rotation · Kinds: twiddle

  8. A time for working (after which you will be relieved by someone else).

    “It's my go.” “A spell of work.”

    Synonyms: go, spell, tour · A kind of: shift

  9. A short theatrical performance that is part of a longer program.

    “He did his act three times every evening.” “She had a catchy little routine.” “It was one of the best numbers he ever did.”

    Synonyms: act, routine, number, bit · A kind of: performance · Kinds: show-stopper

  10. A favor for someone.

    “He did me a good turn.”

    Synonyms: good turn · A kind of: favor

  11. Taking a short walk out and back.

    “We took a turn in the park.”

    A kind of: walk

Phrases and compounds with "turn"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • u-turn (noun) Complete reversal of direction of travel.
  • in turn (adverb) In proper order or sequence.
  • turn in (verb) Make an entrance by turning from a road.
  • turn on (verb) Cause to operate by flipping a switch.
  • turn to (verb) Speak to.
  • turn up (verb) Appear or become visible; make a showing.
  • turn-on (noun) Something causing excitement or stimulating interest.
  • ill turn (noun) An act intended to help that turns out badly.
  • turn off (verb) Cause to stop operating by disengaging a switch.
  • turn out (verb) Be shown or be found to be.
  • good turn (noun) A favor for someone.
  • kick turn (noun) A standing turn made in skiing; one ski is raised to the vertical and pivoted backward to become parallel with the other ski but headed in the opposite direction and then the other ski is aligned with the first.
  • stem turn (noun) A turn made in skiing; the back of one ski is forced outward and the other ski is brought parallel to it.
  • turn away (verb) Move so as not face somebody or something.
  • turn back (verb) Retrace one's course.
  • turn down (verb) Refuse to accept.
