Wave: meaning, synonyms and examples

Wave is used as a noun (9 senses) and verb (5 senses). Its most common meaning is: One of a series of ridges that moves across the surface of a liquid (especially across a large body of water).

In Hindi
लहर; हाथ हिलाना
Synonyms
moving ridge, undulation, waving, wafture, beckon, brandish, flourish, roll, undulate, flap
Word family
wavelet, wavy, waver, waving

Wave as a noun

  1. One of a series of ridges that moves across the surface of a liquid (especially across a large body of water).

    Synonyms: moving ridge · A kind of: movement · Kinds: surf, wake, swash, ripple, swell, lift, billow, tidal wave

  2. A movement like that of a sudden occurrence or increase in a specified phenomenon.

    “A wave of settlers.” “Troops advancing in waves.”

    A kind of: motion

  3. (physics) a movement up and down or back and forth.

    Synonyms: undulation · A kind of: movement · Kinds: gravity wave, sine wave, oscillation, fluctuation, seiche, standing wave, traveling wave, sound wave

  4. Something that rises rapidly.

    “A wave of emotion swept over him.” “There was a sudden wave of buying before the market closed.” “A wave of conservatism in the country led by the hard right.”

    A kind of: rise

  5. The act of signaling by a movement of the hand.

    Synonyms: waving, wafture · A kind of: gesture · Kinds: flourish

  6. A hairdo that creates undulations in the hair.

    A kind of: hairdo · Kinds: finger wave, permanent wave

  7. An undulating curve.

    Synonyms: undulation · A kind of: curve · Kinds: sine curve

  8. A persistent and widespread unusual weather condition (especially of unusual temperatures).

    “A heat wave.”

    A kind of: weather · Kinds: cold wave, heat wave

  9. A member of the women's reserve of the United States Navy; originally organized during World War II but now no longer a separate branch.

    A kind of: reservist, woman

Wave as a verb

  1. Signal with the hands or nod.

    “She waved to her friends.” “He waved his hand hospitably.”

    Synonyms: beckon · A kind of: gesticulate

  2. Move or swing back and forth.

    “She waved her gun.”

    Synonyms: brandish, flourish · A kind of: move · Kinds: wigwag

  3. Move in a wavy pattern or with a rising and falling motion.

    “The curtains undulated.” “The waves rolled towards the beach.”

    Synonyms: roll, undulate, flap · A kind of: move · Kinds: luff

  4. Twist or roll into coils or ringlets.

    “Curl my hair, please.”

    Synonyms: curl · A kind of: twist · Kinds: crimp

  5. Set waves in.

    “She asked the hairdresser to wave her hair.”

    A kind of: dress · Kinds: marcel, gauffer, perm

Phrases and compounds with "wave"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • air wave (noun) A sound wave that is transmitted via air.
  • new wave (noun) An art movement in French cinema in the 1960s.
  • sky wave (noun) A radio wave that is reflected back to earth by the ionosphere or a communications satellite; permits transmission around the curve of the earth's surface.
  • wave off (verb) Dismiss as insignificant.
  • wave-off (noun) An approach that fails and gives way to another attempt.
  • beta wave (noun) The normal brainwave in the encephalogram of a person who is awake and alert; occurs with a frequency between 12 and 30 hertz.
  • cold wave (noun) A wave of unusually cold weather.
  • heat wave (noun) A wave of unusually hot weather.
  • long wave (noun) A radio wave with a wavelength longer than a kilometer (a frequency below 300 kilohertz).
  • sine wave (noun) A wave whose waveform resembles a sine curve.
  • wave form (noun) The shape of a wave illustrated graphically by plotting the values of the period quantity against time.
  • alpha wave (noun) The normal brainwave in the electroencephalogram of a person who is awake but relaxed; occurs with a frequency of 8-12 hertz.
  • blast wave (noun) A region of high pressure travelling through a gas at a high velocity.
  • brain wave (noun) (neurophysiology) rapid fluctuations of voltage between parts of the cerebral cortex that are detectable with an electroencephalograph.
  • crime wave (noun) A sudden rise in the crime rate.
  • delta wave (noun) The normal brainwave in the encephalogram of a person in deep dreamless sleep; occurs with high voltage and low frequency (1 to 4 hertz).
