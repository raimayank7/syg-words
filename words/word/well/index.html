Well: meaning, synonyms and examples

Well is used as an adverb (13 senses), adjective (3 senses), noun (5 senses) and verb (1 sense). Its most common meaning is: (often used as a combining form) in a good or proper or satisfactory manner or to a high standard (`good' is a nonstandard dialectal variant for `well').

In Hindi
अच्छी तरह; कुआँ
Synonyms
good, easily, considerably, substantially, intimately, advantageously, comfortably, wellspring, fountainhead, swell
Opposites
ill, badly
Word family
wellness

Well as an adverb

  1. (often used as a combining form) in a good or proper or satisfactory manner or to a high standard (`good' is a nonstandard dialectal variant for `well').

    “The children behaved well.” “A task well done.” “The party went well.”

    Synonyms: good · Opposite: ill

  2. Thoroughly or completely; fully; often used as a combining form.

    “The problem is well understood.” “She was well informed.” “Shake well before using.”

  3. Indicating high probability; in all likelihood.

    “I might well do it.” “A mistake that could easily have ended in disaster.” “You may well need your umbrella.”

    Synonyms: easily

  4. (used for emphasis or as an intensifier) entirely or fully.

    “A book well worth reading.” “Was well aware of the difficulties ahead.” “Suspected only too well what might be going on.”

  5. To a suitable or appropriate extent or degree.

    “The project was well underway.” “The fetus has well developed organs.” “His father was well pleased with his grades.”

  6. Favorably; with approval.

    “Their neighbors spoke well of them.” “He thought well of the book.”

    Opposite: ill

  7. To a great extent or degree.

    “I'm afraid the film was well over budget.” “Painting the room white made it seem considerably (or substantially) larger.” “The house has fallen considerably in value.”

    Synonyms: considerably, substantially

  8. With great or especially intimate knowledge.

    “We knew them well.”

    Synonyms: intimately

  9. With prudence or propriety.

    “You would do well to say nothing more.” “Could not well refuse.”

  10. With skill or in a pleasing manner.

    “She dances well.” “He writes well.”

    Opposite: badly

  11. In a manner affording benefit or advantage.

    “She married well.” “The children were settled advantageously in Seattle.”

    Synonyms: advantageously · Opposite: badly

  12. In financial comfort.

    “They live well.” “She has been able to live comfortably since her husband died.”

    Synonyms: comfortably

  13. Without unusual distress or resentment; with good humor.

    “Took the joke well.” “Took the tragic news well.”

    Opposite: badly

Well as an adjective

  1. In good health especially after having suffered illness or injury.

    “Appears to be entirely well.” “The wound is nearly well.” “A well man.”

    Opposite: ill · Similar: asymptomatic, cured

  2. Resulting favorably.

    “It's a good thing that I wasn't there.” “It is good that you stayed.” “It is well that no one saw you.”

    Synonyms: good · Similar: fortunate

  3. Wise or advantageous and hence advisable.

    “It would be well to start early.”

    Similar: advisable

Well as a noun

  1. A deep hole or shaft dug or drilled to obtain water or oil or gas or brine.

    A kind of: excavation · Kinds: artesian well, driven well, gas well, oil well, sump

  2. A cavity or vessel used to contain liquid.

    A kind of: vessel · Kinds: inkwell

  3. An abundant source.

    “She was a well of information.”

    Synonyms: wellspring, fountainhead · A kind of: source

  4. An open shaft through the floors of a building (as for a stairway).

    A kind of: shaft · Kinds: stairwell

  5. An enclosed compartment in a ship or plane for holding something as e.g. fish or a plane's landing gear or for protecting something as e.g. a ship's pumps.

    A kind of: compartment · Kinds: bilge well, pump well

Well as a verb

  1. Come up, as of a liquid.

    “Tears well in her eyes.” “The currents well up.”

    Synonyms: swell · A kind of: surface

Phrases and compounds with "well"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • as well (adverb) In addition.
  • be well (verb) Be healthy; feel good.
  • do well (verb) Act in one's own or everybody's best interest.
  • well up (verb) Come up (as of feelings and thoughts, or other ephemeral things).
  • air well (noun) A shaft for ventilation.
  • gas well (noun) A well that yields or has yielded natural gas.
  • get well (verb) Improve in health.
  • oil well (noun) A well that yields or has yielded oil.
  • well out (verb) Flow freely and abundantly.
  • well-fed (adjective) Properly nourished.
  • well-off (adjective) In fortunate circumstances financially; moderately rich.
  • well-set (adjective) Strongly and firmly constructed.
  • pump well (noun) An enclosure in the middle of a ship's hold that protects the ship's pumps.
  • tube well (noun) A well made by driving a tube into the earth to a stratum that bears water.
  • very well (adverb) Quite well.
  • well over (verb) Flow or run over (a limit or brim).
