Wild: meaning, synonyms and examples

Wild is used as an adjective (13 senses), adverb (2 senses) and noun (2 senses). Its most common meaning is: Marked by extreme lack of restraint or control.

In Hindi
जंगली
Synonyms
untamed, violent, baseless, groundless, idle, unfounded, unwarranted, raving mad, hazardous, risky
Opposites
tame
Word family
wildness, wilderness

Wild as an adjective

  1. Marked by extreme lack of restraint or control.

    “Wild talk.” “Wild parties.”

    Opposite: tame · Similar: chaotic, delirious, frenzied, unsubdued

  2. In a natural state; not tamed or domesticated or cultivated.

    “Wild geese.” “Edible wild plants.”

    Synonyms: untamed · Opposite: tame · Similar: feral, semi-wild, unbroken, undomesticated

  3. In a state of extreme emotion.

    “Wild with anger.” “Wild with grief.”

    Similar: passionate

  4. Deviating widely from an intended course.

    “A wild bullet.” “He threw a wild pitch.”

    Similar: uncontrolled

  5. (of colors or sounds) intensely vivid or loud.

    “A violent clash of colors.” “Her dress was a violent red.” “A violent noise.”

    Synonyms: violent · Similar: intense

  6. Without a basis in reason or fact.

    “Baseless gossip.” “The allegations proved groundless.” “Idle fears.”

    Synonyms: baseless, groundless, idle, unfounded, unwarranted · Similar: unsupported

  7. Talking or behaving irrationally.

    “A raving lunatic.”

    Synonyms: raving mad · Similar: insane

  8. Involving risk or danger.

    “Skydiving is a hazardous sport.” “Extremely risky going out in the tide and fog.” “A wild financial scheme.”

    Synonyms: hazardous, risky · Similar: dangerous

  9. Fanciful and unrealistic; foolish.

    “A fantastic idea of his own importance.”

    Synonyms: fantastic · Similar: unrealistic

  10. Located in a dismal or remote area; desolate.

    “A desert island.” “A godforsaken wilderness crossroads.” “A wild stretch of land.”

    Synonyms: godforsaken, waste · Similar: inhospitable

  11. Intensely enthusiastic about or preoccupied with.

    “Crazy about cars and racing.” “He is potty about her.”

    Synonyms: crazy, dotty, gaga · Similar: enthusiastic

  12. Without civilizing influences.

    “Barbarian invaders.” “Barbaric practices.” “A savage people.”

    Synonyms: barbarian, barbaric, savage, uncivilized, uncivilised · Similar: noncivilized

  13. (of the elements) as if showing violent anger.

    “Angry clouds on the horizon.” “Furious winds.” “The raging sea.”

    Synonyms: angry, furious, raging, tempestuous · Similar: stormy

Wild as an adverb

  1. In an uncontrolled and rampant manner.

    “Weeds grew rampantly around here.”

    Synonyms: rampantly

  2. In a wild or undomesticated manner.

    “Growing wild.” “Roaming wild.”

Wild as a noun

  1. A wild primitive state untouched by civilization.

    “He lived in the wild.” “They collected mushrooms in the wild.”

    Synonyms: natural state, state of nature · A kind of: state · Kinds: crudeness

  2. A wild and uninhabited area left in its natural condition.

    “It was a wilderness preserved for the hawks and mountaineers.”

    Synonyms: wilderness · A kind of: geographical area · Kinds: barren, bush, frontier

Phrases and compounds with "wild"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • wild ox (noun) Any of various wild bovines especially of the genera Bos or closely related Bibos.
  • wild ass (noun) Any of several equine mammals of Asia and northeast Africa.
  • wild dog (noun) Any of various undomesticated mammals of the family Canidae that are thought to resemble domestic dogs as distinguished from jackals or wolves.
  • wild fig (noun) A strangler tree native to southern Florida and West Indies; begins as an epiphyte eventually developing many thick aerial roots and covering enormous areas.
  • wild hop (noun) Bryony having fleshy roots pale green flowers and very small red berries; Europe; North Africa; western Asia.
  • wild man (noun) A person who is not socialized.
  • wild oat (noun) Common in meadows and pastures.
  • wild pea (noun) Any of various plants of the family Leguminosae that usually grow like vines.
  • wild rye (noun) Any of several grasses of the genus Elymus.
  • wild yam (noun) Having a rhizome formerly dried and used to treat rheumatism or liver disorders.
  • semi-wild (adjective) Partially wild.
  • wild bean (noun) A North American vine with fragrant blossoms and edible tubers; important food crop of Native Americans.
  • wild boar (noun) Old World wild swine having a narrow body and prominent tusks from which most domestic swine come; introduced in United States.
  • wild card (noun) An unpredictable factor.
  • wild cavy (noun) South American cavy; possibly ancestral to the domestic guinea pig.
  • wild crab (noun) Wild crab apple native to Europe; a chief ancestor of cultivated apples.
