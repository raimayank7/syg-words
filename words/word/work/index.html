Work: meaning, synonyms and examples

Work is used as a verb (14 senses) and noun (7 senses). Its most common meaning is: Exert oneself by doing mental or physical work for a purpose or out of necessity.

In Hindi
काम; काम करना
Synonyms
do work, act, function, operate, go, run, work on, process, exercise, work out
Opposites
idle
Word family
worker, workable

Work as a verb

  1. Exert oneself by doing mental or physical work for a purpose or out of necessity.

    “I will work hard to improve my grades.” “She worked hard for better living conditions for the poor.”

    Opposite: idle · Kinds: work through, putter, carpenter, clerk, page, wait, pull one's weight, electioneer

  2. Be employed.

    “Is your husband working again?” “My wife never worked.” “Do you want to work after the age of 60?”

    Synonyms: do work · Kinds: serve, bank, fill, drive, tinker, serve, fink, labor

  3. Have an effect or outcome; often the one desired or expected.

    “The voting process doesn't work as well as people thought.” “How does your idea work in practice?” “This method doesn't work.”

    Synonyms: act · A kind of: succeed

  4. Perform as expected when applied.

    “The washing machine won't go unless it's plugged in.” “Does this old car still run well?” “This old radio doesn't work anymore.”

    Synonyms: function, operate, go, run · Kinds: double, roll, run, cut, service

  5. Shape, form, or improve a material.

    “Work stone into tools.” “Process iron.” “Work the metal.”

    Synonyms: work on, process · A kind of: transform · Kinds: hot-work, coldwork, overwork, rework, rack, tool, till

  6. Give a workout to.

    “Some parents exercise their infants.” “My personal trainer works me hard.” “Work one's muscles.”

    Synonyms: exercise, work out · A kind of: work · Kinds: warm up

  7. Proceed along a path.

    “Work one's way through the crowd.” “Make one's way into the forest.”

    Synonyms: make · A kind of: pass · Kinds: claw, jostle, bushwhack

  8. Operate in a certain place, area, or specialty.

    “She works the night clubs.” “The salesman works the Midwest.” “This artist works mostly in acrylics.”

    A kind of: work

  9. Proceed towards a goal or along a path or through an activity.

    “Work your way through every problem or task.” “She was working on her second martini when the guests arrived.” “Start from the bottom and work towards the top.”

    A kind of: go

  10. Move in an agitated manner.

    “His fingers worked with tension.”

    A kind of: move

  11. Cause to happen or to occur as a consequence.

    “I cannot work a miracle.” “Wreak havoc.” “Bring comments.”

    Synonyms: bring, play, wreak, make for · A kind of: make

  12. Cause to work.

    “He is working his servants hard.”

    Synonyms: put to work · A kind of: use · Kinds: exercise, warm up, cybernate, rack, overwork

  13. Prepare for crops.

    “Work the soil.” “Cultivate the land.”

    Synonyms: cultivate, crop · A kind of: fix · Kinds: overcrop

  14. Behave in a certain way when handled.

    “This dough does not work easily.” “The soft metal works well.”

    A kind of: be

Work as a noun

  1. Activity directed toward making or doing something.

    “She checked several points needing further work.”

    A kind of: activity · Kinds: wash, action, job, job, operation, service, shining, heavy lifting

  2. A product produced or accomplished through the effort or activity or agency of a person or thing.

    “It is not regarded as one of his more memorable works.” “The symphony was hailed as an ingenious work.” “He was indebted to the pioneering work of John Dewey.”

    Synonyms: piece of work · A kind of: product · Kinds: follow-up, handicraft, ironwork, lacework, lacquerware, leatherwork, masterpiece, metalwork

  3. The occupation for which you are paid.

    “He is looking for employment.” “A lot of people are out of work.”

    Synonyms: employment · A kind of: occupation · Kinds: coaching, engagement, ministry, seafaring, workload, piecework, service, telecommuting

  4. Applying the mind to learning and understanding a subject (especially by reading).

    “Mastering a second language requires a lot of work.” “No schools offer graduate study in interior design.”

    Synonyms: study · A kind of: learning

  5. (physics) a manifestation of energy; the transfer of energy from one physical system to another expressed as the product of a force and the distance through which it moves a body in the direction of that force.

    “Work equals force times distance.”

    A kind of: energy

  6. A place where work is done.

    “He arrived at work early today.”

    Synonyms: workplace · A kind of: geographic point · Kinds: bakery, beehive, brokerage house, central, colliery, creamery, drill site, exchange

  7. The total output of a writer or artist (or a substantial part of it).

    “He studied the entire Wagnerian oeuvre.” “Picasso's work can be divided into periods.”

    Synonyms: oeuvre, body of work · A kind of: end product · Kinds: writing

Phrases and compounds with "work"

These combinations have meanings of their own, which often cannot be guessed from the single word.

  • at work (adjective) On the job.
  • do work (verb) Be employed.
  • work at (verb) To exert effort in order to do, make, or perform something.
  • work in (verb) Add by mixing or blending on or attaching.
  • work on (verb) To exert effort in order to do, make, or perform something.
  • work up (verb) Form or accumulate steadily.
  • work-in (noun) Occasion when workers continue to work as a protest against e.g. proposed dismissal or closure of the factory.
  • hot-work (verb) Roll, press, forge, or shape (metal) while hot.
  • work day (noun) A day on which work is done.
  • work off (verb) Cause to go away through effort or work.
  • work out (verb) Come up with.
  • work-shy (adjective) Disinclined to work or exertion.
  • cold work (verb) Shape (metal) without heat.
  • make-work (noun) Active work of little value.
  • scut work (noun) Trivial, unrewarding, tedious, dirty, and disagreeable chores.
  • work camp (noun) A camp for trustworthy prisoners employed in government projects.
